In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents a framework of Dhammas, including a section on "Which seven dhammas should be realized? Seven strengths of one whose effluents are ended". While the source details only the first of these seven strengths, the underlying concepts are elaborated elsewhere in the sources, particularly in the Aṅguttara Nikāya (AN 8:28), which outlines eight very similar strengths of a monk whose effluents are ended.

Here is an analysis of the detailed meaning of this statement, presented in a Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

### Situation

The statement refers to the profound qualities and insights inherent in an **Arahant**, a monk whose "effluents are ended". This signifies the ultimate realization of the holy life, where the individual has completely extinguished mental impurities and achieved final liberation from suffering. Such a monk is described as one who has **"cut through craving, has ripped off the fetter, and—from rightly breaking through conceit—has put an end to suffering & stress"**. Their state involves **"effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release"**, directly known and realized in the here and now. This profound state is marked by the understanding that **"Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world"**. The Dhamma leading to this state is **"well taught by the Blessed One, to be seen here & now, timeless, inviting verification, pertinent, to be experienced by the observant for themselves"**.

### Complication

The path to ending effluents is challenging, marked by the presence of **"effluents that defile, that lead to renewed becoming, that give trouble, that ripen in stress, and lead to future birth, aging, & death"**. The three main roots of unskillful actions and defilements are **"greed..., aversion... [and] delusion"**. These are described as **"passion, aversion, & delusion"**, with passion being slow to fade and aversion and delusion carrying great blame. The **"seven obsessions"**—sensual-passion, resistance, views, uncertainty, conceit, passion-for-becoming, and ignorance—are also central to what must be overcome. Furthermore, **"ten fetters"**—including self-identification views, uncertainty, grasping at habits & practices, sensual desire, ill will, passion for form, passion for what is formless, conceit, restlessness, and ignorance—bind beings to repeated existence. Without hearing the "Dhamma sequence," wanderers of other sects are lost, and some may even promote wrong views or be "trained in bombast and not in cross-questioning," failing to truly understand the teachings.

### Resolution

The "strengths" of one whose effluents are ended are the foundational, unshakeable qualities that manifest upon full liberation, demonstrating a complete and direct knowledge of the Dhamma. While the DASUTTARA SUTTA refers to seven, a comprehensive list of eight such strengths is provided in the sources for a monk who has ended effluents, allowing them to confidently affirm their liberation. These include:

*   **Discernment of the Inconstancy of All Fabrications**: The monk clearly sees that **"All fabrications are stressful"**, and that they are unstable and impermanent. This profound insight is the first strength.
*   **Mind Not Delighting in Any World**: The liberated mind is free from any attraction or attachment to worldly phenomena. There is **"no I-making or mine-making conceit-obsession"** with the body or external themes.
*   **Mind Rising Above Every World**: This signifies a transcendent detachment, an elevation of the mind beyond all conventional existence and realms.
*   **Heart Inclined to Unbinding (Nibbāna)**: The ultimate goal is the cessation of suffering, a state described as **"pleasant... where there is nothing felt"** in terms of sensual pleasure, but rather the liberation from ill will through goodwill.
*   **Abandoning of Fetters**: All binding ties are discarded. This includes the five lower fetters and five higher fetters, signifying complete freedom from these psychological and existential bonds.
*   **Endowment with Uncommon Knowledge**: The Arahant possesses direct knowledge and clear knowing. This profound understanding allows them to know and see beyond conventional reality.
*   **Rightly Seeing Cause and Causally-Originated Phenomena**: This strength highlights the deep comprehension of dependent co-arising, recognizing the conditions for stress and suffering, and the path to their cessation.
*   **Endowment with Foremost Qualities of the Contemplative Life**: This encompassing strength indicates a complete mastery and perfection in noble virtue, concentration, discernment, and release. Such a person has **"fathomed the high & low in the world, for whom there is nothing perturbing in the world—evaporated, undesiring, untroubled, at peace—he... has crossed over birth & aging"**.

These strengths indicate a complete transformation of being, resulting from a rigorous practice of Dhamma, purifying the basis of skillful mental qualities through well-purified virtue and straightened views, and developing practices like the **"four establishings of mindfulness"** which serve as a **"direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding"**. The culmination of these practices leads to a mind released through lack of clinging or sustenance, ending the cycle of suffering and rebirth.
