In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta outlines a framework of Dhammas categorized by numerical groupings, including the **Seven Factors for Awakening**. This teaching provides a detailed understanding of the qualities necessary for progress on the spiritual path.

### Situation

The fundamental situation addressed by the Dhamma is the pervasive nature of **suffering and stress**. Beings are beset by birth, aging, death, sorrows, lamentations, pains, distresses, and despairs. The world is often seen as unpenetrated and confused, with many people "asleep" and needing to awaken. The Dhamma aims to provide a **way out of this entanglement and suffering**, leading to unbinding and release.

### Complication

The primary complication lies in the presence of **unskillful qualities and hindrances** that prevent beings from realizing the end of suffering. These include:
*   **Passion, aversion, and delusion**, which are causes for the origination of actions and carry significant blame, hindering clear seeing.
*   **Five hindrances**: sensual desire, ill will, sloth & drowsiness, restlessness & anxiety, and uncertainty. These are "defilements of awareness that weaken discernment". When these hindrances are present, the mind is "overcome with passion, not overcome with aversion, not overcome with delusion".
*   **Lack of diligence**: A monk may be "lazy & heedless" or "without ardency, without compunction", which makes them incapable of self-awakening or attaining release. Minds can be "sluggish" or "restless," making it difficult to progress in concentration.
*   **Conceit, restlessness, and anxiety** can also hinder progress, especially for those intent on heightened mind.

Without the development of specific qualities, the mind remains unreleased from effluents, even if one wishes for release.

### Resolution

The resolution involves the **development and pursuit of the Seven Factors for Awakening** (Mindfulness, Analysis of Qualities, Persistence, Rapture, Calm, Concentration, and Equanimity). These factors are considered the **direct path** for the purification of beings, the overcoming of sorrow and lamentation, the disappearance of pain and distress, the attainment of the right method, and the realization of unbinding. They are cultivated **"dependent on seclusion, dependent on dispassion, dependent on cessation, resulting in relinquishment"**. When these factors are developed, the five hindrances cease to exist.

The specific roles of each factor are:

*   **Mindfulness** (Sati): This factor is aroused when a monk remains focused on the body, feelings, mind, or mental qualities, being ardent, alert, and mindful, subduing greed and distress. It **"serves every purpose"** and helps in establishing the mind.
*   **Analysis of Qualities** (Dhamma-vicaya): A monk, remaining mindful, **"examines, analyzes, & comes to a comprehension of that quality with discernment"**. This allows for a deep understanding of phenomena.
*   **Persistence** (Viriya): In one who examines and comprehends qualities with discernment, **"persistence is aroused unflaggingly"**. This persistence is crucial for "abandoning unskillful qualities and taking on skillful qualities," making a monk "steadfast, solid in his effort".
*   **Rapture** (Pīti): Often "born of seclusion," rapture is a joyful state experienced in meditative development.
*   **Calm** (Passaddhi): When the mind is enraptured, **"the body grows calm. One whose body is calmed experiences ease. In one at ease, the mind becomes concentrated"**. Calm can be both bodily and mental.
*   **Concentration** (Samādhi): A **"concentrated monk discerns things as they have come to be"**. Concentration, when developed, leads to "a pleasant abiding in the here & now," "the attainment of knowledge & vision," "mindfulness & alertness," and ultimately, "the ending of the effluents". It makes the mind "pliant, malleable, luminous, & not brittle".
*   **Equanimity** (Upekkhā): This factor involves "carefully watch[ing] the mind thus concentrated with equanimity". When cultivated in conjunction with concentration and energy, it ensures the mind is "pliant, malleable, luminous, & not brittle" and "rightly concentrated for the ending of the effluents". It helps transcend conceit, restlessness, and anxiety.

The development of these factors culminates in **clear knowing and release**. This process leads to the **abandoning of craving** and results in two primary fruits: **"either gnosis right here & now, or—if there be any remnant of clinging-sustenance—non-return"**. With release, there is the knowledge: **"Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world"**. This signifies complete liberation from suffering and stress.
