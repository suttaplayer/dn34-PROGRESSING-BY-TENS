The statement from the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34) highlights seven qualities of a person of integrity that are considered **"hard to penetrate"**. These qualities are fundamental to spiritual development and profound understanding within the Dhamma.

### Situation

The **seven qualities of a person of integrity** are a monk's **sense of Dhamma, a sense of meaning, a sense of self, a sense of moderation, a sense of time, a sense of social gatherings, and a sense of distinctions among individuals**. A monk endowed with these qualities is deemed **"deserving of gifts, deserving of hospitality, deserving of offerings, deserving of respect, an unexcelled field of merit for the world"**.

*   **Sense of Dhamma and Meaning**: This involves not only knowing the Dhamma in its various forms, such as dialogues and question & answer sessions, but also having **"listened to it often, retained, discussed, accumulated, examined it with his mind, & well-penetrated in terms of his views,"** thereby understanding "the meaning of this & that statement".
*   **Sense of Self and Moderation**: It encompasses being **modest** and not desiring personal recognition for one's good qualities. It also means being **content with any old requisites** like robes, almsfood, or lodging, and crucially, having **"no I-making or mine-making conceit-obsession"** with regard to one's body or external themes.
*   **Sense of Time and Social Gatherings**: This quality manifests as speaking "in season, speaks what is based in fact, what is in accordance with the goal, the Dhamma, & the Vinaya". In social settings, it means **"either he speaks Dhamma himself or he invites another to do so, and he feels no disdain for noble silence,"** avoiding excessive chatter.
*   **Sense of Distinctions among Individuals**: This is reflected in the Buddha's approach to teaching the Dhamma, where he acknowledged that **"those who would understand the Dhamma are hard to find"** when considering teaching in brief or detail, thus implying a need to discern the readiness and capacity of listeners.

### Complication

These seven qualities are "hard to penetrate" due to various impediments to deep understanding and spiritual progress:

*   **Superficial Engagement with Dhamma**: Simply studying, describing, reciting, or thinking about the Dhamma, without cultivating **"internal tranquility of awareness"** or discerning its **"higher meaning or goal,"** means one is **"not one dwelling in the Dhamma"**. This indicates a lack of true penetration.
*   **Hindrances to Discernment**: The **"five obstacles, hindrances that overwhelm awareness and weaken discernment"**—sensual desire, ill will, sloth & drowsiness, restlessness & anxiety, and uncertainty—make it **"impossible"** for an individual to understand what is truly for their benefit.
*   **Internal Obstructions**: A person may be **"incapable of alighting on the lawfulness, the rightness of skillful qualities"** even when listening to the true Dhamma if they **lack conviction, have no desire to listen, or possess dull discernment**. Moreover, a mind **"defiled by passion"** cannot be released, and **"defiled by ignorance, discernment does not develop"**.
*   **Preference for Bombast and Unskillful Thinking**: Some individuals or assemblies prefer "discourses that are literary works—the works of poets, artful in sound, artful in expression," and **"don't set their hearts on knowing"** the deep and transcendent discourses of the Tathāgata. Unskillful thinking, such as that **"imbued with sensuality… ill-will… harmfulness,"** actively **"produces blindness, produces lack of vision, produces lack of knowledge, [and] leads to the cessation of discernment"**.

### Resolution

Successfully penetrating and embodying these seven qualities leads to profound spiritual realization and the cessation of suffering:

*   **Release from Defilements**: **Awareness-release and discernment-release** are attained from the **"fading of passion"** and **"fading of ignorance,"** respectively. This state is also achieved when **"right view is supported by virtue, learning, discussion, tranquility, [and] insight,"** leading to its fruit and reward.
*   **Ending of Suffering and Conceit**: A monk who has **"cut through craving," "ripped off the fetter,"** and **"from rightly breaking through conceit—has put an end to suffering & stress"** is one in whom **"no I-making or my-making conceit-obsession"** exists.
*   **Fulfillment of the Holy Life**: When a monk's mind is **"well shored-up by discernment,"** being **"devoid of passion," "devoid of aversion,"** and **"devoid of delusion,"** it becomes suitable for them to declare, **"Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world"**.
*   **Profound Insight and Peace**: One who **"sees pleasure as stress, sees pain as an arrow, sees peaceful neither pleasure nor pain as inconstant"** is a **"monk who's seen rightly,"** is released, and is a **"sage gone beyond bonds"**. Such an individual **"touches liberation with the heart, the state of peace unsurpassed,"** and becomes **"without perplexity, free of longing"**.
*   **Attainment of Unexcelled States**: The culmination of these qualities enables one to enter and remain in the **"effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release, having directly known & realized them for himself right in the here & now,"** a state described as **"the unexcelled cooled state"**.
