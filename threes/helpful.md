In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents a framework of Dhammas that progress by tens, categorizing them based on their helpfulness, what should be developed, comprehended, and so on. Among these, three Dhammas are highlighted as "very helpful": associating with people of integrity, listening to the True Dhamma, and practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhamma.

Here is a detailed analysis of this statement, presented in a Situation, Complication, and Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation**
Individuals embarking on a spiritual path often find themselves in a state of **stress** and **suffering**, beset by birth, aging, death, sorrows, lamentations, pains, distresses, and despairs. There is a fundamental human need to **find an end to this mass of suffering**. However, the landscape of teachings can be complex and confusing, with "wanderers of other sects" often **lost** because they do not hear the true Dhamma sequence. Furthermore, there is a risk of misinterpretation or slander, as some may explain what the Tathāgata did not say as having been said, or vice versa, thereby slandering the Tathāgata. Assemblies might even prefer "literary works" or "bombast" over the deep, transcendent discourses of the Tathāgata, neglecting to lend ear or set their hearts on knowing such profound teachings. Without proper understanding, one risks accumulating demerit and being placed in hell.

**Complication**
The path to liberation is hindered by several internal and external complications. Internally, individuals may be plagued by **impurities** such as being unvirtuous, envious, or stingy, which prevent positive outcomes. A practice that leads to an increase in "unskillful mental qualities" and a decline in "skillful mental qualities" is considered **fruitless**. Many are "incapable of alighting on the orderliness, on the rightness of skillful qualities" even when hearing the Dhamma, due to contempt for the teaching or speaker, or by listening with a "scattered mind" or "dull discernment". External to the individual, other doctrines may lack the "noble eightfold path" and thus be "empty of knowledgeable contemplatives," offering no true means to liberation. Those who are "conquered by evil desires," "friendship with evil people," or who "stopped halfway with a lower modicum of distinctive attainment" are "incurably doomed" to suffering. These conditions highlight the difficulty of discerning and following a genuine path without specific, helpful guidance.

**Resolution**
To overcome these complications and progress towards unbinding, the sources highlight three "very helpful" Dhammas:

*   **Associating with people of integrity:** This is a **foundational element** for spiritual development. These individuals are described as "admirable friends" who are worth associating with because they "give what is hard to give, do what is hard to do, and endure what is hard to endure". They also reveal and keep secrets, and do not abandon one in misfortune. When one's "associating with people of integrity is made full," it **cultivates essential conditions** for practice, including hearing the true Dhamma, developing conviction, appropriate attention, mindfulness, restraint of senses, right conduct, and the factors for awakening, ultimately leading to "clear knowing & release". This association can even cure "blindness" and lead to the understanding of suffering's cessation. The wisdom gained from such association, as exemplified by Venerable Sāriputta instructing Anāthapiṇḍika the householder, is highly valued for its discerning analysis.

*   **Listening to the True Dhamma:** The Dhamma proclaimed by the Blessed One is "admirable in its beginning, admirable in its middle, admirable in its end," expounding the holy life as "entirely perfect, surpassingly pure". It is taught "through direct knowledge," "with a cause," and "with marvels". Listening to this Dhamma yields **five rewards**: one "hears what one has not heard before," "clarifies what one has heard before," "gets rid of doubt," has their "views made straight," and their "mind grows serene". For these benefits to materialize, one must "listen well, give ear, apply his mind to gnosis, reject what is worthless, [and] grasp what is worthwhile". The Dhamma is "conducive to the goal, conducive to the Dhamma, and basic to the holy life," guiding individuals towards "disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding".

*   **Practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhamma:** This refers to the **application of the teachings** in one's life. "Practicing the Dhamma in line with the Dhamma" is the way to "put an end to suffering & stress". Such practice ensures that "unskillful mental qualities decline while one's skillful mental qualities increase," making the holy life fruitful. A monk engaged in this practice "speaks Dhamma and not non-Dhamma," "thinks about Dhamma and not about non-Dhamma," and remains "equanimous, mindful, alert". The Dhamma is specifically for those who cultivate modesty, contentment, reclusiveness, aroused persistence, established mindfulness, concentrated minds, and discernment. This practice includes cultivating "disenchantment with regard to form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness," and focusing on their inconstancy, stress, and not-self nature. Ultimately, this consistent and dedicated practice is the "direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding," realized through practices like the four establishings of mindfulness.
