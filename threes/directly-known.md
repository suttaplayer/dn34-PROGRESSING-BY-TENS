In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta presents a comprehensive framework of Dhammas, including those to be "directly known". Among these are three fundamental properties: the property of sensuality, the property of form, and the property of the formless. To understand the detailed meaning of this statement, we can analyze it through the "Situation, Complication, Resolution" framework.

**Situation: The Properties to be Directly Known**

The statement refers to three key "properties" or "dhammas" that require direct knowledge for a complete understanding of existence and the path to liberation: sensuality, form, and the formless.

*   **The Property of Sensuality (Kāma-dhātu)**: This refers to the realm of sensory experience and desire. It encompasses the five "strands of sensuality," which are forms cognizable by the eye, sounds by the ear, aromas by the nose, flavors by the tongue, and tactile sensations by the body. These are described as agreeable, pleasing, charming, endearing, enticing, and linked to sensual desire. The pleasure or joy that arises in dependence on these five strands is called sensual pleasure. However, sensuality is also characterized as "inconstant, hollow, vain, deceptive, illusory, the babble of fools," and is considered to be "Māra's realm, Māra's domain, Māra's bait, Māra's range".
*   **The Property of Form (Rūpa-dhātu)**: This property relates to all material existence, including the physical body and the environment. Form is described as anything "past, future, or present; internal or external; blatant or subtle; common or sublime; far or near". It is fundamentally composed of the four great existents: the earth, liquid, fire, and wind properties, along with any form derived from them.
*   **The Property of the Formless (Arūpa-dhātu)**: This refers to realms of existence beyond physical form, which are attained through advanced meditative states. These include the dimension of the infinitude of space, the dimension of the infinitude of consciousness, the dimension of nothingness, and the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception.

**Complication: The Dangers of Not Directly Knowing These Properties**

Without direct knowledge and proper comprehension, engagement with these properties leads to suffering and continued existence in the cycle of birth and death.

*   **Sensuality's Drawbacks**: Sensual pleasures are explicitly described as being "of little satisfaction, much stress, much despair, & greater drawbacks". They are likened to a "chain of bones," a "lump of flesh," or a "snake's head", highlighting their inherent danger and unsatisfying nature. Thinking imbued with sensuality "produces blindness, produces lack of vision, produces lack of knowledge, leads to the cessation of discernment, sides with vexation, and is not conducive to Unbinding".
*   **Clinging to Form and Identity**: An "uninstructed run-of-the-mill person" often perceives form, or earth, as "mine" and delights in it, supposing things about it or in it, because they have not comprehended its true nature. This clinging, embracing, grasping, and holding-on to the five clinging-aggregates (which include form) is stated to be "the origination of stress". This is because "form is inconstant. Whatever is inconstant is stressful. Whatever is stressful is not-self".
*   **Limitations of Formless Attainments**: Even the formless attainments, while peaceful and refined, are not the ultimate liberation if clung to. They are still part of "becoming" and therefore subject to origination, which means they are subject to cessation and do not lead to the final end of suffering. Some contemplatives and brahmans might proclaim ultimate unbinding based on these states, but the Tathāgata discerns that "these standpoints, thus seized, thus grasped at, lead to such & such a destination, to such & such a state in the world beyond".
*   **The Cycle of Suffering**: Any desire or passion for these properties is a "defilement of the mind". From the origination of delight in these properties "comes the origination of suffering & stress". Ultimately, clinging to these properties perpetuates "becoming," leading to "future birth, aging-&-death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, & despair".

**Resolution: The Path Through Direct Knowledge to Unbinding**

Directly knowing these properties involves comprehending their true nature, abandoning desire and passion for them, and ultimately achieving unbinding.

*   **Full Comprehension**: One must know each property, its cause, its diversity, its result, its cessation, and the path to its cessation. When a disciple discerns sensuality in this way, they understand the "penetrative holy life as the cessation of sensuality". This applies to form and the formless as well. For example, the escape from sensuality is renunciation, and the escape from form is the formless.
*   **Abandoning Desire and Clinging**: When "defilements of awareness are abandoned," the mind becomes "inclined to renunciation" and "malleable for the direct knowing of those qualities worth realizing". The subduing and abandoning of desire and passion for the five clinging-aggregates (including form) is the "cessation of stress". One who is "unsustained by [doesn't cling to] anything in the world" becomes "unagitated" and "totally unbinds right within".
*   **Realization of Unbinding**: Through this direct knowledge, which involves cultivating "disenchantment, dispassion, [and] cessation" with regard to form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness, one becomes "totally released" from suffering and stress. This culminates in the knowledge that "Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world". This "gnosis of emancipation" is described as the "breaking open of ignorance". Direct knowledge of these properties, therefore, leads to "clear knowing & release", which is the ultimate goal.
