In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta outlines various categories of Dhamma teachings, including knowledges that should be cultivated. Among these, the text states: "Three knowledges: knowledge of the past, knowledge of the future, & knowledge of the present. These three dhammas should be made to arise.".

Here is an analysis of the detailed meaning of this statement, presented in a Situation, Complication, and Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation: The Imperative for Comprehensive Knowledge**

The instruction to develop "knowledge of the past, knowledge of the future, & knowledge of the present" points to a profound understanding that transcends mere historical recounting or prophetic prediction. This comprehensive knowledge is fundamental to the holy life. The **Tathāgata (Buddha) himself is described as having realized this world** with its devas, Māras, and Brahmās, and its human inhabitants, thus being an "expert with regard to the cosmos". His knowledge regarding the past involves remembering whatever he wants, while his knowledge of the future includes the certainty of "no further becoming". When addressing matters of the past, future, and present, the Tathāgata speaks at the appropriate time, stating what is true, factual, meaningful, and in line with the Dhamma and Vinaya. This suggests that the development of these three knowledges in disciples is not for idle curiosity, but for a practical, liberating purpose. These knowledges are critical for the right ending of suffering and stress, leading ultimately to clear knowing and release. More specifically, these three knowledges are interconnected with the **"three forms of clear knowing: the clear knowing of the knowledge of previous lifetimes, the clear knowing of the knowledge of the passing away & arising of beings, the clear knowing of the knowledge of the ending of the effluents"**. This indicates that true insight into temporal aspects of existence leads to the ultimate spiritual goal of liberation.

**Complication: Ignorance, Speculation, and the Cycle of Suffering**

Without these three knowledges, individuals remain immersed in ignorance and subject to suffering. Many contemplatives and brahmans engage in theoretical speculation about the cosmos or the self, asserting views such as eternalism, annihilationism, or resorting to "eel-wriggling" verbal contortions. Such views are often characterized as "agitation & vacillation" driven by craving. The Blessed One explicitly states that these types of views are "not conducive to the goal, are not conducive to the Dhamma, are not basic to the holy life. They don't lead to disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to calm, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding". Furthermore, holding onto wrong views or slandering the Dhamma can lead to dire consequences, potentially placing one in hell. The Dhamma, being "abstruse, subtle, deep, hard to see, going against the flow," is not easily realized by those "overcome with aversion & passion". Due to being **"hindered by ignorance and fettered by craving," beings continue "transmigrating & wandering on" from "an inconceivable beginning," experiencing "stress, experienced pain, experienced loss, swelling the cemeteries"**. This perpetual cycle of rebirth (becoming) is sustained by clinging to views and unskillful qualities. Consciousness "hindered by ignorance & fettered by craving" is established in properties that lead to "the production of renewed becoming in the future".

**Resolution: Developing Insight for Liberation from Time**

The path to developing these knowledges involves a deep and direct understanding of phenomena as they are. This true knowledge of the past, future, and present is gained by seeing the **dependent co-arising and the nature of all phenomena, distinguishing between fabricated and unfabricated states**. It includes the ability to recall previous lifetimes and foresee the passing away and arising of beings, discerning their destinations based on kamma. This discernment culminates in the **"knowledge of ending (of effluents) & knowledge of (their) non-recurrence"**, leading to ultimate freedom from future becoming.

The practice to attain these knowledges is not about theoretical engagement with the past or future, but about discerning their "origination, ending, allure, & drawbacks, along with the escape". This means recognizing the impermanence (inconstancy), stressfulness, and not-self nature of all phenomena, whether past, present, or future. By seeing things in this way, one cultivates dispassion and is released.

The development involves:
*   **Virtue (Sīla):** Purifying the basis of skillful mental qualities, with well-purified virtue and straight views, which supports the development of mindfulness.
*   **Concentration (Samādhi):** Developing concentration allows a monk to discern "in line with what has come into being", leading to an understanding of the Four Noble Truths. This includes **"the knowledge arises right within oneself that 'This concentration is peaceful, exquisite, the acquiring of calm, the attainment of unification, not kept in place by the fabrications of forceful restraint.' [and] 'I enter into this concentration mindfully, & mindfully I emerge from it.'"**.
*   **Discernment (Paññā):** Through these practices, the mind is released from defilements like sensuality, becoming, and ignorance. When one knows and sees in this way, there is the ending of effluents.

The result of cultivating these knowledges is the abandoning of craving and clinging, thereby ending the production of renewed becoming. This leads to **"the cessation of objectification, the stilling of objectification"**. The ultimate outcome is the cutting through of craving, the ripping off of fetters, and the **ending of all suffering and stress**, culminating in "unbinding" and "no further becoming". This profound Dhamma discourse on the Great Forty, which includes these knowledges, is stated to be unassailable: "This Dhamma discourse on the Great Forty has been set rolling and cannot be stopped by any contemplative or brahman or deva or Māra or Brahmā or anyone at all in the world.". The journey culminates in the declaration: **"Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world."**.
