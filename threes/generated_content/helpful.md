> Helpful > Associating with people of integrity, listening to the True Dhamma, practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhammas
1. Definition
This progression describes three interconnected and very helpful qualities that are fundamental to spiritual development.
• Associating with people of integrity: This refers to the practice of associating with individuals recognized as wise and ethical. A person of integrity is characterized by their wisdom, discernment of skillful and unskillful qualities, and the abandonment of passion, aversion, and delusion [4, 255, guide_antidotes.md]. They prioritize Dhamma practice over external distinctions like family or renown, neither exalting themselves nor disparaging others [360, guide_antidotes.md]. Such individuals are also described as freely generous, openhanded, delighting in magnanimity, and responsive to requests, with their awareness cleansed of stinginess.
• Listening to the True Dhamma: This involves hearing the teachings (Dhamma) that are well taught by the Blessed One, characterized as being seen here & now, timeless, inviting verification, pertinent, and to be experienced by the observant for themselves. Hearing the True Dhamma is identified as a factor for stream entry and is conducive to disenchantment, dispassion, cessation, stilling, direct knowledge, self-awakening, and unbinding.
• Practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhammas: This entails living and acting in line with the Dhamma as taught by the Buddha. It involves masterfully practicing and conforming to the teachings, specifically cultivating disenchantment with regard to form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness. It also means focusing on the inconstancy, stress, and not-self nature of these aggregates. This practice includes subduing passion, aversion, and delusion within oneself and encouraging others to do the same.
Collectively, when these three qualities are fully developed, they fill the conditions for clear knowing & release.
2. Considerations
Acquiring these three qualities is paramount for a masterful practice, as they are explicitly called "very helpful" in the sources. They lay the foundational and progressive groundwork for spiritual liberation and the end of suffering.
The overall favorable impact and ultimate positive states or benefits resulting from engaging in these practices are profound:
• A Full Path to Release: When associating with people of integrity is made full, it systematically prepares the practitioner by filling the conditions for hearing the True Dhamma, which in turn fills the conditions for conviction, appropriate attention, mindfulness & alertness, restraint of the senses, the three forms of right conduct, the four establishings of mindfulness, and the seven factors for awakening. Ultimately, these culminate in clear knowing & release.
• Mental Cleansing and Concentration: Listening to the True Dhamma leads to the mind becoming calmed and filled with joy, which then leads to rapture, bodily calm, ease, and ultimately, a concentrated mind. This process helps the mind to be not overcome with passion, aversion, or delusion. It effectively cleanses the defiled mind through proper technique.
• Liberation from Suffering: Practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhamma leads to the total release from sorrows, lamentations, pains, distresses, and despairs. This practice is key to putting an end to suffering & stress.
These practices are crucial for the practitioner, especially when they might be obscured by heedlessness, complacency, or a false sense of confidence:
• Countering Negativity: The lack of associating with people of integrity initiates a cascade of negative conditions, including the absence of True Dhamma, leading to lack of conviction, inappropriate attention, lack of mindfulness and alertness, insufficient restraint of the senses, engagement in misconduct, succumbing to the five hindrances, and ultimately, ignorance [guide_antidotes.md].
• Overcoming Mental Obstructions: The texts distinguish between "upside-down discernment" (like an overturned pot that cannot hold water) and "wide-open discernment" (like a pot set upright that retains water). This illustrates that merely being exposed to the Dhamma is insufficient; active, mindful engagement is required to avoid a failure to grasp the teachings. Right View, cultivated through listening and appropriate attention, is essential as it purges away wrong view [518, guide_dhamma_outcomes_and_consequences.md].
• Preventing Regret: Heedfulness, which is fostered by these practices, is critical in preventing regret and ensuring spiritual progress.
3. Causation
The following outlines the causes, effects, and other causal factors related to associating with people of integrity, listening to the True Dhamma, and practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhammas, indicating when these practices are most beneficial.
Causes/conditions For 'Associating with people of integrity'
• Enables Hearing the True Dhamma: Associating with people of integrity is a factor for hearing the True Dhamma.
• Cultivates Conviction: It is a condition for the arising of conviction.
• Fosters Appropriate Attention: It leads to appropriate attention.
• Develops Mindfulness & Alertness: It helps in the development of mindfulness and alertness.
• Promotes Restraint of the Senses: It fills the conditions for restraint of the senses.
• Leads to Right Conduct: It supports the three forms of right conduct.
• Establishes Mindfulness: It provides conditions for the four establishings of mindfulness.
• Develops Factors for Awakening: It lays the groundwork for the seven factors for awakening.
Effects From 'Associating with people of integrity'
• Attains Clear Knowing & Release: When made full, it leads to clear knowing & release.
• Benefits Self and Others: It helps a lay follower to be consummate in conviction, virtue, generosity, seeing monks, hearing Dhamma, remembering Dhamma, exploring meaning, and practicing Dhamma in line with Dhamma, benefiting both oneself and others.
Other Causal Factors for 'Associating with people of integrity'
• Being one with a sense of Dhamma, a sense of meaning, a sense of himself, a sense of moderation, a sense of time, a sense of social gatherings, & a sense of distinctions among individuals.
Similes for 'Associating with people of integrity'
• Simile: Just as when the devas pour rain in heavy drops & crash thunder on the upper mountains, the water flows down along the slopes, filling ravines, gullies, little lakes, big lakes, little rivers, big rivers, which then fill the great ocean.
• Understanding: This simile illustrates how associating with people of integrity, when cultivated fully, acts as the initial and comprehensive source of nourishment that, through a chain of interconnected conditions (like water flowing through a natural system), ultimately leads to the fullness of clear knowing and release, akin to the great ocean.
Causes/conditions For 'listening to the True Dhamma'
• Calms the Mind and Brings Joy: As one recollects the Buddha, Dhamma, or Saṅgha, the mind becomes calmed, joy arises, and defilements are abandoned. This process leads to rapture, bodily calm, pleasure, and concentration.
• Gains Sense of Goal and Dhamma: The mind, heading straight based on the Tathāgata, Dhamma, or Saṅgha, gains a sense of the goal and the Dhamma.
• Attains Fearlessness and Independence: Hearing the Dhamma can lead to gaining fearlessness and independence with regard to the Teacher's message.
• Develops Dhamma Eye: The "dustless, stainless Dhamma eye" arises, leading to the realization that "Whatever is subject to origination is all subject to cessation".
• Enhances Understanding: It enables one to understand what was previously not understood.
Effects From 'listening to the True Dhamma'
• Increases Skillful Qualities: When one follows a Dhamma life, skillful mental qualities increase.
• Leads to Unbinding: It is conducive to disenchantment, dispassion, cessation, stilling, direct knowledge, self-awakening, and unbinding.
Other Causal Factors for 'listening to the True Dhamma'
• Absence of Contempt: Not holding the talk, the speaker, or oneself in contempt.
• Undivided Attention: Listening to the Dhamma with an unscattered mind, a mind gathered into one.
• Appropriate Attention: Attending appropriately to the Dhamma.
• Discernment: Being discerning, not dim-witted nor a drooling idiot.
• No False Assumptions: Not understanding, one doesn't assume one understands.
• Receptivity: Listening well, giving ear, applying the mind to gnosis, rejecting what is worthless, grabbing hold of what is worthwhile, and being endowed with the patience to comply with the teaching.
Similes for 'listening to the True Dhamma'
• Simile: There are three types of persons: "upside-down discernment" (like an overturned pot where water runs off and doesn't stay), "lap discernment," and "wide-open discernment" (like a pot set right side up where water stays).
• Understanding: This illustrates that passively hearing the Dhamma without genuine attention and retention (upside-down discernment) is fruitless, whereas attentive listening and retention (wide-open discernment) is essential for the Dhamma to take root and lead to understanding.
• Simile: The defiled mind is cleansed through proper technique, just as a head is cleansed with cosmetic paste & clay, a body with scouring balls & bath powder, clothing with salt earth & lye & cow dung, or a mirror with oil & ashes & chamois, all with appropriate human effort.
• Understanding: This emphasizes that the purification of the mind, achieved through recollecting the Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, and one's own virtues, is a deliberate and methodical process, analogous to the specific methods used to clean various physical objects.
• Simile: Just as a clean cloth, free of stains, would properly absorb a dye.
• Understanding: This highlights that a mind made receptive and free from hindrances by hearing the Dhamma is ready to absorb the truths and attain direct knowledge, much like a purified cloth readily takes on color.
Causes/conditions For 'practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhammas'
• Leads to Comprehension and Release: Cultivating disenchantment, inconstancy, stress, or not-self with regard to form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness leads to comprehending them, and thus being totally released from them, and from sorrows, lamentations, pains, distresses, & despairs.
• Arouses Persistence: It involves keeping persistence aroused for abandoning unskillful qualities and taking on skillful qualities, being steadfast and solid in effort.
• Develops Knowledge and Practice: It involves knowing both the Dhamma and its meaning, and practicing it in line with the Dhamma.
Effects From 'practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhammas'
• Achieves Peace Amidst Danger: One will live in peace even when there is danger, especially when making effort to attain the un-yet-attained before unwelcome events occur.
• Attains Supreme Goal: It leads to reaching and remaining in the supreme goal of the holy life.
Other Causal Factors for 'practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhammas'
• Endowment with Faculties: Being endowed with the faculties of conviction, persistence, mindfulness, concentration, and discernment.
• Subduing Defilements: Practicing for the subduing of passion, aversion, and delusion within oneself and encouraging others in the same.
Similes for 'practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhammas'
• Simile: The sage who makes an effort in truth doesn't fall back, and practicing the Dhamma in line with the Dhamma will put an end to suffering & stress.
• Understanding: This highlights that diligent and truthful practice is the unwavering path to liberation from suffering, preventing regression and ensuring ultimate success.
• Simile: A firebrand from a funeral pyre, burning at both ends, covered with excrement in the middle, is used as fuel neither in a village nor in the wilderness. This is a simile for a young man of good family who, having gone forth, is greedy for sensual pleasures, strong in passions, malevolent, muddled in mindfulness, unalert, uncentered, with a scattered mind and uncontrolled faculties; he has missed out on householder's enjoyments and does not fulfill the purpose of the contemplative life.
• Understanding: This vividly illustrates the futile and unproductive outcome of not practicing the Dhamma correctly by indulging in defilements. Such an individual fails to gain benefits in either the worldly life or the contemplative life, emphasizing the critical importance of aligning one's actions with the Dhamma.
4. Complications
When the progression of associating with people of integrity, listening to the True Dhamma, and practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhammas is hindered or absent, several negative states and obstacles arise, indicating where dangers occur in the practitioner's journey.
• Not Associating with People of Integrity:
    ◦ This leads to a detrimental chain of events: not hearing the true Dhamma, which then results in lack of conviction, inappropriate attention, lack of mindfulness and alertness, insufficient restraint of the senses, engagement in three forms of misconduct, succumbing to the five hindrances, and ultimately, ignorance [guide_antidotes.md].
• Not Listening to the True Dhamma:
    ◦ Upside-down Discernment: A person with "upside-down discernment" attends the Dhamma talk but doesn't attend to its beginning, middle, or end, either while sitting or after getting up, like an overturned pot from which water runs off. This indicates a lack of mental absorption and retention.
    ◦ Verbal Contortions: Being dull & exceedingly stupid can lead to resorting to verbal contortions or "eel-wriggling" when questioned, rather than giving clear answers.
    ◦ Contempt and Scattering: If one holds the talk, the speaker, or oneself in contempt, or listens with a scattered mind, they become incapable of alighting on the orderliness or rightness of skillful qualities.
    ◦ Inappropriate Attention: Attending inappropriately is a quality that prevents one from alighting on the orderliness of skillful qualities even when listening to the True Dhamma.
    ◦ Disruption and Aversion: A person who, when asked a question, wanders from one thing to another, pulls the discussion off the topic, shows anger & aversion, and sulks, is unfit for discussion.
    ◦ False Understanding: Not understanding, yet assuming one understands, is a barrier to genuine comprehension.
• Not Practicing the Dhamma in Accordance with the Dhammas:
    ◦ Decline in Skillful Qualities: When one follows a habit or practice where unskillful mental qualities increase while skillful mental qualities decline, that practice is fruitless.
    ◦ Negative Rebirth: Being unvirtuous, envious, and stingy, without abandoning these impurities, can lead to being placed in hell.
    ◦ Spiritual Failure: A young man who goes forth but is greedy for sensual pleasures, strong in passions, malevolent, muddled in mindfulness, unalert, uncentered, with a scattered mind, and uncontrolled faculties, misses out on the householder's enjoyments and fails to fulfill the purpose of the contemplative life.
    ◦ Ineffective Holy Life: Individuals endowed with wrong view, wrong resolve, wrong speech, wrong action, wrong livelihood, wrong effort, wrong mindfulness, & wrong concentration are incapable of obtaining results from the holy life, even if they make wishes for results, because their method is inappropriate.
    ◦ Perpetuation of Suffering: If a person does not abandon passion-obsession, abolish resistance-obsession, and uproot ignorance-obsession, they cannot put an end to suffering & stress in the here and now. Furthermore, adhering to, holding, or being attached to stress, and envisioning 'This is mine; this is my self; this is what I am,' prevents one from comprehending stress or totally destroying it.
    ◦ Obstacles to Development: Qualities such as self-aggrandizement, discontent, entanglement, laziness, confused mindfulness, unconcentrated mind, weak discernment, and delight in objectification are described as not leading to desirable spiritual outcomes.
5. How To
To cultivate and maintain these three qualities for masterful practice, the practitioner should engage in specific methods and apply practical advice.
Cultivating 'Associating with people of integrity':
• Seek Admirable Friends: Befriend a learned, intelligent, and admirable friend.
• Engage with Learned Monks: Periodically approach monks who are learned, knowledgeable in tradition, and holders of the Dhamma, Vinaya, & Mātikā. Question them and quiz them on points of Dhamma, asking "How is this? What is the meaning of this?". This interaction helps clarify the unopen and make plain the unexplained, dispelling doubts.
• Cultivate Conviction: Conviction is most helpful for visiting such individuals, as it prompts the initial engagement.
Cultivating 'Listening to the True Dhamma':
• Be Receptive and Discerning: Listen well, give ear, apply your mind to gnosis, reject what is worthless, and grab hold of what is worthwhile. Be endowed with the patience to comply with the teaching.
• Comprehensive Attention: When listening to a Dhamma talk, attend to its beginning, middle, and end.
• Develop "Wide-Open Discernment": Cultivate discernment that is "wide open," like a pot set right side up, allowing the Dhamma to be grasped and remembered, leading to the end of suffering and stress.
• Recitation and Reflection: Recite the Dhamma in detail as heard and learned. Think about, evaluate, and mentally examine the Dhamma, contemplating it with your intellect.
• Avoid Contempt and Scattering: Do not hold the talk, the speaker, or oneself in contempt. Listen with an unscattered mind, gathered into one, and attend appropriately.
Cultivating 'Practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhammas':
• Develop Inner Peace: Train yourselves to be peaceful in your faculties & peaceful in your hearts, which will lead to peaceful bodily, verbal, and mental actions.
• Arouse Persistence: Keep your persistence aroused for abandoning unskillful qualities and taking on skillful qualities. Be steadfast and solid in your effort, not shirking your duties. This includes the resolve to let flesh and blood dry up if needed to attain what is reachable through manly firmness.
• Cultivate Disenchantment: Continuously cultivate disenchantment with regard to form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness.
• Focus on Impermanence and Not-Self: Keep focusing on the inconstancy, stress, and not-self nature of form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness.
• Embrace Qualities of a Great Person: Cultivate modesty, contentment, reclusiveness, aroused persistence, established mindfulness, a concentrated mind, discernment, and enjoyment of non-objectification.
• Be Heedful, Ardent, and Resolute: Live heedful, ardent, & resolute for the sake of the true goal. Heedfulness is the root of all skillful qualities.
• Develop Factors for Awakening: Develop the seven factors for awakening: mindfulness, analysis of qualities, persistence, rapture, calm, concentration, and equanimity.
• Follow the Noble Eightfold Path: This is the fundamental way, comprising Right View, Right Resolve, Right Speech, Right Action, Right Livelihood, Right Effort, Right Mindfulness, and Right Concentration.
6. Similes
This section provides helpful analogies to understand 'Associating with people of integrity,' 'listening to the True Dhamma,' and 'practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhammas'.
• For 'Associating with people of integrity':
    ◦ Simile: Just as when the devas pour rain in heavy drops & crash thunder on the upper mountains, the water, flowing down along the slopes, fills the branches of the mountain ravines & gullies… the little lakes… the big lakes… the little rivers… the big rivers. When the big rivers are made full, they fill the great ocean.
    ◦ Understanding: This simile illustrates how associating with people of integrity acts as the initial and comprehensive source of nourishment that, when fully developed (like the initial rain), systematically fills the conditions for all subsequent stages of the path, ultimately leading to the fullness of clear knowing and release, analogous to the great ocean.
• For 'listening to the True Dhamma':
    ◦ Simile: There are three types of persons: the person of upside-down discernment, likened to a pot turned upside down where water poured runs off and doesn't stay; the person of "lap discernment"; and the person of wide-open discernment, likened to a pot set right side up where water poured stays and doesn't run off.
    ◦ Understanding: This analogy highlights that merely being present while the Dhamma is taught is insufficient. True listening to the True Dhamma requires attentiveness and receptivity (wide-open discernment) to grasp and retain the teachings, unlike superficial or distracted listening (upside-down discernment) that yields no benefit.
    ◦ Simile: The defiled mind is cleansed through the proper technique, just as a head is cleansed with cosmetic paste & clay, a body with scouring balls & bath powder, clothing with salt earth & lye & cow dung, or a mirror with oil & ashes & chamois, all requiring appropriate human effort.
    ◦ Understanding: This emphasizes that the process of purifying the mind through listening to the True Dhamma (e.g., recollecting the Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, one's virtues) is a deliberate and systematic practice, much like using specific tools and effort to clean and polish physical objects.
    ◦ Simile: Just as a white cloth with stains removed would rightly take dye.
    ◦ Understanding: This illustrates that a mind made ready and free from hindrances by listening to the True Dhamma is receptive and prepared to absorb the deeper truths and attain direct knowledge, just as a clean cloth readily absorbs color.
• For 'practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhammas':
    ◦ Simile: A sage who makes an effort in truth doesn't fall back, and practicing the Dhamma in line with the Dhamma will put an end to suffering & stress.
    ◦ Understanding: This simile emphasizes that diligent and truthful practice is the unwavering path to ultimate liberation from suffering, ensuring continuous progress and preventing regression from the spiritual path.
    ◦ Simile: A firebrand from a funeral pyre—burning at both ends, covered with excrement in the middle—is used as fuel neither in a village nor in the wilderness. This is a simile for a person who, having gone forth, is greedy for sensual pleasures, strong in passions, malevolent, muddled in mindfulness, unalert, uncentered, with a scattered mind, and uncontrolled faculties; such a person has missed out on the householder's enjoyments and does not fulfill the purpose of the contemplative life.
    ◦ Understanding: This vivid simile serves as a cautionary tale, illustrating how not practicing the Dhamma in accordance with its principles, particularly by indulging in defilements, leads to a futile and unproductive spiritual life, where one fails to benefit from either worldly or contemplative pursuits.
