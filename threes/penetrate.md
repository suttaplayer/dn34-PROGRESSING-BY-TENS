In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta outlines a framework of Dhammas, including specific insights that are particularly "hard to penetrate". One such profound insight concerns the three properties for escape: "This is the escape from sensuality: renunciation. This is the escape from form: the formless. As for whatever has come into being, is fabricated, & is dependently co-arisen, the escape from that is cessation".

Let's analyze the detailed meaning of this statement using the "Situation, Complication, Resolution" (SCR) format:

### Situation: The Ensnarement of Beings

The situation describes the state of beings who are entangled in existence, primarily through **passion, desire, and attachment** to phenomena. This entanglement manifests in several ways:

*   **Sensuality (Kāma)**: The mind is not released when defiled by passion. Craving is described as "the ensnarer that has flowed along, spread out, and caught hold, with which this world is smothered & enveloped like a tangled skein… and does not go beyond transmigration". Any desire-passion regarding sensory experiences, such as the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, or intellect, acts as a "defilement of the mind". Delighting in these sensory experiences leads to stress, and one who delights in stress is not released from it. Sensual pleasures are explicitly stated to have "little satisfaction, much stress, much despair, & greater drawbacks". The world itself is seen as being "swept away" and not enduring.

*   **Form (Rūpa)**: Beings are often attached to physical form and the realm of form. This attachment leads to involvement in the cycle of existence.

*   **Fabricated and Dependently Co-arisen Phenomena**: All existing things, or "fabrications," are inherently stressful. The entire mass of stress and suffering arises from a chain of dependent co-arising, starting from ignorance, leading to fabrications, consciousness, name-&-form, the six sense media, contact, feeling, craving, clinging, becoming, birth, and consequently aging-and-death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, and despair. This "wandering-on" has an "inconceivable beginning" where beings, hindered by ignorance and fettered by craving, continue transmigrating.

### Complication: The Difficulty in Penetrating These Escapes

The statement emphasizes that these properties for escape are "hard to penetrate". This difficulty stems primarily from **ignorance (Avijjā) and deeply ingrained attachments**, which blind individuals to the true nature of existence and the path to liberation:

*   **Ignorance and Lack of Discernment**: Discernment does not develop when the mind is "defiled by ignorance". Unskillful thoughts, such as those "imbued with sensuality, ill-will, or harmfulness," produce blindness, lack of vision, and "lead to the cessation of discernment". Individuals with "dull discernment, slow & dull-witted" are incapable of grasping skillful qualities. The conceit "I am" is a quality that must be abandoned, as it fuels attachment and prevents clear seeing.
*   **Deep-Seated Attachments**: The current generation is described as delighting in and being excited by attachment. When one clings, they cling to forms, feelings, perceptions, fabrications, and consciousness, which perpetuates the cycle of stress. The phrase "hard to penetrate" highlights the profound challenge of overcoming these inherent tendencies and recognizing the path to freedom. Ignorance itself is the primary obstacle, and its abandonment leads to "clear knowing".

### Resolution: The Properties for Escape

The Dhamma offers specific "properties for escape" that, when realized and practiced, lead to liberation from the aforementioned entanglements:

*   **Escape from Sensuality: Renunciation**: Renunciation is identified as the direct escape from sensuality. When a monk's mind is "rightly-gone, rightly developed, has rightly risen above, gained release, and become disjoined from sensuality," they are released from the "effluents, torments, & fevers" that arise from sensuality. A mind "fostered by renunciation feels malleable for the direct knowing of those qualities worth realizing". This signifies that dispassion and the fading of passion lead to "awareness-release".

*   **Escape from Form: The Formless**: The escape from form is achieved through the "formless" attainments. These refer to the immaterial meditative states such as the "dimension of the infinitude of space," which is attained "with the complete transcending of perceptions of (physical) form". These are described as "peaceful liberations that transcend form". The progression through formless attainments ultimately contributes to the ending of effluents and liberation.

*   **Escape from Fabricated & Dependently Co-arisen Phenomena: Cessation**: The ultimate escape from "whatever has come into being, is fabricated, & is dependently co-arisen" is **cessation**. This cessation is synonymous with "Unbinding" (Nibbāna) and is achieved through "the ending of passion, the ending of aversion, the ending of delusion," which collectively constitute "the unfabricated". The path involves recognizing that "all fabrications are stressful" and cultivating disenchantment with them. Through this realization, there is "the pacification of all fabrications; the relinquishing of all acquisitions; the ending of craving; dispassion; cessation; unbinding". This cessation applies to all links in the chain of dependent co-arising, meaning that with the cessation of consciousness, name-&-form ceases, and so forth, leading to the complete cessation of stress. Ultimately, "when all phenomena are done away with, all means of speaking are done away with as well".
