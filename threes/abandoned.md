Venerable Sāriputta's comprehensive framework of Dhammas in the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34) includes "Three cravings: craving for sensuality, craving for becoming, craving for non-becoming". These three forms of craving are central to understanding the nature of suffering and the path to its cessation.

Here is a detailed analysis of this statement, presented in a Situation, Complication, and Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation**

The three cravings—craving for sensuality, craving for becoming, and craving for non-becoming—are fundamental categories of desire taught by the Blessed One. These cravings act as the **ensnarer** that has "flowed along, spread out, and caught hold, with which this world is smothered & enveloped like a tangled skein, a knotted ball of string, like matted rushes and reeds, and does not go beyond transmigration, beyond the planes of deprivation, woe, & bad destinations". In essence, these cravings are what bind beings to the cycle of existence.

**Complication**

These three cravings are identified as significant causes for the origination of actions, alongside aversion and delusion. Their presence causes great suffering and obstructs liberation:

*   **Engendering Suffering and Rebirth:** Craving is described as that which "engenders a person" and causes one's mind to "run around," leading beings to "rush toward the wandering-on" and remain "not yet released from suffering". This is because craving is the "seamstress" that stitches one to the production of various forms of becoming. The causal chain is clear: **"From craving as a requisite condition comes clinging or sustenance. From clinging or sustenance as a requisite condition comes becoming. From becoming as a requisite condition comes birth. From birth as a requisite condition, then aging & death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, & despair come into play. Such is the origination of this entire mass of suffering & stress"**. The production of "renewed becoming in the future" is directly linked to consciousness being "hindered by ignorance & fettered by craving".
*   **Sensuality's Allure and Drawbacks:** Sensual desire, a form of craving, is explicitly identified as "an obstacle, a hindrance that overwhelms awareness and weakens discernment". Even when a noble disciple discerns "that sensuality is of much stress, much despair, & greater drawbacks," they can still be tempted if they haven't found a more peaceful rapture and pleasure apart from unskillful qualities. This deep attachment is highlighted by the observation that those "blinded by sensuality, covered by the net, veiled with the veil of craving, bound by the Kinsman of the heedless, like fish in the mouth of a trap, they go to aging & death". Such attachment prevents one from "cross[ing] over the flood so great & wide". Engaging with sense objects with desire and passion defiles the mind. The texts compare sensuality to a "pit of glowing embers," a "dream," or "the fruits of a tree," emphasizing its transient, stressful, and deceptive nature.
*   **The Pervasiveness of Craving:** Craving manifests in 108 verbalizations, encompassing past, future, and present experiences. Furthermore, it is rooted in seeking, acquisition, attachment, possessiveness, stinginess, and defensiveness, leading to conflicts, quarrels, disputes, accusations, divisive speech, and lies. This demonstrates how extensively craving infiltrates and corrupts human experience.

**Resolution**

The path to liberation involves the complete cessation of these three cravings. The Dhamma is taught precisely for the "abandoning of passion, for the abandoning of aversion, for the abandoning of delusion".

*   **The Path to Cessation:** **"Through the abandoning of craving is there said to be unbinding"**. This is achieved by realizing that "Craving is pierced in one who knows; for one who sees, there is nothing". The crucial step is the **"remainderless fading & cessation of that very craving,"** which leads directly to the "cessation of clinging or sustenance," then to the "cessation of becoming," and subsequently to the "cessation of birth, then aging & death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, & despair all cease. Such is the cessation of this entire mass of stress & suffering".
*   **Key Practices:** The "Noble Eightfold Path" is explicitly stated as the "path of practice leading to the cessation of craving", and also to the cessation of clinging. This path "leads to stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding". The result of this practice is a state of "peaceful, permanent, a sphere beyond conjecture, unborn, unproduced, the sorrowless, stainless state, the cessation of stressful qualities, stilling-of-fabrications bliss".
*   **Discernment and Release:** Discernment is paramount. When one sees "a feeling of pleasure as stressful, a feeling of pain as an arrow, and a feeling of neither pleasure nor pain as inconstant," they are said to have "cut off craving" and "put an end to suffering & stress". The "ending of effluents"—which are rooted in passion, becoming, and ignorance—is attainable for "one who knows & sees". This knowledge involves discerning the "origination, ending, allure, & drawbacks of feelings, along with the escape from feelings". When dispassion arises, the practitioner is "released" and "discerns that 'Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world'".
*   **Qualities of an Awakened One:** A person who has "fathomed the high & low in the world, for whom there is nothing perturbing... evaporated, undesiring, untroubled, at peace" is one who has "crossed over birth & aging". The Dhamma fosters qualities like modesty, contentment, reclusiveness, aroused persistence, established mindfulness, concentrated mind, and discernment, all of which are conducive to overcoming craving and achieving liberation. Ultimately, the release of awareness and discernment is directly dependent on the "fading of passion" and "fading of ignorance", which are deeply intertwined with craving.
