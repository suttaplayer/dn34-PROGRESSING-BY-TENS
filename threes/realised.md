Here is an analysis of the detailed meaning of the statement from the DASUTTARA SUTTA, presented in the "Situation, Complication, Resolution" format:

**Situation**
Venerable Sāriputta, in the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), outlines ten sets of Dhamma categories, progressing by tens, that lead to unbinding and the end of suffering. Among the "three dhammas" that "should be realized," he identifies: "the clear knowing of the knowledge of previous lifetimes, the clear knowing of the knowledge of the passing away & arising of beings, [and] the clear knowing of the knowledge of the ending of the effluents". These clear knowings (vijjā) are authentic and should be realized, representing deep, tranquil, and subtle Dhammas that the Tathāgata proclaims after directly knowing and realizing them for himself.

**Complication**
Without the realization of these forms of clear knowing, beings remain entangled in the cycle of suffering (saṃsāra). Ignorance is the primary cause that prevents the cessation of stress. Beings are hindered by ignorance and fettered by craving, continuously transmigrating and wandering on. This fundamental lack of discernment means individuals do not grasp the impermanent, stressful, and not-self nature of phenomena. As a result, they may cling to views about self, cosmos, or existence after death, leading to agitation and vacillation. Such obscured understanding means they miss the true path to liberation, remaining subject to renewed becoming, trouble, and stress. The consequence of not abandoning delusion, greed, and aversion, which are described as "inside stains, inside enemies, inside foes, inside murderers, inside adversaries," is continued suffering. Without cultivating discernment and specific practices, one's mind remains unreleased from effluents.

**Resolution**
The realization of these three forms of clear knowing marks the culmination of the holy life, leading to the total ending of suffering and stress. This process is rooted in developing qualities like heedfulness, ardent persistence, and clear discernment, often facilitated by practices such as the noble eightfold path and jhānas.

1.  **The clear knowing of the knowledge of previous lifetimes:** This refers to the ability to **recollect one's manifold past lives** in extensive detail. An individual can recall their name, clan, appearance, food, experiences of pleasure and pain, and the end of their life in previous existences, as well as how they passed away from one state and re-arose in another. This knowledge helps to understand the long and continuous journey through samsara.

2.  **The clear knowing of the knowledge of the passing away & arising of beings (the divine eye):** Through this faculty, purified and surpassing human vision, one **sees beings passing away and reappearing** according to their actions (kamma). One discerns how beings are inferior or superior, beautiful or ugly, fortunate or unfortunate, based on their conduct of body, speech, and mind. This knowledge is a part of the "eye of discernment," which, when attained, leads to release from all suffering and stress.

3.  **The clear knowing of the knowledge of the ending of the effluents:** This is the ultimate realization, leading to liberation. It involves **directly discerning "This is stress," "This is the origination of stress," "This is the cessation of stress," and "This is the path of practice leading to the cessation of stress"**. Simultaneously, one discerns the effluents (sensuality, becoming, ignorance), their origination, cessation, and the path to their cessation. Upon this realization, one's mind is **released from the effluents of sensuality, becoming, and ignorance**. This brings about a profound state where there is "no I-making or mine-making conceit-obsession," and the individual has "cut through craving, has ripped off the fetter, and—from rightly breaking through conceit—has put an end to suffering & stress". The sage is described as one who has "crossed over the poisons" and is "released in the ending of craving," bearing their "last body" and having "shaken off Māra," thus going "beyond aging". This "unprovoked release of awareness" is a true and genuine Dhamma that should be realized. The outcome of this deep knowing and seeing is the definitive declaration: "**Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world**". This unbinding is experienced "right within," through a lack of clinging or sustenance. The Dhamma taught leads to disenchantment, dispassion, cessation, stilling, direct knowledge, self-awakening, and unbinding.
