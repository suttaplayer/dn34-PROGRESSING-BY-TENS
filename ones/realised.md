In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents a comprehensive framework of Dhammas, including the profound statement: **"Which one dhamma should be realized? Unprovoked release of awareness"**. Analyzing the detailed meaning of this statement involves understanding the current state of beings, the difficulties encountered on the path, and the ultimate resolution through the Dhamma.

### Situation

The sources describe the prevalent human condition as one of pervasive suffering and spiritual stagnation.

*   Individuals are **"beset by birth, aging, & death; by sorrows, lamentations, pains, distresses, & despairs; beset by stress, overcome with stress"**. This fundamental suffering drives the hope that "Perhaps the end of this entire mass of suffering & stress might be known!".
*   The world is metaphorically **"on fire with aging & death"**, indicating a constant state of impermanence and dissatisfaction.
*   An "uninstructed run-of-the-mill person" often perceives the world with attachment, leading to continued suffering because "he has not comprehended it". This attachment manifests as self-identification and conceit, where one **"assumes form to be the self, or the self as possessing form, or form as in the self, or the self as in form"**. This leads to the continuation of suffering as "his self-identification view—fixed & unsubdued in him—is a lower fetter".

### Complication

The path to "unprovoked release of awareness" is fraught with challenges, primarily due to the subtle and counter-intuitive nature of the Dhamma and the inherent defilements of the mind.

*   The "unprovoked release of awareness" (or **"unmediated concentration of awareness"**) is explicitly stated as **"hard to penetrate"**.
*   The Dhamma itself is characterized as **"deep, hard to see, hard to realize, tranquil, refined, beyond the scope of conjecture, subtle, to-be-experienced by the observant"**. Such profound teachings are not easily grasped by those "overcome with aversion & passion" and "cloaked in the mass of darkness".
*   Even with sincere effort, an imbalanced practice can hinder progress. For example, focusing solely on concentration can lead to laziness, while focusing only on uplifted energy can lead to restlessness. This highlights the need for a **balanced approach to mental cultivation**.
*   A superficial understanding or misinterpretation of the Dhamma can be detrimental. The Blessed One explicitly warned against "poor grasp" of the Dhamma, which can lead to "much demerit" and "long-term harm & suffering". Furthermore, holding onto views or doctrines, even if seemingly "pure" or "bright," can prevent the understanding of Dhamma as a means to cross over, not to cling to.
*   The ultimate realization requires a specific mental disposition: **"This Dhamma is for one who enjoys non-objectification, who delights in non-objectification, not for one who enjoys & delights in objectification"**. The tendency to "objectify the unobjectified" creates agitation and prevents genuine insight. The mind's habitual "intending," "arranging," and "obsessing" act as "support for the stationing of consciousness," leading to "renewed becoming in the future" and thus perpetuating suffering. The ending of suffering is not found through literal "traveling" to the "end of the cosmos," but rather by transcending the perception and conception of the cosmos within one's own experience, particularly through the six sense media.

### Resolution

The realization of **"unprovoked release of awareness"** is the ultimate goal, signifying the complete cessation of suffering and the attainment of **Unbinding (Nibbāna)**. This state is achieved through a deliberate, balanced, and profound cultivation of the Dhamma.

*   The "unprovoked release of awareness" is explicitly identified with the **"effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release,"** which is "directly known and realized... right in the here & now". This release is characterized by **no "I-making or mine-making conceit-obsession"** with regard to the body or external themes. It is a state where the mind is **"unagitated"** due to a "lack of clinging or sustenance" and where "there is no seed for the conditions of future birth, aging, death, or stress".
*   This release is the **"fruit of gnosis [arahantship]"**, leading to the discernment that **"Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world"**.
*   The path to this realization involves a **penetrative understanding** of phenomena: knowing what is skillful and unskillful, and discerning the origination, cessation, allure, drawbacks, and escape from all experiences. This includes understanding **"All phenomena are not-self"** and that **"All fabrications are stressful"**.
*   A crucial step is **abandoning desire-passion** for all phenomena, including the eye, forms, and all six sense bases. As one cultivates **disenchantment** (dispassion), they become "released" from stress and suffering.
*   The cultivation of **concentration, virtue, and discernment** is paramount. Specifically, it entails developing the **seven factors for awakening** (mindfulness, analysis of qualities, persistence, rapture, calm, concentration, and equanimity), which, when "developed & pursued," culminate in **"clear knowing & release"**.
*   The path emphasizes **"appropriate attention"**, which is key to overcoming unskillful qualities and promoting skillful ones, leading to mental distinction. It's about seeing phenomena as **"inconstant, changeable, of a nature to become otherwise"**.
*   Ultimately, the **"unprovoked release of awareness"** means that the mind, through diligent practice and the abandonment of all attachments and fabrications, arrives at a natural, unforced state of peace and freedom, where "all means of speaking are done away with as well".
