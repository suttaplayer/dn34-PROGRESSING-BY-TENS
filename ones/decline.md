In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents a framework of Dhammas categorized by numerical groupings. Within this framework, **inappropriate attention** is identified as a single quality leading to decline. The detailed meaning of this statement can be analyzed using a "Situation, Complication, Resolution" format, drawing on various insights from the sources.

### Situation

**Inappropriate attention** (*ayoniso manasikāra*) is highlighted as a singular factor leading to decline. It characterizes an **uninstructed, run-of-the-mill person** who **doesn't discern what ideas are fit for attention or what ideas are unfit for attention**, and consequently **attends to ideas unfit for attention**. This state of mind is further described as having a **scattered mind, a mind not gathered into one**. Such an individual is **undiscerning, dim-witted, and may assume they understand without truly understanding**. When presented with the Dhamma, they **do not listen well, do not give ear, do not apply their mind to gnosis**, and tend to **grab hold of what is worthless while rejecting what is worthwhile**, lacking the patience to comply with the teaching.

### Complication

The presence of inappropriate attention directly leads to the **increase of unskillful mental qualities** and the **decline of skillful mental qualities**, rendering practices fruitless. Specifically, when one attends to unfit ideas, **unarisen effluents of sensuality, becoming, and ignorance arise, and any arisen effluents increase**. This fosters an **increase in passion, aversion, and delusion**, which are the **three roots of what is unskillful**. This can manifest as **thinking imbued with sensuality, ill-will, and harmfulness**, which **produces blindness, lack of vision, and leads to the cessation of discernment**, hindering progress toward Unbinding. A mind rooted in inappropriate attention is **unconcentrated** and **muddled in mindfulness**, and the individual may be **hard to correct**, **lacking endurance** and **not taking instruction respectfully**. Such individuals are **incapable of alighting on the orderliness or rightness of skillful qualities**. Furthermore, inappropriate attention contributes to **wrong view, wrong resolve, wrong speech, wrong action, wrong livelihood, wrong effort, wrong mindfulness, and wrong concentration**, as well as other **unskillful courses of action**. On a broader scale, a Dhamma-Vinaya can be considered **poorly proclaimed, poorly expounded, not leading out, or not conducive to calming** if it is presented by one who is not rightly self-awakened, and a disciple who practices with inappropriate attention will **deviate from the Dhamma**. The absence of attentive teaching by learned monks due to such heedlessness can even lead to the **confusion and disappearance of the True Dhamma**.

### Resolution

The path to distinction and the cessation of suffering lies in cultivating **appropriate attention**. When one practices appropriate attention, **joy is born**, leading progressively to **rapture, calm, pleasure, a concentrated mind, knowledge and seeing of what has come to be, disenchantment, dispassion, and ultimately release**. This involves listening to the Dhamma with an **unscattered mind, gathered into one**, and **attending appropriately**, thereby **applying one's mind to gnosis**. This enables a disciple to discern **what is skillful and its roots**, which include **lack of greed, lack of aversion, and lack of delusion**. A monk who practices appropriate attention dedicates themselves to **internal tranquility of awareness** and **discerns the Dhamma's higher meaning or goal**, embodying what is called **dwelling in the Dhamma**. Such practice cultivates **discernment of arising and passing away** that is **noble, penetrating, and leads to the right ending of stress**. This mindful and discerning engagement allows one to realize the Dhamma as being **visible here and now, timeless, inviting verification, pertinent, and to be experienced by the observant for themselves**. Ultimately, it is **for one who knows and sees** through this right engagement that there is the **ending of effluents**, leading to **purification** and the **realization of unbinding**.
