In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta presents a framework of Dhammas that should be directly known, among which is the statement: **"All beings are maintained by nutriment"**. This profound statement highlights a core principle in the Dhamma, and its detailed meaning can be analyzed through the "Situation, Complication, Resolution" (SCR) format.

### Situation

The fundamental principle to be directly known is that **"All beings are maintained by nutriment"**. This applies to both beings who have already come into existence and those in search of a place to be born. The sources specify four kinds of nutriment: **physical food (gross or refined), contact, intellectual intention, and consciousness**. The Dhamma teaches that this is a truth "to be seen here & now, timeless, inviting verification, pertinent, to be experienced by the observant for themselves". This direct knowledge is crucial for understanding the root of existence and suffering, as the Tathāgata emphasizes that delight is the root of suffering and stress, and from coming-into-being, there is birth, aging, and death.

### Complication

The complication arises because the **origination of nutriment is rooted in craving**. When there is passion, delight, and craving for any of these nutriments, consciousness becomes fixated and expands. This leads to a chain of conditions: the alighting of name-and-form, the growth of fabrications, and the production of renewed becoming in the future. Ultimately, this cyclical process culminates in **future birth, aging, death, sorrow, affliction, and despair**. In essence, **"Whatever stress comes into play is all from nutriment as a requisite condition"**. Without discerning nutriment, its origination, its cessation, and the path leading to its cessation, one cannot be considered a person of right view. Beings are often "smothered & enveloped like a tangled skein, a knotted ball of string" by craving, which prevents them from transcending transmigration and suffering. Life is seen as "swept along, next-to-nothing its span", prompting a need to understand this danger and seek peace.

### Resolution

The resolution to suffering and stress lies in **directly knowing and comprehending nutriment** in all its aspects: its origination, its cessation, and the path of practice that leads to its cessation. The path to the cessation of nutriment is the **noble eightfold path**, comprising right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration. By cultivating this path, one comes to comprehend all nutriment and achieves independence from it, thereby gaining freedom from disease through the complete ending of effluents. When the nutriment of consciousness is fully understood, name-and-form is also comprehended, signifying that "there is nothing further for a disciple of the noble ones to do". This realization fosters **disenchantment, dispassion, and cessation** with regard to physical forms, feelings, perceptions, fabrications, and consciousness, leading to release from clinging or sustenance. The Dhamma teaches that the "ending of effluents is for one who knows & sees", leading to a state where a monk, "free from hunger is totally unbound". This comprehensive understanding, cultivated through qualities such as modesty, contentment, concentration, and discernment, enables one to drop the "world's bait" and "put an end to stress right here".
