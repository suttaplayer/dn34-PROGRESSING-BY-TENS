In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta outlines various aspects of the Dhamma, including "Contact accompanied by effluents & subject to clinging" as a **single dhamma to be comprehended**. This statement points to a fundamental aspect of experience that, when understood deeply, leads to the cessation of suffering.

Here is an analysis of its detailed meaning in a Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

### Situation

**Contact (Phassa)** is the meeting of an internal sense medium (like the eye), an external sense object (like a form), and consciousness arising in dependence on the two. There are six such media of sensory contact: eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and intellect, which engage with forms, sounds, aromas, flavors, tactile sensations, and ideas, respectively. When one sees a form via the eye or cognizes an idea via the intellect, they explore it as a basis for happiness, unhappiness, or equanimity. This fundamental process of contact is constant, yet the contact itself, and whatever arises from it (like feelings), are **inconstant, changeable, and alterable**.

This contact is often "accompanied by effluents" (Āsava) and "subject to clinging" (Upādāna). **Effluents** are mental defilements that lead to renewed existence, suffering, and future birth, aging, and death. The primary effluents are those of **sensuality, becoming, and ignorance**. **Clinging** arises from craving and refers to the desire, embracing, grasping, and holding-on to the five clinging-aggregates (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness).

### Complication

The problem arises when an uninstructed, run-of-the-mill person, who lacks regard for noble ones and is undisciplined in the Dhamma, engages with these contacts. Instead of discerning things as they truly are (i.e., as inconstant, stressful, and not-self), they relish, welcome, and remain fastened to them. This relishing and clinging to contact, and the feelings that arise from it, leads to the **origination of suffering and stress**.

When one is consumed by desire-passion related to these contacts, their mind becomes defiled. Unabandoned effluents lead to delusion, further becoming, and trouble, ripening in stress and continuing the cycle of birth, aging, and death. This is why the Dhamma describes contact "accompanied by effluents & subject to clinging" as something that needs to be comprehended, because it represents the **root of suffering**.

### Resolution

To comprehend "Contact accompanied by effluents & subject to clinging" means to **directly know** it. This comprehension involves discerning contact, its origination, its cessation, and the path leading to its cessation. Such discernment is a crucial aspect of **right view**.

The comprehension of contact leads to the cessation of feeling, and ultimately, to the **ending of suffering and stress**. This process is achieved by cultivating **disenchantment** with regard to form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness. A concentrated mind discerns these things as they have come to be, realizing their inconstancy and stressful nature.

The path to this comprehension is through developing **concentration** and **mindfulness**, supported by practices such as:
*   **Being easy to instruct and having admirable friendship**.
*   **Associating with people of integrity** and **listening to the True Dhamma**.
*   **Appropriate attention** and **practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhamma**.

Ultimately, the comprehension of contact accompanied by effluents and subject to clinging, alongside the cessation of passion, aversion, and delusion, constitutes the **end of suffering** and the realization of **Unbinding (Nibbāna)**. This release means being **unsustained by anything in the world**, leading to a state of **unagitated, total unbinding**.
