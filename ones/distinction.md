Here is an analysis of the statement "Which one dhamma is on the side of distinction? Appropriate attention", presented in the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

### Situation

Individuals engage with various teachings, and their manner of engagement significantly influences their understanding and progress. The sources describe two types of "assemblies": one "trained in bombast and not in cross-questioning," and another "trained in cross-questioning and not in bombast". In the former, monks neglect discourses of the Tathāgata that are "deep, deep in their meaning, transcendent, connected with emptiness". Instead, they prioritize "literary works—the works of poets, artful in sound, artful in expression, the work of outsiders, words of disciples"—and, even after mastering these, they "don't cross-question one another about it, don't dissect". Conversely, the latter assembly values the Tathāgata's deep discourses, listens attentively, and actively dissects and clarifies their meaning. This highlights that simply hearing or studying Dhamma is not sufficient; a specific quality of engagement is necessary for genuine understanding and benefit.

### Complication

The core complication arises from a **lack of appropriate attention**, which the sources identify as a quality "on the side of decline". When individuals do not apply appropriate attention, they fail to gain genuine insight into the Dhamma. For instance, if one attends to "ideas unfit for attention," such as those that increase "unarisen effluent of sensuality… becoming… ignorance," these unskillful qualities will arise or increase. This negligence means that, even if one studies, describes, recites, or thinks about the Dhamma, they "doesn't, with his discernment, discern [the Dhamma's] higher meaning or goal". Such a person may not "alight on the orderliness, on the rightness of skillful qualities" even when listening to the True Dhamma. Without appropriate attention, individuals may "misrepresent the Blessed One with what is unfactual" or remain "unconcentrated" and "undiscerning". This deficiency prevents them from achieving the spiritual progress the Dhamma offers, leaving them unable to "cross over beyond entanglement in the world".

### Resolution

The resolution lies in cultivating **appropriate attention**, explicitly stated as a "dhamma on the side of distinction". This quality is fundamental for true understanding and the realization of unbinding. An individual practicing appropriate attention will discern "what ideas are fit for attention and what ideas are unfit for attention" and "doesn't attend to ideas unfit for attention and attends (instead) to ideas fit for attention". This discernment leads to a proper grasp of the Dhamma, allowing one to understand what is "skillful" and "unskillful," including their roots of greed, aversion, and delusion.

Furthermore, appropriate attention is crucial for discerning the **Four Noble Truths**: "stress, the origination of stress, the cessation of stress, and the way of practice leading to the cessation of stress". This focused mental engagement is a prerequisite for "the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding". By applying appropriate attention, one comes to see things "as they have come to be with right discernment," which leads to "disenchantment, dispassion, cessation, through lack of clinging or sustenance" and ultimately, release from suffering. The Dhamma, when taught and engaged with appropriate attention, is "conducive to the goal, conducive to the Dhamma, and basic to the holy life" and "leads to disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding". This signifies that appropriate attention is a vital practice for anyone seeking their "own good" and aspiring for "greatness" within the teachings.
