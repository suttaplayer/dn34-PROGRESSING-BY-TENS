Here is an analysis of the statement "Which one dhamma is hard to penetrate? Unmediated concentration of awareness" from the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), presented in the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation: What is "Unmediated Concentration of Awareness"?**

"Unmediated concentration of awareness", also referred to as "unprovoked release of awareness", represents a profound state of mental unification and tranquility. It is a concentration that is **peaceful, exquisite, attained through calming, and immersed in unification**. This state is characterized by the mind being **pliant, malleable, luminous, and not brittle**, leading to its being rightly concentrated for the ending of the effluents.

A key aspect of this unmediated state is the **cessation and stilling of objectification**. It is a concentration where one is **"still as a result of release, contented as a result of standing still, and as a result of contentment one is not agitated"**. This state of being "unagitated" stems from a lack of clinging or sustenance, which ultimately means there is **"no seed for the conditions of future birth, aging, death, or stress"**. In this deep state, the Tathāgata, for instance, is described as **"the same with regard to all phenomena that can be seen, heard, sensed, & cognized—is 'Such.' And I tell you: There is no other 'Such' higher or more sublime"**. The Dhamma itself is for **"one who enjoys non-objectification, who delights in non-objectification, not for one who enjoys & delights in objectification"**.

**Complication: Why is "Unmediated Concentration of Awareness" Hard to Penetrate?**

This profound state is "hard to penetrate" because the Dhamma that describes it is **"deep, hard to see, hard to realize, peaceful, refined, beyond the scope of conjecture, subtle, to-be-experienced by the wise"**. It goes against the natural inclinations of most people, as **"this generation delights in attachment, is excited by attachment, enjoys attachment"**. For those immersed in such attachments, **"this or that conditionality & dependent co-arising are hard to see"**.

Furthermore, individuals who are **"delighting in passion, cloaked in the mass of darkness, won't see"**. The presence of "passion, aversion, & delusion" hinders understanding and prevents the arising of clear knowing. Without the proper development of various factors for awakening, even with the desire for release, the mind may not be released from effluents. This state transcends conventional understanding and fixed views, which many are accustomed to holding.

**Resolution: How is "Unmediated Concentration of Awareness" Achieved?**

Penetrating this unmediated concentration requires a dedicated path of practice and specific insights:

*   **Abandoning Hindrances:** The path begins by abandoning defiling qualities such as sensual desire, ill will, sloth and drowsiness, restlessness and anxiety, and uncertainty. When the mind is **"rightly-gone, rightly developed, has rightly risen above, gained release, & become disjoined from sensuality,"** one is released from effluents, torments, and fevers that arise from sensuality.
*   **Direct Knowledge and Comprehension:** The ultimate realization, or **"knowledge of unbinding,"** follows the **"knowledge of the regularity of the Dhamma"** (dependent co-arising). The Buddha, as the **"Eye, he is Knowledge, he is Dhamma, he is Brahmā,"** teaches through **direct knowledge and realization**. This enables one to discern, for example, that **"All fabrications are stressful"**.
*   **Understanding Dependent Co-arising and Noble Truths:** A crucial step is to penetrate the reality of dependent co-arising. **"From ignorance as a requisite condition come fabrications,"** and this chain continues through consciousness, name-and-form, the six sense media, contact, and feeling, ultimately leading to **"aging-&-death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, & despair... the origination of this entire mass of stress & suffering"**. The ending of effluents comes for one who **"knows & sees"** the origination and disappearance of form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness. This leads to the comprehension of the **"four noble truths: stress, the origination of stress, the cessation of stress, & the path of practice leading to the cessation of stress"**.
*   **Cultivating Essential Qualities:** The individual must develop qualities that include conviction, persistence, mindfulness, concentration, and discernment.
*   **Outcome - Unbinding and End of Suffering:** By penetrating these truths and reaching this state of unmediated concentration, one becomes **"unsustained"** by anything in the world. **"Unsustained, he isn't agitated. Unagitated, he totally unbinds right within. He discerns: 'Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world'"**. Ultimately, this leads to the **"end of stress"**.
