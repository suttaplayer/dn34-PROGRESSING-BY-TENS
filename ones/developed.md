In the Dasuttara Sutta of Dīgha Nikāya 34, Venerable Sāriputta presents a framework of Dhamma qualities that are helpful, to be developed, to be comprehended, and so on, progressing by tens. Among these, "Mindfulness immersed in the body connected with joy" is identified as a single Dhamma that should be developed. This statement, though brief, points to a profound and central practice in the path to liberation.

Here's a detailed analysis in the requested SCR format:

**Situation**

The core "dhamma to be developed" is **mindfulness immersed in the body connected with joy**. This practice involves a monk (or noble disciple) remaining focused on the body itself, being ardent, alert, and mindful, while subduing greed and distress related to the world. The presence of "joy" is a significant indicator of progress and a conducive state for deeper concentration and release. The Blessed One has emphasized mindfulness of the body as a practice that, when cultivated, developed, and pursued, leads to great fruit and great benefit, ultimately gaining a footing in the deathless and having the deathless as its final end. This practice is part of a broader instruction to "stay mindful and alert".

**Complication**

Developing this deep level of mindfulness and associated joy is not always straightforward. For a monk who is "heedless" or neglects seclusion, even if they possess confidence in the Buddha, Dhamma, and Saṅgha, joy may not arise, leading to pain and an unconcentrated mind where phenomena remain unmanifest. The Dhamma is specifically for those who are **reclusive, not entangled**, and whose persistence is aroused, not lazy. Without diligence, even a wish for release through lack of clinging may not be fulfilled; development is necessary. If one attends solely to concentration, the mind might tend to laziness; solely to uplifted energy, it might tend to restlessness; solely to equanimity, it might not be rightly concentrated for the ending of effluents.

**Resolution**

The cultivation of "mindfulness immersed in the body connected with joy" involves specific steps and leads to significant spiritual advancement:

*   **Initial Practice:** A monk goes to a secluded place (wilderness, tree shade, empty dwelling) and sits, folding legs crosswise, holding the body erect, and establishing mindfulness to the fore. **Mindfully, they breathe in and out**, discerning long or short breaths, and training to be sensitive to the entire body and calming bodily fabrication. This mindful breathing is classified as focusing on the body itself.
*   **Overcoming Hindrances & Arousing Joy:** As one remains heedful, ardent, and resolute, memories and resolves related to household life are abandoned. With their abandoning, the mind gathers and settles inwardly, becoming unified and concentrated. This seclusion from sensuality and unskillful qualities is foundational for entering the first jhāna, characterized by **rapture and pleasure born of seclusion, accompanied by directed thought and evaluation**. When the mind is directed to an inspiring theme, gladness is born, followed by rapture, a calmed body, pleasure, and a concentrated mind. This state of being "sensitive to the meaning, sensitive to the Dhamma" directly leads to joy, rapture, calm, pleasure, and concentration.
*   **Progression and Release:** The cultivation of joy and concentration progresses through the jhānas. With the stilling of directed thoughts and evaluations, one enters the second jhāna (rapture and pleasure born of concentration). With the fading of rapture, one reaches the third jhāna (equanimous, mindful, alert, sensing pleasure with the body). This progressive refinement leads to a mind that is pliant, malleable, luminous, and not brittle, becoming **rightly concentrated for the ending of effluents**.
*   **Ultimate Outcome:** By developing mindfulness immersed in the body, one gains various benefits, including knowledge and vision, and ultimately reaches the **effluent-free awareness-release and discernment-release**. This leads to the direct realization that "Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world". It is considered a path to the "Unprovoked release" and is capable of leading to **superlative self-awakening**.
