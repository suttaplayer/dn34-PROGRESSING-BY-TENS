In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta presents a comprehensive framework of Dhammas, including a section on the "ten sense media" that should be comprehended. This prompt focuses on analyzing the detailed meaning of this statement.

### Situation

The statement refers to the **ten sense media**: the sense medium of the eye and forms, the ear and sounds, the nose and aromas, the tongue and flavors, and the body and tactile sensations. More broadly, these are understood as the six internal sense media (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, intellect) and their corresponding six external sense media (form, sound, aroma, flavor, tactile sensation, idea). These media define how beings engage with the world. The process begins when, dependent on an internal sense medium and an external sense object, a specific class of consciousness arises (e.g., eye-consciousness from the eye and forms). The **meeting of these three**—the internal sense medium, the external sense object, and the corresponding consciousness—is called **contact**. From contact as a necessary condition, feeling arises, and with feeling as a requisite condition, craving arises.

### Complication

The problem arises when there is **desire-passion** for these sense media, as this constitutes a **defilement of the mind**. If one is "not knowing, not seeing the eye as it has come to be" and is consequently "infatuated with the eye... forms... consciousness at the eye... contact at the eye," they are not truly seeing things as they are. When a monk "relishes them, welcomes them, and remains fastened to them," delight arises, and "from the origination of delight... comes the origination of suffering & stress". Similarly, "whoever delights in forms delights in stress. Whoever delights in stress is not released from stress". This infatuation with sense objects is rooted in ignorance. Not fully comprehending these sense objects (or "signs") can lead one to be bound by them and remain within the "bonds of death". Such a state signifies a mind immersed in ignorance and overcome with craving, leading to agitation and the perpetuation of stress, where "there is no seed for the conditions of future birth, aging, death, or stress". Misunderstanding the Dhamma in this way is considered an "evil viewpoint," leading to self-injury and accumulated demerit. The Buddha emphasizes that "whatever is felt comes under stress", highlighting that all experiences derived from sensory contact inherently carry a stressful nature if clung to.

### Resolution

To achieve liberation, one must comprehend these sense media and their dependent processes. This comprehension is fundamental to **right view**, where one discerns what is unskillful (like clinging to senses) and the root of what is unskillful. When the defilements of awareness related to these six sensory bases are abandoned, the mind becomes inclined to renunciation and becomes pliable for directly knowing what is truly worth realizing. This involves **not relishing, welcoming, or remaining fastened to sensual experiences**.

The core insight for such comprehension is realizing that all phenomena, including those experienced through the senses, are **inconstant, stressful, and not-self**. The practice entails developing mindfulness to observe that "in reference to the seen, there will be only the seen," and similarly for all other senses. This leads to the profound realization that "there is no you in connection with that," and ultimately, "this, just this, is the end of stress". When one understands that all feelings (which arise from contact via sense media) are impermanent, they are released from suffering and stress. The comprehension of feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness leads to "total release from sorrows, lamentations, pains, distresses, & despairs". The ultimate goal is to "abandon desire-passion" for whatever is "not-self". This leads to the "cessation of objectification, the stilling of objectification" with the complete fading and cessation of the six contact-media. This state, where "sensual resolves cease," describes a practitioner who is "free from hunger, unbound, having crossed over & gone to the far shore". Through discernment, the mind is "rightly released from the effluents, i.e., the effluent of sensuality, the effluent of becoming, the effluent of ignorance". Ultimately, "from the abandoning of craving, action [kamma] is abandoned. From the abandoning of action, stress is abandoned". A key practice is to discern whether pursuing a sensory experience leads to an increase in unskillful qualities or a decrease, thus guiding whether it should be pursued. This detailed understanding of the sense media, their dependent origination, and the path to their cessation forms a core aspect of the four noble truths, leading to the right ending of suffering and stress.
