Here is an analysis of the statement from the DASUTTARA SUTTA, presented in the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation**

Venerable Sāriputta outlines "ten dhammas" that "should be developed" for the holy life to endure and for the benefit and happiness of many. Among these are the **"Ten totality-dimensions"**. These dimensions involve perceiving various elements and colors, as well as space and consciousness, as **"above, below, all-around: non-dual [advayaṁ], immeasurable"**. Specifically, these ten totality-dimensions are: earth, water, fire, wind, blue, yellow, red, white, space, and consciousness. These are also referred to as "noble dwellings" in which noble ones have dwelled, dwell, and will dwell. Within this framework, the **"consciousness-totality" is considered supreme**.

**Complication**

While the totality-dimensions represent significant states of concentration to be developed, they are not the ultimate goal of liberation. Even in beings who are "percipient" in the way of the consciousness-totality, there is still **"aberration" and "change"**. The Buddha discerns that these standpoints, though profound and often attained through mental mastery, are ultimately **"fabricated, gross"**. Therefore, merely achieving these concentrations does not signify the final end of suffering. The text indicates that these types of concentration can be "hard to penetrate" if one does not continue to discern beyond them. The Buddha emphasizes that a "cessation of fabrications" exists beyond these states, implying they are not the final liberation.

**Resolution**

The ultimate purpose of developing such profound concentrations is to facilitate the realization of **unbinding (Nibbana)**. This state is described as "pleasant" precisely because **"nothing is felt"** in a sensual or painful way, representing the "cessation of perception & feeling". This cessation involves the ceasing of perceptions and feelings, which are mental fabrications. The path leads to **"effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release,"** which are attained by "directly known and realized them for oneself right in the here & now". This ultimate release is fundamentally characterized by a **"lack of clinging or sustenance"**. The Dhamma is for those who **"enjoy non-objectification, who delight in non-objectification,"** leading to the "stilling of objectification", where "the currents of supposition do not flow," allowing one to be a "sage at peace". The Buddha himself achieved his unexcelled right self-awakening by recognizing that "delight is the root of suffering & stress" and realizing the "total ending, fading away, cessation, letting go, relinquishment of craving".
