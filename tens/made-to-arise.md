In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents a framework of Dhammas that should be "made to arise," which includes ten specific perceptions. These perceptions are fundamental to achieving unbinding and ending suffering and stress.

**Situation**
The Buddhist teachings outline **ten perceptions that should be cultivated** or "made to arise" as a path to profound understanding and ultimate liberation. These perceptions are: the perception of unattractiveness, the perception of death, the perception of loathsomeness in food, the perception of distaste for every world, the perception of inconstancy, the perception of stress in what is inconstant, the perception of not-self in what is stressful, the perception of abandoning, the perception of dispassion, and the perception of cessation. Developing these perceptions is described as being "of great fruit, of great benefit," leading to "a footing in the deathless" and having "the deathless as their final end".

**Complication**
Without cultivating these vital perceptions, individuals remain entangled in the world, succumbing to attachment, craving, and wrong views. An assembly "trained in bombast and not in cross-questioning" neglects deep, transcendent discourses connected with emptiness, instead focusing on literary works that are artful in sound and expression. Similarly, individuals who do not develop these perceptions may find their minds overcome by passion, aversion, and delusion. For instance, a monk who fails to discern an inner blemish and attends to the theme of beauty can have passion assault their mind, leading to a defiled state. Without understanding and penetrating the Dhamma, one's generation can become like "a tangled skein, a knotted ball of string," unable to go beyond transmigration and suffering. Furthermore, being overcome by factors like ill will and passion can prevent one from realizing the deeper, subtle Dhamma. Those who are "senseless, immersed in ignorance, overcome with craving" might even misunderstand or slander the core teachings, leading to long-term harm and demerit.

**Resolution**
The cultivation of these ten perceptions enables a practitioner to develop clear knowing and direct knowledge, leading to the abandonment of defilements, the cessation of suffering, and ultimately, unbinding.

Here’s how developing each perception contributes to this resolution:

*   **Perception of unattractiveness (of the body):** By focusing on the body's unattractiveness, a monk cultivates a mind that **shrinks away from fervor for life**, leading to equanimity or loathing towards it. This practice is "of great fruit" and "gains a footing in the deathless".
*   **Perception of death:** When a monk's awareness is steeped in the perception of death, their mind "shrinks away from fervor for life," bending away and pulling back, resulting in equanimity or loathing. This powerful perception also **gains a footing in the deathless**.
*   **Perception of loathsomeness in food:** Remaining focused on the loathsomeness of food contributes to the path towards the deathless, being "of great fruit".
*   **Perception of distaste for every world:** A monk focusing on distaste for every world cultivates a mind that will "not delight in any world" and will "rise above every world". This leads to the deathless and is of great benefit.
*   **Perception of inconstancy:** Directly knowing that "all fabrications are inconstant" allows the mind to shrink away from gains, offerings, and fame. Even a brief development of this perception is considered **more fruitful than vast generosity** because it leads to the deathless.
*   **Perception of stress in what is inconstant:** Recognizing that "all fabrications are stressful" helps establish a perception of disenchantment, like seeing a "murderer with a drawn sword". This perception also leads to the deathless.
*   **Perception of not-self in what is stressful:** Understanding that "all phenomena are not-self" results in the cessation of "I-making" and "my-making". Like the other perceptions, it is of great benefit and "gains a footing in the deathless".
*   **Perception of abandoning:** This perception is vital for "the abandoning of the gross appropriation of a self" and the cessation of defiling mental qualities, leading to a state of joy, rapture, and calm. It is part of the "five perceptions for the maturing of release".
*   **Perception of dispassion:** Dispassion involves the "subduing of intoxication, the elimination of thirst, the uprooting of attachment, the breaking of the round, the destruction of craving, cessation, the realization of Unbinding". Cultivating dispassion leads directly to release from suffering.
*   **Perception of cessation:** This refers to the cessation of various phenomena such as perception, feeling, and fabrications. It is understood that "the ending of effluents depends on the first jhāna". Ultimately, "with the remainderless fading & cessation of the six contact-media, there comes to be the cessation of objectification, the stilling of objectification", leading to a state where the Tathāgata is "released through lack of clinging or sustenance".

These ten perceptions, when diligently developed and pursued, allow the mind to be "inclined to renunciation" and become "malleable for the direct knowing of those qualities worth realizing," paving the direct path to the purification of beings and the realization of unbinding.
