The "Ten noble abodes" are described in the sources as **qualities that are "hard to penetrate"**. These are the states in which noble ones have dwelled in the past, dwell in the present, and will dwell in the future. A monk who embodies these qualities is said to be dwelling in a noble abode.

**Situation:**
A monk's journey to liberation involves dwelling in specific states that are challenging to attain and understand, referred to as "Ten noble abodes". These qualities serve as the foundation for true spiritual advancement and the cessation of suffering.

**Complication:**
While the general framework of these ten noble abodes is provided, some of their components are presented briefly, requiring further analysis from other parts of the sources to fully grasp their meaning. Specifically, the "six" qualities a monk must be "endowed with" are not explicitly detailed in the provided texts in this specific context.

**Resolution:**
The detailed meaning of the "Ten noble abodes" indicates a profound state of spiritual accomplishment, marked by the following characteristics:

*   **Abandoned five factors:** This refers to the **abandonment of the five hindrances**: sensual desire, ill will, sloth & torpor, restlessness & anxiety, and uncertainty. These are considered **"obstacles, hindrances that overwhelm awareness and weaken discernment,"** making it impossible to understand what is beneficial for oneself or others. Their relinquishment is essential for progress on the path.

*   **Endowed with six:** In the specific context of the "Ten noble abodes," the sources state that a monk is "endowed with six" qualities, but do not explicitly detail what these six qualities are for this factor. However, the sources do mention other important sets of six qualities, such as **"six objects of recollection"** (recollection of the Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, virtue, generosity, and devas), and **"six types of respect"** (for the Teacher, Dhamma, Saṅgha, training, heedfulness, and welcoming manners), which are conducive to distinction and beneficial for spiritual development.

*   **Guards one:** This signifies that a monk is **"endowed with an awareness guarded by mindfulness"**. Mindfulness is presented as a factor for awakening, leading to clear knowing and release, and is an instruction given to monks to "stay mindful, monks, and alert".

*   **Supported in four:** A monk in this state, by carefully reflecting, **"follows one thing, tolerates another, avoids another, and destroys another"**. This illustrates a wise and strategic approach to dealing with various mental qualities and external circumstances.

*   **Shaken off idiosyncratic truths:** This means the monk has **"shaken off the run-of-the-mill idiosyncratic truths of run-of-the-mill contemplatives & brahmans"**. These include speculative views about the cosmos (eternal, not eternal, finite, infinite), the nature of the self (same as body, different from body), and the existence of a Tathāgata after death (exists, does not exist, both, neither). Such views are deemed by the Buddha as **"not conducive to the goal, are not conducive to the Dhamma, are not basic to the holy life,"** and do not lead to disenchantment, dispassion, cessation, calm, direct knowledge, self-awakening, or unbinding.

*   **Thoroughly given up searching:** This implies an end to the spiritual quest or seeking, indicating a state of fulfillment. It is associated with a monk who, **"through the relinquishing of searches & the abolishing of viewpoints… is without perplexity, free of longing"**. This signifies having done what needs to be done for liberation.

*   **Undisturbed in his resolves:** This refers to a mind that is **"calm & unaroused"** and **"composed in mind"**. Such a state reflects mental stability and freedom from the agitation that arises from defilements.

*   **Calmed in his bodily-fabrication:** This is achieved through advanced meditative states, specifically when the **"in-&-out breaths [bodily fabrications] have ceased"** in the fourth jhāna. The fourth jhāna is described as a **"purity of equanimity & mindfulness, neither pleasure nor pain"**.

*   **Well released in mind:** This refers to **"awareness-release,"** where the monk's mind is freed from passion, aversion, and delusion. This leads to the **"total end"** of effluents.

*   **Well released in discernment:** This refers to **"discernment-release,"** which signifies liberation achieved through profound wisdom and insight. The mind **"nurtured with discernment is rightly released from the effluents"**. This highest form of release is tied to the **"gnosis of emancipation"**.
