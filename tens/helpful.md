In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta presents a comprehensive framework of Dhamma teachings, including a discussion on the qualities that make an individual a "protector." The query specifically asks for a detailed analysis of the statement: "Which ten dhammas are very helpful? Ten qualities creating a protector".

Here is an analysis using the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation**
The Buddhist teachings emphasize the importance of individuals who serve as **great benefactors** within the spiritual community, fostering growth and providing a solid foundation for others. Such individuals are described as "protectors" due to their beneficial influence and the positive outcomes associated with their presence and practice. The qualities that constitute a "protector" are crucial for the stability and flourishment of the Dhamma and Saṅgha (community).

**Ten Qualities Creating a Protector**
The sources identify ten specific qualities that enable a monk to be a beneficial force, promoting spiritual growth and preventing decline:

1.  **Virtuous**: A monk who is a protector **dwells restrained in accordance with the Pāṭimokkha, consummate in his behavior & sphere of activity**, and **trains himself, having undertaken the training rules, seeing danger in the slightest faults**.
2.  **Learned**: He **has heard much, has retained what he has heard, has stored what he has heard**. This includes teachings that are "admirable in the beginning, admirable in the middle, admirable in the end, that—in their meaning & expression—proclaim the holy life that is entirely perfect, surpassingly pure". He has "listened to often, retained, discussed, accumulated, examined with his mind, & well-penetrated in terms of his views".
3.  **Eloquent in Dhamma talk**: Such a monk is **eloquent in Dhamma talk, not making Dhamma talk a hard thing**. This implies that his statements are "well-spoken, not ill-spoken," and "blameless & unfaulted by knowledgeable people" because they are "spoken at the right time... in truth... affectionately... beneficially... with a mind of goodwill".
4.  **Aroused persistence**: He **keeps his persistence aroused for abandoning unskillful qualities and taking on skillful qualities**. He is **steadfast, solid in his effort, not shirking his duties** with regard to skillful qualities.
5.  **Content**: A protector is **content with any old robe cloth at all, any old almsfood, any old lodging, any old medicinal requisites for curing sickness at all**.
6.  **Not deceitful, not stubborn**: The monk who is a protector **is not deceitful, not stubborn**. Conversely, monks who are "not deceitful, not talkers, who are enlightened, pliant, & well-centered: they are followers" who attain growth.
7.  **Easy to instruct**: He is **easy to instruct, possessed of qualities that make him easy to instruct**. He actively seeks knowledge by "approaching at regular intervals those monks who are learned…—asks & questions them, 'How is this, venerable sirs? What is the meaning of this?'" and those senior monks "make open what isn't open, make plain what isn't plain, dispel doubt on various doubtful points".
8.  **Has a sense of compunction**: He **has a sense of compunction with regard to skillful qualities**. This quality, along with a sense of shame, is considered a "treasure" within the Dhamma.
9.  **Astute**: This quality, closely related to aroused persistence, signifies an individual's active diligence in **abandoning unskillful qualities and taking on skillful qualities**, maintaining steadfast and solid effort without shirking duties.
10. **Discerning**: He is **discerning, endowed with discernment of arising & passing away—noble, penetrating, leading to the right ending of stress**. This quality is highlighted as "the surpassing state" for a monk.

**Complication**
Without these protective qualities, individuals and the Dhamma itself can face significant challenges and decline. An assembly or individual "trained in bombast" but "not in cross-questioning" may listen to literary works but fail to deeply understand the "discourses of the Tathāgata," neglecting to "cross-question one another about it" or "dispel doubt". This hinders genuine understanding and progress. Furthermore, lacking virtue and abandoning impurities can lead to dire consequences, as an "unvirtuous" individual who has "n't abandoned the impurity of being unvirtuous" may be "placed in hell". Such individuals, if "deceitful, stubborn, talkers, frauds, arrogant, & uncentered are not followers" and "attain, in terms of this Dhamma-&-Vinaya, no growth, increase, or abundance". Even the True Dhamma's longevity is tied to the cultivation of practices like the four establishings of mindfulness, as "from the non-development & non-pursuit... the True Dhamma does not last long".

**Resolution**
Cultivating these ten qualities enables a monk to be a true "protector," ensuring growth in skillful qualities and preventing decline. These qualities contribute directly to the **stability, non-confusion, & non-disappearance of the True Dhamma**. The path cultivated through these qualities is described as the **"direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding"**. Ultimately, when "right view is supported by five factors, it has awareness-release as its fruit... has discernment-release as its fruit". By **"directly knowing what should be directly known, comprehending what should be comprehended, he is one who puts an end to suffering & stress in the here & now"**, achieving a state of release through a deep understanding of the origination and cessation of feelings and clinging.
