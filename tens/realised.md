Here is an analysis of the statement from the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), presented in the "Situation, Complication, Resolution" (SCR) format, drawing on the provided sources:

**Situation**

The holy life in the Dhamma-Vinaya is pursued with the ultimate aspiration of realizing **Unbinding (Nibbāna)** and putting an end to suffering and stress. This pursuit is characterized as a "direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding". The Buddha's teaching, the Dhamma, is consistently described as "admirable in its beginning, admirable in its middle, admirable in its end" and as expounding the holy life "both in its particulars & in its essence, entirely perfect, surpassingly pure". It is understood that the path to this ultimate freedom is the **Noble Eightfold Path**, which is considered the "best" of paths. The essential qualities of the Dhamma are those that "lead to utter disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding".

**Complication**

The journey towards this ultimate realization is fraught with obstacles. The mind is inherently "defiled by passion" and "defiled by ignorance," preventing discernment from developing and release from being attained. The very roots of unskillful actions are identified as **greed, aversion, and delusion**. These "unskillful thoughts," such as those imbued with sensuality, ill-will, or harmfulness, are seen to "produce blindness, produce lack of vision, produce lack of knowledge, leads to the cessation of discernment, side with vexation, and are not conducive to Unbinding". It is by attending to these unfit ideas that "unarisen effluent of sensuality arises... and the arisen effluent of sensuality increases; the unarisen effluent of becoming arises... and the arisen effluent of ignorance increases". Furthermore, "craving is the seamstress," constantly "stitching one to the production of this or that very becoming," thereby perpetuating suffering. Uninstructed individuals, failing to discern views or their cessation, remain entangled in the cycle of birth, aging, and death.

**Resolution**

The statement from the DASUTTARA SUTTA refers to the "ten qualities of one beyond training" that should be realized. "One beyond training" refers to an **Arahant**, a fully enlightened individual who has completed the holy life, signifying that "Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world". An Arahant's effluents (sensuality, becoming, ignorance) are ended. The realization of these ten qualities marks the culmination of the path:

*   **Right View (of one beyond training)**: This perfected view involves discerning what is skillful and unskillful, along with their roots, thereby abandoning passion, aversion, and the conceit of "I am," leading to clear knowing and the end of suffering. It fundamentally means discerning the **four noble truths**: stress, its origination, its cessation, and the path to its cessation, as they truly are. This includes the profound understanding that "All fabrications are stressful" and "All phenomena are not-self".
*   **Right Resolve (of one beyond training)**: The Arahant's resolve is perfected through the **cultivation of thoughts imbued with renunciation, non-ill will, and harmlessness**, deliberately steering the mind away from sensuality, ill-will, and harmful intentions.
*   **Right Speech, Right Action, & Right Livelihood (of one beyond training)**: These virtues represent **consummate ethical conduct**. An Arahant abstains entirely from taking life, stealing, sexual misconduct, lying, divisive speech, harsh speech, and idle chatter, speaking only what is truthful, affectionate, beneficial, and timely. Such conduct is impossible for an Arahant to transgress.
*   **Right Effort (of one beyond training)**: This involves maintaining **persistence aroused for abandoning unskillful qualities and taking on skillful qualities**, being steadfast and solid in effort, and never shirking duties related to skillful qualities.
*   **Right Mindfulness (of one beyond training)**: The Arahant remains **focused on the body, feelings, mind, and mental qualities**, "ardent, alert, & mindful—subduing greed & distress with reference to the world". This mindfulness is cultivated and pursued to bring clear knowing and release to their culmination.
*   **Right Concentration (of one beyond training)**: The Arahant achieves **deep states of mental absorption (jhānas)**, characterized by seclusion from sensuality and unskillful qualities, leading to rapture, pleasure, and the unification of awareness. When fully developed, the mind becomes "pliant, malleable, luminous, & not brittle," and "rightly concentrated for the ending of the effluents". An Arahant enters and emerges from concentration mindfully.
*   **Right Knowledge (of one beyond training)**: This is the **direct knowledge (gnosis)** of the ending of effluents and their non-recurrence. It encompasses the profound understanding of the "regularity of the Dhamma" (dependent co-arising), which precedes the knowledge of unbinding. It is the discernment of "arising & passing away—noble, penetrating, leading to the right ending of stress".
*   **Right Release (of one beyond training)**: This is the **final and complete liberation of the mind** from the effluents of sensuality, becoming, and ignorance, achieved through a profound "lack of clinging or sustenance". Upon achieving this, one discerns that "Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world". This state is also referred to as "awareness-release" and "discernment-release". Such a person, "released through right gnosis, cannot possibly transgress these nine principles", indicating the steadfast and irreversible nature of their attainment.
