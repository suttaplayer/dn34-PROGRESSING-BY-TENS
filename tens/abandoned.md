Venerable Sāriputta, in the DASUTTARA SUTTA, details a framework of Dhammas, including ten forms of wrongness that should be abandoned. This teaching provides a comprehensive understanding of unskillful actions and their consequences, as well as the path to their cessation.

***

### Situation

The fundamental situation described in the sources is the prevalence of **unskillful qualities** and "wrongness" in the world, which can lead to negative outcomes such as harm, suffering, and undesirable rebirths. The Dhamma teaches that "bodily misconduct, verbal misconduct, & mental misconduct should not be done". These forms of misconduct are directly linked to greed, aversion, and delusion, which are identified as causes for the origination of actions and are considered "inside stains, inside enemies, inside foes, inside murderers, inside adversaries". The sources emphasize that these unskillful qualities prevent mental release and the development of discernment.

### Complication

The ten forms of wrongness outlined by Venerable Sāriputta are interconnected and lead to adverse states. They are: wrong view, wrong resolve, wrong speech, wrong action, wrong livelihood, wrong effort, wrong mindfulness, wrong concentration, wrong knowledge, and wrong release.

*   **Wrong view** is identified as a primary cause from which all other forms of wrongness arise. It is characterized by beliefs such as "There is nothing given, nothing offered, nothing sacrificed" or "no fruit or result of good or bad actions". Holding such views means one cannot "pin down as a truth or reality what should & shouldn't be done," leading to a state of being bewildered and unprotected. It's a "mental blockage" that prevents one from alighting on the orderliness of skillful qualities.
*   From wrong view, **wrong resolve, wrong speech, wrong action, wrong livelihood, wrong effort, wrong mindfulness, and wrong concentration** sequentially come into being. These represent the unskillful application of one's will, communication, physical deeds, means of sustenance, mental exertion, presence of mind, and meditative states.
    *   **Bodily misconduct** includes taking life, taking what is not given (stealing), and engaging in sexual misconduct.
    *   **Verbal misconduct** involves telling lies, divisive speech, harsh speech, and idle chatter. Telling a deliberate lie is particularly serious, implying that "there's no evil not to be done" for such a person. Inappropriate formulation and answering of questions are also marks of a fool.
    *   **Mental misconduct** involves covetousness, malevolence (ill will), and wrong view. Passion, aversion, and delusion are significant hindrances that prevent understanding and progress. Delusion is described as "ignorance," which precedes other unskillful qualities like lack of shame and compunction. Ignorance is fed by the five hindrances, which are themselves fed by misconduct, lack of sense restraint, lack of mindfulness, inappropriate attention, and lack of conviction.
*   Ultimately, these lead to **wrong knowledge and wrong release**. The inability to abandon defilements means one remains "deluded" and caught in a cycle of future birth, aging, and death. Such practices are described as "dark with dark result," leading to painful experiences like those in hell.

### Resolution

The resolution to these forms of wrongness lies in their abandonment, leading to the cultivation of their counterparts: **right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration, right knowledge, and right release**. This progressive abandonment enables a monk to increase their skillful mental qualities and decrease unskillful ones, leading to fruitful practice.

The **Noble Eightfold Path**—which encompasses right view, resolve, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, and concentration—is explicitly stated as "kamma that is neither dark nor bright with neither dark nor bright result, leading to the ending of kamma". It is the "middle way realized by the Tathāgata" that leads to stilling, direct knowledge, self-awakening, and unbinding. For those who follow this path, suffering and stress are put to an end.

The Dhamma taught by the Blessed One is described as "unrefuted, undefiled, blameless, not faulted by knowledgeable contemplatives & brahmans," covering essential topics like the six properties, six sensory contact media, eighteen explorations for the intellect, and the four noble truths. Understanding and practicing this Dhamma leads to clarity and well-being.

**Right knowledge and right release** are the culmination of this path. A mind that is "well shored-up by discernment" is devoid of passion, aversion, and delusion, indicating the end of birth and the fulfillment of the holy life. Through the fading of passion comes awareness-release, and through the fading of ignorance comes discernment-release. Release is the "heartwood" of the holy life, with discernment as its "surpassing state" and mindfulness as its "governing principle". This path allows for the complete end of suffering and stress, ultimately leading to total unbinding.

The sources highlight the importance of knowing and seeing for the ending of effluents. This includes understanding the origination and disappearance of phenomena like form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness. When one knows and sees in this way, there is an immediate ending of effluents.

By diligently following the Dhamma, one becomes "consummate in virtue, consummate in concentration, consummate in discernment". This cultivation leads to the attainment of gnosis and the realization of unbinding.

*   "I say categorically, Ānanda, that bodily misconduct, verbal misconduct, & mental misconduct should not be done."
*   "Greed is a cause for the origination of actions. Aversion is a cause for the origination of actions. Delusion is a cause for the origination of actions."
*   "When one falls back on what was done in the past as being essential, monks, there is no desire, no effort (at the thought), 'This should be done. This shouldn't be done.' When one can't pin down as a truth or reality what should & shouldn't be done, one dwells bewildered & unprotected. One cannot righteously refer to oneself as a contemplative."
*   "Defiled by passion, the mind is not released. Defiled by ignorance, discernment does not develop. Thus from the fading of passion is there awareness-release. From the fading of ignorance is there discernment-release."
*   "When you know for yourselves that, 'These qualities are unskillful; these qualities are blameworthy; these qualities are criticized by the observant; these qualities, when adopted & carried out, lead to harm & to suffering'—then you should abandon them."

*   "When—by following a life of habit & practice, a life, a holy life that is followed as of essential worth—one's unskillful mental qualities decline while one's skillful mental qualities increase: That sort of habit & practice, life, holy life that is followed as of essential worth is fruitful."
*   "There is kamma that is dark with dark result. There is the case where a certain person fabricates an injurious bodily fabrication, fabricates an injurious verbal fabrication, fabricates an injurious mental fabrication. Having fabricated an injurious bodily fabrication, having fabricated an injurious verbal fabrication, having fabricated an injurious mental fabrication, he rearises in an injurious world. On rearising in an injurious world, there he is touched by injurious contacts. Touched by injurious contacts, he experiences feelings that are exclusively painful, like those of the beings in hell."

In a person of wrong livelihood, wrong effort. In a person of wrong effort, wrong mindfulness. In a person of wrong mindfulness, wrong concentration. In a person of wrong concentration, wrong knowledge. In a person of wrong knowledge, wrong release."

*   "In any doctrine & discipline where the noble eightfold path is not ascertained, no contemplative of the first… second… third… fourth order [stream-winner, once-returner, non-returner, or arahant] is ascertained. But in any doctrine & discipline where the noble eightfold path is ascertained, contemplatives of the first… second… third… fourth order are ascertained."

*   "Ignorance precedes the arrival of unskillful qualities; lack of shame & lack of compunction follow after. Clear knowing precedes the arrival of skillful qualities; shame & compunction follow after."
*   "In whomever the effluents that defile, that lead to renewed becoming, that give trouble, that ripen in stress, and lead to future birth, aging, & death are not abandoned: Him I call deluded. For it is from not abandoning the effluents that one is deluded."
*   "This Dhamma is for one who is modest, not for one who is self-aggrandizing. This Dhamma is for one who is content, not for one who is discontent. This Dhamma is for one who is reclusive, not for one who is entangled. This Dhamma is for one whose persistence is aroused, not for one who is lazy. This Dhamma is for one whose mindfulness is established, not for one whose mindfulness is confused. This Dhamma is for one whose mind is concentrated, not for one whose mind is unconcentrated. This Dhamma is for one endowed with discernment, not for one whose discernment is weak.
