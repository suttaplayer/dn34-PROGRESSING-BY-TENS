Here is an analysis of the statement from the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34) regarding the "Ten skillful courses of action," presented in the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation**

Venerable Sāriputta, in the DASUTTARA SUTTA, presents a comprehensive framework of Dhammas, or teachings, that are organized by numerical categories for the attainment of unbinding and the ending of suffering and stress. Among these teachings are specific qualities that are explicitly identified as being "on the side of distinction". These are vital for individuals striving for welfare, happiness, and liberation in the world.

**Complication**

Without adherence to these skillful courses of action, various forms of misconduct and unskillful qualities arise, leading to negative consequences and a state of decline. The sources indicate that **bodily misconduct, verbal misconduct, and mental misconduct should not be done**, and failing to abstain from them leads to expected drawbacks. When such "unarisen effluent of sensuality... becoming... ignorance" are allowed to arise or increase, they are considered "ideas unfit for attention". Engaging in "unskillful qualities" or "blameworthy" actions that are "criticized by the observant" will lead to "harm & to suffering". For example, if one lies, there is no "evil not to be done". A person who is "unvirtuous" or "envious" or "stingy" and has not abandoned these "impurities" is said to be "placed in hell". The mind can be defiled by "desire-passion" with regard to perceptions or intentions related to sensory experiences. Moreover, those "conquered by evil desires" or "friendship with evil people" are "incurably doomed to deprivation, to hell". Unskillful thinking also "produces blindness, produces lack of vision, produces lack of knowledge, leads to the cessation of discernment, sides with vexation, and is not conducive to Unbinding". Beings are ultimately "owners of their actions, heirs of their actions, born of their actions, related through their actions, and have their actions as their arbitrator," implying that their actions differentiate them in terms of "baseness & excellence".

**Resolution**

The "ten skillful courses of action" are presented as qualities on the side of distinction, encompassing purification of bodily, verbal, and mental conduct:

*   **Skillful Bodily Actions:** These include **refraining from taking life, refraining from taking what is not given (stealing), and refraining from sexual misconduct**.
    *   A person who abandons taking life "dwells with his rod laid down, his knife laid down, scrupulous, merciful, compassionate for the welfare of all living beings".
    *   Abstaining from taking what is not given means not taking "things in a village or a wilderness that belong to others and have not been given by them".
    *   Abstaining from sexual misconduct means not getting sexually involved with those who are "protected by their mothers, their fathers, their brothers, their sisters, their relatives, or their Dhamma; those with husbands, those who entail punishments, or even those crowned with flowers by another man". This is how one is made "pure in three ways by bodily action".

*   **Skillful Verbal Actions:** These include **refraining from telling lies, refraining from divisive speech, refraining from harsh speech, and refraining from idle chatter**.
    *   Refraining from telling lies means speaking "in truth", accurately reporting what is known and seen without conscious falsehood for oneself or others.
    *   Refraining from divisive speech involves not repeating what is heard to break groups apart, but rather "reconciling those who have broken apart or cementing those who are united," and speaking things that "create concord".
    *   Refraining from harsh speech involves speaking "words that are soothing, pleasing to the ear, that are affectionate, that go to the heart, that are polite, appealing & pleasing to people at large".
    *   Refraining from idle chatter means speaking "in season, speaks what is based in fact, what is in accordance with the goal, the Dhamma, & the Vinaya," using words that are "worth treasuring, timely, reasonable, circumscribed, connected with the goal". This is how one is made "pure in four ways by verbal action".

*   **Skillful Mental Actions:** These include **non-greed, non-ill will, and right view**.
    *   **Non-greed** means not coveting "the belongings of others".
    *   **Non-ill will** means not being malevolent. Both non-greed and non-ill will are identified as "roots of what is skillful". Cultivating these leads to a mind "free from hostility, free from ill will, undefiled, & pure".
    *   **Right view** is a foundational quality, described as the "forerunner" of the noble eightfold path. It is a "categorical teaching" and involves discerning "what is unskillful, discerns the root of what is unskillful, discerns what is skillful, and discerns the root of what is skillful". Right view also encompasses understanding the **Four Noble Truths**: stress, its origination, its cessation, and the path to its cessation. These truths are considered "real, not unreal, with no alteration".

These ten skillful courses of action contribute to the increase of skillful mental qualities and the decline of unskillful ones, leading to the "stability, non-confusion, & non-disappearance of the True Dhamma". They are qualities that lead to "disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding". By developing these qualities, one is able to "cut through craving, has ripped off the fetter, and—from rightly breaking through conceit—has put an end to suffering & stress". Such conduct results in "growth, increase, & abundance" in the Dhamma-Vinaya.
