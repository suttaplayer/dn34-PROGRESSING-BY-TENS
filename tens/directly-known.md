In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents a structured framework of Dhammas, which includes "Ten grounds for abolishing" as qualities to be directly known. This particular teaching outlines how right qualities counteract and eliminate their corresponding wrong qualities, leading to spiritual development and the cessation of suffering.

Here is an analysis of this statement using the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation**
The framework begins by identifying a state of "wrongness," encompassing wrong view, resolve, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, concentration, knowledge, and release. These are described as qualities that lead to **failure**, not success. In contrast, there are also "right" qualities corresponding to each of these, representing "rightness".

**Complication**
The presence of these "wrong" or "unskillful" qualities is depicted as a significant impediment to spiritual progress and well-being.
*   **Roots of Unskillfulness**: Unskillful qualities are fundamentally rooted in **greed, aversion, and delusion**. These are seen as internal "stains," "enemies," "foes," "murderers," and "adversaries" that overwhelm the mind.
*   **Manifestations of Misconduct**: These roots manifest as bodily, verbal, and mental misconduct. Examples include taking life, stealing, engaging in sexual misconduct, telling lies, divisive speech, harsh speech, idle chatter, covetousness, and malevolence.
*   **Consequences**: Such actions and views are "blameworthy" and "criticized by the observant". When adopted and carried out, they lead to **harm and suffering**. Furthermore, dwelling in wrong views, particularly those of inaction (such as believing all experiences are caused by past actions, a supreme being, or are uncaused), leaves one "bewildered & unprotected" and unable to righteously refer to oneself as a contemplative. Clinging to these unskillful qualities or views can result in undesirable rebirths, such as in hell, the animal womb, or the realm of hungry ghosts, or in inopportune situations for leading the holy life.
*   **Obstruction to Dhamma**: The presence of such defilements (like having a present kamma obstruction, defilement obstruction, or dull discernment) makes a person incapable of "alighting on the lawfulness, the rightness of skillful qualities" even when listening to the true Dhamma.

**Resolution**
The solution lies in the **development of the "right" qualities**, which systematically abolish their "wrong" counterparts, leading to liberation from suffering.
*   **The Path of Rightness**: The Blessed One teaches that **right view** is the forerunner. In one who cultivates right view, the corresponding wrong view is abolished. Simultaneously, "the many evil, unskillful qualities that come into play with wrong view as their condition are also abolished, while the many skillful qualities that have right view as their condition go to the culmination of their development".
*   **Components of the Path**: This principle extends across the entire **noble eightfold path**, which includes:
    *   **Right View**: Involves discerning what is skillful and unskillful, their roots (lack of greed, aversion, delusion), and the four noble truths (stress, its origination, cessation, and the path). It leads to "verified confidence in the Dhamma".
    *   **Right Resolve, Speech, Action, Livelihood, Effort, Mindfulness, and Concentration**: Each of these "right" factors, when developed, leads to the purging away and abolishment of its respective "wrong" counterpart.
*   **Culmination in Release**: This systematic abandoning of unskillful qualities, rooted in passion, aversion, and delusion, culminates in **right knowledge** and **right release**. This release is characterized as "effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release". It signifies the **total ending of craving, becoming, and effluents**, leading to the "right ending of suffering & stress" and the realization of unbinding (Nibbāna). The individual becomes "unagitated" from lack of clinging or sustenance, with "no seed for the conditions of future birth, aging, death, or stress". This is the ultimate goal, where birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, and the task done, with "nothing further for this world".

By applying these "grounds for abolishing," a practitioner effectively purifies their mind, sheds burdens, and crosses over from the realm of suffering to a state of peace and freedom.
