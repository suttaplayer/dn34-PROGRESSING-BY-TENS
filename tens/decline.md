Here is an analysis of the "Ten unskillful courses of action" presented in the DASUTTARA SUTTA, structured in a Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format, drawing on the provided sources:

**Situation**

The Dhamma outlines qualities and practices that lead to a person's decline, as well as those that lead to stability and distinction. One category of actions identified as leading to decline encompasses "Ten unskillful courses of action". These actions are considered blameworthy and, when adopted and carried out, lead to harm and suffering. Such unskillful qualities arise from fundamental roots within an individual.

**Complication**

The ten unskillful courses of action represent a detailed exposition of behaviors that lead to decline, encompassing bodily, verbal, and mental misconduct.

1.  **Taking life (killing living beings):** This is characterized by the conscious intention to deprive a living being of life. Engaging in this action is considered un-Dhamma conduct.
    *   "Abandoning the taking of life, the contemplative Gotama abstains from the taking of life. He dwells with his rod laid down, his knife laid down, scrupulous, merciful, compassionate for the welfare of all living beings".
    *   "Here a certain person, abandoning the taking of life, abstains from taking life. Laying aside rod & weapon, he is conscientious & kind, dwelling for the welfare of all living beings".
2.  **Taking what is not given (stealing):** This involves taking what belongs to another in a manner of stealing. It is a form of un-Dhamma conduct.
    *   "Abandoning the taking of what is not given, the contemplative Gotama abstains from taking what is not given. He takes only what is given, accepts only what is given, lives not by stealth but by means of a self that has become pure".
3.  **Sexual misconduct:** This refers to engaging in illicit sexual activity, specifically with those protected by others or those who entail punishments.
    *   "He doesn't get sexually involved with those who are protected by their mothers, their fathers, their brothers, their sisters, their relatives, or their Dhamma; those with husbands, those who entail punishments, or even those crowned with flowers by another man".
4.  **Telling lies (false speech):** This involves consciously telling untruths for one's own sake, for another's, or for a reward. The sources indicate that for a person who tells a deliberate lie, there is no evil deed that is not to be done.
    *   "For the person who lies, who transgresses in this one thing, transcending concern for the world beyond: there's no evil not to be done".
    *   "If he doesn't know, he says, 'I know.' If he does know, he says, 'I don't know.' If he hasn't seen, he says, 'I have seen.' If he has seen, he says, 'I haven't seen.' Thus he consciously tells lies for his own sake, for the sake of another, or for the sake of a certain reward".
5.  **Divisive speech:** This involves repeating what one has heard to others in order to break apart united people or stir up strife between those already separated, delighting in factionalism.
    *   "What he has heard here he tells there to break those people apart from these people here. What he has heard there he tells here to break these people apart from those people there. Thus breaking apart those who are united and stirring up strife between those who have broken apart, he loves factionalism, delights in factionalism, enjoys factionalism, speaks things that create factionalism".
6.  **Harsh speech:** This entails speaking words that are insolent, cutting, mean, reviling, and provoking anger, which can destroy concentration.
    *   "He speaks words that are insolent, cutting, mean to others, reviling others, provoking anger and destroying concentration".
7.  **Idle chatter:** This is characterized by speaking out of season, speaking what is unfactual, or irrelevant to the goal, Dhamma, or Vinaya, words not worth treasuring.
    *   "He speaks out of season, speaks what isn't factual, what isn't in accordance with the goal, the Dhamma, & the Vinaya, words that are not worth treasuring".
8.  **Greed:** Greed (or covetousness) is identified as one of the three roots of what is unskillful. It is an "inside stain, inside enemy, inside foe, inside murderer, inside adversary".
    *   "Greed is a cause for the origination of actions".
    *   "Greed, great king, is a quality of the world that, when arising, arises for harm, stress, & discomfort".
    *   A covetous person thinks, "O, that what belongs to others would be mine!".
9.  **Ill will (aversion):** This is another root of unskillful actions, described as an "inside stain". It also leads to harm, stress, and discomfort.
    *   "Aversion is a cause for the origination of actions".
    *   "Aversion is an inside stain, inside enemy, inside foe, inside murderer, inside adversary".
    *   In the context of mental misconduct, ill will is defined as "malicious in mind, thinking, 'May these beings be killed or slaughtered or annihilated or destroyed, or may they not exist!'".
10. **Wrong view (delusion):** Delusion is the third root of unskillful actions. It is described as producing blindness, lack of vision, and lack of knowledge, leading to the cessation of discernment and siding with vexation. Wrong view leads to failure, not success.
    *   "Delusion is a cause for the origination of actions".
    *   "Delusion carries great blame and is slow to fade".
    *   "From wrongness comes failure, not success. And how is it, monks, that from wrongness comes failure, not success? In a person of wrong view, wrong resolve comes into being… In a person of wrong knowledge, wrong release".

**Resolution**

To move away from these unskillful courses of action and towards a path of distinction, one must undertake and maintain their opposites, which are skillful qualities. This involves abandoning the three roots of unskillfulness: greed, aversion, and delusion, and instead cultivating their opposites: lack of greed, lack of aversion, and lack of delusion.

The path leading to the ending of kamma that is neither dark nor bright with neither dark nor bright result is the noble eightfold path: right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration. Adherence to this path promotes the stability, non-confusion, and non-disappearance of the True Dhamma.

*   "Refraining from taking life, refraining from taking what is not given, refraining from sexual misconduct, refraining from telling lies, refraining from divisive speech, refraining from harsh speech, refraining from idle chatter, non-greed, non-ill will, right view. These ten dhammas are on the side of distinction".
*   "When you know for yourselves that, 'These qualities are skillful; these qualities are blameless; these qualities are praised by the observant; these qualities, when adopted & carried out, lead to welfare & to happiness'—then you should enter & remain in them".
*   "The mind nurtured with discernment is rightly released from the effluents, i.e., the effluent of sensuality, the effluent of becoming, the effluent of ignorance".
