Thus, the quote regarding securing both kinds of benefits through heedfulness is entirely consistent with the broader teaching that **heedfulness is a necessary, active quality for practitioners at all stages of the path leading to unbinding, until full arahantship is achieved.**

**2. Non-Returners and "Further Becoming"**

You cite: **"'Monks, just as even a tiny amount of feces is foul-smelling, in the same way, I don't praise even a tiny amount of becoming—even as much as a finger-snap.'"** [AN_nblm.txt 1]. You then ask if beings in the Pure Abodes (non-returners) are creating further becoming.

The sources consistently describe a non-returner as one who, **"with the ending of the five lower fetters, he is due to spontaneously reappear (in the Pure Abodes), there to be totally unbound, destined never again to return from that world."** [AN_nblm.txt 18, 101, 103; DN_nblm.txt 162; MN_nblm.txt 328, 348, 349, 350, 433].

Crucially, another passage clarifies the nature of a non-returner: **"Untied from the yoke of sensuality but tied by the yoke of becoming, one is a non-returner, one who doesn't return to this state."** [KN_Iti_nblm.txt 253]. This statement explicitly confirms that a non-returner is *still tied by the yoke of becoming*, even though they have transcended sensual desires and will not return to *this world*. Their rebirth in the Pure Abodes is indeed a form of "becoming," albeit a highly refined and final one.

Therefore, according to the Buddha's teaching, even this subtle "becoming" in the Pure Abodes would still be considered "foul-smelling" in the sense that it is a condition of existence that still needs to be brought to an end to achieve complete unbinding. The non-returner's "task" is precisely to eliminate this remaining "craving for becoming" [KN_Iti_nblm.txt 230, 233] to realize total unbinding within those abodes.

**3. The Buddha's Last Exhortation to "Reach Consummation Through Heedfulness"**

The Buddha's final words were: **"Monks, I exhort you: All fabrications are subject to ending & decay. Reach consummation through heedfulness."** [DN_nblm.txt 173, 182]. You rightly note that this was addressed to a group of noble monks, the most backward of whom was a stream-enterer, implying the presence of more advanced practitioners, including non-returners [DN_nblm.txt 182].

This final exhortation directly supports the view that **heedfulness is an active and continuous mental state required until the very culmination of the path, full arahantship.**

*   For stream-enterers, once-returners, and non-returners, heedfulness is the **means** by which they continue to practice and attain the "as-yet-unattained, the reaching of the as-yet-unreached, the realization of the as-yet-unrealized" [AN_nblm.txt 48, 49, 50, 51, 52].
*   The goal for these practitioners is "consummation," which implies achieving the final stage of the holy life, the complete ending of effluents, also known as the "effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release" [AN_nblm.txt 28, 29, 45, 110, 111, 113, 114, 118, 119; DN_nblm.txt 149, 163, 168, 170, 175, 176; MN_nblm.txt 309, 311, 334, 335].
*   The fact that non-returners are still "tied by the yoke of becoming" [KN_Iti_nblm.txt 253] means they still have a task to complete. This task requires active heedfulness.
*   Only Arahants "have done their task with heedfulness. They are incapable of being heedless" [MN_nblm.txt 369, SN_nblm.txt 510]. This implies that for *everyone else*, including non-returners, the capacity for heedlessness remains, and thus the need for active heedfulness is present.

In summary, the sources indicate that heedfulness is not a state that automatically arises upon the *completion* of abandoning unskillful qualities, but rather the **driving force and active quality that enables that abandonment throughout the entire spiritual journey, culminating in full unbinding.** For non-returners, even in the Pure Abodes, this active engagement with heedfulness is necessary to transcend the final vestiges of becoming and reach the ultimate goal.
