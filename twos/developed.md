Tranquility (samatha) and insight (vipassanā) are two essential qualities in the Buddhist path, both contributing to clear knowing and release [^AN_nblm_3].

## Definition

**Tranquility (samatha)** is described as a quality that, when developed, leads to the development of the mind, which in turn results in the abandonment of passion [^AN_nblm_3]. It is associated with states of deep concentration (jhāna), characterized by rapture, pleasure, unification of awareness, equanimity, and internal assurance, achieved through seclusion from sensuality and unskillful qualities [^AN_nblm_29], [^AN_nblm_41], [^AN_nblm_51], [^AN_nblm_52], [^AN_nblm_77], [^AN_nblm_84], [^AN_nblm_86], [^AN_nblm_132], [^AN_nblm_170], [^AN_nblm_184], [^AN_nblm_206], [^AN_nblm_362], [^AN_nblm_378], [^AN_nblm_392], [^AN_nblm_409], [^AN_nblm_423], [^AN_nblm_427].

**Insight (vipassanā)** is a quality that, when developed, leads to the development of discernment (paññā), which in turn results in the abandonment of ignorance [^AN_nblm_3]. It involves seeing and investigating phenomena as they truly are, particularly in terms of their arising, passing away, allure, drawbacks, and escape.

## Benefits

Developing tranquility and insight offers significant benefits:

*   **For Tranquility:**
    *   It serves to **develop the mind** and helps **abandon passion** [^AN_nblm_3].
    *   It leads to a **pleasant abiding in the here and now**, the **attainment of knowledge and vision**, and the development of **mindfulness and alertness** , [^AN_nblm_41].
    *   A mind developed through tranquility is described as **pliant, malleable, luminous, and not brittle**, making it rightly concentrated for the ending of effluents [^AN_nblm_37], [^AN_nblm_67].
    *   It leads to **blissful concentration** in the present and future, is noble, peaceful, exquisite, and results in the **acquiring of calm** and the **attainment of unification** [^AN_nblm_75], [^AN_nblm_76], [^AN_nblm_271].
*   **For Insight:**
    *   It serves to **develop discernment** and helps **abandon ignorance** [^AN_nblm_3].
    *   It contributes to **clear knowing (vijjā)** and **release (vimutti)** [^AN_nblm_3].
    *   By correctly contemplating dualities such as stress, its origination, cessation, and the path to its cessation, one can expect **gnosis right here and now, or non-return**.
*   **For Both (Tranquility & Insight in tandem):**
    *   These two qualities "have a share in clear knowing" [^AN_nblm_3].
    *   When developed in tandem, "the path is born" leading to the **abandonment of fetters** and the **destruction of obsessions** .
    *   Right view, when supported by tranquility and insight, leads to **awareness-release** and **discernment-release** [^AN_nblm_68], [^AN_nblm_69].
    *   They are considered of "great help in the attainment of the cessation of perception & feeling".
    *   They constitute a path leading to the **unfabricated** (the ending of passion, aversion, and delusion).

## Causation and Conditions

Tranquility and insight arise from and contribute to various conditions:

*   **Conditions for Tranquility:**
    *   Developing **immeasurable concentration** (based on goodwill, compassion, empathetic joy, or equanimity) leads to tranquility, calmness, unification, and luminosity of mind [^AN_nblm_74], [^AN_nblm_75], [^AN_nblm_76], [^AN_nblm_150], [^AN_nblm_151].
    *   **Seclusion from sensuality and unskillful qualities** is a prerequisite for entering the jhānas, which are states of deep concentration and tranquility [^AN_nblm_29], [^AN_nblm_41], [^AN_nblm_51], [^AN_nblm_52], [^AN_nblm_77], [^AN_nblm_84], [^AN_nblm_86], [^AN_nblm_132], [^AN_nblm_170], [^AN_nblm_184], [^AN_nblm_206], [^AN_nblm_362], [^AN_nblm_378], [^AN_nblm_392], [^AN_nblm_409], [^AN_nblm_423], [^AN_nblm_427].
    *   Recollection of the Tathāgata, Dhamma, Saṅgha, one's own virtues, or devas can cleanse the defiled mind, bringing **calm and joy**, which fosters concentration [^AN_nblm_24], [^AN_nblm_25], [^AN_nblm_26], [^AN_nblm_27], [^AN_nblm_28], [^AN_nblm_190], [^AN_nblm_191], [^AN_nblm_192], [^AN_nblm_193].
*   **Conditions for Insight:**
    *   **Discernment of the four noble truths** (stress, its origination, cessation, and the path) is fundamental for insight [^AN_nblm_36], [^AN_nblm_209], [^AN_nblm_213], [^AN_nblm_214], [^AN_nblm_215], [^AN_nblm_354], [^AN_nblm_369], [^AN_nblm_412], [^AN_nblm_506], [^AN_nblm_507], [^AN_nblm_508], [^AN_nblm_509].
    *   **Appropriate attention** and **the voice of another** are conditions for the arising of right view, which then can be assisted by tranquility and insight [^MN_nblm_372].
    *   **Investigating phenomena** such as form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness as inconstant, stressful, and not-self contributes to insight [^AN_nblm_169], [^AN_nblm_396], [^AN_nblm_517].
    *   Scrutinizing the drawbacks of unskillful thoughts can lead to a steadied and concentrated mind, aiding insight [^MN_nblm_342].
*   **Conditions for Both:**
    *   **Developing the seven factors for awakening** (mindfulness, analysis of qualities, persistence, rapture, calm, concentration, equanimity) dependent on seclusion, dispassion, and cessation, can lead to both calm and insight, culminating in direct knowledge and unbinding [^MN_nblm_322], [^MN_nblm_436], [^SN_nblm_553], [^SN_nblm_554].
    *   **Having admirable friends, companions, and colleagues** is the first quality that brings awareness-release (supported by both) to maturity[^MN_nblm_155].
    *   **Listening to, teaching, reciting, and mentally examining the Dhamma** can cultivate sensitivity to meaning and Dhamma, giving rise to joy, rapture, calm, and concentrated mind, leading to release [^AN_nblm_70], [^AN_nblm_71], [^AN_nblm_72], [^AN_nblm_73], [^AN_nblm_251], [^AN_nblm_252], [^AN_nblm_253], [^AN_nblm_254], [^AN_nblm_255], [^AN_nblm_271], [^AN_nblm_272], [^AN_nblm_273], [^AN_nblm_274], [^AN_nblm_275].

## How To Develop

The sources outline various methods for developing tranquility and insight:

*   **General Practices:**
    *   **Relentless exertion, firmness, persistence, and striving** are emphasized as fundamental training .
    *   Cultivating **peacefulness in faculties and hearts** leads to peaceful bodily, verbal, and mental actions .
    *   **Abandoning qualities of a fool** (e.g., inappropriate questioning/answering, disapproving of appropriate answers) and **undertaking qualities of a wise person** , .
    *   Cultivating **lack of greed, aversion, and delusion** is a root of skillful qualities , .
    *   Engaging in the **three trainings**: heightened virtue, heightened mind, and heightened discernment [^AN_nblm_31], [^AN_nblm_32], [^AN_nblm_33], [^AN_nblm_34], [^AN_nblm_35], [^AN_nblm_32], [^DN_nblm_242]. This process helps abandon passion, aversion, and delusion [^AN_nblm_31].
    *   **Abandoning the five hindrances** (sensual desire, ill will, sloth & drowsiness, restlessness & anxiety, uncertainty) is crucial, as they overwhelm awareness and weaken discernment [^AN_nblm_67], [^AN_nblm_80], [^AN_nblm_84], [^AN_nblm_86], [^AN_nblm_170], [^AN_nblm_173].
*   **Developing Tranquility (Samatha):**
    *   Practice of the **four jhānas** is a core method for achieving heightened mind and pleasant abiding [^AN_nblm_29], [^AN_nblm_41], [^AN_nblm_51], [^AN_nblm_52], [^AN_nblm_77], [^AN_nblm_84], [^AN_nblm_86], [^AN_nblm_132], [^AN_nblm_170], [^AN_nblm_184], [^AN_nblm_206], [^AN_nblm_362], [^AN_nblm_378], [^AN_nblm_392], [^AN_nblm_409], [^AN_nblm_423], [^AN_nblm_427], . This involves progressing from seclusion, rapture, pleasure, to unification of awareness, equanimity, and pure mindfulness [^AN_nblm_29].
    *   Developing **immeasurable concentration** through goodwill, compassion, empathetic joy, and equanimity, which makes the mind pliant, malleable, luminous, and rightly concentrated [^AN_nblm_74], [^AN_nblm_75], [^AN_nblm_76], [^AN_nblm_150], [^AN_nblm_151], [^MN_nblm_379], [^MN_nblm_380].
    *   Attending periodically to the **themes of concentration, uplifted energy, and equanimity** to ensure the mind is pliant, malleable, luminous, and not brittle, leading to the ending of effluents [^AN_nblm_37], .
*   **Developing Insight (Vipassanā):**
    *   **Discerning the Four Noble Truths** (stress, its origination, cessation, and the path to cessation) as they have come to be [^AN_nblm_36], [^AN_nblm_209], [^AN_nblm_213], [^AN_nblm_214], [^AN_nblm_215], [^AN_nblm_354], [^AN_nblm_369], [^AN_nblm_412], [^AN_nblm_506], [^AN_nblm_507], [^AN_nblm_508], [^AN_nblm_509].
    *   **Investigating and seeing fabrications** (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness) as inconstant, stressful, a disease, an arrow, painful, an affliction, alien, a disintegration, an emptiness, and not-self [^AN_nblm_169], [^AN_nblm_396], [^AN_nblm_517]. This turns the mind towards deathlessness [^AN_nblm_169], [^AN_nblm_396].
    *   Focusing on the **origination and passing away of the five aggregates** (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness) is a key aspect of discernment.
    *   Developing **mindfulness of in-and-out breathing** by being sensitive to the entire body, calming bodily fabrication, and focusing on inconstancy, dispassion, cessation, and relinquishment [^MN_nblm_394], [^MN_nblm_395], [^MN_nblm_433], [^MN_nblm_434].
    *   Cultivating specific **perceptions conducive to penetration**, such as unattractiveness of the body, death, loathsomeness in food, distaste for every world, inconstancy, stress in inconstancy, not-self in stress, abandoning, and dispassion.

## Areas Of Application

Tranquility and insight are applied throughout the spiritual training and in daily life to overcome obstacles and achieve the ultimate goal:

*   **In the Holy Life:** Both qualities are fundamental to living the holy life, with tranquility supporting the mind and insight supporting discernment [^AN_nblm_3], [^AN_nblm_290]. They are integral to the **three trainings** (heightened virtue, heightened mind, and heightened discernment) [^AN_nblm_31], [^AN_nblm_32], [^AN_nblm_33], [^AN_nblm_34], [^AN_nblm_35], [^DN_nblm_242]. The holy life is lived "with training as a reward, with discernment as its surpassing state, with release as its heartwood, and with mindfulness as its governing principle" [^AN_nblm_59], [^AN_nblm_60], [^AN_nblm_61], [^AN_nblm_62].
*   **Overcoming Defilements:** They are crucial for abandoning passion, aversion, and delusion [^AN_nblm_31], [^AN_nblm_178], [^AN_nblm_264], . The abandonment of the **five hindrances** is a prerequisite for entering meditative states where tranquility and insight flourish [^AN_nblm_67], [^AN_nblm_80], [^AN_nblm_84], [^AN_nblm_86], [^AN_nblm_170], [^AN_nblm_173], [^AN_nblm_184], [^AN_nblm_209], [^AN_nblm_353], [^AN_nblm_362], [^AN_nblm_378], [^AN_nblm_392], [^AN_nblm_423], [^AN_nblm_427], [^AN_nblm_445], [^AN_nblm_558]. Guarding the **six sense faculties** is also a key area of application to prevent unskillful qualities from assailing the mind , [^MN_nblm_357], [^MN_nblm_366], [^MN_nblm_382], [^MN_nblm_383], [^MN_nblm_426], [^SN_nblm_535].
*   **Meditative Development:** The **four jhānas** are explicitly states of heightened concentration (tranquility) [^AN_nblm_29], [^AN_nblm_41], [^AN_nblm_51], [^AN_nblm_52], [^AN_nblm_77], [^AN_nblm_84], [^AN_nblm_86], [^AN_nblm_132], [^AN_nblm_170], [^AN_nblm_184], [^AN_nblm_206], [^AN_nblm_362], [^AN_nblm_378], [^AN_nblm_392], [^AN_nblm_409], [^AN_nblm_423], [^AN_nblm_427], . **Mindfulness of breathing** (ānāpānasati) is a primary practice that develops concentration and subsequently leads to clear knowing and release [^MN_nblm_394], [^MN_nblm_395], [^MN_nblm_433], [^MN_nblm_434]. The **four divine abodes** (goodwill, compassion, empathetic joy, equanimity) are also cultivated to develop immeasurable concentration [^MN_nblm_379], [^MN_nblm_380].
*   **Direct Knowledge and Release:** Ultimately, the development of tranquility and insight leads to **awareness-release** and **discernment-release**, which signify the ending of effluents (sensuality, becoming, ignorance) and the complete cessation of suffering and stress in the here and now [^AN_nblm_4], [^AN_nblm_10], [^AN_nblm_37], [^AN_nblm_68], [^AN_nblm_69], [^AN_nblm_85], [^AN_nblm_87], [^AN_nblm_142], [^DN_nblm_209], [^DN_nblm_213], [^DN_nblm_214], [^DN_nblm_215], [^DN_nblm_233], [^DN_nblm_234], [^DN_nblm_237], [^DN_nblm_238], [^DN_nblm_264], [^DN_nblm_278], [^MN_nblm_354], [^MN_nblm_369], [^MN_nblm_372], [^MN_nblm_429], [^MN_nblm_436], [^MN_nblm_441], [^MN_nblm_447], [^SN_nblm_502], [^SN_nblm_504], [^SN_nblm_511], [^SN_nblm_554]. These states are seen as the culmination of the holy life [^MN_nblm_429].
