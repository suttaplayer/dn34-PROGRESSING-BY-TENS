In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents a structured framework of Dhamma qualities that progress by tens, including qualities to be developed. Among these, **tranquility (samatha) and insight (vipassanā)** are highlighted as two dhammas that should be developed. This statement, though brief, points to a profound aspect of the Buddhist path to liberation.

**Situation: The Qualities and Their Purpose**

Tranquility and insight are recognized as two qualities that **"have a share in clear knowing"**. Their development serves distinct yet complementary purposes essential for spiritual progress. When **tranquility is developed, it leads to the development of the mind, which in turn results in the abandonment of passion**. Similarly, when **insight is developed, it fosters the development of discernment, leading to the abandonment of ignorance**. Essentially, these practices are foundational for a "heightened mind" and ultimately for the **"culmination of clear knowing & release"**. The Dhamma itself is described as leading to "disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding", which is precisely what these two qualities facilitate.

**Complication: The Hindrances to Release**

The necessity of developing tranquility and insight arises from the pervasive **defilements of passion and ignorance** that obstruct liberation. The mind, when "defiled by passion, is not released", and when "defiled by ignorance, discernment does not develop". These defilements are rooted in "unskillful qualities", specifically the "three roots of what is unskillful: greed, aversion, & delusion". Delusion, in particular, "carries great blame and is slow to fade," while passion is "slow to fade," and aversion "quick to fade". Without addressing these underlying issues, true release cannot be achieved. The Buddha also emphasized that "Ignorance precedes the arrival of unskillful qualities; lack of shame & lack of compunction follow after", further illustrating the foundational role of ignorance as a complication.

**Resolution: The Path to Unbinding and Freedom**

The development of tranquility and insight provides the means to overcome these complications and achieve profound release. With the **"fading of passion is there awareness-release," and "from the fading of ignorance is there discernment-release"**. This leads to the ultimate goal of "unbinding" (nibbāna).

The development of **tranquility (samatha)** involves practices that bring the mind to a state of calm and stillness. A monk "intent on heightened mind should attend periodically to three themes: He should attend periodically to the theme of concentration; he should attend periodically to the theme of uplifted energy; he should attend periodically to the theme of equanimity". This balanced approach makes the mind "pliant, malleable, luminous, & not brittle," and "rightly concentrated for the ending of the effluents". Furthermore, **concentration, when "nurtured with virtue, is of great fruit, great reward"**, highlighting the interconnectedness of ethical conduct and mental development. The ultimate state of concentration is described as one where the mind is "neither pressed down nor forced back, nor kept in place by the fabrications of forceful restraint—still as a result of release, contented as a result of standing still, and as a result of contentment one is not agitated," which is "the fruit of gnosis [arahantship]".

The development of **insight (vipassanā)**, often arising from a concentrated mind, leads to **discernment (paññā)**. A "concentrated monk discerns in line with what has come into being," specifically discerning "stress," its "origination," "cessation," and the "path of practice leading to the cessation of stress". This process culminates in the profound realization that **"Whatever is subject to origination is all subject to cessation"**, leading to "disenchantment" and "dispassion". Discernment is described as the "surpassing state" for the "right ending of suffering & stress". When one "knows & sees what has come to be" through concentration, they become "disenchanted" and "dispassionate," leading to **release**.

Ultimately, the combined development of tranquility and insight results in a monk whose "mind is rightly released from the effluents, i e, the effluent of sensuality, the effluent of becoming, the effluent of ignorance". The goal is to **"enter & remain in the effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release, having directly known & realized them for himself right in the here & now"**. This path allows for the "abandoning of craving," leading to the "ending of action" and the "ending of stress", bringing one to a state where there is "no more questioning" and fearlessness. The Buddha's final exhortation encapsulates this entire journey: **"All fabrications are subject to ending & decay. Reach consummation through heedfulness"**. Those who follow this path attain gnosis and liberation, putting an end to suffering and stress.
