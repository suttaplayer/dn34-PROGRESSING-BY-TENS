In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta presents a comprehensive framework of Dhamma qualities that are to be abandoned, among which he specifies **ignorance (avijjā)** and **craving for becoming (bhava-taṇhā)**. An analysis of the detailed meaning of this statement reveals the problematic situation created by these qualities, the complications they entail, and the resolution achieved through their abandonment.

**Situation:**
The initial state is one obscured by **ignorance**, which precedes the arising of all unskillful qualities and leads beings towards unfavorable destinations. This ignorance acts as a fundamental obstruction for individuals, preventing them from discerning what should or should not be done, thereby leaving them bewildered and unprotected. The Dhamma states that **discernment does not develop when the mind is defiled by ignorance**. This "ignorance" refers to not discerning, as they have come to be, the origination, disappearance, allure, drawbacks, and escape from phenomena like form. It is a profound delusion that constitutes an effluent. This deep-seated ignorance is why beings continue to wander through birth and death for prolonged periods, experiencing suffering.

**Complication:**
The presence of **ignorance** and **craving for becoming** creates a complicated entanglement in the cycle of suffering. Ignorance acts as a requisite condition for the origination of fabrications. It is not without its own conditions, and its "food" is identified as the five hindrances: sensual desire, ill will, sloth and drowsiness, restlessness and anxiety, and uncertainty. When craving is a person's companion, they **wander on for a long, long time**, unable to transcend this continuous wandering. Craving for becoming is listed among the three main types of craving, alongside craving for sensuality and craving for non-becoming, all of which are to be abandoned. The process is described as: focusing on the allure of clingable phenomena leads to the development of craving; craving then leads to clinging; clinging leads to becoming; and becoming leads to birth, followed by aging-and-death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, and despair. Without the abandonment of both ignorance and craving, a person remains unfree, heading towards new bodies and continuously experiencing stress and suffering.

**Resolution:**
The resolution lies in the **abandonment of both ignorance and craving for becoming**. This abandonment directly leads to the development of **clear knowing (vijjā)** and the ultimate cessation of suffering. When a monk focuses on the inconstancy of all fabrications, ignorance is abandoned, and clear knowing arises. The Dhamma is taught precisely for the purpose of abandoning delusion (ignorance), which, when rooted out, ensures that one is not destined for future arising. Abandoning craving is paramount because it is the very root of suffering and the pathway to unbinding. By being free from the craving for becoming and non-becoming, monks achieve a state of **no further becoming**. The fully awakened one has destroyed the root of craving, thereby putting an end to suffering and stress. This process involves comprehending all phenomena as **unworthy of attachment**, which in turn leads to a state of being unagitated due to a lack of clinging or sustenance, thereby eliminating the conditions for future suffering. Understanding the cosmos not as an external destination but as an internal experience, perceived and conceived through the senses and intellect, highlights that the "end of the cosmos" (and thus stress) is realized through internal non-attachment, not external travel. This is the path to liberation, peace, and the breaking open of ignorance.
