In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents a comprehensive framework of Dhammas, including the realization of "Clear knowing & release". This statement, though brief, carries a profound meaning that can be analyzed through the Situation, Complication, and Resolution (SCR) framework, drawing on various sources that elaborate on these core concepts.

### Situation

The fundamental situation that necessitates the realization of clear knowing and release is the pervasive experience of **suffering and entanglement in the world**. Beings are described as being "beset by birth, aging, & death; by sorrows, lamentations, pains, distresses, & despairs; beset by stress, overcome with stress". This "entire mass of suffering & stress" arises from an "inconceivable beginning" and involves beings "hindered by ignorance and fettered by craving... transmigrating & wandering on". The inherent nature of existence is characterized by stress, as "All fabrications are stressful," and by the absence of a permanent self, as "All phenomena are not-self". Understanding these realities is crucial, as they highlight the need for a path to liberation.

### Complication

The primary complications that prevent beings from achieving clear knowing and release are **defilements, clinging, and various wrong views** that cloud discernment. The sources identify "greed," "aversion," and "delusion" as the "three roots of what is unskillful", which "produce blindness, produce lack of vision, produce lack of knowledge, lead to the cessation of discernment, side with vexation, and are not conducive to Unbinding".

Furthermore, clinging and attachment to mental constructs perpetuate suffering: "Whatever one intends, what one arranges, and what one obsesses about: This is a support for the stationing of consciousness. There being a support, there is a landing of consciousness. When that consciousness lands and grows, there is the production of renewed becoming in the future". This cycle leads directly to future suffering.

Wrong views and conceit are also significant barriers. An "evil viewpoint" can lead to "long-term harm & suffering". The presence of "I-making or my-making conceit-obsession" actively prevents one from entering the states of awareness-release and discernment-release. Additionally, assemblies "trained in bombast and not in cross-questioning" may fail to truly grasp or master the deeper Dhamma, prioritizing "literary works" over "discourses of the Tathāgata—deep, deep in their meaning, transcendent, connected with emptiness".

### Resolution

The resolution to this situation and its complications lies in a comprehensive path of practice that culminates in **clear knowing (Vijjā) and release (Vimutti)**.

1.  **Nature of Clear Knowing and Release**:
    *   **Clear knowing and release** are explicitly stated as "two dhammas" that "should be realized".
    *   **Awareness-release** occurs from the "fading of passion," while **discernment-release** arises from the "fading of ignorance".
    *   When **tranquility** is developed, the mind is developed, leading to the **abandonment of passion**. When **insight** is developed, discernment is developed, leading to the **abandonment of ignorance**. This highlights how tranquility and insight are integral to achieving clear knowing and, consequently, release.
    *   Ultimately, the Dhamma leads to "utter disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding".

2.  **The Path and Its Components**:
    *   **Good conduct**: "Good bodily conduct, good verbal conduct, & good mental conduct should be done". This practice brings rewards such as self-praise, praise from observant people, a good reputation, a peaceful death, and a good destination after death. More deeply, "acting in line with the Dhamma" prevents one from falling back.
    *   **The Noble Eightfold Path**: This is the "middle way realized by the Tathāgata—producing vision, producing knowledge—leads to stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding". It encompasses **right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration**.
    *   **Four Noble Truths**: These truths—stress, the origination of stress, the cessation of stress, and the path to the cessation of stress—are "real, not unreal, with no alteration". Their comprehension is fundamental for discernment.
    *   **Four Establishings of Mindfulness**: This is described as the "direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding". It involves ardently focusing on the body, feelings, mind, and mental qualities to subdue greed and distress.
    *   **Seven Factors for Awakening**: These factors are the "food" for clear knowing and release. They include **mindfulness, analysis of qualities, persistence, rapture, calm, concentration, and equanimity**.

3.  **The Process and Results of Realization**:
    *   The path involves the **abandonment of defilements and clinging**. "Any ending of passion, ending of aversion, ending of delusion: This is called comprehension", leading to a state where one is "free from hunger, totally unbound".
    *   Through this practice, a monk "directly knows every dhamma" by hearing "All dhammas are unworthy of attachment". This leads to comprehending every dhamma, focusing on "inconstancy, dispassion, cessation, relinquishing," and becoming "unsustained by anything in the world." The result is that one "isn't agitated," "totally unbinds right within," and "discerns: 'Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world'". This is the attainment of clear knowing and release.
    *   Upon release, the mind is freed "from the effluent of sensuality, the effluent of becoming, the effluent of ignorance. With release, there is the knowledge, 'Released'".
    *   The effects of this release include being **unagitated**, **at peace**, and having "crossed over beyond entanglement in the world". Such individuals are "incapable of falling away" from their attainment.
    *   The **Tathāgata (Buddha)** serves as the ultimate exemplar, as he "directly knows unbinding as unbinding" and "does not grasp at it," experiencing "unbinding [nibbuti] right within". He teaches the Dhamma "through direct knowledge", which leads to the "right ending of suffering & stress".

These interconnected principles and practices demonstrate the comprehensive nature of clear knowing and release as the ultimate goal within the Dhamma framework.
