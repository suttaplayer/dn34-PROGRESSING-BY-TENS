In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta presents a framework of Dhammas categorized by numerical groupings for their comprehension and practice, including the instruction that "Name & form" are two dhammas that should be comprehended.

Here is a detailed analysis of this statement:

**Situation**

"Name & form" (**nāma-rūpa**) are foundational concepts within the Dhamma, representing the physical and mental constituents of existence. "Form" refers to the **physical body** and its associated sense media, while "name" encompasses **mental aspects** such as feeling, perception, fabrications (mental formations), and consciousness. These two are deeply interconnected; from consciousness as a requisite condition comes name-&-form, and conversely, from name-&-form as a requisite condition comes consciousness, highlighting their mutual dependency in the cycle of existence. Understanding these properties, including earth, liquid, fire, wind, space, and consciousness, is fundamental to comprehending reality as taught by the Blessed One.

**Complication**

The non-comprehension of "name & form" is a significant complication that perpetuates suffering and the cycle of rebirth. The Dhamma explains that **ignorance** is a requisite condition for fabrications, which then leads to consciousness, and from consciousness comes name-&-form, a causal chain that ultimately brings about aging-&-death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, and despair. An uninstructed person, immersed in ignorance, **does not discern** form as being subject to origination, passing away, or both. Such lack of discernment leaves individuals "thoroughly confused" and "asleep," meaning they haven't awakened to these fundamental truths. Furthermore, holding **wrong views** about the self—such as believing the "soul is the same as the body" or "the soul is one thing and the body another"—prevents one from leading the holy life. The Tathāgata teaches a middle way that avoids these extremes, emphasizing the principle of dependent co-arising instead. Grasping at such fixed views (view-dependencies concerning beginning-points and endpoints) can lead to detrimental outcomes in the world beyond.

**Resolution**

Comprehending "name & form" is crucial for spiritual progress and the cessation of suffering. When a disciple of the noble ones discerns **name-&-form**, its origination, cessation, and the path leading to its cessation, they attain **right view**. This understanding leads to **disenchantment, dispassion, and cessation** with regard to what has come into being, resulting in release. The Blessed One emphasizes that once name-&-form is comprehended, "there is **nothing further for a disciple of the noble ones to do**", indicating the completion of the path to liberation. This profound realization, leading to the **ending of suffering & stress**, is attained by cutting through craving and conceit, including "I-making" and "mine-making" conceit-obsession concerning the conscious body and external themes. This Dhamma is presented as "visible here & now, timeless, inviting verification, pertinent, to be experienced by the observant for themselves," making it accessible for direct realization. The attainment of this **unbinding (Nibbāna)** represents the total cessation of future becoming.
