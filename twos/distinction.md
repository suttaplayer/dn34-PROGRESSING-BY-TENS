In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta outlines various classifications of Dhamma that are conducive to spiritual progress and the attainment of unbinding. Among these, **"being easy to instruct" and "admirable friendship"** are highlighted as two Dhammas that are **"on the side of distinction"**, signifying their importance in leading practitioners towards liberation.

**Situation:**
Individuals undertaking the holy life and seeking to practice the Dhamma aim to abandon unskillful mental qualities and cultivate skillful ones for their well-being and happiness. To achieve this, certain conducive conditions and relationships are essential to support their journey.

**Complication:**
Conversely, there are obstacles that can impede this progress. Being **"hard to instruct"** is explicitly stated as a quality **"on the side of decline"**, as it indicates a lack of receptiveness to teachings. Such an individual may find it hard to grasp what they have learned or to learn with deference. Similarly, engaging in **"evil friendship"** is also listed as being **"on the side of decline"**, as monks who are "deceitful, stubborn, talkers, frauds, arrogant, & uncentered" are not considered true followers and will not achieve growth in the Dhamma-Vinaya. If a disciple lacks conviction, does not lend ear, or fails to apply their mind to gnosis, they are described as having "lost the way" and "strayed from this Dhamma & Discipline".

**Resolution:**
The cultivation of **"being easy to instruct"** and **"admirable friendship"** are key to distinction and genuine progress in the Dhamma:

*   **Being Easy to Instruct:**
    A person who is **easy to instruct** possesses a mind that is **ready, malleable, unhindered, elevated, and clear**, enabling them to profoundly understand the Dhamma, including the noble truths of stress, its origination, cessation, and the path to its cessation. Such a disciple demonstrates **conviction in the Teacher's message** and lives to penetrate it, understanding that the Teacher is the source of knowledge. This receptivity is a vital quality, particularly for those who are older, indicating their capacity to grasp and deferentially learn the teachings. Furthermore, monks who are "pliant" and "well-centered" are identified as true followers of the Dhamma-Vinaya, indicating an openness to instruction that leads to "growth, increase, & abundance".

*   **Admirable Friendship:**
    **Admirable friendship** involves associating with **people of integrity** who embody characteristics beneficial to spiritual growth. These friends are those who:
    *   **Give what is hard to give, do what is hard to do, and endure what is hard to endure**.
    *   **Reveal their secrets** to you and **keep your secrets**.
    *   **Do not abandon you** in misfortunes and **do not look down on you** when you are struggling.
    *   Are **reverential, respectful**, and act in accordance with sound advice.

    The sources explicitly recommend associating with admirable friends, stating that one "should associate with admirable friends" and "the best". This association is not merely social; it is fundamental to spiritual development. A monk who is a "friend to admirable people," respectful, and mindful will **"attain step by step the ending of all fetters"**. In fact, **association with people of integrity** is highlighted as one of the **"four stream-entry factors"**, which signifies the initial profound realization of the Dhamma, leading to verifiable confidence in the Awakened One, the Dhamma, and the Saṅgha. An admirable friend helps one undertake the complete training and maintain obedience to teachers, which is fitting for a contemplative. Ultimately, both **"being easy to instruct" and "admirable friendship"** are crucial for the **"stability, non-confusion, & non-disappearance of the True Dhamma"** and are instrumental in leading to **unbinding, the cessation of suffering and stress, and release from all ties**.
