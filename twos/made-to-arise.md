In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta presents a comprehensive framework of Dhammas, categorized by number, that are crucial for spiritual progress. Among these, he highlights two specific knowledges that "should be made to arise": **knowledge of the ending (of the effluents) & knowledge of (their) non-recurrence**. This statement encapsulates a core teaching on achieving liberation from suffering.

Here is an analysis of its detailed meaning:

**Situation**
The fundamental situation addressed by these two knowledges is the ongoing experience of **suffering and stress** within the cycle of birth, aging, and death. Beings are caught in this "wandering-on" (samsara) due to **ignorance** and being "fettered by craving". This ignorance and craving manifest as "effluents" (āsava) – specifically, the **effluent of sensuality, the effluent of becoming, and the effluent of ignorance**. These defilements cloud the mind and prevent true release.

**Complication**
The primary complication is that as long as these defilements (effluents, passion, aversion, delusion) persist, the cycle of suffering continues. Mere philosophical speculation or attachment to various views ("idiosyncratic truths") without genuine understanding and abandonment of these roots does not lead to freedom. Many contemplatives and brahmans, due to their limited understanding, remain stuck in theories that do not lead to the cessation of suffering. The teachings that are truly "deep, hard to see, hard to realize, tranquil, refined, beyond the scope of conjecture, subtle, to-be-experienced by the wise" are those that enable this definitive release.

**Resolution**
The resolution to this cycle of suffering lies in cultivating the two crucial knowledges: **knowledge of the ending of the effluents** and **knowledge of their non-recurrence**. These are the hallmarks of ultimate liberation and the state of an Arahant.

1.  **Knowledge of the ending (of the effluents):** This refers to the **direct insight and comprehension** that the defiling mental qualities—passion, aversion, and delusion (the three effluents)—have been completely abandoned. This comprehension involves truly knowing and seeing:
    *   **Stress (suffering), its origination, its cessation, and the path to its cessation** (the Four Noble Truths). Without this understanding, ending stress is impossible.
    *   The **dependent co-arising** of phenomena, understanding how everything arises from conditions, and thus how suffering arises from craving, ignorance, and clinging. When craving is abandoned, stress ends.
    *   The nature of the "All" (sense media and their objects) as something to be abandoned, leading to release from the cycle.

    This knowledge is achieved through diligent practice, notably:
    *   **Developing tranquility (samatha) and insight (vipassanā)**, which are two qualities that share in clear knowing. Tranquility leads to the development of the mind and the abandonment of passion, while insight leads to the development of discernment and the abandonment of ignorance.
    *   **Cultivating the Noble Eightfold Path** (right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration), which is the path to the ending of kamma and leads to stillness, direct knowledge, and unbinding.
    *   **Practicing the four establishings of mindfulness** (focusing on body, feelings, mind, and mental qualities) as the "direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation... and for the realization of unbinding". Developing these for even a short period can lead to "gnosis right here & now".
    *   **Achieving meditative states (jhānas)** and the **cessation of perception and feeling**, which are progressive stages of stilling mental qualities, ultimately leading to the ending of effluents.
    *   **Developing heedfulness, ardor, and resolute effort** is essential for being "appropriately aroused for the ending of the effluents".

2.  **Knowledge of (their) non-recurrence:** This is the profound, irreversible certainty that the work is done and there will be no further suffering or rebirth. It is the **knowledge of final release** and the attainment of Arahantship. This means:
    *   The complete cutting off of craving, which leads to the cessation of kamma and, consequently, the cessation of stress.
    *   The understanding that the Dhamma is like a **raft, to be used for crossing over, not for holding onto**, signifying non-attachment even to the teachings once the goal is reached.
    *   Reaching the state of "Such," where one has "crossed over birth & aging" and is "unfashioned with regard to all things".

In essence, the two knowledges represent the experiential understanding that all defilements (effluents) have been eradicated (ending) and will never re-arise (non-recurrence), leading to a definitive, irreversible liberation from the cycle of suffering.
