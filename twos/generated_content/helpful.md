# Two > Helpful > Mindfulness & alertness
## Definition

"Mindfulness & alertness" refers to a state of being where one is **fully aware and present** during various activities and when observing internal phenomena [DN_nblm.txt, 197, 208, 223, 312, 345, 349, 358, 369, 517], [SN_nblm.txt, 565]. It involves **making oneself alert** when performing actions such as going forward and returning, looking toward and away, bending and extending limbs, carrying one's robes and bowl, eating, drinking, chewing, tasting, urinating, defecating, walking, standing, sitting, falling asleep, waking up, talking, and remaining silent [DN_nblm.txt, 197, 208, 223, 312, 345, 349, 358, 369, 517], [SN_nblm.txt, 565]. Beyond physical actions, it also entails having **feelings, perceptions, and thoughts known** to oneself as they arise, persist, and subside [AN_nblm.txt, 45, 139], [DN_nblm.txt, 232], [SN_nblm.txt, 566]. This quality is distinct from generalized "mindfulness" (sati) or the "four establishings of mindfulness" (satipaṭṭhāna), though it is often developed in conjunction with them [MN_nblm.txt, 349].

## Benefits

Cultivating mindfulness and alertness yields significant benefits, primarily related to mental clarity and the eradication of defilements:

*   **Mental Pliability and Concentration**: When mindfully and astutely developed, it makes the mind **pliant, malleable, luminous, and not brittle**, leading to a mind rightly concentrated for the ending of effluents [AN_nblm.txt, 38, 39], [AN_nblm.txt, 65].
*   **Abandonment of Unskillful Qualities**: It helps in **cleansing the mind of qualities that would hold it in check**, such as sensual desire, ill will, sloth & drowsiness, restlessness & anxiety, and uncertainty [MN_nblm.txt, 348, 350], [AN_nblm.txt, 65].
*   **Progress Towards Unbinding**: It is conducive to the attainment of the right method and the **realization of unbinding (Nibbāna)** [DN_nblm.txt, 218, 322], [MN_nblm.txt, 322, 356]. For those in training, it can lead to **gnosis (direct knowledge) right here and now, or non-return** if there is any remnant of clinging [KN_Iti_nblm.txt, 277, 278].
*   **Enhanced Discernment**: It is a development of concentration that leads to **mindfulness and alertness** itself [AN_nblm.txt, 44, 45], implying a deepening of these qualities through practice [DN_nblm.txt, 232].
*   **Overcoming Mental Obstacles**: Developing mindfulness immersed in the body, which includes alertness, helps conquer **displeasure, delight, fear, and dread** [MN_nblm.txt, 424]. It also helps prevent memories and resolves related to household life from interfering with concentration [MN_nblm.txt, 420, 421, 423].

## Causation and Conditions

Mindfulness and alertness are not isolated qualities but are deeply interconnected with other aspects of the holy life:

*   **Food for Appropriate Attention**: Lack of mindfulness and alertness is a "food" for inappropriate attention, suggesting that cultivating these qualities nourishes appropriate attention [AN_nblm.txt, 172, 173].
*   **Requisite for Holy Life**: Without being endowed with mindfulness and alertness, it is difficult to follow a **perfect and pure holy life** [AN_nblm.txt, 137, 139].
*   **Interdependence with Other Factors**: Mindfulness and alertness are essential links in a chain of skillful qualities. Appropriate attention supports conviction, which supports hearing the true Dhamma, which supports associating with people of integrity. Conversely, associating with people of no integrity leads to not hearing the Dhamma, which leads to lack of conviction, which leads to inappropriate attention, then lack of mindfulness and alertness, and ultimately to the five hindrances and ignorance [AN_nblm.txt, 172, 173].
*   **Supported by Virtues and Practices**: The Buddha encourages monks to "stay mindful, monks, and alert," framing this as a direct instruction [DN_nblm.txt, 222, 223], [SN_nblm.txt, 565]. This implies a continuous effort and self-application to maintain these states.

## Other Factors

Several factors are conducive to acquiring and strengthening mindfulness and alertness:

*   **Heedfulness and Resolve**: One must dwell heedful, ardent, and resolute to release the mind and end effluents [AN_nblm.txt, 67, 70], [MN_nblm.txt, 359], [SN_nblm.txt, 562]. These qualities are often mentioned in conjunction with mindfulness and alertness.
*   **Developing Concentration**: Mindfulness and alertness are developed in the context of concentration, particularly through practices like the **four jhānas** [AN_nblm.txt, 28, 34, 52, 78, 79, 143], [MN_nblm.txt, 365, 398, 423], [DN_nblm.txt, 231]. The mind becomes concentrated when one feels pleasure from a calm body, which arises from rapture, which is born of joy [AN_nblm.txt, 68, 69], [DN_nblm.txt, 240, 241, 258, 259, 260].
*   **Overcoming Hindrances**: The mind, when freed from the five defilements (sensual desire, ill will, sloth & drowsiness, restlessness & anxiety, uncertainty), becomes pliant, malleable, and luminous, allowing it to be rightly concentrated for the ending of effluents [AN_nblm.txt, 65]. Alertness is also linked to abandoning these hindrances [MN_nblm.txt, 350, 397, 406, 434].
*   **Devotion to Wakefulness**: Being devoted to wakefulness, which involves active cleansing of the mind during various watches of the day and night, is a prerequisite for being possessed of mindfulness and alertness [MN_nblm.txt, 348, 349], [AN_nblm.txt, 43, 138], [MN_nblm.txt, 362, 403, 404, 431, 432].
*   **Sense Restraint and Moderation in Eating**: Guarding the doors of one's senses and knowing moderation in eating contribute to the development of mindfulness and alertness [MN_nblm.txt, 347, 349], [DN_nblm.txt, 196], [AN_nblm.txt, 137, 139], [MN_nblm.txt, 361, 402, 403, 430, 431], [SN_nblm.txt, 509, 510].
*   **Training in Practice**: Continuous training in heightened virtue, heightened mind (concentration), and heightened discernment is essential [AN_nblm.txt, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34]. Mindfulness and alertness are integral to this broader training [AN_nblm.txt, 277].

## Areas Of Application

Mindfulness and alertness are applied across numerous aspects of practice and daily life, underpinning the path to liberation:

*   **Daily Activities**: As part of "mindfulness & alertness," one makes oneself alert in virtually **all bodily movements and states**: going forward, returning, looking, bending, extending, carrying, eating, drinking, chewing, tasting, urinating, defecating, walking, standing, sitting, falling asleep, waking up, talking, and remaining silent [DN_nblm.txt, 197, 208, 223, 312, 345, 349, 358, 369, 517], [MN_nblm.txt, 349, 369, 421, 432], [SN_nblm.txt, 565].
*   **Meditative Practice**:
    *   **Mindfulness of In-and-Out Breathing (Anapanasati)**: A monk pursues this by sitting, holding the body erect, and establishing mindfulness to the fore, breathing in and out mindfully, sensitive to the entire body, calming bodily fabrications, being sensitive to the mind, gladdening, steadying, and releasing the mind, and focusing on inconstancy, dispassion, cessation, and relinquishment [MN_nblm.txt, 371, 411, 412, 420], [SN_nblm.txt, 592, 593, 594, 597, 598, 600, 601, 605, 606, 607].
    *   **Four Establishings of Mindfulness (Satipaṭṭhāna)**: Mindfulness and alertness are crucial for focusing on the body, feelings, mind, and mental qualities "in & of itself," subduing greed and distress [DN_nblm.txt, 230, 251], [MN_nblm.txt, 355, 356, 412, 413, 414, 435, 457], [SN_nblm.txt, 555, 556, 557, 558, 559, 561, 562, 564, 565, 566, 567, 568, 569, 570, 571, 572, 573]. This practice is described as the "direct path for the purification of beings" [DN_nblm.txt, 218, 322], [MN_nblm.txt, 322].
*   **Cultivating Factors for Awakening**: Mindfulness is explicitly listed as the first of the seven factors for awakening, which are developed to bring clear knowing and release to culmination [DN_nblm.txt, 214, 215, 246, 264], [MN_nblm.txt, 305, 318, 319, 385, 415, 418, 451], [SN_nblm.txt, 526, 528, 532, 534, 538, 542, 551, 552, 553, 554, 608, 609, 610, 611]. Alertness is implied in the cultivation of these factors for proper discernment and effort.
*   **Mental Development**: For a monk intent on heightened mind, regularly attending to the themes of concentration, uplifted energy, and equanimity ensures the mind becomes pliant, malleable, and luminous, suitable for ending effluents [AN_nblm.txt, 38, 39]. Mindfulness and alertness contribute to the mind's ability to maintain these themes effectively.
*   **Abandoning Unskillful Thinking**: When evil thoughts arise, a monk uses alertness to identify them and then redirect attention to skillful themes or scrutinize the drawbacks of the unskillful thoughts, thereby abandoning them and concentrating the mind [MN_nblm.txt, 327, 328].
