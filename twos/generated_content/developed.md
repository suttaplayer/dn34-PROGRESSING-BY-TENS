Tranquility (samatha) and insight (vipassanā) are two essential qualities in the Buddhist path, both contributing to clear knowing and release [AN_nblm.txt, 3].

## Definition

**Tranquility (samatha)** is described as a quality that, when developed, leads to the development of the mind, which in turn results in the abandonment of passion [AN_nblm.txt, 3]. It is associated with states of deep concentration (jhāna), characterized by rapture, pleasure, unification of awareness, equanimity, and internal assurance, achieved through seclusion from sensuality and unskillful qualities [AN_nblm.txt, 29, 41, 51, 52, 77, 84, 86, 132, 170, 184, 206, 362, 378, 392, 409, 423, 427].

**Insight (vipassanā)** is a quality that, when developed, leads to the development of discernment (paññā), which in turn results in the abandonment of ignorance [AN_nblm.txt, 3]. It involves seeing and investigating phenomena as they truly are, particularly in terms of their arising, passing away, allure, drawbacks, and escape [SN_nblm.txt, 506, 507, 508, 509].

## Benefits

Developing tranquility and insight offers significant benefits:

*   **For Tranquility:**
    *   It serves to **develop the mind** and helps **abandon passion** [AN_nblm.txt, 3].
    *   It leads to a **pleasant abiding in the here and now**, the **attainment of knowledge and vision**, and the development of **mindfulness and alertness** [AN_nblm.txt, 40, 41, 244, 245].
    *   A mind developed through tranquility is described as **pliant, malleable, luminous, and not brittle**, making it rightly concentrated for the ending of effluents [AN_nblm.txt, 37, 38, 67].
    *   It leads to **blissful concentration** in the present and future, is noble, peaceful, exquisite, and results in the **acquiring of calm** and the **attainment of unification** [AN_nblm.txt, 75, 76, 270, 271].
*   **For Insight:**
    *   It serves to **develop discernment** and helps **abandon ignorance** [AN_nblm.txt, 3].
    *   It contributes to **clear knowing (vijjā)** and **release (vimutti)** [AN_nblm.txt, 3, 240, 266, 472, 474].
    *   By correctly contemplating dualities such as stress, its origination, cessation, and the path to its cessation, one can expect **gnosis right here and now, or non-return** [KN_StNp_nblm.txt, 307, 308, 309, 310, 311, 312].
*   **For Both (Tranquility & Insight in tandem):**
    *   These two qualities "have a share in clear knowing" [AN_nblm.txt, 3].
    *   When developed in tandem, "the path is born" leading to the **abandonment of fetters** and the **destruction of obsessions** [AN_nblm.txt, 53].
    *   Right view, when supported by tranquility and insight, leads to **awareness-release** and **discernment-release** [AN_nblm.txt, 68, 69, 372].
    *   They are considered of "great help in the attainment of the cessation of perception & feeling" [SN_nblm.txt, 545].
    *   They constitute a path leading to the **unfabricated** (the ending of passion, aversion, and delusion) [SN_nblm.txt, 547, 548].

## Causation and Conditions

Tranquility and insight arise from and contribute to various conditions:

*   **Conditions for Tranquility:**
    *   Developing **immeasurable concentration** (based on goodwill, compassion, empathetic joy, or equanimity) leads to tranquility, calmness, unification, and luminosity of mind [AN_nblm.txt, 74, 75, 76, 150, 151].
    *   **Seclusion from sensuality and unskillful qualities** is a prerequisite for entering the jhānas, which are states of deep concentration and tranquility [AN_nblm.txt, 29, 41, 51, 52, 77, 84, 86, 132, 170, 184, 206, 362, 378, 392, 409, 423, 427].
    *   Recollection of the Tathāgata, Dhamma, Saṅgha, one's own virtues, or devas can cleanse the defiled mind, bringing **calm and joy**, which fosters concentration [AN_nblm.txt, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 190, 191, 192, 193].
*   **Conditions for Insight:**
    *   **Discernment of the four noble truths** (stress, its origination, cessation, and the path) is fundamental for insight [AN_nblm.txt, 36, 209, 213, 214, 215, 354, 369, 412, 447, 506, 507, 508, 509, 606, 607].
    *   **Appropriate attention** and **the voice of another** are conditions for the arising of right view, which then can be assisted by tranquility and insight [MN_nblm.txt, 372].
    *   **Investigating phenomena** such as form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness as inconstant, stressful, and not-self contributes to insight [AN_nblm.txt, 169, 396, 517].
    *   Scrutinizing the drawbacks of unskillful thoughts can lead to a steadied and concentrated mind, aiding insight [MN_nblm.txt, 342].
*   **Conditions for Both:**
    *   **Developing the seven factors for awakening** (mindfulness, analysis of qualities, persistence, rapture, calm, concentration, equanimity) dependent on seclusion, dispassion, and cessation, can lead to both calm and insight, culminating in direct knowledge and unbinding [MN_nblm.txt, 322, 436, SN_nblm.txt, 553, 554].
    *   **Having admirable friends, companions, and colleagues** is the first quality that brings awareness-release (supported by both) to maturity [KN_Ud_nblm.txt, 318, 319, MN_nblm.txt, 155].
    *   **Listening to, teaching, reciting, and mentally examining the Dhamma** can cultivate sensitivity to meaning and Dhamma, giving rise to joy, rapture, calm, and concentrated mind, leading to release [AN_nblm.txt, 70, 71, 72, 73, 251, 252, 253, 254, 255, 271, 272, 273, 274, 275].

## How To Develop

The sources outline various methods for developing tranquility and insight:

*   **General Practices:**
    *   **Relentless exertion, firmness, persistence, and striving** are emphasized as fundamental training [AN_nblm.txt, 2].
    *   Cultivating **peacefulness in faculties and hearts** leads to peaceful bodily, verbal, and mental actions [AN_nblm.txt, 5].
    *   **Abandoning qualities of a fool** (e.g., inappropriate questioning/answering, disapproving of appropriate answers) and **undertaking qualities of a wise person** [AN_nblm.txt, 6, 7].
    *   Cultivating **lack of greed, aversion, and delusion** is a root of skillful qualities [AN_nblm.txt, 23, 241].
    *   Engaging in the **three trainings**: heightened virtue, heightened mind, and heightened discernment [AN_nblm.txt, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35, 32][DN_nblm.txt, 242]. This process helps abandon passion, aversion, and delusion [AN_nblm.txt, 31].
    *   **Abandoning the five hindrances** (sensual desire, ill will, sloth & drowsiness, restlessness & anxiety, uncertainty) is crucial, as they overwhelm awareness and weaken discernment [AN_nblm.txt, 67, 80, 84, 86, 170, 173][DN_nblm.txt, 221, 327][MN_nblm.txt, 353, 362, 378, 392, 423, 427, 445][SN_nblm.txt, 558].
*   **Developing Tranquility (Samatha):**
    *   Practice of the **four jhānas** is a core method for achieving heightened mind and pleasant abiding [AN_nblm.txt, 29, 41, 51, 52, 77, 84, 86, 132, 170, 184, 206, 362, 378, 392, 409, 423, 427][DN_nblm.txt, 245]. This involves progressing from seclusion, rapture, pleasure, to unification of awareness, equanimity, and pure mindfulness [AN_nblm.txt, 29].
    *   Developing **immeasurable concentration** through goodwill, compassion, empathetic joy, and equanimity, which makes the mind pliant, malleable, luminous, and rightly concentrated [AN_nblm.txt, 74, 75, 76, 150, 151][MN_nblm.txt, 379, 380][SN_nblm.txt, 561, 562, 563].
    *   Attending periodically to the **themes of concentration, uplifted energy, and equanimity** to ensure the mind is pliant, malleable, luminous, and not brittle, leading to the ending of effluents [AN_nblm.txt, 37, 38].
*   **Developing Insight (Vipassanā):**
    *   **Discerning the Four Noble Truths** (stress, its origination, cessation, and the path to cessation) as they have come to be [AN_nblm.txt, 36, 209, 213, 214, 215, 354, 369, 412, 447, 506, 507, 508, 509, 606, 607, 608].
    *   **Investigating and seeing fabrications** (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness) as inconstant, stressful, a disease, an arrow, painful, an affliction, alien, a disintegration, an emptiness, and not-self [AN_nblm.txt, 169, 396, 517]. This turns the mind towards deathlessness [AN_nblm.txt, 169, 396].
    *   Focusing on the **origination and passing away of the five aggregates** (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness) is a key aspect of discernment [AN_nblm.txt, 139, 149].
    *   Developing **mindfulness of in-and-out breathing** by being sensitive to the entire body, calming bodily fabrication, and focusing on inconstancy, dispassion, cessation, and relinquishment [AN_nblm.txt, 180][KN_Iti_nblm.txt, 295][MN_nblm.txt, 394, 395, 433, 434, 587, 590, 592, 594].
    *   Cultivating specific **perceptions conducive to penetration**, such as unattractiveness of the body, death, loathsomeness in food, distaste for every world, inconstancy, stress in inconstancy, not-self in stress, abandoning, and dispassion [AN_nblm.txt, 161][DN_nblm.txt, 256, 260].

## Areas Of Application

Tranquility and insight are applied throughout the spiritual training and in daily life to overcome obstacles and achieve the ultimate goal:

*   **In the Holy Life:** Both qualities are fundamental to living the holy life, with tranquility supporting the mind and insight supporting discernment [AN_nblm.txt, 3, 290]. They are integral to the **three trainings** (heightened virtue, heightened mind, and heightened discernment) [AN_nblm.txt, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35][DN_nblm.txt, 242]. The holy life is lived "with training as a reward, with discernment as its surpassing state, with release as its heartwood, and with mindfulness as its governing principle" [AN_nblm.txt, 59, 60, 61, 62].
*   **Overcoming Defilements:** They are crucial for abandoning passion, aversion, and delusion [AN_nblm.txt, 31, 178, 264][KN_Iti_nblm.txt, 298]. The abandonment of the **five hindrances** is a prerequisite for entering meditative states where tranquility and insight flourish [AN_nblm.txt, 67, 80, 84, 86, 170, 173, 184, 209, 353, 362, 378, 392, 423, 427, 445, 558]. Guarding the **six sense faculties** is also a key area of application to prevent unskillful qualities from assailing the mind [DN_nblm.txt, 205][MN_nblm.txt, 357, 366, 382, 383, 426, 444][SN_nblm.txt, 535].
*   **Meditative Development:** The **four jhānas** are explicitly states of heightened concentration (tranquility) [AN_nblm.txt, 29, 41, 51, 52, 77, 84, 86, 132, 170, 184, 206, 362, 378, 392, 409, 423, 427, 245]. **Mindfulness of breathing** (ānāpānasati) is a primary practice that develops concentration and subsequently leads to clear knowing and release [MN_nblm.txt, 394, 395, 433, 434, 587, 590, 592, 594, 596, 597, 598]. The **four divine abodes** (goodwill, compassion, empathetic joy, equanimity) are also cultivated to develop immeasurable concentration [MN_nblm.txt, 379, 380].
*   **Direct Knowledge and Release:** Ultimately, the development of tranquility and insight leads to **awareness-release** and **discernment-release**, which signify the ending of effluents (sensuality, becoming, ignorance) and the complete cessation of suffering and stress in the here and now [AN_nblm.txt, 4, 10, 37, 68, 69, 85, 87, 142][DN_nblm.txt, 209, 213, 214, 215, 233, 234, 237, 238, 264, 278][KN_Iti_nblm.txt, 289, 290, 292, 293][MN_nblm.txt, 354, 369, 372, 412, 429, 436, 441, 447][SN_nblm.txt, 502, 504, 511, 554]. These states are seen as the culmination of the holy life [MN_nblm.txt, 429].
