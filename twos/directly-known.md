Here is an analysis of the statement "Which two dhammas should be directly known? Two properties: the fabricated property & the unfabricated property", presented in the "Situation, Complication, Resolution" (SCR) format:

**Situation**

The path to spiritual insight and liberation involves the direct knowledge of **two fundamental properties**: the fabricated and the unfabricated. These two properties define the nature of existence within the world. Fabricated phenomena are characterized by their **arising, passing away, and alteration while staying**. This encompasses all phenomena that are "born, become, produced, made, fabricated, impermanent, fabricated of aging & death, a nest of illnesses, perishing, come-into-being through nourishment". The Buddha taught that "All fabrications are inconstant" and "All fabrications are stressful", meaning whatever is inconstant is stressful, and whatever is stressful is not-self. Understanding these characteristics is foundational to discerning the true nature of reality.

In contrast, the unfabricated property is defined by the absence of these characteristics: **"No arising is discernable, no passing away is discernable, no alteration while staying is discernable"**. It is described as "the ending of passion, the ending of aversion, the ending of delusion", which is **"peaceful, permanent, a sphere beyond conjecture, unborn, unproduced, the sorrowless, stainless state, the cessation of stressful qualities, stilling-of-fabrications bliss"**. This unfabricated state is also referred to as "unprovoked release of awareness".

**Complication**

Without directly knowing and comprehending the distinction between these two properties, individuals remain entangled in suffering and unskillful states. An "uninstructed run-of-the-mill person" perceives the world and its components, such as earth, in terms of 'I-making' and 'mine-making' conceit, delighting in them because they have not been fully comprehended. This lack of comprehension leads to attachment and continued suffering. For example, failing to discern form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness as "inconstant, stressful, subject to change" prevents one from abandoning concepts like "This is mine. This is my self. This is what I am".

The failure to directly know these properties means one remains subject to phenomena that are "unfit for delight" and bound by "obscurations". The Dhamma teaches that "the ending of effluents is for one who knows & sees, I tell you, not for one who does not know & does not see". Without this crucial understanding, one's mind remains unreleased from defilements, leading to continued "birth, aging, death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, & despair". Such a person is described as "deluded" because the defiling effluents that lead to renewed becoming, trouble, and stress are not abandoned.

**Resolution**

The resolution lies in the **direct knowledge** and comprehension of both fabricated and unfabricated properties, which leads to liberation from suffering and stress. This direct knowledge is achieved by discerning the true nature of all phenomena. The Buddha taught the Dhamma "through direct knowledge, not without direct knowledge". By knowing and seeing, one understands that "Whatever is subject to origination is all subject to cessation".

The cultivation of this direct knowledge and the realization of unbinding require a dedicated path of practice, described as **"the direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding—in other words, the four establishings of mindfulness"**. Specific practices include:
*   Developing **mindfulness immersed in the body**, which is described as a path leading to the unfabricated.
*   Cultivating **tranquility and insight**, which are two dhammas that should be developed to facilitate this direct knowledge.

When a monk scrutinizes and understands the origination and cessation of stress (and its roots like acquisition), and practices in accordance with this understanding, "he is totally released from sorrows, lamentations, pains, distresses, & despairs. He is totally released... from suffering & stress". This complete release signifies that **"Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world"**. Such a state also marks the "ending of the obsessions of passion, the obsessions of resistance, the obsessions of views, the obsessions of uncertainty, the obsessions of conceit, the obsessions of passion for becoming, & the obsessions of ignorance". Ultimately, this direct knowledge of the fabricated and unfabricated leads to "disenchantment, dispassion, cessation, to stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, [and] to unbinding". The Tathāgata himself attained "the unexcelled right self-awakening" by comprehending that delight is the root of suffering and through "the total ending, fading away, cessation, letting go, relinquishment of craving".
