In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta presents a detailed framework of Dhamma qualities, including the "nine step-by-step cessations". This profound sequence describes a progressive stilling of mental and bodily phenomena, leading to ultimate liberation.

Here is an analysis of the detailed meaning of this statement, presented in a Situation, Complication, and Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation**

The path described in the sources is undertaken by a practitioner of wide discernment who aims to achieve a profound state of concentration and release. This spiritual endeavor seeks to eliminate the ingrained sense of "I-making" and "mine-making" conceit-obsession regarding both one's conscious body and all external themes. The ultimate goal is to **put an end to suffering & stress** and realize unbinding. The Dhamma is for those who are **modest, content, reclusive, whose persistence is aroused, whose mindfulness is established, whose mind is concentrated, and who are endowed with discernment**. The practitioner strives to enter and remain in "effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release, having directly known and realized them for himself right in the here & now".

**Complication**

The inherent challenge lies in the nature of existence itself. The Blessed One teaches that **"All fabrications are stressful"** and **"All phenomena are not-self"**. These fabrications include bodily, verbal, and mental processes. Suffering arises because **"Craving is the seamstress—for craving stitches one to the production of this or that very becoming"**. The roots of unskillful actions are greed, aversion, and delusion. Any **"desire-passion with regard to the eye is a defilement of the mind"**, and this applies to all six sense bases (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, intellect) and their corresponding objects (forms, sounds, aromas, flavors, tactile sensations, ideas). The practitioner must **"regard whatever phenomena there that are connected with form, feeling, perception, fabrications, & consciousness, as inconstant, stressful, a disease, a cancer, an arrow, painful, an affliction, alien, a disintegration, an emptiness, not-self"**. Consciousness itself does not arise without a requisite condition. Furthermore, "Perceiving in terms of signs, beings take a stand on signs. Not fully comprehending signs, they come into the bonds of death". This continuous arising and passing away of phenomena, and the clinging to them, is the source of agitation and suffering.

**Resolution**

The path to liberation involves a systematic process of stilling and cessation, known as the nine step-by-step cessations. This process begins with cultivating jhānas (mental absorptions):

1.  **First Jhāna**: The perception of sensuality ceases. This is achieved by becoming **"quite secluded from sensuality, secluded from unskillful mental qualities, [entering] & [remaining] in the first jhāna: rapture & pleasure born of seclusion, accompanied by directed thought & evaluation"**.
2.  **Second Jhāna**: Directed thoughts and evaluations (verbal fabrications) cease. This occurs **"with the stilling of directed thoughts & evaluations, [entering] & [remaining] in the second jhāna: rapture & pleasure born of concentration, unification of awareness free from directed thought & evaluation—internal assurance"**.
3.  **Third Jhāna**: Rapture ceases. In this state, the practitioner **"With the fading of rapture, he remains equanimous, mindful, & alert, and senses pleasure with the body. He enters & remains in the third jhāna, of which the noble ones declare, 'Equanimous & mindful, he has a pleasant abiding'"**.
4.  **Fourth Jhāna**: In-and-out breaths (bodily fabrications) cease. This is attained **"With the abandoning of pleasure & pain—as with the earlier disappearance of elation & distress—he enters & remains in the fourth jhāna: purity of equanimity & mindfulness, neither pleasure nor pain"**.

Beyond the four jhānas, the path continues through the formless dimensions:

5.  **Dimension of the Infinitude of Space**: The perception of forms ceases. This involves the **"complete transcending of perceptions of (physical) form, with the disappearance of perceptions of resistance, and not attending to perceptions of multiplicity, (perceiving,) 'Infinite space'"**.
6.  **Dimension of the Infinitude of Consciousness**: The perception of the dimension of the infinitude of space ceases.
7.  **Dimension of Nothingness**: The perception of the dimension of the infinitude of consciousness ceases.
8.  **Dimension of Neither Perception Nor Non-Perception**: The perception of the dimension of nothingness ceases.

The ultimate step in this sequence is:

9.  **Cessation of Perception & Feeling**: Perceptions and feelings (mental fabrications) cease.

This systematic cessation leads to a profound state of liberation. As a result, the practitioner **"discerns that 'Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world'"**. This state is described as **"peace, this is exquisite—the pacification of all fabrications; the relinquishing of all acquisitions; the ending of craving; dispassion; cessation; unbinding"**. When consciousness is **"neither externally scattered & diffused, nor internally positioned—from lack of clinging or sustenance he would be unagitated, there is no seed for the conditions of future birth, aging, death, or stress"**. A monk whose **"mind is well shored-up by discernment, it is suitable for him to say, 'Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world'"**. This is how **unbinding [nibbuti] is experienced right within**.
