In the *DASUTTARA SUTTA* (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents a comprehensive framework of *Dhammas* for attaining unbinding and putting an end to suffering and stress. This framework includes "nine ways of subduing hatred" as qualities on the side of distinction.

Here is an analysis of the detailed meaning of this statement, presented in a "Situation, Complication, Resolution" (SCR) format:

### Situation

The sources identify **hatred, aversion, and ill will as fundamental unskillful qualities** that arise from within an individual and are detrimental to their well-being and spiritual progress. These qualities are described as "inside stains, inside enemies, inside foes, inside murderers, inside adversaries". An individual consumed by anger may not recognize their own benefit or even the *Dhamma*, and can be enveloped in a "mass of darkness". Such states manifest when one perceives harm (past, present, or future) directed at oneself or loved ones, or when perceived aid is given to those one dislikes.

For example, hatred can arise from thoughts such as:
*   "He has done me harm."
*   "He is doing me harm."
*   "He is going to do me harm."
*   "He has done harm to people who are dear & pleasing to me."
*   "He is doing harm to people who are dear & pleasing to me."
*   "He is going to do harm to people who are dear & pleasing to me."
*   "He has aided people who are not dear or pleasing to me."
*   "He is aiding people who are not dear or pleasing to me."
*   "He is going to aid people who are not dear or pleasing to me."

### Complication

The presence of hatred and other unskillful qualities **obstructs mental release and the development of discernment**. When one's mind is "overcome with aversion & passion," the *Dhamma* is "not easily realized". These unskillful thoughts, "imbued with ill-will" or "harmfulness," lead to "blindness, produces lack of vision, produces lack of knowledge, leads to the cessation of discernment, sides with vexation, and are not conducive to Unbinding".

Furthermore, if a person allows anger to arise in response to dispraise or criticism, "that would be an obstruction for you yourselves". If one is unable to make efforts to abandon what should not be done, they dwell "bewildered & unprotected," unable to righteously consider themselves a contemplative. The sources emphasize that it is **impossible** for ill will to overpower the mind when goodwill has been genuinely "developed, pursued, handed the reins, taken as a basis, steadied, consolidated, and well-undertaken as an awareness-release". This highlights that the continued presence of hatred indicates a lack of proper spiritual development and acts as a significant barrier to achieving genuine release.

### Resolution

The core method for subduing hatred, as presented in the query, involves a shift in perspective encapsulated by the repeated phrase: **"But what should I expect?"**. This rhetorical question encourages a detached and realistic view of situations that might otherwise provoke anger, fostering an acceptance that external actions or events are often beyond one's control or are simply the natural consequence of conditions.

Beyond this specific phrase, the sources offer a variety of interconnected strategies to overcome hatred and cultivate its opposite, goodwill:

*   **Cultivating Positive Mental States**: When hatred arises, one should **actively develop goodwill, compassion, or equanimity** toward the individual in question. This involves intentionally pervading the individual and, by extension, the entire world, with an "awareness imbued with goodwill—abundant, enlarged, immeasurable, free from hostility, free from ill will".
*   **Understanding Karma**: Directing one's thoughts to the principle that an individual is the "doer of his actions, heir of his actions, born of his actions, related by his actions, and has his actions as his arbitrator" helps to subdue hatred by recognizing the natural consequences of actions.
*   **Non-Retaliation and Calmness**: When faced with provocation or abusive words, a practitioner should "endure them" and **"not flare up"** at someone who is angry, thereby winning "a battle hard to win" and living "for the good of both".
*   **Discerning and Abandoning Unskillful Qualities**: The practice involves recognizing "evil as evil" and then becoming "disenchanted there, dispassionate there, released". The ultimate goal is the "ending of passion, ending of aversion, ending of delusion," which is termed "comprehension". This is achieved by cultivating disenchantment with phenomena like form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness, understanding them as "inconstant, stressful," and "not-self," and thereby abandoning "desire-passion" towards them.
*   **Adhering to the Noble Eightfold Path**: The holistic path taught by the Blessed One, including right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration, is the foundation for overcoming these defilements. This path leads to "dispassion, unfettering, shedding, modesty, contentment, reclusiveness, aroused persistence, and being unburdensome". By practicing this well-taught and entirely perfect holy life, one puts an end to suffering and stress.
