In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents a comprehensive framework of Dhammas, or teachings, categorized by numerical sets, ranging from single qualities to tens. Among these, the "Nine abodes for beings" are specifically identified as Dhammas that "should be comprehended". This instruction emphasizes the importance of understanding the nature of different realms of existence as a crucial step towards liberation.

Here's a detailed analysis of this statement:

**Situation: The Nine Abodes for Beings**

The "Nine abodes for beings" (also referred to as "nine stations of consciousness" or "nine step-by-step dwellings") describe various states of existence or levels of consciousness within the cosmos. These abodes encompass a wide range of experiences, from the most physical and diversified to the most subtle and formless:

*   **1. Multiplicity of body & multiplicity of perception:** This refers to beings who experience both physical diversity and diverse perceptions, such as human beings, some devas, and some beings in the lower realms.
*   **2. Multiplicity of body & singularity of perception:** This applies to beings with multiple bodies but a single perception, like the devas of Brahmā's retinue, generated by the first jhāna (meditative absorption).
*   **3. Singularity of body & multiplicity of perception:** This describes beings with a single body but multiple perceptions, exemplified by the Radiant Devas.
*   **4. Singularity of body & singularity of perception:** This refers to beings who manifest with a single body and singular perception, such as the Beautiful-black Devas.
*   **5. Dimension of the infinitude of space:** Beings here arrive after completely transcending physical form and perceiving "Infinite space". This marks a shift to formless realms.
*   **6. Dimension of the infinitude of consciousness:** This abode is attained by beings who have transcended the dimension of infinite space and perceive "Infinite consciousness".
*   **7. Dimension of nothingness:** Beings reach this state by completely transcending the dimension of infinite consciousness and perceiving "There is nothing".
*   **8. Dimension of neither perception nor non-perception:** This is a very subtle state attained by fully transcending the dimension of nothingness.
*   **9. Cessation of perception & feeling:** This is the highest and most profound abode, where all perceptions and feelings cease. It represents the ultimate stilling of mental fabrications.

These descriptions are presented as "true, genuine, real, not unreal, not otherwise" dhammas, or realities, that were "rightly awakened to by the Tathāgata". They represent the range of existence that can be experienced within conditioned reality.

**Complication: The Need for Comprehension**

The instruction that these nine abodes "should be comprehended" implies that simply existing within them, or even striving for them without full understanding, is insufficient for ultimate liberation. The very act of existence within these abodes is linked to the cycle of "wandering-on" or "transmigrating," driven by "ignorance and fettered by craving". As long as one is caught within these realms, whether physical or subtle, one remains within the scope of suffering.

The sources emphasize that "the ending of passion, ending of aversion, ending of delusion" is what "is called comprehension". Therefore, merely identifying these abodes is not enough; one must deeply understand their nature to release attachment to them. Without this deep comprehension, one remains "deluded," as delusion stems from "not abandoning the effluents", which are the defilements that bind beings to these states of renewed becoming.

**Resolution: Attaining Unbinding and Ending Suffering**

Comprehending the nine abodes for beings means perceiving their impermanent, stressful, and not-self nature, and thereby releasing oneself from any attachment or identification with them. This comprehension is not merely intellectual but involves a profound realization that leads to liberation. The Dhamma is taught precisely for this purpose, as its qualities "lead to utter disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding".

The ultimate goal is "attaining unbinding" and "putting an end to suffering & stress," leading to "release from all ties". When one comprehends the nature of these abodes, it leads to:

*   **Cessation of Objectification**: By understanding the impermanence and non-self nature of these states, particularly as one progresses through the formless dimensions, one realizes that "with the remainderless fading & cessation of the six contact-media, there comes to be the cessation of objectification, the stilling of objectification".
*   **Transcending the Cosmos**: The Buddha explicitly states that "It's not through that sort of traveling that the end of the cosmos is known, seen, or reached. But at the same time, I tell you that there is no making an end of suffering & stress without reaching the end of the cosmos". This implies that the "end of the cosmos" is an internal realization achieved by fully comprehending and transcending these abodes, not by physical travel.
*   **Liberation from Afflictions**: The entire purpose of the Dhamma is to facilitate "dispassion," from which "one is released". By truly understanding the nature of these various existences and releasing craving for them, one's "effluents" (deep-seated defilements) go to their total ending, resulting in "effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release, having directly known & realized them for himself right in the here-&-now". This signifies complete liberation from the cycle of birth and death, which the nine abodes represent in their various manifestations. The Dhamma supports those who "enjoy non-objectification" and "delight in non-objectification," leading away from attachment to any form of existence.
