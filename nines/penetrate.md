Here is a detailed analysis of the statement regarding "nine multiplicities" from the DASUTTARA SUTTA, presented in the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation**

The DASUTTARA SUTTA, a discourse delivered by Venerable Sāriputta, outlines various categories of *Dhammas* (teachings or qualities) that progress by tens, covering aspects from those that are very helpful to those that should be realized. Among the *Dhammas* that are explicitly stated as "hard to penetrate" are **"nine multiplicities"**. These multiplicities describe a causal chain, illustrating how various phenomena, when grasped or clung to, lead to further complexity and ultimately, suffering.

The statement details this chain: "Dependent on a **multiplicity of properties**, there arises a multiplicity of **contacts**. Dependent on a multiplicity of contacts, there arises a multiplicity of **feelings**. Dependent on a multiplicity of feelings, there arises a multiplicity of **perceptions**. Dependent on a multiplicity of perceptions, there arises a multiplicity of **resolves**. Dependent on a multiplicity of resolves, there arises a multiplicity of **desires**. Dependent on a multiplicity of desires, there arises a multiplicity of **fevers**. Dependent on a multiplicity of fevers, there arises a multiplicity of **searches**. Dependent on a multiplicity of searches, there arises a multiplicity of **gains**".

**Complication**

These "nine multiplicities" are hard to penetrate because they reveal the intricate and often subtle ways in which the mind becomes entangled in suffering through dependent co-arising and clinging.

*   **Multiplicity of Properties**: The fundamental building blocks of experience are the six properties: earth, liquid, fire, wind, space, and consciousness. When one develops desire-passion for these properties, it becomes a defilement of the mind. This "multiplicity" refers to the diverse nature of these properties and the various ways beings experience them, laying the groundwork for further entanglement.
*   **Multiplicity of Contacts**: Dependent on these properties, or more broadly, the meeting of a sense organ, its corresponding object, and consciousness, "contact" arises. For example, eye-contact arises dependent on the eye and forms. Desire-passion with regard to contact, whether through the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, or intellect, is a defilement of the mind. This highlights how initial interactions with the world, when accompanied by desire, become a source of complexity.
*   **Multiplicity of Feelings**: From contact, feelings arise. The Blessed One speaks of three types of feelings: pleasant, painful, and neither pleasant nor painful. All these feelings are ultimately characterized as stressful. Desire-passion for feelings also defiles the mind.
*   **Multiplicity of Perceptions**: Following feelings, perceptions (such as forms, sounds, aromas, flavors, tactile sensations, and ideas) arise. When mindfulness is lapsed, attending to the "theme of endearing" with regard to a form, for example, can lead to the mind becoming impassioned and fixated. Conversely, perceptions of unattractiveness, death, loathsomeness, distaste for the world, inconstancy, stress in inconstancy, and not-self in stress, along with perceptions of abandoning and dispassion, are conducive to penetrating and gaining footing in the deathless.
*   **Multiplicity of Resolves**: Perceptions lead to the arising of resolves or fabrications. The unskillful roots of actions include greed, aversion, and delusion, which influence these resolves. All fabrications are inherently stressful.
*   **Multiplicity of Desires**: From these resolves, desires emerge. Craving, which encompasses craving for sensuality, becoming, and non-becoming, is described as "the ensnarer that has flowed along, spread out, and caught hold, with which this world is smothered & enveloped like a tangled skein, a knotted ball of string". This craving is the cause for the origination of actions and leads to the origination of suffering and stress. The world is ensnared because it does not go beyond transmigration due to craving. There are 108 craving-verbalizations, showing the extensive nature of desire.
*   **Multiplicity of Fevers**: The proliferation of desires gives rise to "fevers", indicating a state of mental agitation and unease resulting from unfulfilled or proliferating desires.
*   **Multiplicity of Searches**: Fevers, in turn, lead to "searches". These can be "ignoble searches" for happiness in things subject to aging, illness, death, and defilement. Such searches prolong suffering rather than alleviating it.
*   **Multiplicity of Gains**: The culmination of this chain, "gains," implies that the continuous pursuit and acquisition of these phenomena, driven by craving and searches, perpetuate the cycle of stress. The sources also link craving to acquisition and other negative phenomena like conflict and disputes.

The difficulty in penetrating these multiplicities stems from the fact that they are deeply intertwined with the process of dependent co-arising (or dependent origination). The Blessed One teaches that it is "because of not understanding and not penetrating this Dhamma that this generation is like a tangled skein, a knotted ball of string, like matted rushes and reeds, and does not go beyond transmigration, beyond the planes of deprivation, woe, & bad destinations". Clinging to these aggregates (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness) leads to the origination of stress. The mind, when not free from clinging, can become agitated.

**Resolution**

Penetrating and ultimately transcending these nine multiplicities involves a profound understanding and diligent practice of the Dhamma, leading to liberation from suffering.

*   **Understanding Dependent Co-Arising and the Four Noble Truths**: The key to penetrating these multiplicities is to discern how they originate and cease. "Whoever sees dependent co-arising sees the Dhamma; whoever sees the Dhamma sees dependent co-arising". This means understanding stress, its origination, its cessation, and the path to its cessation.
*   **Abandoning Desire-Passion and Clinging**: The path to liberation requires abandoning desire-passion and clinging for all phenomena. When desire and passion for the five clinging-aggregates are subdued and abandoned, it leads to the cessation of stress.
    *   This includes the "ending of craving," which implies "ripping off the fetter" and putting an end to suffering.
    *   The "ending of effluents" is for one who knows and sees these phenomena as they truly are.
*   **Cultivating Discernment and Mindfulness**: To overcome these multiplicities, one must cultivate discernment, which allows one to see things as inconstant, stressful, and not-self. Mindfulness helps in observing phenomena as they arise, persist, and subside, which is crucial for cultivating alertness.
*   **Developing Concentration and Non-Objectification**: Developing concentration allows a monk to discern things as they have come to be, seeing the inconstant nature of the eye, forms, and consciousness, and whatever arises from them. The Dhamma is for those who "enjoy non-objectification, who delight in non-objectification, not for one who enjoys & delights in objectification". This refers to the mind being released in the cessation of objectification, signifying a state beyond the proliferation of concepts and attachments described in the multiplicities.
*   **Reaching Unbinding (Nibbāna)**: The ultimate goal is *unbinding*, or *Nibbāna*, which is described as pleasant because it is a state "where there is nothing felt" in terms of sensual pleasure, and where suffering ceases. This release is achieved by transcending all forms of clinging and becoming.

By understanding this causal chain of multiplicities and actively practicing the abandonment of desire and clinging through discernment and concentration, one can penetrate these difficult truths and attain ultimate release from suffering.
