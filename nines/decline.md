In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta presents a comprehensive framework of Dhammas, including qualities that lead to decline. One such category highlights the "nine grounds for hatred," which bind hatred and contribute to a person's decline.

Here is an analysis of this statement:

### Situation

The statement identifies **nine specific scenarios that serve as "grounds for hatred," meaning they are catalysts for binding and perpetuating this unskillful quality**. These scenarios revolve around perceived harm, either inflicted by an individual on oneself or on those dear, or aid given to those disliked. The specific grounds are:
*   Thinking, "He has done me harm".
*   Thinking, "He is doing me harm".
*   Thinking, "He is going to do me harm".
*   Thinking, "He has done harm to people who are dear & pleasing to me".
*   Thinking, "He is doing harm to people who are dear & pleasing to me".
*   Thinking, "He is going to do harm to people who are dear & pleasing to me".
*   Thinking, "He has aided people who are not dear or pleasing to me".
*   Thinking, "He is aiding people who are not dear or pleasing to me".
*   Thinking, "He is going to aid people who are not dear or pleasing to me".

These are considered "dhammas that are on the side of decline" because they represent ways in which an individual fosters and perpetuates ill will, moving away from skillful qualities.

### Complication

Hatred, rooted in aversion, is repeatedly identified as an **"unskillful quality" and a "defilement of the mind"**. Its presence leads to numerous negative outcomes and hinders spiritual progress:

*   **Internal Corruption**: Aversion is an "inside stain, inside enemy, inside foe, inside murderer, inside adversary". Conquered by it, one's mind can be overwhelmed, leading to a "state of deprivation, headed for hell". The Dhamma states that anger destroys the person of evil awareness "like its own fruit, the reed".
*   **Behavioral Manifestations**: An angry person "doesn't know his own benefit," "doesn't see the Dhamma," and is in a "mass of darkness." Such a person "has no shame, no compunction, is not respectful in speech". It is one of the "three kinds of misconduct" (verbal misconduct).
*   **Obstruction to Dhamma**: Hatred and aversion are barriers to understanding the Dhamma, which is "not easily realized by those overcome with aversion & passion". It obstructs one's ability to develop discernment and achieve "unprovoked release of awareness". The presence of "ill will" keeps the mind from being released, even if goodwill is cultivated, indicating it must be overcome.
*   **Source of Conflict and Suffering**: Hatred can cause "arguments, quarrels, disputes, accusations, divisive speech, & false speech". It can manifest in destructive ways, even metaphorically turning places to ash with a "single mental act of hatred". The Dhamma explicitly states that it is not conducive to disenchantment, dispassion, cessation, calm, direct knowledge, self-awakening, or unbinding.
*   **Impediment to Arahantship**: Not abandoning "aversion" is listed among the "nine things" that make one "incapable of realizing arahantship". It is a "fetter" (ill will) that must be removed for ultimate release.

### Resolution

The cessation or subduing of hatred and aversion is central to progress in the holy life, leading to release from suffering. The sources provide clear methods for this:

*   **Developing Counter-Qualities**:
    *   **Goodwill, Compassion, Equanimity**: When hatred arises, one should "develop goodwill for that individual," "develop compassion for that individual," or "develop equanimity toward that individual". These "sublime attitudes" (Brahmavihāras) are to be developed as an "awareness-release," pervading the entire cosmos, leading to immeasurable and well-developed minds free from hostility and ill will.
    *   **Not Returning Harm**: A practical approach is "not return insult to one who has insulted him, doesn't return anger to one who is angry at him, doesn't quarrel with one who is quarreling".
*   **Mindful Reflection**: One can subdue hatred by reflecting on the individual being "the product of his actions," understanding that their actions, good or evil, are their own. This aligns with knowing that one experiences the results of one's actions, and not getting "worked up over impossibilities".
*   **Abandoning Aversion**: The Dhamma is taught "for the abandoning of passion, for the abandoning of aversion, for the abandoning of delusion". The "lack of aversion" is a "root of what is skillful".
*   **Cultivating Discernment and Practice**:
    *   **Understanding Dhamma**: By comprehending the Dhamma, especially the nature of stress and its cessation, one gains "dispassion," which leads to release.
    *   **Heedfulness and Resolve**: Being "heedful, ardent, & resolute" is crucial for abandoning unskillful qualities.
    *   **Concentration**: Developing "immeasurable concentration" based on goodwill, compassion, empathetic joy, or equanimity, brings five realizations, including release from "ill will". A concentrated mind is free from the defilements of awareness.
    *   **Perception of Dispassion**: Cultivating perceptions such as "dispassion" and "cessation" are "of great fruit, of great benefit," gaining "a footing in the deathless".

By implementing these practices, one moves away from the "side of decline" and towards a path of purity, wisdom, and unbinding.
