**Resolution**

The "Nine step-by-step dwellings" refer to a sequence of advanced meditative attainments, also known as "step-by-step cessations". Directly knowing these dwellings means gaining direct experience and insight into them, which leads to the ending of suffering and stress. These nine dwellings are:

*   **First Jhāna:** A state achieved by being secluded from sensuality and unskillful qualities. It is characterized by **rapture and pleasure**, accompanied by directed thought and evaluation.
*   **Second Jhāna:** With the stilling of directed thoughts and evaluations, one enters a state of **rapture and pleasure born of concentration**, marked by unification of awareness and internal assurance.
*   **Third Jhāna:** As rapture fades, the practitioner remains **equanimous, mindful, and alert**, experiencing pleasure with the body. This is a state praised by noble ones as a pleasant abiding.
*   **Fourth Jhāna:** By abandoning pleasure and pain, one enters a state of **purity of equanimity and mindfulness**, where there is neither pleasure nor pain.
*   **Dimension of the Infinitude of Space:** Upon attaining this, the **perception of forms ceases**.
*   **Dimension of the Infinitude of Consciousness:** When this dimension is attained, the **perception of the dimension of the infinitude of space ceases**.
*   **Dimension of Nothingness:** The **perception of the dimension of the infinitude of consciousness ceases** with the attainment of this dimension.
*   **Dimension of Neither Perception Nor Non-perception:** At this stage, the **perception of the dimension of nothingness ceases**.
*   **Cessation of Perception and Feeling:** This is the highest dwelling, where **perceptions and feelings cease**.

Directly knowing these step-by-step dwellings means experiencing them for oneself, which is crucial for realizing the ultimate goal of clear knowing and release (arahantship). This process is described as leading to the complete cessation of suffering and stress, signifying the ending of defilements and attachments.
