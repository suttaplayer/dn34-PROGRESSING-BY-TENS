Venerable Sāriputta, in the DASUTTARA SUTTA, highlights the development of six objects of recollection as crucial Dhamma practices. This framework provides a path for cultivating positive mental states and progressing towards ultimate liberation.

**Situation:**
The cultivation of these recollections is presented within the broader context of a spiritual urgency to overcome suffering and attain liberation. Individuals are subject to **future dangers**, necessitating heedful, ardent, and resolute effort to achieve what is yet unattained, unreached, or unrealized. The teachings also highlight the temporary nature of external support and the importance of internal development, implying a constant need to purify the mind and abandon unskillful qualities. Without these practices, the true Dhamma itself may not endure.

**Complication:**
Various obstacles and unskillful qualities can impede spiritual progress if these recollections are not developed or are misunderstood. Such complications include:
*   **Misrepresentation of teachings**: There is a risk of slandering the Tathāgata by explaining what was not said as said, or vice-versa. Slander and wrong views can lead to severe negative consequences, such as being "placed in hell".
*   **Unskillful thinking**: Thoughts imbued with sensuality, ill-will, or harmfulness can "produce blindness, produce lack of vision, produce lack of knowledge, lead to the cessation of discernment, side with vexation, and are not conducive to Unbinding".
*   **Imbalance in practice**: Solely attending to concentration might lead to laziness, while focusing only on uplifted energy might result in restlessness, and exclusive equanimity might hinder proper concentration for ending effluents. This emphasizes the need for a balanced approach that these diverse recollections can offer.
*   **Attachment and Fettering**: Desire-passion can act as a "fetter" related to the six sense media, binding beings to suffering.
*   **Stinginess**: Specifically, "stinginess as to the Dhamma" is described as the meanest form of stinginess, which can hinder one's capacity to alight on the orderliness and rightness of skillful qualities, even when listening to the true Dhamma.
*   **Lack of direct understanding**: Without proper understanding, one might be like a person seeking the heartwood of a tree but only gathering twigs and leaves, failing to achieve their purpose.

**Resolution:**
The development of these six objects of recollection systematically addresses these complications, leading to a profound transformation of mind and progress towards liberation. The general resolution common to all these recollections is:
*   **Mental Purification and Concentration**: When a noble disciple recollects any of these objects (the Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, virtue, generosity, or devas), their mind "is not overcome with passion, not overcome with aversion, not overcome with delusion. **His mind heads straight**, based on the Tathāgata [or the respective object]. And when the mind is headed straight, the disciple of the noble ones gains a sense of the goal, gains a sense of the Dhamma, gains joy connected with the Dhamma. In one who is joyful, rapture arises. In one whose mind is enraptured, the body grows calm. One whose body is calmed experiences ease. In one at ease, the mind becomes concentrated". This consistent process leads to a mind that is "pliant, malleable, luminous, & not brittle".

More specifically, the benefits of each recollection contribute to this overall path:
*   **Recollection of the Buddha**: This involves reflecting on the Tathāgata as "worthy & rightly self-awakened, consummate in clear-knowing & conduct, well-gone, an expert with regard to the cosmos, unexcelled trainer of people fit to be tamed, teacher of devas & human beings, awakened, blessed". This provides a powerful source of **conviction and inspiration**, grounding the practitioner in the ultimate attainment.
*   **Recollection of the Dhamma**: This entails remembering the Dhamma as "well taught by the Blessed One, to be seen here & now, timeless, inviting verification, pertinent, to be experienced by the observant for themselves". This cultivates a deep **understanding and appreciation for the path**, recognizing it as "unrefuted, undefiled, blameless, not faulted by knowledgeable contemplatives & brahmans". This practice directly supports the purification of beings, the overcoming of sorrow, and the realization of unbinding. The core insight of the Dhamma, "Whatever is subject to origination is all subject to cessation," becomes clear.
*   **Recollection of the Saṅgha**: This focuses on the community of the Blessed One's disciples as those who "have practiced well… practiced straight-forwardly… practiced methodically… practiced masterfully… the incomparable field of merit for the world". This strengthens **faith in the community** that upholds and transmits the Dhamma, fostering respect and a sense of shared purpose.
*   **Recollection of Virtue**: This involves reflecting on one's own virtues as "untorn, unbroken, unspotted, unsplattered, liberating, praised by the observant, ungrasped at, conducive to concentration". This reinforces a **foundation of moral conduct**, as "concentration nurtured with virtue is of great fruit, great reward". Virtue is also considered one of the seven noble treasures, signifying its intrinsic value for a well-lived life.
*   **Recollection of Generosity**: This cultivates awareness of generosity as being "freely generous, openhanded, delighting in being magnanimous, responsive to requests, delighting in the distribution of alms". This practice counters stinginess and fosters a mindset of **selflessness and compassion**, acknowledging that "what's given is well salvaged".
*   **Recollection of the Devas**: This involves contemplating the conviction, virtue, learning, generosity, and discernment that enabled devas to attain their heavenly states, and reflecting on these same qualities within oneself. This helps to align one's mind with **higher, more refined states of being** and encourages the cultivation of virtues observed in those who have achieved auspicious rebirths.

Ultimately, the development and cultivation of these six recollections contribute to the noble eightfold path, leading to the knowledge that "Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for the sake of this world".
