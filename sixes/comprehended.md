Venerable Sāriputta's statement in the DASUTTARA SUTTA outlines the six internal sense media as fundamental aspects of reality that should be **comprehended**. This comprehension goes beyond mere intellectual understanding, aiming for a deep realization that leads to the ending of passion, aversion, and delusion.

Here is an analysis of this statement in the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

### Situation

The six internal sense media are the **eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and intellect**. These are the foundational means through which an individual experiences and engages with the world. They are paired with their corresponding external sense media: forms, sounds, aromas, flavors, tactile sensations, and ideas. The interaction between an internal sense medium and its corresponding external medium gives rise to consciousness (e.g., eye-consciousness from eye and forms). The meeting of these three—internal sense medium, external sense medium, and consciousness—constitutes contact. With contact as a requisite condition, feeling arises, and with feeling as a requisite condition, craving arises. The Blessed One emphasizes that **all beings are maintained by nutriment and by fabrication**.

### Complication

Without proper comprehension of these six internal sense media, individuals become prone to various forms of suffering and entanglement. The **lack of discernment** and **inappropriate attention** are key factors leading to decline. When one **relishes, welcomes, and remains fastened to forms, sounds, aromas, flavors, tactile sensations, or ideas cognizable via the intellect**, delight arises, which leads to the origination of suffering and stress. This attachment forms the **"six classes of craving"** and contributes to the **"five clinging-aggregates"** (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness).

Furthermore, **sensual desire, ill will, sloth and drowsiness, restlessness and anxiety, and uncertainty are obstacles and hindrances that overwhelm awareness and weaken discernment**. Unskillful thinking imbued with sensuality, ill-will, or harmfulness produces blindness and leads to the cessation of discernment, siding with vexation and not conducive to unbinding. The world itself is described as "smothered & enveloped like a tangled skein, a knotted ball of string" by craving. This is because **whatever desire-passion exists with regard to these six bases (sense media) is a defilement of the mind and acts as a fetter or clinging**. Such lack of restraint means that **unskillful qualities like greed or distress might assail the individual**. The failure to abandon desire and passion for these five clinging-aggregates leads to the origination of stress.

Moreover, the **"conceit 'I am'"**, along with self-identification views, views, and uncertainty, can develop, preventing release. When individuals are immersed in ignorance and overcome with craving, they may cling to the idea of a 'self' being touched by actions done by what is not-self, perpetuating a cycle of suffering.

### Resolution

To comprehend the six internal sense media fully means to **discern their origination, cessation, allure, drawbacks, and the escape from them**. This understanding leads to dispassion and cessation. When one does not relish, welcome, or remain fastened to cognizable phenomena, delight ceases, leading to the cessation of suffering and stress.

The path to this comprehension involves:
*   **Mindfulness and alertness**: Being mindful, one observes how feelings, thoughts, and perceptions arise, persist, and subside. Mindfulness immersed in the body, developed and pursued, is of great fruit and benefit.
*   **Right discernment**: A concentrated monk discerns things as they have come to be, realizing that the eye, forms, consciousness at the eye, contact at the eye, and feelings arising from eye-contact are all **inconstant, stressful, and not-self**. This discernment allows one to abandon desire-passion for these phenomena.
*   **Restraint of the senses**: Not grasping at themes or details through the sense faculties prevents unskillful qualities like greed or distress from arising. When mindfulness is not lapsed, and one is not delighted or saddened, but remains equanimous, mindful, and alert upon seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching, or cognizing, one is said to be in the **presence of unbinding**. This leads to the **unexcelled development of the faculties** where equanimity takes a stance.
*   **Letting go and abandoning**: The ultimate goal is to **abandon craving**, leading to the cessation of kamma and stress. This involves abandoning the five hindrances, the three roots of what is unskillful (greed, aversion, delusion), and the ten forms of wrongness (wrong view, resolve, etc.).
*   **Developing skillful qualities**: Cultivating conviction, persistence, mindfulness, concentration, and discernment, along with appropriate attention, leads to a focused mind that can realize the ending of effluents.
*   **The Dhamma as the guide**: The Dhamma emphasizes what is conducive to disenchantment, dispassion, cessation, calming, direct knowledge, self-awakening, and unbinding. The practice involves developing the **four establishings of mindfulness** as the direct path to unbinding.

By comprehending the six internal sense media, their impermanent and stressful nature, and by actively abandoning attachment and cultivating skillful qualities, one progresses towards the total ending of suffering and stress, ultimately achieving release.
