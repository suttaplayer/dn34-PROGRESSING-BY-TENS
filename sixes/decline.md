In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents a comprehensive framework of Dhammas, including qualities that lead to decline. One such category describes six types of disrespect as "dhammas on the side of decline".

### Situation

The statement highlights six specific areas where a lack of respect or deference by a monk indicates a path of decline. These are:
*   **Disrespect for the Teacher**
*   **Disrespect for the Dhamma**
*   **Disrespect for the Saṅgha**
*   **Disrespect for the Training**
*   **Disrespect for Heedfulness**
*   **Disrespect for Welcoming Manners** (or hospitality)

These six types of disrespect are explicitly identified as "dhammas that are on the side of decline".

### Complication

The sources indicate that these types of disrespect lead to significant negative consequences, not only for the individual but also for the True Dhamma itself:

*   **Confusion and Disappearance of the True Dhamma**: Living "without respect, without deference, for the Teacher… for the Dhamma… for the Saṅgha… for the training… for concentration" are "five downward-leading qualities that tend to the confusion and disappearance of the true Dhamma". It is "worthless people who arise right here [within the Saṅgha] who make the true Dhamma disappear".
*   **Hindrance to Growth and Attainment**: Monks who are "unconcerned with the qualities of a contemplative, with no respect for the training" and are "luxurious, lax, foremost in falling back; who shirk the duties of solitude; who are lazy, lowly in their persistence, of muddled mindfulness, unalert, unconcentrated, their minds scattered, undiscerning, drivelers," are "incapable of attaining growth, increase, & abundance in this Dhamma-Vinaya".
*   **Cultivation of Unskillful Qualities and Disputes**: A monk who is "angry & spiteful," and "dwells without respect & without deference for the Teacher, for the Dhamma, for the Saṅgha, and does not fulfill the training," can give rise to disputes in the Saṅgha that are "for the harm & suffering of many". Disrespect for one's fellows in the holy life leads to "wasting away from the True Dhamma like a fish in next to no water".
*   **Inability to Gain Insight**: If a monk "does not listen well, does not give ear, does not apply his mind to gnosis, grabs hold of what is worthless, rejects what is worthwhile, and is not endowed with the patience [or: preference] to comply with the teaching" when the Dhamma is taught, they are "incapable of alighting on the lawfulness, the rightness of skillful qualities". This implies a lack of respect for the Dhamma and its teaching.

### Resolution

Conversely, cultivating respect and deference in these six areas leads to stability, spiritual growth, and the flourishing of the Dhamma:

*   **Stability and Non-Disappearance of the True Dhamma**: When monks, nuns, and lay followers live "with respect, with deference, for the Teacher… for the Dhamma… for the Saṅgha… for the training… for concentration," these are "five qualities that tend to the stability, the non-confusion, the non-disappearance of the true Dhamma". Such qualities lead to a monk being "incapable of decline" and "right in the presence of unbinding".
*   **Personal Growth and Attainment**: A monk "endowed with these eleven factors is capable of attaining growth, increase, & abundance in this Dhamma-Vinaya", which includes showing "extra respect for the elder monks with seniority". Respect for one's fellows in the holy life fosters growth "like an excellent seed in a field" and leads one "right in the presence of unbinding".
*   **Harmony and Fellowship**: Respect for welcoming manners "is a condition that is conducive to amiability, that engenders feelings of endearment, engenders feelings of respect, leading to a sense of fellowship, a lack of disputes, harmony, & a state of unity" among companions in the holy life.
*   **Avenues for Discernment**: Living "in apprenticeship to the Teacher or to a respectable companion in the holy life in whom he has established a strong sense of shame & compunction, love, & respect" is a primary cause for "acquiring of the as-yet-unacquired discernment that is basic to the holy life". This also includes "respect for the Dhamma".
*   **Qualities on the Side of Distinction**: The cultivation of "six types of respect"—for the Teacher, Dhamma, Saṅgha, training, heedfulness, and welcoming manners—are listed as "dhammas that are on the side of distinction".
