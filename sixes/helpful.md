**Situation**
Venerable Sāriputta, in the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), outlines a framework of Dhammas categorized by tens, among which he specifically highlights six conditions that are "very helpful" because they are "conducive to amiability". These conditions are said to "engender feelings of endearment, engender feelings of respect, leading to a sense of fellowship, a lack of disputes, harmony, & a state of unity" among companions in the holy life.

**Complication**
Without a clear understanding and diligent practice of these specific conditions, individuals within a community may struggle to foster genuine harmony and mutual respect. This lack of amiability can lead to disunity and disputes, potentially hindering the "stability, non-confusion, & non-disappearance of the True Dhamma". Such an environment would not be conducive to the collective welfare and happiness of both human and divine beings.

**Resolution**
The sources provide detailed explanations for each of these six conditions, offering a guide for cultivating the practices and perspectives that are essential for establishing a harmonious and unified community, thereby ensuring the long-term endurance of the holy life.

Here is a detailed analysis of the six conditions conducive to amiability:

*   **1. Bodily acts of goodwill**: This means a monk is **set on bodily acts of goodwill** toward their companions in the holy life, whether present or absent. This involves:
    *   **Abandoning the taking of life**, dwelling "with his rod laid down, his knife laid down, scrupulous, merciful, compassionate for the welfare of all living beings".
    *   **Abstaining from taking what is not given** (stealing), taking "only what is given, accepts only what is given, lives not by stealth but by means of a self that has become pure".
    *   **Abstaining from sensual misconduct**, meaning they do not engage in sexual involvement with those protected by their mothers, fathers, brothers, sisters, relatives, Dhamma, or who have husbands or entail punishments.

*   **2. Verbal acts of goodwill**: A monk expresses goodwill through their speech, consistently speaking kindly to companions, both in their presence and absence. This includes:
    *   **Abandoning divisive speech**, not repeating what they hear to cause separation, but instead speaking to **reconcile those who have broken apart or to cement those who are united**, loving, delighting in, and enjoying concord.
    *   **Abstaining from harsh speech**, choosing words that are **"soothing, pleasing to the ear, that are affectionate, that go to the heart, that are polite, appealing & pleasing to people at large"**.
    *   **Abandoning idle chatter**, speaking "in season, speaks what is based in fact, what is in accordance with the goal, the Dhamma, & the Vinaya" and speaking "words worth treasuring, timely, reasonable, circumscribed, connected with the goal". A well-spoken statement is characterized by being "spoken at the right time... in truth... affectionately... beneficially... with a mind of goodwill".

*   **3. Mental acts of goodwill**: This condition requires a monk to cultivate mental goodwill towards their companions, encompassing both their presence and absence. This means:
    *   **Not being covetous**, not desiring the belongings of others.
    *   **Not being malevolent**, wishing, for example, "May these beings be free from ill will, safe & sound, & free from trouble!".
    *   These skillful mental qualities are rooted in the **absence of greed, aversion, and delusion**, as greed, aversion, and delusion are identified as causes for the origination of unskillful actions.

*   **4. Sharing of righteous gains**: A monk is to **share in common with their virtuous companions** any gains obtained in accordance with the Dhamma, even if it is "up to & including the contents of his bowl". This reflects a **freely generous, openhanded** disposition, "delighting in being magnanimous, responsive to requests, delighting in the distribution of alms," and being "cleansed of the stain of stinginess". The act of giving is seen as a way to "salvage (your wealth) by giving," especially as the world is "on fire with aging & death".

*   **5. Consummation in virtue**: A monk demonstrates amiability by living **consistently with their companions in the holy life, in virtue**, both openly and privately. This means being "virtuous," dwelling "restrained in accordance with the Pāṭimokkha, consummate in his behavior & sphere of activity," and training oneself "seeing danger in the slightest faults". Such virtue is described as "untorn, unbroken, unspotted, unsplattered, liberating, praised by the observant, ungrasped at, conducive to concentration".

*   **6. Consummation in view**: This condition involves a monk dwelling with their **view in tune with that of their companions**, specifically the **noble view that leads to the right ending of suffering & stress**. This refers to **right view**, which is achieved when one "discerns what is unskillful, discerns the root of what is unskillful, discerns what is skillful, and discerns the root of what is skillful". The core of this understanding includes the **four noble truths**: "stress, the origination of stress, the cessation of stress, [and] the path of practice leading to the cessation of stress". Furthermore, a monk who is "consummate in his backing" will actively "make open what isn't open, make plain what isn't plain, [and] dispel doubt on its various doubtful points" regarding the Dhamma, contributing to shared understanding and unity in view.
