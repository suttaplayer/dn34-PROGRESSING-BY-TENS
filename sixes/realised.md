**Situation:**
Venerable Sāriputta, a foremost disciple of the Blessed One, delivered a comprehensive discourse known as the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), which systematically outlines various Dhamma teachings categorized by numerical sets. Within the "Sixes" section of this framework, Sāriputta enumerated "Six direct knowledges" as specific Dhammas that "should be realized". This teaching provides a clear path for practitioners aiming to put an end to suffering and stress, and attain unbinding.

**Complication:**
While the DASUTTARA SUTTA identifies these "Six direct knowledges" that should be realized, the specific passage detailing them in the provided sources offers an explicit description for the first (supranormal powers) and then describes the ultimate outcome of realizing all six (the ending of effluents), without providing detailed definitions for the intermediate four knowledges within that precise context. **Resolution:**
The "Six direct knowledges" (often known as *abhijñā* or *vijjā*) are profound insights and abilities attained through dedicated spiritual practice, culminating in release from all defilements. The Blessed One himself taught the Dhamma "through direct knowledge, not without direct knowledge", indicating the profound significance of these realizations. When monks develop the factors for awakening, their minds can be released from effluents, leading to the knowledge of release and the discernment that "Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done".

The six direct knowledges that a monk "should be realized" are detailed as follows:

*   **Wielding manifold supranormal powers (Iddhividha)**: This direct knowledge enables a monk to perform various extraordinary feats. These include **transforming from one into many or many into one, appearing and vanishing, passing unimpeded through solid objects like walls and mountains as if through space**, diving in and out of the earth as if it were water, walking on water without sinking, flying cross-legged through the air like a winged bird, and even physically touching the sun and moon. Such a practitioner can "exercise influence with his body even as far as the Brahmā worlds". This power demonstrates mastery over the physical realm.
*   **The Divine Ear-element (Dibbasota)**: While not explicitly defined as a "direct knowledge" to be *realized* within the specific "Sixes" section of DASUTTARA SUTTA, the sources mention "unsurpassed hearing" as one of the "six things that are unsurpassed". This implies the development of an extraordinary auditory faculty, allowing one to hear sounds both human and divine, far and near, that are beyond the range of ordinary hearing.
*   **Knowledge of others' minds (Paracittavijñāna)**: This knowledge involves the ability to discern the minds of other beings. It is described as "knowledge with regard to encompassing (the minds of others)" and is listed as one of the "four knowledges" that "should be made to arise", highlighting its status as a cultivated insight into the mental states of others.
*   **Recollection of past lives (Pubbenivāsanussati)**: Through this direct knowledge, a monk gains the ability to **recollect numerous past lives in intricate detail**. This includes remembering former names, clans, physical appearances, foods consumed, experiences of pleasure and pain, lifespans, and the conditions of passing away from one state and re-arising in another. This is explicitly referred to as the "clear knowing of the knowledge of previous lifetimes" that "should be realized", and "previous lives, to be realized through mindfulness".
*   **The Divine Eye (Cutūpapāta ñāṇa) – Knowledge of the passing away and re-appearance of beings**: This profound visual faculty, "purified and surpassing the human," allows the practitioner to **perceive beings as they pass away and re-appear in different realms**. One can discern their varied destinies—inferior or superior, beautiful or ugly, fortunate or unfortunate—all "in accordance with their kamma". This is identified as the "clear knowing of the knowledge of the passing away & arising of beings" that "should be realized", and "the falling away & reappearing of beings, to be realized through the [divine] eye".
*   **Knowledge of the ending of effluents (Āsavakkhaya ñāṇa)**: This is the ultimate and most crucial of the direct knowledges. It signifies the **complete eradication of all defilements**—passion, aversion, and delusion—that lead to renewed becoming and suffering. With this realization, the monk "enters & remains in the effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release, having directly known & realized them for himself right in the here-&-now". This is also stated as the "clear knowing of the knowledge of the ending of the effluents" that "should be realized", leading to "the immediate ending of effluents". The holy life taught by the Blessed One leads to this unbinding for the sake of "direct knowledge & full comprehension".

The attainment of these direct knowledges signifies that a monk is "fulfilled & fully accomplished in this Dhamma & Vinaya—the ultimate person", having achieved true liberation from suffering.
