Here is a detailed analysis of the statement from the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34) regarding the "six unsurpassed things" that should be directly known, presented in a Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation**

The Dhamma, as expounded by the Blessed One, includes specific categories of teachings that are intended to be directly known and realized for the attainment of unbinding and the end of suffering. Among these are "six things that are unsurpassed: unsurpassed vision, unsurpassed hearing, unsurpassed gains, unsurpassed trainings, unsurpassed service, and unsurpassed objects of recollection". These represent the highest forms of achievement and engagement within the holy life.

**Complication**

While these "unsurpassed" qualities are stated as fundamental for direct knowledge, their brief mention requires a deeper analysis to understand their full meaning and implications for practice. Without this detailed understanding, practitioners might fail to recognize the specific practices and realizations that constitute these unsurpassed states, potentially hindering their progress towards the cessation of suffering and the ultimate goal of the holy life. The challenge lies in clarifying what each "unsurpassed" category entails according to the Dhamma.

**Resolution**

The detailed meaning of these six unsurpassed things encompasses core Dhamma teachings, practices, and their fruits, which are all interconnected and lead to the purification and release of beings.

1.  **Unsurpassed Vision**: This refers to the **right discernment and direct knowledge** of reality as it truly is, which is crucial for self-awakening and unbinding. It involves the ability to see things as "deep, hard to see, hard to realize, tranquil, refined, beyond the scope of conjecture, subtle, to-be-experienced by the wise". This vision allows one to discern the possible from the impossible, the nature and results of actions (kamma), and ultimately the Four Noble Truths. The Tathāgata, being "the Eye" and "Knowledge," reveals and explains these truths, making them plain. This leads to the cessation of suffering and stress by understanding delight as the root of suffering.

2.  **Unsurpassed Hearing**: This involves **diligent engagement with the True Dhamma**. It means listening attentively, retaining what has been heard, discussing it with others, and examining it with one's mind. The teachings should be regarded as worth grasping and mastering, and one should be prepared to cross-question and dissect them to dispel any doubts and make what is not open, plain. The "rewards in listening to the Dhamma" include hearing what was not heard, clarifying what was heard, getting rid of doubt, straightening one's views, and serene mind.

3.  **Unsurpassed Gains**: These are the **profound spiritual benefits and attainments** resulting from the practice of the Dhamma. These gains include achieving direct knowledge (gnosis) in the here-and-now and reaching non-return, or ultimately, total unbinding. This includes the fruit of stream-entry, which ensures certainty in the true Dhamma and freedom from falling back to lower realms. The Dhamma's aim is also for the "benefit, welfare, & happiness of the multitude".

4.  **Unsurpassed Trainings**: This refers to the **cultivation of the holy life** through heightened virtue, concentration, and discernment, which are unexcelled qualities attained by the Awakened One. It involves developing the five faculties of conviction, persistence, mindfulness, concentration, and discernment. This training includes "abandoning unskillful qualities and taking on skillful qualities," being steadfast and solid in effort. The core of this training is the Noble Eightfold Path and the four establishings of mindfulness, which are a "direct path for the purification of beings".

5.  **Unsurpassed Service**: This refers to the **profound impact of the Tathāgata's presence and teachings**, which offers liberation and clarity to beings. It includes the service of teaching the Dhamma "admirable in its beginning, admirable in its middle, admirable in its end" and proclaiming a "holy life both in its particulars & in its essence, entirely perfect, surpassingly pure". Disciples who practice according to this well-proclaimed Dhamma are said to produce much merit. It also involves rendering peaceful service to one's fellows in the holy life.

6.  **Unsurpassed Objects of Recollection**: These are the **fundamental subjects for sustained mindfulness and confidence** that support a practitioner's path. These include the **recollection of the Buddha, the Dhamma, and the Saṅgha**. Recollecting these leads to a mind not overcome by passion, aversion, or delusion, generating joy, rapture, calm, and concentration. One's own virtues and generosity can also be objects of recollection, further aiding concentration.

In essence, these six unsurpassed qualities highlight the comprehensive and transformative nature of the Dhamma, from the intellectual understanding (vision, hearing) and the practices (trainings, recollection) to the tangible results (gains, service) that lead to the complete cessation of suffering and stress. This knowledge is essential for cultivating the path to unbinding.
