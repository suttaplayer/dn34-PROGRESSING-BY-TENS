In the *DASUTTARA SUTTA* (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents a framework for various aspects of Dhamma, including qualities that should be abandoned. One such directive is that the **six classes of craving**—craving for forms, sounds, aromas, flavors, tactile sensations, and ideas—should be abandoned.

Here is a detailed analysis of this statement, presented in a Situation, Complication, and Resolution (SCR) format:

### Situation

The **situation** describes the pervasive nature of craving for the six classes of sensory objects in the world and its fundamental role in perpetuating suffering and existence.

*   The Buddha teaches that **craving** is "the ensnarer that has flowed along, spread out, and caught hold, with which this world is smothered & enveloped like a tangled skein, a knotted ball of string, like matted rushes and reeds, and does not go beyond transmigration, beyond the planes of deprivation, woe, & bad destinations". This craving manifests in various forms, including the six classes: craving for forms, sounds, aromas, flavors, tactile sensations, and ideas.
*   This desire for sensory objects arises as a **requisite condition from feeling**.
*   Craving is metaphorically described as the "seamstress" because "craving stitches one to the production of this or that very becoming", indicating its role in binding beings to the cycle of existence.
*   Beings often find themselves "bound with the bondage of craving," their minds "smitten with becoming & non-", illustrating their deep entanglement in this cycle.

### Complication

The **complication** highlights the detrimental effects and inherent drawbacks of clinging to these six classes of craving, emphasizing why their abandonment is necessary for liberation.

*   **Sensual desire** is identified as a significant "obstacle, a hindrance that overwhelms awareness and weakens discernment".
*   **Sensuality** itself is described as "inconstant, hollow, vain, deceptive," and as "Māra's realm" that leads to "evil, unskillful mental states: greed, ill will, & contentiousness," which actively obstruct a disciple's training.
*   The Blessed One has consistently stated that **sensual pleasures are of little satisfaction, much stress, much despair, & greater drawbacks**.
*   Any **desire-passion** for sensory objects (forms, sounds, aromas, flavors, tactile sensations, ideas) and their corresponding sense faculties (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, intellect) is considered a **defilement of the mind**.
*   If a monk "relishes them, welcomes them, and remains fastened to them," then "delight" arises in him, and "From the origination of delight… comes the origination of suffering & stress".
*   Those who are "attached to sensual pleasures, attached to sensual ties," and "seeing no blame in the fetter," will "never… cross over the flood so great & wide".
*   At its root, **greed**, which fuels craving, is identified as a primary "cause for the origination of actions" and an "inside stain, inside enemy, inside foe, inside murderer, inside adversary".

### Resolution

The **resolution** outlines the path and practices necessary for abandoning these six classes of craving, leading to release from suffering and the attainment of unbinding (Nibbāna).

*   The path to the **cessation of craving**—and thus the abandonment of these six classes—is primarily the **noble eightfold path**: right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration. This practice leads to the cessation of craving that arises from feeling, contact, the six sense media, name-&-form, consciousness, fabrication, and ignorance.
*   When a monk "does not relish them, welcome them, or remain fastened to them," no delight arises, and "From the cessation of delight… comes the cessation of suffering & stress".
*   The abandonment of "defilements of awareness" related to the six senses and their objects leads the mind to be **inclined to renunciation** and "malleable for the direct knowing of those qualities worth realizing".
*   One who is "undesiring, untroubled, at peace" and has "fathomed the high & low in the world" is said to have "crossed over birth & aging".
*   A monk who has **cut through craving** and "ripped off the fetter" by "rightly breaking through conceit" puts "an end to suffering & stress." This state is achieved by eliminating "I-making or my-making conceit-obsession" concerning the body or external themes, and dwelling in "awareness-release & discernment-release".
*   Developing "mindfulness always established, continually immersed in the body," accompanied by the reflection "'It should not be, it should not be mine; it will not be, it will not be mine'," enables one to "cross over attachment" swiftly.
*   The **seven factors for awakening**—mindfulness, analysis of qualities, persistence, rapture, calm, concentration, and equanimity—are crucial. When these factors are developed and pursued, they lead to the "ending of craving".
*   Ultimately, the cessation of craving leads to the abandoning of "action [kamma]," and "From the abandoning of action, stress is abandoned".
