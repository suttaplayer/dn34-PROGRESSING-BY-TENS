In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents a comprehensive framework of Dhammas, categorizing them by numerical groups for deeper understanding. Within this framework, he highlights specific qualities that are conducive to progress and liberation.

**Situation**

The statement in question identifies **five faculties** as being "on the side of distinction": **the faculty of conviction, the faculty of persistence, the faculty of mindfulness, the faculty of concentration, and the faculty of discernment**. These faculties represent fundamental strengths for a monk in training. Their profound importance is underscored by the fact that a disciple of the noble ones who truly understands their **origination, passing away, allure, drawbacks, and the escape** from them, is considered to have attained the stream of liberation, assuring their path towards self-awakening. Indeed, the Blessed One himself asserted that he did not claim to have directly awakened to unexcelled right self-awakening until he had **direct knowledge of these five faculties** in this comprehensive manner. A monk who has fully developed and pursued these faculties is able to affirm the ending of their effluents.

**Complication**

The "distinction" inherent in these faculties becomes clearer when contrasted with states that signify spiritual decline or obstruction. The absence of these qualities is associated with a lack of spiritual progress, as indicated by the description of "untrue dhammas." For instance, a monk described as "without conviction, without a sense of shame, without compunction, without learning, lazy, one of muddled truth, & undiscerning" embodies traits that are explicitly stated to be "on the side of decline".

Furthermore, the path to cultivating these distinguishing faculties is fraught with various obstacles. The sources identify **five mental blockages** that can prevent the mind from tending towards "ardency, commitment, perseverance, or exertion," which are crucial for spiritual development. These blockages include "doubtful, uncertain, indecisive about the Teacher," and a lack of confidence in the Dhamma, the Saṅgha, or the training.

Most notably, the **five hindrances** are described as qualities that "overwhelm awareness and weaken discernment". These hindrances—**sensual desire, ill will, sloth & drowsiness, restlessness & anxiety, and uncertainty**—make it **impossible** for an individual to understand what is for their own benefit, the benefit of others, or to realize any "superior human state" or "truly noble distinction in knowledge & vision". Without abandoning these hindrances, one is also **incapable of entering and remaining in higher meditative states (jhānas)**, or realizing the ultimate fruits of the holy life, such as stream-entry, once-returning, non-returning, or arahantship.

**Resolution**

The diligent cultivation of these five faculties serves as the path to profound spiritual distinction and the ultimate cessation of suffering:

*   **Faculty of Conviction (Saddhā):** This is characterized by **unwavering certainty in the Tathāgata's awakening**. Conviction acts as a "foundation post" for a noble disciple, enabling them to abandon unskillful qualities and diligently develop skillful ones. When a disciple recollects the Buddha, Dhamma, or Saṅgha with conviction, their mind is not overcome by passion, aversion, or delusion, but instead becomes straight, leading to a sense of the goal, joy, rapture, calm, and ultimately **concentration**. For lay practitioners, conviction also brings tangible rewards, such as compassion from virtuous individuals and a favorable rebirth in heavenly realms.
*   **Faculty of Persistence (Viriya):** This faculty manifests as **aroused, steadfast, and solid effort** in abandoning unskillful qualities and embracing skillful ones, without shirking one's duties. It is a crucial aspect of astuteness in practice.
*   **Faculty of Mindfulness (Sati):** Mindfulness signifies an **established and unmuddled awareness**, allowing one to remember and recall even things done and said long ago. When mindfulness, especially mindfulness of breathing, is developed and pursued with supporting qualities, it leads to penetrating release from effluents relatively quickly.
*   **Faculty of Concentration (Samādhi):** Concentration refers to the unification of awareness, which, when developed, leads to various benefits, including **a pleasant abiding in the present moment** and ultimately the **ending of effluents**. This faculty is foundational for entering and remaining in the four jhānas, states of profound mental absorption and purity.
*   **Faculty of Discernment (Paññā):** This is described as the "surpassing state" because it enables a practitioner to **scrutinize and rightly understand the Dhamma for the ending of suffering and stress**. It is the **penetrating wisdom** that discerns the arising and passing away of phenomena, leading to the cessation of stress. Discernment is also critical for understanding what is skillful and unskillful, thus aligning one's view with the true Dhamma.

The profound significance of these five faculties is that their combined development and pursuit are essential for gaining a **"footing in the deathless"** and achieving the "deathless as its final end & consummation". When these faculties are cultivated, individuals transcend doubt and uncertainty, moving towards complete liberation.
