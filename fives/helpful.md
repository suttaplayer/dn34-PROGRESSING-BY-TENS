The statement "Which five dhammas are very helpful? Five factors for exertion" refers to five qualities that are highly beneficial for those engaged in spiritual practice. This statement is from the DASUTTARA SUTTA, where Venerable Sāriputta outlines various aspects of the Dhamma, progressing by categories of ten.

Here is an analysis of the detailed meaning of this statement in a "Situation, Complication, Resolution" format:

**Situation**

Beings in the world are constantly in a state of flux and impermanence, living in a realm where "All fabrications are inconstant" and "All fabrications are stressful". The world is described as being "swept away" and not enduring. It is also characterized as being "without shelter, without protector," and "without ownership," requiring one to eventually leave everything behind. Furthermore, the world is "insufficient, insatiable, a slave to craving". This general state highlights a fundamental need for guidance and practices that can lead to an escape from suffering.

**Complication**

Despite the availability of teachings, individuals face numerous internal and external challenges that hinder their spiritual progress. One significant complication is the presence of "five obstacles, hindrances that overwhelm awareness and weaken discernment". These include sensual desire, ill will, sloth & drowsiness, restlessness & anxiety, and uncertainty. When these hindrances are present and unabandoned, it becomes "impossible" for a monk "to understand what is for his own benefit, to understand what is for the benefit of others, to understand what is for the benefit of both, to realize a superior human state, a truly noble distinction in knowledge & vision". Furthermore, a practice can be "fruitless" if one's "unskillful mental qualities increase while one's skillful mental qualities decline". Unskillful qualities stem from roots such as greed, aversion, and delusion, which are causes for the origination of actions. This inherent susceptibility to unskillful states and hindrances makes a coherent, effective path to liberation challenging to pursue without specific, helpful qualities.

**Resolution**

The five factors for exertion are presented as qualities that are "very helpful" in navigating these complications and progressing towards the goal of ending suffering and stress. These factors facilitate the development of skillful qualities and the abandonment of unskillful ones, leading to freedom and genuine insight.

The five factors are:

*   **Strength of Conviction:** This is described as a monk having conviction and being "convinced of the Tathāgata's awakening: 'Indeed, the Blessed One is worthy & rightly self-awakened, consummate in clear-knowing & conduct, well-gone, an expert with regard to the cosmos, unexcelled trainer of people fit to be tamed, teacher of devas & human beings, awakened, blessed'". This conviction serves as a foundational quality, allowing one to approach the Teacher and the Dhamma with trust. It is regarded as "most helpful for visiting" a teacher and leads to hearing, remembering, penetrating the meaning, and understanding the Dhamma. A person of conviction gains five rewards, including compassion from good people, being visited by them, having their gifts accepted, being taught the Dhamma, and arising in a good destination after death. The Buddha himself is the "Eye," "Knowledge," "Dhamma," "Brahmā," and the "speaker, the proclaimer, the elucidator of meaning, the giver of the deathless, the lord of the Dhamma, the Tathāgata", providing a solid basis for this conviction.

*   **Physical Well-being and Fitness for Exertion:** This factor entails being "free from illness & discomfort, endowed with good digestion—not too cold, not too hot, of moderate strength—fit for exertion". This quality underscores the practical necessity of a healthy body for sustained effort in spiritual practice, ensuring that one's "persistence will be aroused & not lax".

*   **Honesty and Self-Declaration:** This factor is characterized by being "neither fraudulent nor deceitful," and willing to "declare himself to the Teacher or to his observant companions in the holy life in line with what he actually is". This relates to the quality of a "person of integrity," who, "when asked, doesn't reveal his own good points, to say nothing of when unasked". Such openness and truthfulness about one's spiritual state are crucial for receiving accurate guidance and fostering genuine self-awareness.

*   **Aroused Persistence:** This means keeping one's "persistence aroused for abandoning unskillful qualities & taking on skillful qualities," being "steadfast, solid in his effort, not shirking his duties with regard to skillful qualities". This active and sustained effort directly combats laziness and leads to the "perception of abandoning" unskillful states. "Right effort" is a component of the noble eightfold path, reinforcing its importance in the journey towards liberation.

*   **Penetrating Discernment:** This factor signifies being "discerning, endowed with discernment leading to the arising of the goal—noble, penetrating, leading to the right ending of stress". Discernment is vital for understanding the Dhamma. It leads to "clear knowing & release", and when "nurtured with concentration," it yields "great fruit, great reward," resulting in a mind "rightly released from the effluents, i e, the effluent of sensuality, the effluent of becoming, the effluent of ignorance". A person "of poor discernment" is one who does not discern stress, its origination, cessation, or the path leading to its cessation. Through discerning "what has come to be," one develops disenchantment, dispassion, and eventually release. Discernment is also the means by which one can see "dependent co-arising" and thereby "sees the Dhamma".

By cultivating these five factors for exertion, an individual is better equipped to overcome obstacles, sustain their practice, and progress on the path to the ending of suffering and stress.
