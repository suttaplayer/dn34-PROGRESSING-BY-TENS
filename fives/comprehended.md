Here is an analysis of the statement from the DASUTTARA SUTTA regarding the comprehension of the five clinging-aggregates, presented in the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation**
Venerable Sāriputta's framework of Dhammas, progressing by tens in the `DASUTTARA SUTTA`, highlights the **five clinging-aggregates** (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness) as essential "dhammas" that **should be comprehended**. These aggregates encompass all phenomena that can be subject to clinging. The Buddha states that "the five clinging-aggregates" constitute "the burden," and "the carrier of the burden is the person". The act of clinging is rooted in "whatever desire & passion there is with regard to the five clinging-aggregates".

**Complication**
The failure to adequately comprehend these five clinging-aggregates is a significant source of suffering. An "uninstructed, run-of-the-mill person" who lacks regard for noble ones and is not disciplined in their Dhamma, may mistakenly assume that form, feeling, perception, fabrications, or consciousness are the self, or belong to the self, or are within the self, or that the self is within them. When these aggregates then change or alter, such a person experiences **sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, and despair**. If one's consciousness is not "unagitated" due to a lack of clinging, it becomes a "seed for the conditions of future birth, aging, death, or stress". This continued clinging, even to views perceived as "pure" or "bright," signifies a misunderstanding of the Dhamma, which is taught as "analogous to a raft, for crossing over, not for holding on to". Such a state perpetuates transmigration, as the world becomes "smothered & enveloped like a tangled skein, a knotted ball of string" by craving. When the mind is "overcome with passion, not overcome with aversion, not overcome with delusion" (i.e. passion, aversion, and delusion are present), it indicates that "desire-passion for the five strings of sensuality has not been abandoned".

**Resolution**
To truly comprehend the five clinging-aggregates means to understand their **origination, passing away, allure, drawbacks, and the escape from them**. This comprehension is achieved by attending to these aggregates as **inconstant, stressful, a disease, a cancer, an arrow, painful, an affliction, alien, a dissolution, an emptiness, and not-self**. For a monk who practices the Dhamma in line with the Dhamma, what accords with the Dhamma is to "keep cultivating disenchantment with regard to form... feeling... perception... fabrications... consciousness". This approach is described as the "direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding".

The core of this comprehension involves the **abandoning of desire-passion** with regard to these aggregates. This abandonment is crucial because "any desire, embracing, grasping, & holding-on to these five clinging-aggregates is the origination of stress. Any subduing of desire & passion, any abandoning of desire & passion for these five clinging-aggregates is the cessation of stress". In fact, "any ending of passion, ending of aversion, ending of delusion" is itself called comprehension. For those who know and see in this way, there is the **immediate ending of effluents**. Ultimately, those who "have confidence in the dhamma of dispassion have confidence in what is supreme; and for those with confidence in the supreme, supreme is the result". By directly knowing the meaning and Dhamma of these questions, one "would go to the far shore of birth & death".
