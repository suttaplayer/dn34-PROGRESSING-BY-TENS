Here is an analysis of the detailed meaning of the statement from DASUTTARA SUTTA Dīgha Nikāya 34, presented in the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation**

The DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34) presents a structured framework of Dhammas, categorized by numerical progression, including those that "should be realized". Among these, Venerable Sāriputta states that "Five dhamma aggregates: the aggregate of virtue, the aggregate of concentration, the aggregate of discernment, the aggregate of release, the aggregate of knowledge & vision of release" should be realized. This statement points to a progressive path of spiritual development within the holy life.

**Complication**

While the statement lists the five "dhamma aggregates" that require realization, its brevity necessitates a deeper analysis to understand the detailed meaning of each aggregate, their interconnectedness, and why their "realization" is crucial. Realizing these aggregates involves more than intellectual understanding; it implies a profound, direct experience and embodiment of these qualities, leading to the ultimate goal of liberation from suffering. Without further elucidation, the full significance of these aggregates and the transformative process of their realization remain unclear.

**Resolution**

The realization of these five dhamma aggregates constitutes the core progression of the holy life, leading to liberation from suffering and stress. The path is characterized by heedfulness, ardor, and resolute practice, ultimately culminating in the ending of effluents and the cessation of suffering. The Buddha's teachings are consistently aimed at "the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding".

Here is a detailed analysis of each aggregate:

*   **1. The Aggregate of Virtue (Sīla)**
    *   **Meaning:** Virtue forms the essential foundation of the holy life. It involves ethical conduct, such as abstaining from taking life, stealing, sexual misconduct, and false speech. Virtues are described as "untorn, unbroken, unspotted, unsplattered, liberating, praised by the observant, ungrasped at, conducive to concentration".
    *   **Realization:** Realizing virtue means establishing oneself firmly in these ethical principles, purifying one's actions, and living in a way that fosters inner purity and freedom from blameworthiness. This provides a stable base for further spiritual development.

*   **2. The Aggregate of Concentration (Samādhi)**
    *   **Meaning:** Concentration refers to heightened mental states where the mind is unified and focused, often achieved through practices like the four jhānas. The sources describe four developments of concentration: leading to a pleasant abiding, to the attainment of knowledge & vision, to mindfulness & alertness, and to the ending of the effluents. When developed, the mind becomes "pliant, malleable, luminous, & not brittle," and "rightly concentrated for the ending of the effluents".
    *   **Realization:** Realizing concentration involves developing a deeply stable and collected mind, free from the distractions of the five hindrances—sensual desire, ill will, sloth & drowsiness, restlessness & anxiety, and uncertainty—which "overwhelm awareness and weaken discernment". This focused state provides the necessary mental clarity for deeper insight.

*   **3. The Aggregate of Discernment (Paññā)**
    *   **Meaning:** Discernment is the penetrating wisdom that sees things "as they have come to be". It involves understanding unskillful and skillful qualities and their roots (greed, aversion, delusion, and their absence). A monk endowed with discernment is "noble, penetrating, leading to the right ending of stress". This discernment is crucial for recognizing fundamental truths like "All fabrications are inconstant" and "All phenomena are not-self".
    *   **Realization:** Realizing discernment means developing a profound understanding of stress, its origination, its cessation, and the path leading to its cessation—the Four Noble Truths. Through this deep seeing, one grows "disenchanted with stress," which is "the path to purity". Discernment is considered the "surpassing state" because it scrutinizes the Dhamma for the right ending of suffering and stress.

*   **4. The Aggregate of Release (Vimutti)**
    *   **Meaning:** Release signifies liberation from suffering and the cycle of rebirth (becoming). It is the experience of unbinding (*nibbuti*) right within, characterized by a lack of clinging or sustenance. This release comes from "the ending of craving" and the abandonment of "all passion".
    *   **Realization:** Realizing release means attaining a state where one is "totally unbound, never again to return from that world". It is the "unexcelled safety from bonds", where the "unreleased mind… becomes released, or his unended effluents go to their total ending".

*   **5. The Aggregate of Knowledge & Vision of Release (Vimutti-ñāṇadassana)**
    *   **Meaning:** This is the ultimate culmination of the holy life, signifying the direct knowledge and complete insight into one's own liberation. It is the certainty and understanding that "Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world". This aggregate encompasses "knowledge with regard to ending (of effluents) & knowledge of (their) non-recurrence".
    *   **Realization:** The realization of this aggregate means not only being released but also having the direct knowledge and vision of that release. It is a state where the practitioner "enters & remains in the effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release, having directly known & realized them for himself right in the here & now". The entire path, from associating with people of integrity to developing the factors for awakening, ultimately fills the conditions for this "clear knowing & release".

In essence, these five aggregates represent a sequential and interdependent process: virtue lays the groundwork for concentration, which in turn enables deep discernment. This discernment leads to release from all defilements, culminating in the complete and direct knowledge that one is, in fact, truly liberated. As stated, "Concentration nurtured with virtue is of great fruit, great reward. Discernment nurtured with concentration is of great fruit, great reward. The mind nurtured with discernment is rightly released from the effluents". The realization of these aggregates thus charts the complete journey towards unbinding.
