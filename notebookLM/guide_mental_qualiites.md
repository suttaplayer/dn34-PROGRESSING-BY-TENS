### Mental Qualities (Skillful & Unskillful): An Identifier's Guide

This guide catalogs and defines the myriad mental states, intentions, and qualities discussed in the Dhamma, clarifying their characteristics and the conditions for their arising or cessation. Understanding these mental qualities is fundamental to discerning the path to liberation described in the sources.

---

#### 1. Unskillful Mental Qualities (Akusala Dhamma)

These are mental states and intentions that are described as leading to harm, suffering, or impeding progress on the path.

*   **Mind (when undeveloped/untamed/unguarded/unprotected/unrestrained):**
    *   **Unpliant and leads to great harm**.
    *   **Sullied nature** prevents knowing one's own or others' benefit and realizing superior knowledge & vision.
    *   **Defiled by incoming defilements**, and an uninstructed person does not discern this, leading to no development of the mind.
    *   **Not released** when defiled by passion or aversion.
    *   Can be **consumed** by various unskillful qualities.
    *   If **sluggish**, it's hard to raise up with calm, concentration, or equanimity.
    *   If **restless**, it's hard to still with analysis of qualities, persistence, or rapture.

*   **Greed (Lobha) / Passion (Rāga) / Sensual Desire (Kāmacchanda) / Craving (Taṇhā):**
    *   **Causes for the origination of actions**.
    *   **"Roots of what is unskillful"**.
    *   **Carries little blame but is slow to fade**.
    *   When an action is fashioned by greed, it is **unskillful, blameworthy, and ripens in pain**, leading to the origination of further action and not cessation.
    *   An **"effluent"** (specifically sensual desire, becoming, ignorance) that overwhelms awareness and weakens discernment.
    *   An **"obstacle"** or **"hindrance"** that overwhelms awareness and weakens discernment, making understanding and realization impossible.
    *   An **"impediment"** and one of the **"three fires"**.
    *   Causes **pain and distress** when one is of an intensely passionate nature.
    *   Makes the mind **"not released"** and prevents discernment from developing.
    *   If not abandoned, leads to **sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, and despair** from any change in the object of passion.
    *   **Subjugates the mind**.
    *   **"Ensnarer"** that has flowed along, spread out, caught hold, and envelops the world, preventing escape from transmigration and bad destinations.
    *   Manifests as **18 craving-verbalizations dependent on what is internal** ("I am," "I am here," "I am like this," "I am otherwise," "I am bad," "I am good," "I might be," "I might be here," "I might be like this," "I might be otherwise," "May I be," "May I be here," "May I be like this," "May I be otherwise," "I will be," "I will be here," "I will be like this," "I will be otherwise").
    *   Manifests as **18 craving-verbalizations dependent on what is external** ("I am because of this," "I am here because of this," etc.).
    *   **"Seamstress"** that stitches one to the production of becoming.
    *   The **origination of the four nutriments** (physical food, contact, intellectual intention, consciousness).
    *   Is **inconstant, changeable, and alterable**.
    *   Is the **root and cause of stress**.

*   **Aversion (Dosa) / Ill Will (Vyāpāda):**
    *   **Causes for the origination of actions**.
    *   **"Roots of what is unskillful"**.
    *   **Carries great blame and is quick to fade**.
    *   When an action is fashioned by aversion, it is **unskillful, blameworthy, and ripens in pain**, leading to the origination of further action and not cessation.
    *   An **"obstacle"** or **"hindrance"** that overwhelms awareness and weakens discernment.
    *   An **"impediment"** and one of the **"three fires"**.
    *   Causes **pain and distress** when one is of an intensely aversive nature.
    *   Makes the mind **"not released"**.
    *   **Impossible for ill will to overpower the mind when goodwill is developed**.
    *   **Subjugates the mind**.
    *   A monk who **returns anger or quarrels** is said to "push away".

*   **Delusion (Moha) / Ignorance (Avijjā) / Uncertainty (Vicikicchā):**
    *   **Causes for the origination of actions**.
    *   **"Roots of what is unskillful"**.
    *   **Carries great blame and is slow to fade**.
    *   When an action is fashioned by delusion, it is **unskillful, blameworthy, and ripens in pain**, leading to the origination of further action and not cessation.
    *   An **"effluent"**.
    *   An **"impediment"** and one of the **"three fires"**.
    *   Causes **pain and distress** when one is of an intensely deluded nature.
    *   Prevents discernment from developing and makes the mind **"not released"**.
    *   The **ignorance with which the fool is obstructed** and the craving with which he is conjoined, results in a new body and continued suffering.
    *   **Ignorance rising causes fabrications to rise**.
    *   **Ignorance and craving for becoming** are qualities to be abandoned through direct knowledge.
    *   One of the **"five hindrances"** (uncertainty) that overwhelm awareness and weakens discernment.
    *   Can cause **concentration to fall away** and lead to the disappearance of light and vision.
    *   Being **dull & exceedingly stupid** leads to verbal contortions.

*   **Conceit (Māna):**
    *   "I-making" or "mine-making" conceit-obsession.
    *   **Rightly breaking through conceit** puts an end to suffering & stress.
    *   Related to **restlessness** when one thinks of one's psychic powers.

*   **Restlessness (Uddhacca) & Anxiety (Kukkucca):**
    *   A **"hindrance"** that overwhelms awareness and weakens discernment.
    *   **Over-aroused persistence leads to restlessness**.
    *   The mind tends to **restlessness** if one solely attends to the theme of uplifted energy.
    *   Anxiety is related to the mind not being released from effluents.
    *   A **restless mind is hard to still** with analysis of qualities, persistence, or rapture.

*   **Sloth & Drowsiness (Thīna-middha):**
    *   A **"hindrance"** that overwhelms awareness and weakens discernment.
    *   **Overly slack persistence leads to laziness**.
    *   The mind tends to **laziness** if one solely attends to the theme of concentration.
    *   The mind can be **overwhelmed with sloth & torpor**.
    *   A **sluggish mind is hard to raise up** with calm, concentration, or equanimity.

*   **Covetousness (Abhijjā):**
    *   A monk should examine if he usually remains covetous, and if so, put forth extra effort to abandon it.
    *   One of the three sorts of **un-Dhamma conduct** with the mind.
    *   **Subjugates the mind**.

*   **Stinginess (Macchariya):**
    *   An **"impurity"**; if not abandoned, one may be placed in hell.
    *   Without abandoning **stinginess as to one's monastery, family, gains, status, or Dhamma**, one is incapable of entering or remaining in the first jhāna, or realizing higher fruits like stream-entry or arahantship.

*   **Envy (Issā):**
    *   An **"impurity"**; if not abandoned, one may be placed in hell.

*   **Anger (Krodha):**
    *   A person unfit to talk with will **show anger & aversion and sulk**.
    *   A monk should examine if he usually remains angry, and if so, put forth extra effort to abandon it.
    *   To be **abandoned** by contemplatives.

*   **Ill Will (Vyāpāda):** (See Aversion).

*   **Soiled Thoughts:** A monk should examine if he has soiled thoughts, and if so, exert effort to abandon them.

*   **Laziness (Kusīta):** One of the six qualities that leads to the **decline of a monk in training**. Also one of the six qualities making a monk **incapable of attaining arahantship**.

*   **Lack of Conviction (Assaddhiya):** One of the six qualities making a monk **incapable of attaining arahantship**.

*   **Shameless (Ahirika):** One of the six qualities making a monk **incapable of attaining arahantship**.

*   **Without Compunction (Anottappa):** One of the six qualities making a monk **incapable of attaining arahantship**.

*   **Undiscerning (Appañña):** One of the six qualities making a monk **incapable of attaining arahantship**.

*   **Wrong View (Micchā Diṭṭhi):**
    *   Leads to **harm & suffering**.
    *   To be **abandoned**.
    *   Defined as believing "there is nothing given, nothing offered, nothing sacrificed; no fruit or result of good or bad actions; no this world, no next world," etc..
    *   A person of no integrity is characterized by mental misconduct which includes wrong view.
    *   Purged away by right view.
    *   One of the three actions for **making evil kamma**.

*   **Fraudulent / Deceitful:**
    *   A quality that hinders a monk's ability to train. A monk should be neither fraudulent nor deceitful.

*   **Impurity / Defilements (Mala / Saṅkilesa):**
    *   Stinginess and envy are impurities that, if not abandoned, lead to hell.
    *   The luminous mind is defiled by **"incoming defilements"**.
    *   A **"defiled mind"** is neither pliant nor malleable nor luminous, but brittle and not rightly concentrated.
    *   The deeds of one who lives slovenly **lead him to a bad destination**.
    *   **Doubt** is a defilement of the mind.
    *   **Inattention, sloth-&-drowsiness, panic, excitement, boredom, excess persistence, slack persistence, a perception of multiplicity, excessive absorption in forms** are also defilements of the mind.
    *   **Desire-passion** with regard to the six sense bases (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, intellect; and forms, sounds, aromas, flavors, tactile sensations, ideas; and eye-consciousness, etc.; and contact, etc.; and perception, etc.; and intentions, etc.; and properties, etc.; and aggregates, etc.) is a **defilement of the mind**.

*   **Mental Blockages (Nīvaraṇa):**
    *   **Doubtful, uncertain, indecisive about the Teacher and not confident in him** causes the mind not to tend toward ardency, commitment, perseverance, or exertion.

*   **"Smoldering":** A state associated with "I am" thinking, like "I am here," "I am bad," "I will be," etc.. This ties into the concept of craving verbalizations.

*   **"Eel-wriggling":** A verbal contortion adopted by some contemplatives and brahmans when asked questions about right and wrong, often out of fear of clinging, loathing for clinging, or fear of interrogation, or due to dullness and stupidity. This is described as an **agitation and vacillation** felt by those immersed in craving, arising from contact as a requisite condition.

#### 2. Skillful Mental Qualities (Kusala Dhamma)

These are mental states and intentions that are described as conducive to benefit, happiness, and liberation.

*   **Mind (when developed/tamed/guarded/protected/restrained):**
    *   **Pliant and leads to great benefit**.
    *   **Luminous and freed from incoming defilements**.
    *   The **"development of the mind"** is possible for the well-instructed disciple.
    *   **Pliant, malleable, luminous, and not brittle** when concentration themes are attended to periodically.
    *   **Rightly concentrated** for the ending of the effluents.
    *   When freed from five defilements, it is **pliant, malleable, luminous**, not brittle, and rightly concentrated.
    *   **Calmed, and joy arises** when recollecting devas; defilements are abandoned.
    *   Can be **reined in, exerted, gladdened, watched over** as needed to realize the unexcelled cooled state.
    *   **Established inwardly, well-composed**, so no evil, unskillful qualities consume it.
    *   Its purity leads to **direct knowing** of qualities worth realizing.

*   **Shame (Hiri) & Compunction (Ottappa):**
    *   Two **"bright qualities"** that guard the world, enabling recognition of familial respect.
    *   A **"strength"** of one in training.
    *   A monk feels **shame at bodily, verbal, mental misconduct** and **compunction** for their consequences.
    *   Essential for living in apprenticeship to a Teacher with love & respect.
    *   One of the six qualities making a monk **capable of attaining arahantship**.

*   **Development of what is Skillful (Kusala Dhamma):**
    *   **Possible and conducive to benefit and pleasure**.
    *   Leads to **growth, increase, and abundance** in the Dhamma-Vinaya.
    *   One should **generate desire, endeavor, arouse persistence, uphold & exert intent** for the sake of arising, maintenance, non-confusion, increase, plenitude, development, & culmination of skillful qualities.

*   **Dispassion (Virāga) / Lack of Greed (Alobha):**
    *   A **"root of what is skillful"**.
    *   **Fabricates skillfully** in body, speech, or intellect.
    *   Many skillful qualities **"come into play"** born of lack of greed.
    *   The mind is **not overcome with greed, not consumed**.
    *   **From the fading of passion there is awareness-release**.
    *   **Disenchantment leads to dispassion**, which in turn leads to liberation.
    *   The **"ending of passion"** is referred to as the "unfabricated".
    *   The mind is **inclined to renunciation** when defilements of awareness are abandoned.
    *   Seeing the **danger in sensuality** fosters renunciation.
    *   **Thinking imbued with renunciation** produces non-blindness, vision, knowledge, fosters discernment, sides with non-vexation, and is conducive to Unbinding.

*   **Lack of Aversion (Adosa) / Non-Ill-Will (Abyāpāda) / Goodwill (Mettā):**
    *   A **"root of what is skillful"**.
    *   A mind **free from hostility, free from ill will, undefiled, & pure** acquires assurances of well-being.
    *   **From the fading of aversion there is discernment-release**.
    *   **Awareness-release through good-will** can be cultivated, developed, pursued, and is the escape from ill will.
    *   **Thinking imbued with non-ill-will** produces non-blindness, vision, knowledge, fosters discernment, sides with non-vexation, and is conducive to Unbinding.
    *   **Pervading the all-encompassing world with an awareness imbued with goodwill** is a central training.

*   **Lack of Delusion (Amoha) / Discernment (Paññā) / Wisdom (Paññā):**
    *   A **"root of what is skillful"**.
    *   **From the fading of ignorance discernment develops**.
    *   Discernment is **"developed"** when it leads to great benefit.
    *   A monk is **astute** and discerns what is skillful/unskillful.
    *   Endowed with **discernment of arising & passing away**—noble, penetrating, leading to the right ending of stress.
    *   **Clear knowing** is the leader in the attainment of skillful qualities.
    *   **Acquiring and increasing discernment** is supported by having strong shame and compunction, approaching a teacher for questions, listening, remembering Dhamma, pondering, generating desire, effort, and realizing truth.
    *   The **"eye of discernment"** is unsurpassed and leads to release from suffering & stress.
    *   Skillful thinking produces **non-blindness, vision, knowledge, and fosters discernment**.
    *   One of the **"five faculties"**.
    *   Being **strong in discernment** makes understanding own and others' benefit, and realizing superior human states possible.
    *   A **wise person is characterized by actions** and their discernment shines through the activities of their life. They formulate and answer questions appropriately and approve of others' appropriate answers.
    *   A monk should be **discerning** and unconcerned with his body & life to attain arahantship.
    *   Mental action is described as the **most greatly blameworthy** for evil kamma by the Buddha, implying its significant role in discerning outcomes.
    *   The **mind becomes pure** through proper technique, leading to joy and abandonment of defilements.
    *   Wisdom is expressed in **"sagacity"** (bodily, verbal, mental) and **"skillfulness"** (in going forward, decline, tactics).
    *   Leads to **awareness-release and discernment-release**.
    *   An individual with **insight into phenomena through heightened discernment** should seek internal tranquility of awareness.
    *   **Knowing and seeing things as they have come to be** leads to disenchantment and dispassion.
    *   One of the **"qualities creating a protector"**.
    *   Hatthaka of Alavi is described as discerning.

*   **Conviction (Saddhā) / Faith:**
    *   A **"strength"** of one in training.
    *   Has **verified confidence in the Tathāgata's awakening**.
    *   One of the **"five faculties"**.
    *   A **"stream-entry factor"**.
    *   **Most helpful for visiting a teacher**, which is a prerequisite for developing discernment.
    *   One of the ten **"qualities creating a protector"**.
    *   A **"person of conviction & confidence"** wants to see virtuous people, hear the True Dhamma, and is freely generous, openhanded, delighting in magnanimity and distributing alms.
    *   The **"strength of conviction"** goes to the culmination of its development when the mind is guarded from effluents.
    *   A **"faith-follower"** is incapable of bad rebirths until stream-entry.
    *   Hatthaka of Alavi is described as endowed with conviction.

*   **Persistence (Vīriya) / Effort (Vīriya):**
    *   **Aroused for abandoning unskillful and taking on skillful qualities**; steadfast, solid in effort, not shirking duties.
    *   A **"strength"** of one in training.
    *   One of the **"five faculties"**.
    *   **Over-aroused persistence leads to restlessness; overly slack persistence leads to laziness**. One should determine the right pitch.
    *   **Manly firmness, persistence, striving** can lead to attaining what can be reached.
    *   A monk should **put forth extra desire, effort, diligence, endeavor, relentlessness** for abandoning unskillful qualities.
    *   **Persistence as a "factor for awakening"**.
    *   One of the **"fabrications of exertion"**.
    *   One of the ten **"qualities creating a protector"**.
    *   A factor for exertion for full purity.
    *   Hatthaka of Alavi is described as diligent.

*   **Mindfulness (Sati):**
    *   **Established and unmuddled**.
    *   **Always mindful** when going forward, returning, standing, sitting, lying down, or determining an action; leads to mindfulness & alertness.
    *   **Highly meticulous, remembering & able to call to mind even things done & said long ago**.
    *   One of the **"five faculties"**.
    *   **Mindfulness as a "factor for awakening"**.
    *   A monk should **act with alertness** when going forward and returning, etc..
    *   **Mindfulness immersed in the body** is like a "strong post or stake" for grounding the mind.
    *   The **"establishing of mindfulness"** is to be practiced with the thought, "I'll watch after myself" or "I'll watch after others".
    *   Recollecting the Tathāgata, Dhamma, Saṅgha, or devas leads to the mind not being overcome by defilements, gaining joy, rapture, calm, and concentration.

*   **Concentration (Samādhi) / Calm (Samatha):**
    *   When developed, the mind is **pliant**.
    *   A monk should attend periodically to the **theme of concentration** to make the mind pliant, malleable, luminous, and not brittle, and rightly concentrated for ending effluents.
    *   **Immeasurable concentration** (based on goodwill, compassion, empathetic joy, or equanimity) leads to five realizations.
    *   The **five-factored noble right concentration** leads to knowing and realizing qualities whenever there's an opening.
    *   The mind should be **steadied, settled down, unified, and concentrated**.
    *   One of the **"five faculties"**.
    *   **Concentration as a "factor for awakening"**.
    *   Can be developed with **directed thought & evaluation**, **without directed thought but with a modicum of evaluation**, or **without directed thought or evaluation**.
    *   **Emptiness concentration, themeless/signless concentration, and undirected concentration** are types of concentration.
    *   A monk's three urgent duties include the **training in heightened mind**.
    *   A monk who is **centered in concentration** is aroused to practice.
    *   **Jhānas** are heightened mental states providing pleasant abiding. Incapable of attaining jhānas without abandoning certain qualities like stinginess.
    *   **Internal tranquility of awareness** and insight into phenomena through heightened discernment can be attained.
    *   A **concentrated monk discerns in line with what has come into being** (origination & disappearance of aggregates).
    *   **For a person whose mind is concentrated, there is no need to actively will to know & see things as they have come to be; it is in the nature of things that they know & see**.
    *   Two qualities are of great help in the attainment of the **cessation of perception & feeling**: tranquility & insight.

*   **Alertness (Sampajañña):**
    *   Accompanies mindfulness.
    *   A monk makes himself **fully alert** when going forward and returning, etc..
    *   A monk's **self-examination** is productive for skillful qualities if he is alert.
    *   To be **mindful & alert** at the time of death.

*   **Equanimity (Upekkhā):**
    *   One of the four factors of **immeasurable concentration**.
    *   A monk should attend periodically to the **theme of equanimity** to make the mind pliant, malleable, luminous, and not brittle, and rightly concentrated for ending effluents.
    *   **Purified equanimity** is a factor for the gnosis of emancipation.
    *   **Equanimity as a "factor for awakening"**.
    *   When the mind becomes refined, **only equanimity remains**, pure & bright, pliant, malleable, & luminous, serving one's purpose for concentration on higher dimensions.
    *   **Equanimity takes a stance** when agreeable/disagreeable reactions cease.

*   **Modesty:** Hatthaka of Alavi is described as modest.

*   **Contentment:** Hatthaka of Alavi is described as contented.

*   **Seclusion (Viveka):**
    *   A path leading to the unfabricated can be **dependent on seclusion**.
    *   A monk who is secluded from sensuality and unskillful qualities can enter and remain in the first jhāna.
    *   Mind can lean, tend, incline to **seclusion** when emerging from cessation of perception & feeling.

*   **Truth (Sacca):**
    *   A sage who makes an effort in truth **doesn't fall back**.
    *   **Determination for truth** is one of four determinations.
    *   To **be truthful**, not insolent or deceptive, and remote from divisiveness.
    *   The **Awakened One** is a speaker and asserter of truths.

*   **Renunciation (Nekkhamma):**
    *   **Right resolve for renunciation** is part of the Noble Eightfold Path.
    *   **Thinking imbued with renunciation** produces non-blindness, vision, knowledge, fosters discernment, sides with non-vexation, and is conducive to Unbinding.
    *   The mind is **inclined to renunciation** when defilements of awareness are abandoned.

*   **Harmlessness (Avihiṁsā):**
    *   **Right resolve for harmlessness** is part of the Noble Eightfold Path.
    *   **Thinking imbued with harmlessness** produces non-blindness, vision, knowledge, fosters discernment, sides with non-vexation, and is conducive to Unbinding.

*   **Generosity (Cāga):**
    *   A person of conviction and confidence lives at home, **freely generous, openhanded, delighting in being magnanimous, responsive to requests, delighting in the distribution of alms**.
    *   The **recollection of generosity** is one of the six qualities to develop for Mahānāma.
    *   Hatthaka of Alavi is described as generous.
    *   Developing generosity is an **act of merit** that yields bliss.

*   **Purity (Suddhi):**
    *   **Bodily pure, verbally pure, mentally pure, without effluent** means one is cleansed of evil.
    *   **Three purities**: bodily, verbal, mental.
    *   The **defiled mind is cleansed through proper technique** like purifying gold.
    *   **Exertion for full purity** includes purity of virtue, mind, view, overcoming doubt, knowledge & vision of path, and release.
    *   A monk should look after himself with **purity**.

*   **Steadfast (Dhīra):**
    *   A quality of persistence: **steadfast, solid in effort**.
    *   Mind can grow **confident, steadfast, and released** in renunciation.

*   **Ardency (Ātappa):**
    *   A monk should remain **ardent, alert, & mindful** in practices like focusing on the body, feelings, mind, or mental qualities.
    *   A monk should dwell heedful, ardent, and resolute to release the mind or end effluents.

*   **Resoluteness:** (See Ardency).

*   **Unperturbed:** Sages are described as unperturbed.

*   **Self-controlled:** Sages are described as self-controlled.

*   **Peaceful:**
    *   A monk should train to be **peaceful in faculties & hearts**, which leads to peaceful bodily, verbal, and mental actions.

*   **Good Conduct (Sucarita):**
    *   **Good bodily, verbal, and mental conduct** characterize a wise person.
    *   When adopted & carried out, **lead to welfare & happiness**.
    *   Shunning unskillful activities and adopting skillful activities leads to the **rewards of renunciation, resembling cleansing**.

*   **Right View (Sammā-diṭṭhi):**
    *   The **forerunner** of noble right concentration.
    *   Defined as **knowledge of stress, its origination, its cessation, and the path** leading to its cessation.
    *   In one who has right view, **wrong view is purged away**, and skillful qualities go to culmination.
    *   To be developed as an instance of heedfulness.
    *   A monk should be **not warped in the way he sees things**.

*   **Sagacity (Moneyya):**
    *   Three forms: **bodily sagacity** (abstaining from taking life, theft, uncelibacy), **verbal sagacity** (abstaining from lying, divisive speech, harsh speech, idle chatter), and **mental sagacity** (ending effluents, entering awareness-release & discernment-release).

*   **Skillfulness (Kosalla):**
    *   Three forms: **skillfulness in (knowing about) going forward, skillfulness in (knowing about) decline, skillfulness in tactics**.

*   **Good Digestion:** Being endowed with good digestion (moderate strength) is a factor for exertion, making one **fit for exertion**.

*   **Non-Agitation:** When consciousness is neither externally scattered nor internally positioned, **from lack of clinging or sustenance, there is no seed for future birth, aging, death, or stress**.

*   **Joy (Pīti) / Rapture (Pīti):**
    *   When one is sensitive to the meaning and Dhamma, **joy is born**, and in one who is joyful, **rapture is born**.
    *   In one whose mind is enraptured, the body grows calm.
    *   **Rapture as a "factor for awakening"**.

*   **Calm (Passaddhi):**
    *   When the mind is enraptured, the **body grows calm**.
    *   **Calm as a "factor for awakening"**.
    *   The **body is calm & unaroused**.
    *   One of two qualities of great help in the attainment of the **cessation of perception & feeling**.

*   **Virtue (Sīla):**
    *   A monk should be **virtuous**, dwelling restrained in accordance with the Pāṭimokkha, consummate in behavior & sphere of activity, training in rules, seeing danger in slightest faults.
    *   **"Heightened virtue"** is one of the "three trainings".
    *   **Good bodily, verbal, and mental conduct** are forms of virtue.
    *   **Refraining from killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, lying, divisive speech, abusive speech, & idle chatter** are examples of right conduct.
    *   Cultivating virtue is an **act of merit** that yields bliss.
    *   One of the **"qualities creating a protector"**.
    *   A disciple feels **shame at bodily, verbal, mental misconduct**.
    *   **Bodily, verbal, mental conduct, and livelihood should be pure, clear & open, unbroken & restrained**.
    *   Hatthaka of Alavi is described as virtuous.
    *   **Right action and right livelihood** are part of the Noble Eightfold Path.

*   **Heedfulness (Appamāda):**
    *   Should be **exercised in abandoning bodily, verbal, mental misconduct, and wrong view**.
    *   **All skillful qualities are rooted in, converge in, and heedfulness is reckoned the foremost among them**.
    *   Monks should **not be heedless**.
    *   Leads to the **stability, non-confusion, & non-disappearance of the True Dhamma**.
    *   Monks in higher training have a **task to do with heedfulness** for realizing the supreme goal.
    *   **Heedfulness, mindfulness, and a protection of awareness** should be practiced regarding the six sense strings.
    *   When one quality (heedfulness) is established, the **five faculties (conviction, persistence, mindfulness, concentration, discernment) are developed & developed well**.
    *   A monk should **live heedfully**.

*   **Direct Knowledge (Abhiññā):**
    *   **Gnoses of emancipation** is called the breaking open of ignorance.
    *   The **"perfection & culmination of direct knowledge"** is attained by many disciples in developing faculties, mind-made body, mind reading, and ending effluents.
    *   **Tranquility & insight are qualities to be developed** through direct knowledge.
    *   **Clear knowing & release are qualities to be realized** through direct knowledge.
    *   The **seven factors for awakening** (mindfulness, analysis of qualities, persistence, rapture, calm, concentration, equanimity) lead to direct knowledge.

*   **Self-Awakening (Sambodhi) / Awakened (Buddha):**
    *   The **Tathāgata is "rightly self-awakened"**.
    *   The **faculties of conviction, persistence, mindfulness, concentration, and discernment** go to "self-awakening".
    *   The goal for **breaking through to the four noble truths**.
    *   **The path to self-awakening** is gradual.
    *   The **Dustless, stainless Dhamma eye** arising leads to abandonment of fetters and entering jhāna.
    *   The **Noble Eightfold Path** leads to self-awakening.

*   **Unbinding (Nibbāna) / Release (Vimutti) / Cooled (Sīta):**
    *   **From the fading of passion is awareness-release; from the fading of ignorance is discernment-release**.
    *   **"Awareness-release & discernment-release"** where there is no "I-making" or "mine-making" conceit-obsession; leads to cutting craving, ripping fetters, ending suffering & stress.
    *   **Ending of effluents** leads to effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release.
    *   **Thinking imbued with renunciation, non-ill-will, and harmlessness** is conducive to Unbinding.
    *   **Disenchantment leads to dispassion, which leads to cessation, stilling, direct knowledge, self-awakening, and unbinding**.
    *   The **"liberation of the mind through lack of clinging or sustenance"** is deathless.
    *   The **"unexcelled cooled state"** can be realized by reining in, exerting, gladdening, and watching over the mind, being intent on the exquisite, and delighting in unbinding.
    *   **Crossing over birth & aging** is achieved by those at peace, whose vices have evaporated, who are undesiring and untroubled.
    *   One who neither torments self nor others dwells **free of hunger, unbound, cooled, sensitive to pleasure, with a Brahmā-like mind**.
    *   **No further becoming** for those who are enlightened and have abandoned birth & death.
    *   The "Dhamma progressing by tens" is **for attaining unbinding, for putting an end to suffering & stress, for releasing from all ties**.
    *   The mind that is **free from five defilements** is rightly concentrated for the ending of effluents and can witness qualities to be known and realized.
    *   **Tranquility & insight** are qualities that are to be realized through direct knowledge leading to unbinding.
    *   The **"ending of passion, the ending of aversion, the ending of delusion"** is called the "unfabricated".
    *   Various paths, including the Noble Eightfold Path, lead to unbinding, the secure, the ultimate, and other positive states.

**Analogy for Understanding Mental Qualities:**

Imagine your mind as a garden. Unskillful mental qualities are like weeds: they are easy to sprout and can quickly choke out beneficial growth if left untended. Some, like passion, are deeply rooted and slow to fade, requiring persistent effort to remove. Others, like anger, might flare up quickly but can also be extinguished if addressed promptly. Skillful mental qualities, on the other hand, are like precious seeds or carefully cultivated plants: they require consistent effort, nurturing, and protection to grow and flourish. When they are well-developed, they transform the garden into a beautiful, productive, and peaceful sanctuary, free from the harm and agitation that weeds bring.
