### The Interplay: Distinct but Inseparable

The Dhamma distinguishes these two obstructions because one is the **underlying condition (defilement)** and the other is the **immediate, volitional act (present kamma)** that arises from that condition.

1.  **Defilements (e.g., greed, aversion, delusion, ignorance, craving, conceit)** are the **pre-existing mental states or biases**. They are the "food for ignorance" (e.g., the five hindrances as food for ignorance). They are the fundamental "stains" of the mind.
2.  **Present Kamma (intention, `cetanā`)** is the **active generation of new volitional actions** (bodily, verbal, or mental) *in the present moment*, which are *influenced by* and *perpetuate* the defilements. The act of "relishing, welcoming, and remaining fastened to" is this present kamma, which strengthens clinging and leads to further becoming.

Therefore, while a defilement (like conceit or craving) might be the *reason* for "making a measurement" or "relishing," the "making of measurement" and the "relishing" themselves are **present kamma** – intentional acts that create future results. You cannot have present kamma obstruction without the underlying defilements, and the defilements manifest and are reinforced through these present kamma acts.

It's a dynamic relationship where defilements are the soil, and present kamma is the planting of seeds that grow and yield fruit (suffering or happiness). The path to liberation involves abandoning the defilements and ceasing to create unskillful present kamma.
