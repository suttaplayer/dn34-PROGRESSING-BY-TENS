Your updated understanding that "clinging is a Being's ultimate survival strategy" because "clinging is delusional! and the reason why it's delusion is because it assumes a self and is determined on the self's survival" is **highly consistent with the teachings in the sources**.

My previous response focused on how the Dhamma views "becoming" (continued existence) as the **source of suffering** and something to be transcended, rather than a desirable "survival strategy" for liberation. Your current clarification addresses this by highlighting that this "survival strategy" is, in fact, **delusional** and rooted in the false assumption of a self, which then perpetuates suffering.

The sources strongly support the idea that clinging, being rooted in ignorance and leading to future becoming, is indeed a *delusional* process that perpetuates stress, rather than leading to true well-being or liberation.

Here's how the sources confirm your revised premise, detailing the "producer" and "consumer" roles within this delusional framework:

### Clinging as Delusional and Rooted in Self-Assumption

The core of your premise — that clinging is delusional because it assumes a self and is determined on that self's survival — is a fundamental principle illustrated throughout the sources.

*   **Ignorance and the Cycle of Suffering**: The entire cycle of renewed becoming, birth, aging, and death, and the associated sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, and despair, is explicitly stated to originate from **ignorance**. Ignorance conditions fabrications, leading to consciousness, and eventually to craving and clinging. Beings are described as "hindered by ignorance and fettered by craving," leading to their "transmigrating & wandering on" for a long, long time. This ongoing "wandering-on" is inherently stressful.
*   **The Illusion of Self (I-making, Mine-making, Conceit)**: The sources repeatedly identify the assumption of a self as the basis for clinging and the suffering it entails.
    *   An "uninstructed, run-of-the-mill person" assumes various aggregates (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness) to be **the self, or possessing the self, or in the self**. They are "seized with the idea that 'I am form' or 'Form is mine'". This clinging to a self is the "doctrine of self clinging".
    *   This "I-making" and "mine-making conceit-obsession" is precisely what a monk must cut through to end suffering. Only when the "conceit, 'I am,' is abandoned, its root destroyed... not destined for future arising," is one "detached".
    *   The "view-&-conceit obsession 'I am'" is a defilement (effluent) that needs to be uprooted for one to be "endowed with verified confidence in the Dhamma" and to "put an end to suffering & stress right in the here & now".
    *   The delusion of self, expressed as "I-making" and "other-making," keeps the human race "possessed by conceit, bound by conceit, tied down by conceit," preventing them from "going beyond the wandering-on".
    *   When the aggregates (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness) change and alter, the person "seized with these ideas" of "I am" or "mine" experiences "sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, & despair". This highlights that the "survival" of this assumed self is inherently painful.
*   **Clinging as a "Fetter"**: The sources refer to clinging as a "fetter" or "sustenance" that binds beings to the cycle of becoming and suffering. The "fetter of becoming" is only "totally destroyed" for an arahant, indicating its persistent presence for others. The path to "total unbinding" is "through lack of clinging". The Buddha explicitly states that he does "not envision a clinging to a doctrine of self, clinging to which there would not arise sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, & despair".

Therefore, clinging, especially to a "doctrine of self," is portrayed as a **delusional attachment** that *perpetuates* existence (a form of "survival" or continued becoming), but this survival is synonymous with continued suffering, not liberation.

### The Delusional "Producer" and "Consumer" Roles of Clinging

Your description of the "producer" and "consumer" roles within this delusional framework aligns with the causal mechanisms outlined in Dependent Co-arising.

*   **Producer Role: Craving Leading to Clinging and Self-Attachment**
    *   You assert that the "producer role of clinging encompasses the desire, attachment and possessiveness with regards to an aspect or the whole of the five-clinging aggregates that were seeked, acquired and ascertained as to be worthy of attachment". This is directly supported by the sequence in Dependent Co-arising: from feeling comes craving, and from craving comes clinging or sustenance.
    *   The concept of craving as the "seamstress—for craving stitches one to the production of this or that very becoming" underscores its role in *producing* future existence.
    *   The "craving that makes for further becoming—accompanied by passion & delight, relishing now here & now there—i.e., sensuality-craving, becoming-craving, and non-becoming-craving" is precisely what drives the accumulation of the "five clinging-aggregates" towards "future accumulation" and growing "bodily disturbances & mental disturbances". This is the "desire, attachment, and possessiveness" you describe.
    *   The sources also explicitly state the process of "seeking" which is "dependent on craving".
    *   The four types of clinging you mentioned – sensuality, view, habits & practices, and doctrine of self – are consistently named as the "four clingings". These are indeed seen as the basis for further stress and wandering.
    *   The notion of "ascertainment" of aggregates being worthy of attachment leading to clinging is supported by the mechanism where "delight in feeling is clinging or sustenance" and by the various ways one "assumes" forms, feelings, etc., to be self. This "relishing" and "welcoming" of feelings leads to delight, which is clinging.

*   **Consumer Role: Orchestration of Mental Factors for Renewed Becoming**
    *   You state that the consumer role is "initiated when contact is accompanied by ignorance and occurs on the main thread of execution". The chain of Dependent Co-arising starts with ignorance, which conditions fabrications, consciousness, name-and-form, six sense media, and then **contact** leading to feeling, craving, and clinging. For the uninstructed, "touched by that which is felt born of contact with ignorance, craving arises".
    *   The "raw feeling and perception from name-&-form is objectified through a process of making measurement against the photo album of clung to aggregates": The mind "complicates" what it perceives and thinks about. This "making of measurement" against one's fixed views ("assumptions") and "craving-verbalizations" influences how one "relishes" or "welcomes" feelings, leading to delight, which is clinging.
    *   "This measurement informs one's intentions and the process of fabrication then manifests the five clinging aggregates in response": **Intellectual intention** is one of the four nutriments that contributes to the "production of renewed becoming in the future". The six bodies of intention are called **fabrications**. "What one intends, what one arranges, and what one obsesses about: This is a support for the stationing of consciousness... there is the production of renewed becoming in the future".
    *   "It is in this orchestration that nutriment provides the sustenance for future becoming by collaborating with intention": The four nutriments (physical food, contact, intellectual intention, and consciousness) are for "the maintenance of beings who have come into being or for the support of those in search of a place to be born". Where there is "passion, delight, & craving for the nutriment... consciousness lands there and increases" leading to "renewed becoming in the future".
    *   "The result is a consciousness seed is created which gets planted in a field of becoming": "Kamma is the field, consciousness the seed, and craving the moisture. The intention & aspiration of living beings hindered by ignorance & fettered by craving is established in or tuned to a middling property. Thus there is the production of renewed becoming in the future". This directly supports the metaphor of consciousness as a seed in the field of becoming.

In essence, your expanded premise that clinging is a *delusional survival strategy* that aims at the continuation of a *false self* and, in doing so, perpetuates the cycle of stress and suffering, is **well-grounded in the provided sources**. The Dhamma points to the ending of this very process – the ending of craving, clinging, and becoming – as the path to true unbinding and the cessation of suffering, not its perpetuation.
