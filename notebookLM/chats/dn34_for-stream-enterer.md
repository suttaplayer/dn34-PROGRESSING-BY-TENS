Here is a minimal set of Dhamma topics for a practitioner intent on gaining stream-entry, listed in causal order, drawing directly from the provided sources. Each item is presented as a single record with its progression index, category, and a brief Dhamma title.

*   **1. Foundational Support (Social & Attitudinal)**
    *   [1, "very helpful", "Association with People of Integrity"]. Associating with people of integrity is a factor for stream-entry and a requisite condition for conviction.
    *   [2, "very helpful", "Hearing the True Dhamma"]. This is also a factor for stream-entry and a requisite condition for conviction. It should be heard with an "unscattered mind, a mind gathered into one".
    *   [3, "on the side of distinction", "Appropriate Attention"]. Appropriate attention is a factor for stream-entry and leads to "alighting on the orderliness, on the rightness of skillful qualities". It is also a requisite condition for mindfulness and alertness.
    *   [4, "noble treasure", "Verified Confidence (Triple Gem)"]. Verified confidence in the Buddha, Dhamma, and Saṅgha is a stream-entry factor, a "noble treasure", and it leads to a mind not overcome by passion, aversion, or delusion.
    *   [5, "Dhamma aggregate", "Purification of Virtue"]. Virtue is the "basis of skillful mental qualities" and is "conducive to concentration". Stream-winners are described as "endowed with virtues that are appealing to the noble ones: untorn, unbroken, unspotted, unsplattered, liberating, praised by the observant, ungrasped at, leading to concentration".

*   **2. Core Insight (Direct Knowing)**
    *   [6, "should be directly known", "The Four Noble Truths"]. This is the "categorical teaching" that is "conducive to the goal, conducive to the Dhamma, and basic to the holy life". It is explicitly stated as something that "should be directly known".
    *   [7, "should be comprehended", "The Five Clinging-Aggregates (as Impermanent, Stressful, Not-self)"]. Understanding these aggregates as impermanent, stressful, and not-self is explicitly linked to stream-entry, as a "virtuous monk... would realize the fruit of stream-entry" by attending to them this way. This comprehension is key to abandoning self-identification view.
    *   [8, "hard to penetrate", "Dependent Co-arising"]. This is described as "deep, hard to see", but "whoever sees dependent co-arising sees the Dhamma". Knowledge of "the regularity of the Dhamma" (dependent co-arising) precedes "the knowledge of unbinding".

*   **3. Path Development (Practice)**
    *   [9, "should be developed", "The Noble Eightfold Path"]. This is the "path of practice leading to the cessation of stress" and is "the foremost of paths". It also leads to the "ending of kamma".
    *   [10, "should be developed", "The Four Establishings of Mindfulness"]. This is described as the "direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation... & for the realization of unbinding". Its development can lead to gnosis or non-return within seven days to seven years.
    *   [11, "should be developed", "Concentration for Ending Effluents"]. Concentration (Samādhi) is the "presiding state" for all phenomena. A concentrated mind is "pliant, malleable, luminous, & not brittle" and "discerns in line with what has come into being," including the Four Noble Truths and effluents. It leads to "the knowledge of the ending of the effluents".
    *   [12, "very helpful", "Discernment of Arising & Passing Away"]. Discernment is the "surpassing state" for all phenomena. Specifically, discerning "arising & passing away" with regard to the five aggregates is the insight that leads to the "dustless, stainless Dhamma eye: Whatever is subject to origination is all subject to cessation". This direct knowledge culminates in the abandoning of the three lower fetters.

*   **4. Direct Result (Fetter Abandonment)**
    *   [13, "should be abandoned", "Three Lower Fetters (Self-identification view, Doubt, Grasping at habits & practices)"]. Stream-entry is specifically defined by the abandonment of these three fetters. The practitioner becomes "certain, never again destined for the lower realms, headed for self-awakening".

For a practitioner who has already attained stream-entry (sotāpanna) and is intent on gaining once-return (sakadāgāmi), the focus shifts from eliminating the first three fetters (self-identification view, doubt, and grasping at habits & practices) to the **attenuation of passion, aversion, and delusion** [AN_nblm.txt 28, 31, 33, 46, 102, 188, 198, 351, 599; MN_nblm.txt 309]. This stage involves a deeper and more direct engagement with the roots of unskillful qualities.

Here is a minimal set of practices and priorities, maintaining the previous record structure and causal sort order:

### **Phase 1: Foundational Reinforcement (Essential and ongoing for all progress)**

These foundational elements, while already strong for a stream-enterer, remain crucial and require continuous reinforcement as the basis for further progress towards once-return.

*   [1, "Requisite Condition", "Association with People of Integrity"]
    *   Associating with people of integrity continues to be a "requisite condition for conviction" and is vital for continued guidance and support on the path, helping to abandon what is unskillful and develop what is skillful [AN_nblm.txt 8, 296, 590; SN_nblm.txt 590].
*   [2, "Requisite Condition", "Hearing the True Dhamma"]
    *   Ongoing "hearing the True Dhamma" is a "requisite condition for conviction" [AN_nblm.txt 12, 590; MN_nblm.txt 430; SN_nblm.txt 590], enabling the deepening of understanding necessary for more subtle insights.
*   [3, "on the side of distinction", "Appropriate Attention"]
    *   Appropriate attention (yoniso manasikāra) remains a quality "on the side of distinction" [DN_nblm.txt 227] and is fundamental for rightly discerning phenomena and progressing in skillful qualities [AN_nblm.txt 7, 590; DN_nblm.txt 213].
*   [4, "noble treasure", "Verified Confidence (Triple Gem)"]
    *   Verified confidence in the Buddha, Dhamma, and Saṅgha is a "noble treasure" [AN_nblm.txt 98, 99; DN_nblm.txt 213]. This confidence, when present, ensures the mind is "not overcome with passion, not overcome with aversion, not overcome with delusion" [AN_nblm.txt 150, 151, 156, 157], thereby fostering joy, rapture, calm, and concentration.
*   [5, "Dhamma aggregate", "Purification of Virtue (Bodily, Verbal, Mental Conduct)"]
    *   Virtue (sīla), as the "basis of skillful mental qualities" [MN_nblm.txt 311], must be continuously purified and maintained. A virtuous person is "restrained in accordance with the Pāṭimokkha, consummate in behavior & sphere of activity, seeing danger in the slightest faults" [AN_nblm.txt 2, 27, 28, 31, 258; MN_nblm.txt 311, 389; Iti_nblm.txt 258]. This involves abandoning "crookedness, faults, & flaws in bodily action… verbal action… mental action" [AN_nblm.txt 6; MN_nblm.txt 373, 374, 379] and is conducive to concentration [AN_nblm.txt 23].

### **Phase 2: Active Attenuation of Defilements (Targeted Practice for Once-Return)**

This phase directly addresses the **reduction of passion, aversion, and delusion**, which is the hallmark of a once-returner.

*   [6, "should be abandoned", "Abandoning Unskillful Qualities (especially those rooted in passion, aversion, delusion)"]
    *   The explicit instruction is to "**Abandon what is unskillful, monks. It is possible to abandon what is unskillful**" [AN_nblm.txt 6]. This directly targets the "roots of what is unskillful," namely greed, aversion, and delusion [DN_nblm.txt 207; KN_Iti_nblm.txt 249; MN_nblm.txt 312]. This includes "unskillful bodily actions, unskillful verbal actions, evil means of livelihood" [MN_nblm.txt 417].
*   [7, "should be developed", "Developing Skillful Qualities (especially those rooted in lack of greed, lack of aversion, lack of delusion)"]
    *   The counterpart to abandoning unskillful qualities is to "**Develop what is skillful, monks. It is possible to develop what is skillful**" [AN_nblm.txt 7]. The "roots of what is skillful" are lack of greed, lack of aversion, and lack of delusion [DN_nblm.txt 208; MN_nblm.txt 312]. This cultivation leads to "benefit and pleasure" [AN_nblm.txt 7].
*   [8, "very helpful", "Heedfulness"]
    *   Heedfulness (appamāda) is "very helpful with regard to skillful qualities" [AN_nblm.txt 8] and is crucial for those in "higher training who have not yet reached their heart's goal" [SN_nblm.txt 549]. It enables a practitioner to "make an end of stress" and prevents "wandering-on through birth" [SN_nblm.txt 200, 264; Dhp_nblm.txt 239; KN_Iti_nblm.txt 248]. The Buddha's final exhortation was to "reach consummation through heedfulness" [SN_nblm.txt 200].
*   [9, "should be developed", "Right Effort / Arousing Persistence"]
    *   Persistence should be "aroused for abandoning unskillful qualities and taking on skillful qualities" [AN_nblm.txt 8, 9; MN_nblm.txt 390, 417, 420; DN_nblm.txt 217, 230]. This sustained effort (viriya) is essential to overcome defilements and is a factor of the noble eightfold path [SN_nblm.txt 581, 582, 584].
*   [10, "should be developed", "Cultivating Goodwill (Metta)"]
    *   Developing a mind of goodwill (mettā) is a powerful practice. It is mentioned as one of "three things that bring about bliss" [KN_Iti_nblm.txt 248, 250]. Cultivating it "even for the time it takes to pull on a cow's udder" is more fruitful than significant almsgiving [AN_nblm.txt 126] and is the "escape from ill will" [AN_nblm.txt 80; DN_nblm.txt 219, 232].
*   [11, "should be developed", "Recollections (Triple Gem, Virtue, Generosity, Devas)"]
    *   Periodically recollecting the Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, one's own virtue, generosity, and the devas is key. When these recollections are developed, the mind is "not overcome with passion, not overcome with aversion, not overcome with delusion" [AN_nblm.txt 150, 151, 152, 153, 156, 157, 158, 159, 160; MN_nblm.txt 361], directly strengthening wholesome states and weakening defilements. This leads to joy, rapture, calm, and concentration [AN_nblm.txt 150, 151, 152, 153, 156, 157, 158, 159, 160].
*   [12, "should be practiced", "Guarding the Sense Faculties"]
    *   A practitioner should "guard the doors to [their] sense faculties" to prevent "evil, unskillful qualities such as greed or distress" from assailing the mind upon seeing forms, hearing sounds, smelling aromas, tasting flavors, feeling tactile sensations, or cognizing ideas [MN_nblm.txt 376, 389, 448, 476]. This restraint is crucial for directly managing the arising of passion and aversion.
*   [13, "should be abandoned", "Abandoning Five Obstacles/Hindrances (especially sensuality, ill will, sloth & torpor, restlessness & remorse)"]
    *   Abandoning the "five obstacles, hindrances that overwhelm awareness and weaken discernment" is critical [AN_nblm.txt 69, 70; DN_nblm.txt 140, 218]. While doubt is abandoned by stream-entry, the remaining four (sensual desire, ill will, sloth & torpor, restlessness & remorse) must be actively attenuated as they directly impede the development of deeper mental states and insights necessary for once-return. Removing "stinginess as to one's monastery, family, gains, status, and Dhamma" is also stated as crucial for higher attainments including once-returning [AN_nblm.txt 76, 77, 78, 79].

### **Phase 3: Deepening Insight & Mental States (Refining the Mind for Attenuation)**

These practices provide the direct knowledge and mental stability necessary to thoroughly attenuate remaining defilements.

*   [14, "should be directly known", "Understanding the Four Noble Truths (Stress, Origination, Cessation, Path)"]
    *   This "categorical teaching" [DN_nblm.txt 172] is fundamental. Deepening the understanding of stress, its origination, cessation, and the path to cessation is critical, as it leads to "disenchantment, dispassion, cessation, stilling, direct knowledge, self-awakening, to unbinding" [DN_nblm.txt 171, 172, 175, 180, 415; MN_nblm.txt 333, 334]. It is impossible to end stress without breaking through these truths [SN_nblm.txt 605, 608].
*   [15, "should be comprehended", "Comprehending the Five Clinging-Aggregates (as Impermanent, Stressful, Not-self)"]
    *   Continuously attending to the five clinging-aggregates (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness) as "inconstant, stressful, a disease, a cancer, an arrow, painful, an affliction, alien, a dissolution, an emptiness, not-self" is essential [SN_nblm.txt 527, 528, 529, 530; MN_nblm.txt 1, 431, 432, 433, 434, 520, 521, 522]. This comprehension is crucial for disenchantment and loosening attachment [SN_nblm.txt 514, 515, 516, 517, 518, 526].
*   [16, "hard to penetrate", "Understanding Dependent Co-arising"]
    *   This deep teaching reveals the causal interconnectedness of suffering: "Whoever sees dependent co-arising sees the Dhamma; whoever sees the Dhamma sees dependent co-arising" [MN_nblm.txt 362, 363]. Understanding this chain, from ignorance to aging-&-death, is critical for "abandoning desire & passion" for the clinging-aggregates, leading to the "cessation of stress" [MN_nblm.txt 362, 363; SN_nblm.txt 494, 498, 499].
*   [17, "should be developed", "The Four Establishings of Mindfulness (Body, Feelings, Mind, Mental Qualities)"]
    *   This is the "direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation... & for the realization of unbinding" [DN_nblm.txt 185, 336; MN_nblm.txt 336]. Remaining focused on body, feelings, mind, and mental qualities, "ardent, alert, & mindful—subduing greed & distress" [DN_nblm.txt 191, 210, 386], directly addresses the root causes of suffering and is essential for developing insight.
*   [18, "should be developed", "Concentration (Samādhi) / Jhānas (for Ending Effluents)"]
    *   Concentration (samādhi) nurtured with virtue is of "great fruit, great reward" [AN_nblm.txt 37]. The four jhānas are "heightened mental states, pleasant abidings in the here & now" [AN_nblm.txt 38, 44, 45, 47, 48, 49, 50, 106; DN_nblm.txt 162, 167, 447, 49; MN_nblm.txt 387, 397]. The purpose of concentration is the "ending of the effluents" [AN_nblm.txt 34, 35]. Practicing seclusion and rapture is stated to eliminate "pain & distress dependent on sensuality" and "pain & distress dependent on what is unskillful" [AN_nblm.txt 73].
*   [19, "should be directly known", "Perception of Inconstancy / Stress / Not-self in all fabrications/phenomena"]
    *   Establishing the **perception of inconstancy** (anicca saññā) regarding all fabrications leads to the mind not delighting in any world, rising above every world, being inclined to unbinding, and fetters abandoning [AN_nblm.txt 95; KN_Iti_nblm.txt 253; Dhp_nblm.txt 243]. Similarly, the **perception of stress** (dukkha saññā) leads to disenchantment [AN_nblm.txt 96], and the **perception of not-self** (anattā saññā) stops "I-making" and "my-making" [AN_nblm.txt 97; SN_nblm.txt 525]. This direct discernment leads to the uprooting of conceit.
*   [20, "should be cultivated", "Cultivating Modesty, Contentment, Reclusiveness, Aroused Persistence, Established Mindfulness, Concentrated Mind, Discernment, Non-objectification (Eight Thoughts of a Great Person)"]
    *   These "Eight Thoughts of a Great Person" are specific qualities to cultivate: modesty, contentment, reclusiveness, aroused persistence, established mindfulness, a concentrated mind, discernment, and enjoying non-objectification [AN_nblm.txt 110, 111, 114, 115]. These attitudes directly support the deeper mental development and insight needed to attenuate remaining defilements and realize higher attainments including jhānas and unbinding [AN_nblm.txt 112, 113].
*   [21, "should be abandoned", "Abandoning 'I-making' and 'mine-making' conceit"]
    *   The practice should lead to "no I-making or mine-making conceit-obsession" with regard to the conscious body or external themes [AN_nblm.txt 9, 11]. This signifies a loosening of deep-seated attachment and the "conceit 'I am'" [DN_nblm.txt 227; SN_nblm.txt 525; KN_StNp_nblm.txt 266; KN_Ud_nblm.txt 305], which is necessary for transcending the state of a stream-enterer.

### **Phase 4: Realization (The Outcome of Once-Return)**

This is the direct outcome of diligently applying the practices and insights in the preceding phases.

*   [22, "Outcome Achieved", "Attenuation of Passion, Aversion, & Delusion"]
    *   Upon abandoning the first three fetters (self-identification view, doubt, and grasping at habits & practices), the practitioner experiences the **attenuation of passion, aversion, & delusion** [AN_nblm.txt 28, 31, 33, 46, 102, 188, 198, 351, 599; MN_nblm.txt 309]. This signifies a substantial reduction in the strength and influence of these unskillful roots, though they are not yet fully eradicated.
*   [23, "should be realized", "Realization of Once-Returning Fruit"]
    *   With the ending of the first three fetters and the attenuation of passion, aversion, & delusion, the practitioner becomes a **once-returner (sakadāgāmin)**, destined to return to this world only one more time to put an end to stress [AN_nblm.txt 28, 31, 33, 46, 102, 188, 198, 351, 599; MN_nblm.txt 309; DN_nblm.txt 229]. This is the explicit goal of this stage of practice.
