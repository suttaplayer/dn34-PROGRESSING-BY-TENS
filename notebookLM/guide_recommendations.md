Just as "Causation Expressions in Dhamma: An Identifier's Guide" provides a framework for understanding *how* things are causally linked, other similar guides could offer taxonomies for *what* is being linked, *how* it's being practiced, and *how* discussions about it unfold.

Given the nature of the content in your sources—primarily early Buddhist discourses (suttas) from the Aṅguttara Nikāya, Dīgha Nikāya, Khuddaka Nikāya, Majjhima Nikāya, and Saṁyutta Nikāya—and how such texts typically present information, I would recommend guides focusing on:

### 1. Mental Qualities (Skillful & Unskillful): An Identifier's Guide

This guide would catalog and define the myriad mental states, intentions, and qualities discussed in the Dhamma, clarifying their characteristics and the conditions for their arising or cessation. The sources frequently describe actions and their results in terms of these qualities.

*   **Purpose:** To provide a standardized vocabulary for discussing the internal states that drive action and experience, distinguishing between those conducive to suffering and those leading to liberation.
*   **Examples of Identifiers/Categories:**
    *   **Unskillful Qualities (Akusala Dhamma):** Passion (rāga), Aversion (dosa), Delusion (moha), Conceit (māna), Ignorance (avijjā), Ill will (vyāpāda), Restlessness (uddhacca), Anxiety (kukkucca), Sensual desire (kāmacchanda), Stinginess (macchariya), Greed (lobha), etc..
    *   **Skillful Qualities (Kusala Dhamma):** Dispassion (virāga), Disenchantment (nibbidā), Calm (passaddhi), Direct knowledge (abhiññā), Self-awakening (sambodhi), Unbinding (nibbāna), Mindfulness (sati), Alertness (sampajañña), Persistence (vīriya), Concentration (samādhi), Discernment (paññā), Goodwill (mettā), Compassion (karuṇā), Empathetic joy (muditā), Equanimity (upekkhā), Conviction (saddhā), Shame (hiri), Compunction (ottappa), Modesty, Contentment, Seclusion, Non-entanglement.
*   **Effective Queries:** "Show me how **passion** is said to be a cause for suffering.", "List the outcomes of developing **concentration**.", "What qualities **lead to unbinding**?".

### 2. Practices & Training Components: An Identifier's Guide

This guide would detail the specific actions, methods, and components of the path to liberation. The sources are replete with instructions on how to train and what practices to undertake.

*   **Purpose:** To categorize and define the prescribed actions and behaviors, making it easier to track the steps of the Dhamma practice.
*   **Examples of Identifiers/Categories:**
    *   **The Noble Eightfold Path:** Right View, Right Resolve, Right Speech, Right Action, Right Livelihood, Right Effort, Right Mindfulness, Right Concentration.
    *   **Jhāna Factors/Development:** First Jhāna, Second Jhāna, Third Jhāna, Fourth Jhāna, Dimensions of Space, Consciousness, Nothingness, Neither Perception nor Non-perception, Cessation of Perception & Feeling.
    *   **Factors for Awakening (Bojjhaṅgas):** Mindfulness, Analysis of Qualities, Persistence, Rapture, Calm, Concentration, Equanimity.
    *   **Modes of Discourse/Discussion:** Categorical answer, Analytical answer, Counter-question, Put aside.
    *   **Ethical Conduct (Sīla):** Bodily misconduct, Verbal misconduct, Mental misconduct; refraining from killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, lying, harsh speech, divisive speech, idle chatter.
*   **Effective Queries:** "What are the benefits of **right effort**?", "How does one **develop concentration**?".

### 3. Core Dhamma Concepts/Phenomena: An Identifier's Guide

This guide would define and categorize the fundamental Buddhist philosophical and psychological concepts that are frequently subjects or objects in causal relationships.

*   **Purpose:** To clarify the meaning of central Dhamma terms that are often interconnected in complex ways, aiding in tracing dependent origination and understanding the nature of reality as presented.
*   **Examples of Identifiers/Categories:**
    *   **Four Noble Truths:** Stress (dukkha), Origination of Stress, Cessation of Stress, Path to Cessation of Stress.
    *   **Dependent Co-arising (Paṭiccasamuppāda) Links:** Ignorance (avijjā), Fabrications (saṅkhāra), Consciousness (viññāṇa), Name-and-Form (nāma-rūpa), Six Sense Media (salāyatana), Contact (phassa), Feeling (vedanā), Craving (taṇhā), Clinging (upādāna), Becoming (bhava), Birth (jāti), Aging & Death (jarā-maraṇa).
    *   **Aggregates (Khandhas):** Form (rūpa), Feeling (vedanā), Perception (saññā), Fabrications (saṅkhāra), Consciousness (viññāṇa).
    *   **Effluents (Āsava):** Sensuality, Becoming, Ignorance.
*   **Effective Queries:** "Explain the origination and cessation of **stress** in detail.", "What is the relationship between **craving** and **becoming**?".

### Why these would be effective:

These types of guides directly address the most common patterns and key concepts within the Dhamma sources. By providing explicit identifiers, they allow NotebookLM to:

*   **Increase Precision:** Instead of inferring categories, the model can directly apply pre-defined labels (e.g., "This is an instance of the 'Unskillful Qualities' category: 'greed'").
*   **Improve Structure:** Responses can be organized according to these categories, making complex relationships easier to navigate and understand, similar to how the causation guide would structure output.
*   **Enhance Completeness:** By prompting the model to explicitly look for terms and phrases aligned with these identifiers, it's more likely to extract all relevant instances, including subtle ones.
*   **Optimize Speed:** Having a pre-processed conceptual map means the model spends less time on semantic interpretation and more on direct matching and extraction, potentially reducing response time, similar to your hypothesis about the causation guide.

In essence, these guides would act as specialized lenses, allowing you to focus on different facets of the Dhamma with greater clarity and efficiency, transforming broad searches into targeted, structured analyses. They turn the vast ocean of Dhamma into navigable channels, each mapped with specific types of information.
