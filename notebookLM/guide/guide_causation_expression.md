### Causation Expression Identifiers

This document catalogs terms and phrases used in the sources to describe cause, effect, conditions, and their interrelationships, providing a framework for analyzing causal patterns.

---

**1. Direct Causation (A leads to B)**

*   **`_ is a cause / causes for the origination of _`**: Indicates a primary factor initiating a phenomenon.
    *   Example: "Greed is a cause for the origination of actions."
    *   Example: "Envy & stinginess have dear-&-not-dear as their cause, have dear-&-not-dear as their origination, have dear-&-not-dear as what gives them birth, have dear-&-not-dear as their source."
*   **`From _ comes _`**: Denotes direct sequence or result from a preceding factor. Often used in Dependent Co-arising.
    *   Example: "From ignorance as a requisite condition come fabrications."
    *   Example: "From contact as a requisite condition comes feeling."
*   **`_ brings _ into play` / `_ come into play`**: Describes the manifestation or activation of phenomena due to a cause.
    *   Example: "Greed... (with the thought,) 'I have power. I want power,' that too is unskillful. Thus it is that many evil, unskillful qualities or events—born of greed, caused by greed, originated through greed, conditioned by greed—come into play."
    *   Example: "When what exists do they come into being? When what doesn't exist do they not?"
*   **`_ is born` / `_ arise`**: Indicates the beginning or emergence of something.
    *   Example: "To one who... is thinking & evaluating with one's awareness, desire is born."
    *   Example: "Dependent on the eye & forms there arises eye-consciousness."
*   **`_ produces _`**: Denotes active generation or creation.
    *   Example: "One who feels a feeling produces a corresponding state of existence, on the side of merit or demerit."
    *   Example: "An individual with form as self, taking a stance on form, produces merit or demerit."
*   **`_ ripens in _`**: Describes the outcome or consequence, often specifically in terms of pain or pleasure.
    *   Example: "Any action fashioned by greed... it ripens in pain..."
*   **`_ leads to _`**: Indicates a path or progression towards a state or outcome.
    *   Example: "These qualities, when adopted & carried out, lead to harm & to suffering."
    *   Example: "Over-aroused persistence leads to restlessness, overly slack persistence leads to laziness."
    *   Example: "Right view... leads to the acquiring of the as-yet-unacquired discernment."
*   **`_ makes _`**: Shows a direct causative influence or transformation.
    *   Example: "The way leading to short life makes people short-lived."
    *   Example: "The enlightened are makers of light."
*   **`_ engenders _`**: Suggests giving birth to or generating.
    *   Example: "Craving engenders a person."
*   **`_ causes _ to rise` / `_ rising causes _ to rise`**: Implies a direct increase or manifestation.
    *   Example: "Ignorance rising causes fabrications to rise."

**2. Conditions & Prerequisites (A enables B)**

*   **`_ as a requisite condition`**: A necessary or essential condition for something else to occur. Central to dependent co-arising.
    *   Example: "From feeling as a requisite condition comes craving."
    *   Example: "The knowledge of ending in the presence of ending has its prerequisite... Release, it should be said."
*   **`Conditioned by _`**: States that something is dependent upon a specific condition.
    *   Example: "Conditioned by name-&-form is contact."
*   **`Dependent on _`**: Expresses reliance or contingency.
    *   Example: "Dependent on the eye & forms there arises eye-consciousness."
    *   Example: "Stress comes into play in dependence on every acquisition."
*   **`_ is rooted in _`**: Indicates the fundamental or originating cause.
    *   Example: "All phenomena are rooted in desire."
    *   Example: "These five clinging-aggregates are rooted in desire."
*   **`_ provides the occasion for _`**: Signifies enabling or facilitating the arising of something.
    *   Example: "With reference to things in the past that provide the occasion for desire & passion..."
*   **`_ is supported by _`**: Denotes enabling or strengthening factors.
    *   Example: "Right view is supported by virtue, supported by learning..."
*   **`_ as a support for _`**: Indicates something that enables the establishment or continuation of another.
    *   Example: "What one intends... is a support for the stationing of consciousness."
*   **`hindered by _ & fettered by _`**: Describes obstacles or restraints that affect outcomes.
    *   Example: "The consciousness of living beings hindered by ignorance & fettered by craving is established in or tuned to a lower property."

**3. Consequences & Results (B from A)**

*   **`_ is the origination of _`**: States the point of beginning or formation.
    *   Example: "This is the origination of stress."
    *   Example: "Any desire, embracing, grasping, & holding-on to these five clinging-aggregates is the origination of stress."
*   **`_ is the result of _`**: Explicitly states an outcome.
    *   Example: "One who feels a feeling produces a corresponding state of existence... This is called the result of feeling."
*   **`_ follows _`**: Describes a subsequent outcome.
    *   Example: "If you speak or act with a corrupted heart, then suffering follows you."
*   **`_ (are) the fruit / reward of _`**: Indicates beneficial outcomes or results from a practice.
    *   Example: "It has awareness-release as its fruit, awareness-release as its reward."
*   **`_ is what differentiates _`**: Identifies the factor responsible for distinctions.
    *   Example: "Action is what differentiates beings in terms of baseness & excellence."
*   **`_ is the drawback of _`**: Points to negative aspects or consequences inherent in something.
    *   Example: "The fact that the cosmos is inconstant, stressful, subject to change: That is the drawback of the cosmos."

**4. Cessation (Absence of A leads to absence of B)**

*   **`From the fading of _ is there _-release`**: Describes liberation through the diminishing of a cause.
    *   Example: "From the fading of passion is there awareness-release."
*   **`From the cessation of _ comes the cessation of _`**: Indicates the direct, sequential ending of phenomena.
    *   Example: "From the cessation of ignorance comes the cessation of fabrications."
*   **`_ is abandoned` / `_ is destroyed` / `_ is cut through`**: Refers to the removal or elimination of a condition or obstacle.
    *   Example: "When greed is gone, that action is thus abandoned, its root destroyed."
    *   Example: "Cut through its root with discernment."
*   **`_ does not arise` / `_ no longer comes about`**: Indicates the prevention of occurrence.
    *   Example: "Unarisen passion does not arise and arisen passion is abandoned."
    *   Example: "This, monk, is how self-identification view no longer comes about."
*   **`_ is the ending of _`**: States the ultimate cessation of something.
    *   Example: "The ending of effluents, attained."
*   **`_ ebbing causes _ to ebb`**: Describes a sequential diminishing or reduction.
    *   Example: "Ignorance ebbing causes fabrications to ebb."

**5. Purpose & Means (A for B)**

*   **`for the purpose of _`**: States the intention or goal.
    *   Example: "I will teach you the Dhamma compared to a raft, for the purpose of crossing over, not for the purpose of holding onto."
*   **`for the _ of _`**: Indicates the aim or objective (e.g., for the purification of, for the sake of).
    *   Example: "For the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding."
*   **`by means of _` / `through _`**: Specifies the method or instrument used to achieve a result.
    *   Example: "Whatever a greedy person fabricates by means of body, speech, or intellect, that too is unskillful."
    *   Example: "It's through discussion that a person's discernment may be known."
    *   Example: "The liberation of the mind through lack of clinging or sustenance."
*   **`is the way leading to the cessation of _`**: Describes the practice or path required for cessation.
    *   Example: "Just this noble eightfold path... is the way leading to the cessation of feeling."
*   **`_ should be done / made to arise / developed / pursued / comprehended / abandoned / directly known`**: Prescriptive statements indicating actions for specific outcomes.
    *   Example: "Sensuality should be known."
    *   Example: "Your duty is the contemplation, 'This is stress…'"
*   **`train oneself / yourselves`**: Instruction for personal development towards a goal.
    *   Example: "That is how you should train yourself."

**6. Characterization & Definition (A is B because of C)**

*   **`_ is characterized by _`**: Defines a person or thing based on its actions or attributes.
    *   Example: "A fool is characterized by his or her actions. A wise person is characterized by his or her actions."
*   **`_ is called _`**: Provides a definition or nomenclature.
    *   Example: "From contact as a requisite condition comes feeling. This is called feeling."
    *   Example: "'It is afflicted,' thus it is called 'form.'"
*   **`_ is the field, _ the seed, and _ the moisture`**: Metaphorical representation of causal components.
    *   Example: "Kamma is the field, consciousness the seed, and craving the moisture."
*   **`_ is the seamstress—for _ stitches one to the production of _`**: Metaphor for an underlying force linking phenomena.
    *   Example: "Craving is the seamstress—for craving stitches one to the production of this or that very becoming."
*   **`_ is the yoke of _` / `unyoking from _`**: Metaphor for something that binds or the act of release.
    *   Example: "This is the yoke of sensuality & the yoke of becoming."
    *   Example: "This is unyoking from sensuality."
*   **`_ is a hindrance`**: Identifies a negative condition that impedes progress.
    *   Example: "Any sensual desire for what is internal is a hindrance."

**7. Absence of Causation / Lack of Conditions**

*   **`Without _ or without _`**: Explicitly states the absence of causal factors.
    *   Example: "'Whatever a person experiences… is all without cause & without condition.'"
*   **`It's not possible that _`**: Denotes an impossibility due to missing conditions.
    *   Example: "It is not possible that he would run after the past."
*   **`There is no _`**: Indicates non-existence or lack.
    *   Example: "Apart from a requisite condition, there is no coming-into-play of consciousness."

**8. Inquiry into Causation**

*   **`What is the cause, what is the reason, why _?`**: Direct questions seeking causal explanations.
    *   Example: "What is the cause, Master Kaccāna, what is the reason, why noble warriors dispute with noble warriors...?"
*   **`When what exists does _ exist? When what does not exist does _ not exist?`**: Inquires about necessary and sufficient conditions.
    *   Example: "When what exists does aging-&-death exist? When what does not exist does aging-&-death not exist?"
*   **`Why is that? Because _`**: A common explanatory pattern, offering reasons for observed phenomena.
    *   Example: "Why is that? Because he has not comprehended it, I tell you."
    *   Example: "Why is that? Because the seed is evil."
*   **`If _ then _`**: Conditional statements exploring hypothetical causal relationships.
    *   Example: "If you speak or act with a corrupted heart, then suffering follows you."
