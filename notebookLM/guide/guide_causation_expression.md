##### Causation Expression Identifiers
This document catalogs terms and phrases used in the sources to describe cause, effect, conditions, and their interrelationships, providing a framework for analyzing causal patterns.

**1. Direct Causation (A leads to B)**
*   **_ is a cause / causes for the origination of _**: Indicates a primary factor initiating a phenomenon.
    *   Example: "Greed is a cause for the origination of actions".
*   **From _ comes _**: Denotes direct sequence or result from a preceding factor. Often used in Dependent Co-arising.
    *   Example: "From ignorance as a requisite condition come fabrications".
*   **_ brings _ into play / _ come into play**: Describes the manifestation or activation of phenomena due to a cause.
    *   Example: "When a monk attends inappropriately, unarisen effluents arise, and arisen effluents increase".
*   **_ is born / _ arise**: Indicates the beginning or emergence of something.
    *   Example: "In one who is joyful, rapture arises".
*   **_ produces _**: Denotes active generation or creation.
    *   Example: "One who is immersed in ignorance produces a corresponding state of existence, on the side of merit or demerit".
*   **_ ripens in _**: Describes the outcome or consequence, often specifically in terms of pain or pleasure.
    *   Example: "Effluents that defile… that ripen in stress".
*   **_ leads to _**: Indicates a path or progression towards a state or outcome.
    *   Example: "These qualities, when adopted & carried out, lead to harm & to suffering".
*   **_ makes _**: Shows a direct causative influence or transformation.
    *   Example: "The enlightened are makers of light".
*   **_ engenders _**: Suggests giving birth to or generating.
    *   Example: "The Tathāgata… engenders the path (previously) unengendered".
*   **_ causes _ to rise / _ rising causes _ to rise**: Implies a direct increase or manifestation.
    *   Example: "Ignorance ebbing causes fabrications to ebb".

**2. Conditions & Prerequisites (A enables B)**
*   **_ as a requisite condition**: A necessary or essential condition for something else to occur. Central to dependent co-arising.
    *   Example: "From ignorance as a requisite condition come fabrications".
*   **Conditioned by _**: States that something is dependent upon a specific condition.
    *   Example: "Conditioned by name-&-form is contact".
*   **Dependent on _**: Expresses reliance or contingency.
    *   Example: "Dependent on the eye & forms there arises eye-consciousness".
*   **_ is rooted in _**: Indicates the fundamental or originating cause.
    *   Example: "All phenomena are rooted in desire".
*   **_ provides the occasion for _**: Signifies enabling or facilitating the arising of something.
    *   Example: "With reference to things in the past that provide the occasion for desire & passion...".
*   **_ is supported by _**: Denotes enabling or strengthening factors.
    *   Example: "This consciousness of mine is supported here and bound up here".
*   **_ as a support for _**: Indicates something that enables the establishment or continuation of another.
    *   Example: "Acts of merit are the support for beings in their after-death world".
*   **hindered by _ & fettered by _**: Describes obstacles or restraints that affect outcomes.
    *   Example: "Beings hindered by ignorance and fettered by craving are transmigrating & wandering on".

**3. Consequences & Results (B from A)**
*   **_ is the origination of _**: States where something begins or comes from.
    *   Example: "From acquisition as cause the many forms of stress come into being in the world".
*   **_ is the result of _**: Direct outcome or consequence.
    *   Example: "Good bodily conduct... these are the rewards one can expect when doing what should be done: One doesn't fault oneself... one reappears in a good destination".
*   **_ follows _**: Implies a direct consequence, often negative.
    *   Example: "If you speak or act with a corrupted heart, then suffering follows you".
*   **_ (are) the fruit/reward of _**: Benefits derived from an action or quality.
    *   Example: "The holy life is lived… for the purpose of crossing over the flood, for making a right end to stress".
*   **_ is what differentiates _**: Highlights distinctions based on actions or qualities.
    *   Example: "Passion carries little blame and is slow to fade. Aversion carries great blame and is quick to fade. Delusion carries great blame and is slow to fade".
*   **_ is the drawback of _**: Negative aspects or disadvantages.
    *   Example: "I foresaw in unskillful qualities drawbacks, degradation, & defilement".

**4. Cessation (Absence of A leads to absence of B)**
*   **From the fading of _ is there _-release**: Denotes liberation through the diminishment of a cause.
    *   Example: "From the fading of passion is there awareness-release".
*   **From the cessation of _ comes the cessation of _**: Direct discontinuation of an effect due to the ending of its cause.
    *   Example: "From the cessation of ignorance is the cessation of effluents".
*   **_ is abandoned / destroyed / cut through**: Indicates the elimination or overcoming of a quality or obstacle.
    *   Example: "Abandon what is unskillful".
*   **_ does not arise / no longer comes about**: Implies prevention or non-occurrence.
    *   Example: "When a monk attends appropriately, unarisen effluents do not arise".
*   **_ is the ending of _**: Marks the completion or termination of something.
    *   Example: "The ending of the effluents is for one who knows & sees".
*   **_ ebbing causes _ to ebb**: Describes a gradual reduction leading to diminishment.
    *   Example: "Ignorance ebbing causes fabrications to ebb".

**5. Purpose & Means (A for B)**
*   **for the purpose of _**: States the intention or goal.
    *   Example: "For the purpose of crossing over the flood, for making a right end to stress".
*   **for the _ of _**: Indicates the aim or objective (e.g., for the purification of, for the sake of).
    *   Example: "For the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation".
*   **by means of _ / through _**: Specifies the method or instrument used to achieve a result.
    *   Example: "The defiled mind is cleansed through the proper technique".
*   **is the way leading to the cessation of _**: Describes the practice or path required for cessation.
    *   Example: "Just this noble eightfold path... is the way leading to the cessation of effluents".
*   **_ should be done / made to arise / developed / pursued / comprehended / abandoned / directly known**: Prescriptive statements indicating actions for specific outcomes.
    *   Example: "Abandon what is unskillful, monks. It is possible to abandon what is unskillful".
*   **train oneself / yourselves**: Instruction for personal development towards a goal.
    *   Example: "Thus you should train yourselves: 'We will relentlessly exert ourselves... there will be no relaxing our persistence'".

**6. Queries & Explanations**
*   **What is the cause, what is the reason, why _?**: Direct questions seeking causal explanations.
    *   Example: "What is the cause, lord, what is the reason, why before there were fewer training rules and yet more monks established in final gnosis…?".
*   **When what exists does _ exist? When what does not exist does _ not exist?**: Inquires about necessary and sufficient conditions.
    *   Example: "When what exists does aging-&-death exist? When what does not exist does aging-&-death not exist?".
*   **Why is that? Because _**: A common explanatory pattern, offering reasons for observed phenomena.
    *   Example: "Why is that? Mother & father do much for their children".
*   **If _ then _**: Conditional statements exploring hypothetical causal relationships.
    *   Example: "If you speak or act with a corrupted heart, then suffering follows you".

**7. Negated Causation**
*   **_ does not / will not _**: Indicates that a cause does not lead to a particular effect.
    *   Example: "Defiled by passion, the mind is not released".
*   **_ is impossible / cannot / is incapable of _**: States an absolute inability or lack of potential for an outcome.
    *   Example: "For him to understand... that is impossible".
*   **_ without _**: Indicates an absence or lack of a necessary component, leading to a negative result.
    *   Example: "Beings are defiled without cause, without requisite condition".
*   **_ is not in vain / not without reward**: Indicates a positive outcome, implying that effort or action was effective.
    *   Example: "The training rules, I tell you, are not in vain".
