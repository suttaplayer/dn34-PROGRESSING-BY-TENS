Here is a comprehensive analysis of the ten groups of 'dhammas' that lead to unfavourable outcomes, drawing upon the provided sources:

### Analysis of Unfavourable Dhammas and Their Antidotes

Many of these 'dhammas' are deeply interconnected, often forming a causal chain that perpetuates suffering. The overarching principle is that **ignorance** is a primary condition for many unfavourable states, leading to fabrications, consciousness, name-and-form, the six sense media, contact, feeling, craving, clinging, and ultimately, birth, aging, death, and stress. **Craving**, in particular, is repeatedly identified as the "ensnarer", the root of all phenomena, and the direct cause of clinging.

#### 1. Desire & Passion (often linked together)

*   **Causes/Requisite Conditions (after Contact):**
    *   **Feeling** is a requisite condition for craving, which then gives rise to desire and passion. This occurs when one relishes, welcomes, or remains fastened to feelings, particularly pleasant ones.
    *   **Sensuality itself** (forms, sounds, aromas, flavors, tactile sensations) can directly occasion desire and passion.
    *   **Intoxication** with youth, health, or life leads to bad bodily, verbal, and mental conduct, indicating underlying desire/passion. Similarly, desire and passion for "things dear & appealing" lead to bad conduct.
    *   **Delighting in sense media** (eye, forms, contact, etc.) is a sign of attachment and leads to stress.
    *   **Absorbed speculation** about this world or the other, or engaging in arguments, can indicate a mind subjugated by desire.
*   **Relationships with other groups:** Desire and passion are closely linked to **craving** (a central element), **sensual desire** (a key hindrance in Group 4), and **obsessions** (Group 5), particularly sensual passion obsession. They are also directly linked to **taking measurement** (Group 9) and **biases** (Group 10).
*   **Specific Antidotes:**
    *   **Abandoning sensual pleasures** is crucial.
    *   **Developing contemplation of the unattractive** (regarding the body) is a specific antidote for lust and passion.
    *   **Discerning the future *kammic* results** of things that arouse desire/passion, then letting them go through discernment.
    *   **Developing immeasurable concentration** rooted in goodwill, compassion, empathetic joy, or equanimity, directly counters passion.
    *   **Right View, Right Resolve, Right Speech, Right Action, Right Livelihood, Right Effort, Right Mindfulness, and Right Concentration** (the Noble Eightfold Path) have the **subduing of passion** as their end-point.
    *   **Focusing on the inconstancy, stress, and not-self nature** of forms, feelings, perceptions, fabrications, and consciousness helps abandon desire-passion.
    *   **Restraining the sense faculties** prevents the mind from being stained by pleasing or unpleasing stimuli, thus preventing the arising of defilements like greed/distress.
    *   **Recollection of the Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, one's virtues, generosity, and devas** strengthens the mind, making it "not overcome with passion, not overcome with aversion, not overcome with delusion".

#### 2. Delight

*   **Causes/Requisite Conditions (after Contact):**
    *   **Craving** makes one relish and thus delight in things.
    *   **Relishing, welcoming, or remaining fastened to feelings** (pleasure, pain, neither-pleasure-nor-pain) directly causes delight to arise.
    *   **Infatuation and captivation** with forms (and other sense objects) lead to defilement, which is connected to delight.
*   **Relationships with other groups:** Directly related to **Craving** (a root cause) and **Relishing, Welcoming & Remaining Fastened to** (Group 3). Its cessation is crucial for the ending of clinging and stress.
*   **Specific Antidotes:**
    *   The **ending of craving** leads to the end of all suffering and stress.
    *   **Not relishing, welcoming, or remaining fastened to feelings** prevents delight from arising, leading to the cessation of clinging and subsequent cessation of stress.
    *   **Disenchantment and dispassion** with various phenomena are effective against delight.
    *   **Delighting in the ending of craving** itself is a skillful redirection.
    *   **Recollection practices** (Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, virtues, generosity, devas) cultivate a mind "not overcome with passion, not overcome with aversion, not overcome with delusion," thereby fostering Dhamma-connected joy, rapture, calm, and concentration, which are distinct from delusive sensual delight.

#### 3. Relishing, Welcoming & Remaining Fastened to

*   **Causes/Requisite Conditions (after Contact):**
    *   Occurs when there is **ignorance of the origination, passing away, allure, drawback, and escape from feeling**.
    *   Driven by **infatuation with pleasing ideas** or distress over unpleasing ones.
    *   **Sensual desire** and general craving are underlying drivers.
*   **Relationships with other groups:** This quality directly leads to the arising of **delight** (Group 2). It is also how **passion-obsession** and **resistance-obsession** (elements of Group 5) become ingrained.
*   **Specific Antidotes:**
    *   **Discerning the origination, passing away, allure, drawback, and escape from feelings**.
    *   **Not relishing, welcoming, or remaining fastened to feelings**.
    *   **Abandoning passion-obsession, resistance-obsession, and ignorance-obsession**.
    *   **Developing awareness-release and discernment-release** where evil, unskillful qualities cease.

#### 4. Decomposed Defilements (e.g., Greed, Ill Will, Anger, Conceit, Heedlessness)

*   **Causes/Requisite Conditions (after Contact):**
    *   **Greed, aversion, and delusion** are the fundamental "roots of what is unskillful". They are described as internal stains, enemies, and murderers that destroy the person from whom they arise.
    *   **Lack of guarding sense faculties** (lack of restraint) allows evil, unskillful qualities like greed and distress to assail the mind.
    *   **Lack of moderation in food** also contributes to these defilements.
    *   **Wrong view** is particularly destructive, leading to a cascade of wrong resolves, speech, actions, and other unskillful activities.
    *   A chain of negative conditions leads to these: **not associating with people of integrity** leads to not hearing true Dhamma, then lack of conviction, inappropriate attention, lack of mindfulness and alertness, lack of restraint of the senses, the three forms of misconduct, the five hindrances, and finally ignorance.
    *   **Self-aggrandizement, discontent, entanglement, laziness, confused mindfulness, unconcentrated mind, weak discernment, and delight in objectification** are all aspects of unskillful states of mind.
    *   **Stinginess** (as to monastery, family, gains, status, Dhamma) prevents entry into *jhāna* and realization of noble fruits.
*   **Relationships with other groups:** Greed, aversion, and delusion are often the underlying "roots" (Mūla Sutta) of many other defilements and lead to **unskillful actions** (bodily, verbal, mental), and they are described as "a making of measurement" (Group 9). They also contribute to the **five hindrances** (sensual desire, ill will, sloth & drowsiness, restlessness & anxiety, uncertainty), which are themselves mind defilements that weaken discernment.
*   **Specific Antidotes:**
    *   **Abandoning greed, aversion, and delusion** is paramount for ending suffering.
    *   The **Noble Eightfold Path** is the primary method for their abandonment.
    *   **Practicing good bodily, verbal, and mental conduct**.
    *   **Goodwill, compassion, empathetic joy, and equanimity** as awareness-releases are specific antidotes for ill will and other defilements.
    *   **Appropriate attention** and **non-delusion** counter delusion.
    *   **Discerning drawbacks of unskillful thoughts** leads to their abandonment.
    *   **Modesty, contentment, reclusiveness, aroused persistence, established mindfulness, concentrated mind, discernment, enjoying non-objectification, and being unburdensome** are positive qualities to cultivate.
    *   **Abandoning the five hindrances**.
    *   **Cultivating virtue and views made straight** as a basis for mindfulness development.
    *   **Respect and deference** for the Teacher, Dhamma, Saṅgha, training, and heedfulness.
    *   **Nine ways of subduing hatred** (e.g., "He has done me harm. But what should I expect?").
    *   **Abandoning all forms of stinginess**.

#### 5. Obsessions (Sensual Passion, Resistance, Views, Uncertainty, Conceit, Passion for Becoming, Ignorance)

*   **Causes/Requisite Conditions (after Contact):**
    *   **Ignorance** is the cause by which the effluents (which include some obsessions like sensual, becoming, and ignorance) come into play.
    *   **Relishing, welcoming, or remaining fastened to feelings** causes passion and resistance obsessions.
    *   **Not discerning the origination, passing away, allure, drawback, or escape from feeling** causes ignorance obsession.
    *   **Relishing, welcoming, or remaining fastened to "perceptions & categories of objectification"** leads to the arising of all seven obsessions.
    *   **Clinging or sustenance** also contributes to the conditions where obsessions manifest.
*   **Relationships with other groups:** These are directly linked to **ignoring the true nature of feelings and objectification** (Groups 3 and 8). The eradication of obsessions is a hallmark of liberation and is directly tied to cutting off **craving** and breaking through **conceit** (elements within Group 4 and also a separate obsession).
*   **Specific Antidotes:**
    *   **Ending of obsessions** is achieved by cutting through craving, ripping off the fetter, and rightly breaking through conceit.
    *   **Not relishing, welcoming, or remaining fastened to feelings**.
    *   **Discerning the origination, passing away, allure, drawback, and escape from feelings**.
    *   **Abandoning ignorance and giving rise to clear knowing** is fundamental to ending obsessions.
    *   **Developing factors for awakening** (mindfulness, analysis of qualities, persistence, rapture, calm, concentration, equanimity) "dependent on seclusion, dispassion, cessation, resulting in letting go".
    *   **Focusing on arising & passing away with regard to the five clinging-aggregates** obliterates the lingering residual "I am" conceit, "I am" desire, and "I am" obsession.
    *   **Directly knowing that "all dhammas are unworthy of attachment"** leads to comprehending every *dhamma* and seeing all themes as separate, abandoning ignorance and giving rise to clear knowing.

#### 6. Clingings (Sensuality, Views, Habits & Precepts, Doctrines of the Self)

*   **Causes/Requisite Conditions (after Contact):**
    *   **Craving** is the direct cause of clinging.
    *   **Desire & passion** with regard to the five clinging-aggregates are themselves clinging.
    *   **Relishing, welcoming, and remaining fastened to feelings** leads to delight, which then leads to clinging.
    *   **Ignorance** is the root of craving, which leads to clinging.
    *   **Assuming form/feeling/perception/fabrications/consciousness to be the self** (for an uninstructed person) leads to agitation arising from clinging.
*   **Relationships with other groups:** Clinging is presented as a direct consequence of **craving** and a necessary condition for **becoming** and thus for the cycle of birth, aging, and death. It is intimately related to **desire & passion** (Group 1) and **relishing** (Group 3).
*   **Specific Antidotes:**
    *   The **cessation of craving** directly leads to the cessation of clinging.
    *   The **Noble Eightfold Path** is the way leading to the cessation of craving and thus clinging.
    *   **Discerning the origination, passing away, allure, and drawbacks of feelings, along with the escape from feelings**, leads to release through lack of clinging.
    *   **Subduing desire-passion** for forms, feelings, etc., is the "escape" from them.
    *   **Focusing on the drawbacks of clingable phenomena** causes craving to cease, which then eliminates clinging.
    *   **Not assuming form/feeling/perception/fabrications/consciousness to be the self** leads to non-agitation.
    *   **Realizing that acquisitions are the root of suffering** is a crucial insight.
    *   **Seeing dependent co-arising** and understanding the five clinging-aggregates as dependently co-arisen helps subdue desire and passion for them, leading to the cessation of stress.
    *   **Developing perceptions of inconstancy, stress, and not-self** with regard to the five clinging-aggregates is directly beneficial for realizing stream-entry and ending the "I am" conceit.

#### 7. Obstructions (Present Kamma, Defilement, Result-of-Past-Kamma, Lack of Conviction, No Desire to Listen, Dull Discernment)

*   **Causes/Requisite Conditions (after Contact):**
    *   Specific grave **kamma obstructions** include killing parents or an arahant, causing a Tathāgata's blood to flow, or causing a split in the Saṅgha.
    *   **Defilement obstructions** are primarily passion, aversion, and delusion.
    *   **Lack of conviction, no desire (to listen), and dull discernment** are direct causes of this type of obstruction.
    *   **Being hard to instruct** and **evil friendship** contribute to decline.
    *   The chain of negativity starting from **not associating with people of integrity** directly leads to "lack of conviction" and "dull discernment" as part of a larger cascade towards ignorance.
    *   **Indolence & lack of initiative**, as well as **unwillingness to listen or engage in questioning**, are direct obstacles to discernment and acquiring Dhamma.
*   **Relationships with other groups:** Defilement obstructions are essentially **greed, aversion, and delusion** (Group 4). Lack of conviction and dull discernment are worsened by associating with **evil friendships** (related to Group 4).
*   **Specific Antidotes:**
    *   Cultivating the **opposite qualities**: conviction, desire (to listen), and discernment enable one to alight on skillful qualities.
    *   **Associating with people of integrity** is a critical antidote, leading to hearing the true Dhamma, conviction, appropriate attention, mindfulness and alertness, restraint of the senses, good conduct, the four establishings of mindfulness, the seven factors for awakening, and ultimately clear knowing and release.
    *   **Non-indolence & initiative** are beneficial.
    *   **Willingness to listen and engage in questioning**.
    *   **Commitment & reflection** on the Dhamma.

#### 8. Objectification

*   **Causes/Requisite Conditions (after Contact):**
    *   **Relishing, welcoming, or remaining fastened to perceptions & categories of objectification** causes the seven obsessions (Group 5) to arise.
    *   Unskillful thoughts (sensuality, ill will, harmfulness) lead to these perceptions.
*   **Relationships with other groups:** Directly linked to **obsessions** (Group 5). The concept of "I-making" and "mine-making" conceit-obsession (also an obsession) implies objectification of a self or things belonging to a self.
*   **Specific Antidotes:**
    *   **Not relishing, welcoming, or remaining fastened to perceptions & categories of objectification** is the means to end obsessions and disputes.
    *   **Enjoying and delighting in non-objectification** is a thought of a great person.
    *   **Directing the mind to the deathless property** (which is "non-fashioning") is a way to overcome conceit related to jhānas, implying a release from objectification.
    *   **Entering and remaining in internal emptiness** (a mental dwelling) helps detach the mind from themes.
    *   **Developing the four right exertions** for abandoning unskillful qualities and cultivating skillful ones helps in countering objectification.
    *   The **Noble Eightfold Path** leads to release from craving and clinging, which underpin objectification.

#### 9. Taking Measurement

*   **Causes/Requisite Conditions (after Contact):**
    *   **Passion, aversion, and delusion** are explicitly stated to be "a making of measurement".
    *   **Conceit** ("I'm better, not better. I'm worse. I'm like that") is a direct form of taking measurement.
    *   **Self-exaltation and disparagement of others** based on external factors like family, renown, gains, or being a Dhamma-speaker are qualities of "a person of no integrity" that involve taking measurement.
*   **Relationships with other groups:** Closely linked to **passion, aversion, and delusion** (elements of Group 4). Conceit is also one of the **obsessions** (Group 5) and **higher fetters**.
*   **Specific Antidotes:**
    *   **Abandoning passion, aversion, and delusion**, which are "destroyed at their root". This is achieved through the Noble Eightfold Path.
    *   Cultivating an **unprovoked awareness-release** that is "empty of passion, empty of aversion, empty of delusion".
    *   **Rightly breaking through conceit** is necessary for ending suffering and stress.
    *   **Uprooting the conceit, 'I am'** through the perception of inconstancy and not-self.
    *   A **person of integrity** prioritizes Dhamma practice over external distinctions, neither exalting themselves nor disparaging others based on family, renown, gains, or being a Dhamma-speaker. They also recognize "non-fashioning" even in jhāna.

#### 10. Biases (Desire, Aversion, Fear, Delusion)

*   **Causes/Requisite Conditions (after Contact):**
    *   These are listed as the four ways of "going off course".
    *   **Lack of right gnosis** in an *arahant* (though *arahants* are stated to be incapable of following bias) indicates that unabandoned defilements would lead to these biases.
*   **Relationships with other groups:** These are directly linked to **desire, aversion, and delusion** (Group 4). An *arahant's* inability to follow these biases signifies the complete destruction of **effluents** (which include passion, aversion, delusion).
*   **Specific Antidotes:**
    *   **Not going off course through desire, aversion, delusion, or fear** leads to increased honor.
    *   An **arahant** is described as "incapable of giving rise to" or "following" these biases, due to their effluents being ended and being released through right gnosis.
    *   The **Noble Eightfold Path** aims at the subduing of passion, aversion, and delusion.

This comprehensive analysis highlights the interconnected nature of these unfavourable 'dhammas' and the holistic approach of the Dhamma in providing specific, interconnected practices for their abandonment, leading towards unbinding and the end of suffering.

Here are the antidotes for the 16 defilements of the mind, drawing on information from the sources:

*   **Possessiveness-and-inordinate-greed**:
    *   The sources state that by **knowing** that possessiveness-and-inordinate-greed is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   More broadly, **lack of greed** is identified as a root of what is skillful.
    *   An ungreedy person avoids wrongly inflicting suffering on others.
    *   Many evil, unskillful qualities born of greed are abandoned when greed is gone.
    *   Greed is considered an "inside stain, inside enemy, inside foe, inside murderer, inside adversary". **Abandoning greed** means one feels no greed for what would merit greed, shedding it like water off a lotus leaf.
    *   **Dispassion** is a key antidote to passion/greed, leading to release.
    *   Developing **contemplation of the unattractive** is a practice for abandoning lust or passion.
    *   Cleansing the mind of covetousness is achieved by **abandoning covetousness** and dwelling with an awareness devoid of it.
    *   The **Noble Eightfold Path** is a path to abandoning passion.
    *   Cultivating an awareness imbued with **goodwill, compassion, empathetic joy, or equanimity** (the sublime attitudes) helps cleanse the mind of covetousness and ill will.
    *   A generous person's awareness is cleansed of the stain of stinginess. The stain of selfishness can be overcome by **giving and sharing**.

*   **Ill will**:
    *   By **knowing** that ill will is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   **Non-ill will** is identified as a root of what is skillful.
    *   Abandoning ill will leads to many skillful qualities.
    *   Ill will is an "inside stain, inside enemy, inside foe, inside murderer, inside adversary". **Abandoning aversion** leads to feeling no aversion for what would merit aversion.
    *   The mind free from aversion is **well released**.
    *   One cleanses their mind of ill will and anger by **abandoning them** and dwelling with an awareness devoid of ill will, sympathetic to the welfare of all living beings.
    *   **Goodwill** (mettā) is the means of escape from ill will. Developing goodwill is specifically for abandoning ill will.
    *   The **Noble Eightfold Path** helps abandon aversion.
    *   The fire of aversion is put out with **goodwill**.

*   **Anger**:
    *   **Developing goodwill** for an individual is a primary way to subdue hatred (anger).
    *   Other methods to subdue hatred include developing **compassion**, **equanimity**, **paying no mind and no attention** to the thoughts, and **directing thoughts to the fact of the individual being the product of their actions**.
    *   The **fire of anger** is quenched with self-control, discernment, persistence, and right view.
    *   Restraint in mind helps guard against anger.
    *   Abandoning anger is a skillful quality.

*   **Spitefulness**:
    *   By **knowing** that spitefulness is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   The cultivation of **goodwill** and the absence of ill will would serve as antidotes, as these promote harmonious interactions.

*   **Dismissiveness**:
    *   By **knowing** that dismissiveness is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   Being **easy to admonish/instruct** is a contrasting quality.

*   **Antagonism**:
    *   By **knowing** that antagonism is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   Conditions conducive to **amiability, lack of disputes, harmony, and a state of unity** serve as antidotes.

*   **Envy**:
    *   By **knowing** that envy is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   A person of integrity is characterized as **not envious**.
    *   **Lack of envy** is also associated with those who attain stream-entry.

*   **Selfishness**:
    *   By **knowing** that selfishness is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   **Generosity** is a direct antidote. A disciple of the noble ones lives with awareness cleansed of the stain of stinginess, is freely generous, openhanded, and delights in distributing alms.
    *   Beings would not eat without having given, nor would the stain of selfishness overcome their minds, if they knew the results of giving and sharing.

*   **Deceit**:
    *   By **knowing** that deceit is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   **Truthfulness** and abstaining from deceit are mentioned as skillful qualities.
    *   Abstaining from forms of scheming and persuading are part of virtue.

*   **Pretentiousness**:
    *   By **knowing** that pretentiousness is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   **Modesty** is a contrasting quality, as the Dhamma is for one who is modest, not self-aggrandizing.

*   **Stubbornness**:
    *   By **knowing** that stubbornness is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   Being **easy to instruct** is a beneficial quality.

*   **Competitiveness**:
    *   By **knowing** that competitiveness is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   The development of **amiability, harmony, and lack of disputes** can serve as an antidote.

*   **Conceit**:
    *   By **knowing** that conceit is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   **Rightly breaking through conceit** leads to the end of suffering and stress.
    *   Discerning that "This is not mine. This is not my self. This is not what I am" is crucial for overcoming conceit.
    *   Developing the **perception of inconstancy** makes the perception of not-self firm, which in turn uproots the conceit "I am".
    *   **Casting out conceit** through pursuing disenchantment and developing the theme-less concentration is also mentioned.
    *   Letting go of conceit and its destruction leads to release.
    *   **Humility** and refraining from exalting oneself or disparaging others are important practices.
    *   The Dhamma is explicitly for those who are **modest**, not self-aggrandizing.

*   **Arrogance**:
    *   By **knowing** that arrogance is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   Similar to conceit, **humility** and **modesty** are the counter-qualities.

*   **Intoxication**:
    *   By **knowing** that intoxication is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   Practicing **heedfulness** is a direct antidote, particularly against "intoxication with youth/health/life".
    *   **Abstaining from distilled and fermented drinks that cause heedlessness** is a fundamental training rule for lay followers and monks.

*   **Heedlessness**:
    *   By **knowing** that heedlessness is a defilement of the mind, a monk **abandons** it.
    *   **Heedfulness** is presented as a fundamental quality that should be exercised.
    *   Specific practices for developing heedfulness include:
        *   **Abandoning bodily, verbal, and mental misconduct**, and developing good conduct in these areas.
        *   **Guarding the sense faculties** (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, intellect) so that the mind does not become impassioned, averse, deluded, or intoxicated by external stimuli.
        *   **Knowing moderation in food**.
        *   Being **devoted to wakefulness** and cleansing the mind of hindering qualities throughout the day and night.
        *   Being possessed of **mindfulness and alertness** in all actions (going, returning, standing, sitting, etc.).
        *   Heedfulness, initiative, restraint, and self-control help build a strong foundation for spiritual progress.
    *   Heedfulness, when established, leads to the well-development of the five faculties (conviction, persistence, mindfulness, concentration, discernment).
