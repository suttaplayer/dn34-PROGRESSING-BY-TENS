### How to Practice Guide

This guide compiles practical advice and methods for cultivating essential qualities and progressing on the path to liberation, based on the Dhamma as expounded in the sources.

#### 1. How to Practice "Awakening to the Truth"
To awaken to the truth, a step-by-step process of engagement with the Dhamma is outlined:
*   **Cultivate Conviction**: When conviction has arisen, it is the initial step.
*   **Visit a Teacher**: Having conviction, visit a teacher or respectable companion.
*   **Grow Close**: After visiting, grow close to the teacher/companion.
*   **Lend Ear**: Having grown close, lend ear to the teachings.
*   **Hear the Dhamma**: Actively listen to the Dhamma.
*   **Remember the Dhamma**: After hearing, remember the Dhamma.
*   **Penetrate Meaning**: Remembering the Dhamma, penetrate the meaning of the teachings.
*   **Ponder Teachings**: Penetrating the meaning, come to an agreement through pondering the teachings.
*   **Arouse Desire**: When agreement through pondering arises, desire for practice arises.
*   **Be Willing**: When desire has arisen, be willing to practice.
*   **Contemplate**: Be willing, then contemplate the teachings.
*   **Make Exertion**: Having contemplated, make an exertion in practice.
*   **Realize & See**: Having made an exertion, realize the ultimate truth with the body and penetrate it with discernment.

#### 2. How to Practice "For One in Training" (MN 53)
A monk "in training" (Sekha) is one who has not yet reached their heart's goal but still aspires for unexcelled freedom from bondage, and thus has a task to do with heedfulness. To "break out, awaken, and attain supreme rest from the yoke," one should practice:
*   **Be Consummate in Virtue**: Dwell restrained in accordance with the Pāṭimokkha, be consummate in behavior & sphere of activity, and train oneself, having undertaken the training rules, seeing danger in the slightest faults.
*   **Guard Sense Faculties**: When seeing a form with the eye, do not grasp at any theme or variations that might lead to evil, unskillful qualities like greed or distress. Practice for restraint, protect the faculty, and achieve restraint (similarly for ear, nose, tongue, body, and intellect).
*   **Know Moderation in Eating**: Consume food for sufficient support of the holy life, not for play, intoxication, or adornment.
*   **Be Devoted to Wakefulness**: During the day and the first watch of the night, sit and pace back and forth, cleansing the mind of hindering qualities. During the second watch, recline in the lion's posture, mindful, alert, with the mind set on getting up. During the last watch, return to sitting and pacing, cleansing the mind.
*   **Be Endowed with Mindfulness & Alertness**: Act with alertness when going forward and returning, looking toward and away, bending and extending limbs, carrying robes and bowl, eating, drinking, chewing, tasting, urinating, defecating, walking, standing, sitting, falling asleep, waking up, talking, and remaining silent.
*   **Attain Jhānas**: Strive to obtain, at will, without trouble or difficulty, the four jhānas (heightened mental states, pleasant abidings in the here & now).
*   **Achieve Release**: Ultimately, through these practices, enter & remain in the effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release, having directly known & realized them for oneself right in the here & now.

#### 3. How to Practice as a Contemplative (MN 39)
The practice of a contemplative involves:
*   **Abstaining from Wrong Livelihood**: This includes avoiding 'animal' arts.
*   **Virtuous Conduct**: Being virtuous, dwelling restrained in accordance with the Pāṭimokkha, consummate in behavior & sphere of activity, and training in rules, seeing danger in the slightest faults.
*   **Guarding Sense Faculties**: On seeing a form with the eye, not grasping at any theme or variations by which evil, unskillful qualities might assail oneself. This involves practicing for restraint and protecting the faculty (and similarly for other senses).
*   **Moderation in Eating**: Taking food for sufficient support of the holy life, not for play, intoxication, or adornment.
*   **Devotion to Wakefulness**: During the day and the first watch of the night, sitting and pacing back and forth to cleanse the mind of qualities that would hold it in check. During the second watch of the night, reclining in the lion's posture, mindful, alert, with the mind set on getting up. During the last watch, sitting and pacing back and forth, cleansing the mind.
*   **Possession of Mindfulness & Alertness**: Acting with alertness in all daily activities such as going forward and returning, looking, bending and extending limbs, carrying robes, eating, drinking, urinating, defecating, walking, standing, sitting, falling asleep, waking up, talking, and remaining silent.
*   **Attainment of Jhānas**: Attaining, whenever desired and without difficulty, the four jhānas, which are heightened mental states and pleasant abidings in the here & now.
*   **Realization of Release**: Through the ending of effluents, entering and remaining in the effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release, having directly known and realized them for oneself right in the here & now.

*   **Purity-of-knowledge-&-vision-of-what-is-&-is-not the-path factor of exertion for full purity**.
*   **Purity-of-knowledge-&-vision-of-the-way factor of exertion for full purity**.

#### 6. How to Acquire Discernment: "Eight Causes, Eight Requisite Conditions" (DN 34)
These eight causes and requisite conditions lead to acquiring unacquired discernment and to its increase, plenitude, development, and culmination:
*   **Live in Apprenticeship**: Live in apprenticeship to the Teacher or a respectable companion in the holy life in whom a strong sense of shame & compunction, love, & respect has been established.
*   **Quiz & Cross-Question**: Approach the Teacher or companion at appropriate times to quiz & cross-question them on the meaning of statements, allowing them to reveal what is hidden, make plain what is obscure, and dispel perplexity.
*   **Achieve Twofold Seclusion**: Having heard the Dhamma, achieve seclusion in body & seclusion in mind.
*   **Be Virtuous**: Dwell restrained in accordance with the Pāṭimokkha, be consummate in behavior & sphere of activity, and train oneself, having undertaken the training rules, seeing danger in the slightest faults.
*   **Have Heard Much**: Retain, store, listen to often, discuss, accumulate, examine with the mind, and well-penetrate in terms of views, those teachings that are admirable in the beginning, middle, and end, proclaiming the holy life entirely perfect and surpassingly pure.
*   **Keep Persistence Aroused**: Generate desire, endeavor, activate persistence, uphold & exert intent for the non-arising and abandoning of unskillful qualities, and the arising and development of skillful qualities.
*   **Be Mindful**: Be mindful, endowed with excellent proficiency in mindfulness, remembering & able to call to mind even things done & said long ago.
*   **Be Discerning**: Be endowed with discernment of arising & passing away—noble, penetrating, leading to the right ending of stress.

#### 7. How to Develop "Five-Factored Right Concentration" (DN 34)
The five factors for developing right concentration are:
*   **Suffusion with rapture**.
*   **Suffusion with pleasure**.
*   **Suffusion with awareness**.
*   **Suffusion with light**.
*   **The theme of reflection**.

#### 8. How to Make "Six Persevering Dwellings" Arise (DN 34)
The specific phrase "Six persevering dwellings" is not explicitly found under the "made to arise" category for "SIXES" in DN 34. However, the sources describe "Six objects of recollection" which are also referred to as "dwelling places (for the mind)". These are qualities one should develop to straighten the mind, leading to joy, rapture, calm, and concentration.
To make these "dwelling places for the mind" arise, one should recollect the following, ensuring the mind is not overcome by passion, aversion, or delusion, and heads straight based on the recollection:
*   **Recollection of the Tathāgata (Buddha)**: Recalling the Blessed One as worthy, rightly self-awakened, consummate in clear-knowing & conduct, well-gone, expert with regard to the cosmos, unexcelled trainer, teacher of devas & human beings, awakened, blessed. This recollection should be developed while walking, standing, sitting, lying down, busy at work, or resting in a crowded home.
*   **Recollection of the Dhamma**: Recalling the Dhamma as well taught by the Blessed One, to be seen here & now, timeless, inviting verification, pertinent, to be experienced by the observant for themselves. This recollection should be developed in all activities.
*   **Recollection of the Saṅgha**: Recalling the Saṅgha of the Blessed One's disciples as having practiced well, straightforwardly, methodically, masterfully; deserving of gifts, hospitality, offerings, respect, and being the incomparable field of merit for the world. This recollection should be developed in all activities.
*   **Recollection of one's own virtues**: Recalling one's virtues as untorn, unbroken, unspotted, unsplattered, liberating, praised by the observant, ungrasped at, conducive to concentration. This recollection should be developed in all activities.
*   **Recollection of one's own generosity**: Recalling one's free generosity, openhandedness, delight in magnanimity, responsiveness to requests, and delight in alms distribution, especially among people overcome with possessiveness. This recollection should be developed in all activities.
*   **Recollection of the devas**: Recalling the conviction, virtue, learning, generosity, and discernment of devas (e.g., of the Four Great Kings, Thirty-three, Hours, Contented, Delighting in Creation, Wielding Power over Creations of Others, Brahmā's Retinue, and beyond), and recognizing similar qualities in oneself. This recollection should be developed in all activities.

#### 9. How to be "Seven Grounds for Praise" (DN 34)
To be praised on these seven grounds, one should have keen desire and not discard love for undertaking them:
*   **Undertaking the training**.
*   **Attending carefully to the Dhamma**.
*   **Subduing one's wants**.
*   **Finding solitude**.
*   **Arousing persistence**.
*   **Being mindful & adept**.
*   **Penetrating (right) view**.

#### 10. Practices with Respect to Self-Reflection
Self-reflection is a crucial aspect of Dhamma practice:
*   **Dhamma as Governing Principle**: Reflect on why one has gone forth from home life into homelessness (not for robes, almsfood, lodgings, or future becoming, but because beset by birth, aging, death, sorrows, etc., hoping for the end of suffering). Realize that being lazy & heedless is not fitting. This reflection leads to aroused persistence, established mindfulness, calm body, and a centered mind, resulting in abandoning unskillful and developing skillful qualities.
*   **Inner Scrutiny**: Conduct an inner scrutiny by asking, "The many-faceted, multifarious stress of aging-&-death that arises in the world has what as its cause, what as its origination, what as its source, what as that which brings it into play?" Discern its cause, origination, cessation, and the path to cessation. This is practicing for the total right ending of stress.
*   **Self-Referential Dhamma Sequence**: Reflect on the principle: "I love life & don't love death. I love happiness & abhor pain. If I—loving life & not loving death, loving happiness & abhorring pain—were to be killed, that would be displeasing & disagreeable to me. And if I were to kill another who loves life & doesn't love death, who loves happiness & abhors pain, that would be displeasing & disagreeable to the other. What is displeasing & disagreeable to me is displeasing & disagreeable to others. How can I inflict on others what is displeasing & disagreeable to me?". This reflection leads to refraining from taking life, getting others to refrain, and praising refraining, thus purifying bodily behavior. This principle applies to abstaining from stealing, sexual misconduct, lying, divisive speech, harsh speech, and idle chatter, and to developing generosity and right livelihood.
*   **Contemplation of Unattractiveness**: Remain focused on unattractiveness with regard to the body.
*   **Perception of Death**: Establish the perception of death well within oneself.
*   **Perception of Loathsomeness in Food**: Be percipient of loathsomeness in food.
*   **Perception of Distaste for Every World**: Be percipient of distaste for every world.
*   **Focus on Inconstancy**: Remain focused on inconstancy with regard to all fabrications.
*   **Recollection of Virtues**: Recall one's own virtues, knowing them to be untorn, unbroken, unspotted, unsplattered, liberating, praised by the observant, ungrasped at, and conducive to concentration.
*   **Recollection of Generosity**: Recall one's own generosity, recognizing it as a great gain to live with an awareness cleansed of possessiveness, being freely generous, openhanded, delighting in magnanimity, responsive to requests, and delighting in the distribution of alms.
*   **Self-Reflection (on failings and attainments)**: Periodically reflect on:
    *   **One's own failings**
    *   **Failings of others**
    *   **One's own attainments**
    *   **Attainments of others**


#### 11. Practice of Investigation
Investigation in Dhamma is crucial for understanding and progress:
*   **Cross-Questioning Dhamma**: Monks should cross-question one another about discourses, dissecting them: "How is this? What is the meaning of this?" to make open what isn't open, make plain what isn't plain, and dispel doubt.
*   **Seeking Guidance from Learned Monks**: Approach learned monks at regular intervals and ask & question them, "How is this, venerable sirs? What is the meaning of this?" to clarify hidden or unclear points and dispel doubts.
*   **Thinking & Evaluating Dhamma**: Think about and evaluate the Dhamma as heard and remembered, contemplating it with the intellect.
*   **Mental Examination**: In one's awareness, direct thoughts to the Dhamma in detail, evaluate it, and mentally examine it. This leads to sensitivity to meaning, joy, rapture, calm, and concentration.
*   **Three Modes of Investigation**: Investigate in terms of properties, sense media, and dependent co-arising.
*   **Analysis of Qualities**: As a factor for awakening, this involves investigating, carefully attending to, and examining internal and external qualities with discernment.
*   **Discernment**: Scrutinize all Dhamma with discernment for the right ending of suffering & stress.

#### 12. Unique "You Should Train Yourselves:" Instructions
The following are direct instructions on how practitioners should train themselves, as found in the sources:
*   **"Gladly would we let the flesh & blood in our bodies dry up, leaving just the skin, tendons, & bones, but if we have not attained what can be reached through manly firmness, manly persistence, manly striving, there will be no relaxing our persistence.' That's how you should train yourselves."**
*   **"'We will be peaceful in our faculties & peaceful in our hearts.' That's how you should train yourselves. When you are peaceful in your faculties & peaceful in your hearts, peaceful will be your bodily actions, peaceful your verbal actions, peaceful your mental actions. [Thinking,] 'We will render only peaceful service to our fellows in the holy life': That's how you should train yourselves."**
*   **"'We will avoid the three things that, endowed with which, one is to be recognized as a fool. We will undertake & maintain the three things that, endowed with which, one is to be recognized as a wise person.' That's how you should train yourselves."**
*   **"'There will be no I-making or mine-making conceit-obsession with regard to this conscious body. There will be no I-making or mine-making conceit-obsession with regard to all external themes [topics of concentration]. We will enter & remain in the awareness-release & discernment-release where there is no I-making or my-making conceit-obsession for one entering & remaining in it.' That's how you should train yourselves."**
*   **"'Strong will be our desire for undertaking the training in heightened virtue; strong will be our desire for undertaking the training in heightened mind; strong will be our desire for undertaking the training in heightened discernment.' That is how you should train yourselves."**
*   **"'We will dwell heedfully. We will develop mindfulness of death acutely for the sake of ending the effluents.' That is how you should train yourselves."**
*   **"Being Dhamma-devotee monks, we will speak in praise of jhāna monks.' That's how you should train yourselves."**
*   **"Being jhāna monks, we will speak in praise of Dhamma-devotee monks.' That's how you should train yourselves."**
*   **"'We will be easy to admonish and make ourselves easy to admonish purely out of esteem for the Dhamma, respect for the Dhamma, reverence for the Dhamma.' That's how you should train yourselves."**
*   **"'Our awareness-release through good-will will be cultivated, developed, pursued, given a means of transport, given a grounding, steadied, consolidated, & well-undertaken. That's how you should train yourselves."**
*   **"'We will listen when discourses that are words of the Tathāgata—deep, deep in their meaning, transcendent, connected with emptiness—are being recited. We will lend ear, will set our hearts on knowing them, will regard these teachings as worth grasping & mastering.' That's how you should train yourselves."**
