This integration emphasizes the progressive nature of the path, highlighting foundational ethical conduct and the cultivation of mental discipline as steps towards ultimate liberation.

###### A Guide to Dhamma Practice

**1. Foundational Principles for Living the Holy Life**
The holy life is lived for the ultimate purpose of **direct knowledge and full comprehension**, aiming to **know, see, attain, realize, and break through** to what has been unknown, unseen, unattained, and unrealized. This specifically includes understanding **stress, its origination, cessation, and the path to its cessation**. The practice is **for the right ending of suffering & stress**, and for **purification, overcoming sorrow and lamentation, and realizing unbinding**. It is not for deceiving, inveigling, or seeking worldly gains and tribute.

For monks and brahmans, claiming to be "contemplatives" or "brahmans" means they should **undertake and practice qualities that make one a contemplative and a brahman**, so their designation and claim will be true and accurate. This ensures that the services (robes, alms-food, lodging, medicinal requisites) provided by supporters will bring them great fruit and reward, making the act of going forth fruitful and fertile.

*   **Benefit for Many**: Practice for the **welfare and happiness of many people**, including human beings and devas. Explaining Dhamma as Dhamma and not-Dhamma as not-Dhamma serves this purpose and helps the True Dhamma to remain.
*   **Skillful vs. Unskillful**: **Abandon what is unskillful** because it leads to harm and pain. **Develop what is skillful** as it is conducive to benefit and pleasure. When one's unskillful mental qualities decline and skillful ones increase, the practice is fruitful. A monk who has abandoned passion, aversion, and delusion will not do anything unskillful or engage in any evil.
    *   **Abandoning Defilements**: A monk does not practice properly if possessiveness, ill will, anger, spitefulness, dismissiveness, antagonism, envy, selfishness, deceit, pretentiousness, evil wishes, or wrong view have not been abandoned. Conversely, a monk practices properly when these stains, flaws, and defects are abandoned.
*   **Self-Reflection**: Periodically **reflect on your own failings and attainments**, as well as those of others.
*   **Heedfulness (Appamada)**: **Exercise heedfulness** in abandoning misconduct and developing good conduct and right view. Heedfulness is the root of all skillful qualities, converging in it and being considered foremost. It is crucial to prevent regret. Monks in higher training have a **task to do with heedfulness** for realizing the supreme goal. One should dwell heedful, ardent, & resolute to release the mind or end effluents.

**2. Ethical Conduct (Virtue)**
The initial foundation of the holy life involves perfecting ethical conduct, which extends beyond mere rules to a deep internal purity.

*   **Consummate in Virtue**: A monk is virtuous, dwelling restrained in accordance with the Pāṭimokkha, consummate in behavior & sphere of activity, seeing danger in the slightest fault. This virtue should be **pure, clear & open, unbroken & restrained**.
*   **Shame & Compunction (Hiri & Ottappa)**: These two **bright qualities guard the world** and are essential for ethical conduct. Their presence enables heedfulness and their absence leads to lack of restraint. A monk should ensure that their bodily, verbal, and mental conduct, and livelihood are pure, clear, open, unbroken, and restrained, and should not exalt themselves or disparage others on account of this.
*   **Good Bodily, Verbal, Mental Conduct**: These should be done. They are forms of sagacity.
    *   One should reflect on bodily, verbal, and mental actions to determine if they lead to self-affliction, affliction of others, or both, or if they are unskillful with painful results. If so, they should be given up.
    *   If such actions have already been done and were unskillful, one should confess them, reveal them, and lay them open to the Teacher or an observant companion, then exercise restraint in the future.
    *   If actions are skillful, one should stay mentally refreshed and joyful, training day and night in skillful qualities.
*   **Pure Livelihood**: Livelihood should be pure, clear, open, unbroken, and restrained. Abandoning dishonest livelihood leads to right livelihood.
*   **Restraint of the Sense Faculties**: This is crucial to prevent unskillful qualities like greed or distress from arising. Upon seeing a form, hearing a sound, smelling an aroma, tasting a flavor, feeling a tactile sensation, or cognizing an idea, a monk should **not grasp at any theme or variations** that would lead to unskillful qualities. They should practice for its restraint, guard the faculty, and achieve restraint. This ensures peace in faculties.
*   **Moderation in Eating**: A monk should know moderation in eating.
*   **Devotion to Wakefulness**: During the day and the first watch of the night (dusk to 10 pm), a monk should sit and pace back and forth, cleansing the mind of any qualities that would hold it in check. During the second watch (10 pm to 2 am), they should recline in the lion's posture, mindful, alert, with the mind set on getting up. During the last watch (2 am to dawn), they should again sit and pace back and forth, cleansing the mind. This devotion to wakefulness helps to keep the holy life perfect and pure.
*   **Mindfulness & Alertness**: A monk should be possessed of mindfulness & alertness. When going forward and returning, looking toward and away, bending and extending limbs, carrying robes and bowl, eating, drinking, chewing, tasting, urinating, defecating, walking, standing, sitting, falling asleep, waking up, talking, and remaining silent, they should act with alertness.

**3. Mental Cultivation and Higher Training**
After establishing virtue and foundational practices, the focus shifts to developing mental faculties for deeper purification and insight. This is where the three trainings (heightened virtue, heightened mind, heightened discernment) are central.

*   **Mindfulness (Sati)**: **Highly meticulous, remembering & able to call to mind even things done & said long ago**. Mindfulness is the governing principle of the holy life.
    *   **Mindfulness of Breathing**: A **peaceful and exquisite abiding** that immediately disperses & allays any evil, unskillful mental qualities that have arisen. It is developed by being mindful when breathing in and out long or short, sensitive to the entire body, calming bodily fabrication, sensitive to rapture, pleasure, mental fabrication, and gladdening, steadying, and releasing the mind. It also involves focusing on inconstancy, dispassion, cessation, and relinquishment.
    *   **Mindfulness Immersed in the Body**: A path leading to the unfabricated. Developed by reflecting on the body's constituents and stages of decay, abandoning memories and resolves related to household life, leading the mind to gather, settle inwardly, grow unified & concentrated.
    *   **Recollections**: Recollecting the Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, virtue, generosity, and devas straightens the mind and leads to joy, rapture, calm, and concentration. These recollections cleanse the defiled mind.
*   **Concentration (Samādhi)**: Singleness of mind.
    *   **Jhanas**: **Heightened mental states, pleasant abidings**, attainable by those who bring precepts to perfection, are committed to inner tranquility, don't neglect jhāna, are endowed with insight, and frequent empty dwellings. Achieving these states allows the abandonment of hindrances and cessation of various fabrications.
    *   **Themes of Concentration**: Periodically attend to themes of concentration, uplifted energy, and equanimity to make the mind pliant, malleable, and luminous for ending effluents.
    *   **Awareness-release through goodwill**: Cultivated and developed, it prevents ill will from overpowering the mind. This awareness-release can be immeasurable.
*   **Discernment (Paññā) / Wisdom**: Noble, penetrating, leading to the right ending of stress. It is the surpassing state of the holy life.
    *   **Knowledge of the Four Noble Truths**: Discerning stress, its origination, cessation, and the path to cessation. This is right view.
    *   **Investigation and Cross-questioning**: Essential for understanding and dispelling doubt.
    *   **Discernment as a "supassing state"**: When Dhamma is scrutinized with discernment, it leads to the right ending of suffering and stress.
    *   **Skillfulness in tactics**: Knowing how to engage in discussion, formulate questions, and answer appropriately.
    *   **Insight preceded by tranquility, or tranquility preceded by insight, or tranquility in tandem with insight**: These are paths to arahantship where fetters are abandoned and obsessions destroyed.

**4. Abandoning the Hindrances**
Integral to mental cultivation is the abandonment of the five hindrances, which are imperfections of awareness that weaken discernment. Seeking a secluded dwelling (forest, shade of a tree, empty dwelling, etc.) is a supportive condition for this.

*   **Abandoning Covetousness**: Dwelling with an awareness devoid of covetousness, cleansing the mind of it.
*   **Abandoning Ill Will and Anger**: Dwelling with an awareness devoid of ill will, sympathetic with the welfare of all living beings, cleansing the mind of ill will and anger.
*   **Abandoning Sloth and Drowsiness**: Dwelling with an awareness devoid of sloth and drowsiness, mindful, alert, percipient of light, cleansing the mind of sloth and drowsiness.
*   **Abandoning Restlessness and Anxiety**: Dwelling undisturbed, with the mind inwardly stilled, cleansing the mind of restlessness and anxiety.
*   **Abandoning Uncertainty**: Dwelling having crossed over uncertainty, with no perplexity with regard to skillful qualities, cleansing the mind of uncertainty.

**5. Stages of Purity and Attainment**
The path is a step-by-step training, culminating in stages of purity and ultimate liberation.

*   **Purity of Virtue (Sīla-visuddhi)**: Initial stage of ethical restraint and good conduct.
*   **Purity of Mind (Citta-visuddhi)**: Achieved through concentration and calming the mind, moving beyond hindrances.
*   **Purity of View (Diṭṭhi-visuddhi)**: Discernment of the Four Noble Truths, leading to the abandonment of self-identification view, doubt, and grasping at habits & practices.
*   **Purity of Overcoming Perplexity (Kaṅkhā-vitaraṇa-visuddhi)**: Resolving doubts through clear understanding.
*   **Purity of Knowledge & Vision of what is & is not the Path (Maggāmagga-ñāṇadassana-visuddhi)**: Clear insight into the correct path.
*   **Purity of Knowledge & Vision of the Way (Paṭipadā-ñāṇadassana-visuddhi)**: Deeper realization of the practice for liberation.
*   **Purity of Knowledge & Vision (Ñāṇadassana-visuddhi)**: The culminating stage before unbinding, where one directly knows and realizes effluent-free awareness-release and discernment-release.

    *These stages are mentioned as "nine factors of exertion for full purity" in one source, including "purity-of-virtue factor... purity-of-mind factor... purity-of-view factor... purity-of-overcoming-doubt factor... purity-of-knowledge-&-vision-of-what-is-&-is-not the-path factor... purity-of-knowledge-&-vision-of-the-way factor... purity-of-knowledge-&-vision factor... purity-of-discernment factor... purity-of-release factor of exertion for full purity". This implies a progressive, interwoven development leading to liberation.*

**6. Stages of Attainment and Liberation**
The culmination of the path leads to direct knowledge and various levels of release.

*   **Monk in Training (Sekha)**: A monk who has not yet reached their heart's goal but **still aspires for the unexcelled freedom from bondage**. They have a **task to do with heedfulness**. They are capable of breaking out, awakening, and attaining supreme rest from the yoke if their practices are unspoiled and they are consummate in virtue, sense restraint, moderation in eating, wakefulness, and equipped with seven qualities (conviction, persistence, mindfulness, concentration, discernment, modesty, truthfulness).
*   **Noble Disciples (Ariya-puggala)**:
    *   **Stream-enterer**: A virtuous monk attending to the five clinging-aggregates as inconstant, stressful, etc., can realize the **fruit of stream-entry**. These are the **first order** contemplatives. They abandon self-identification view, doubt, and grasping at habits & practices.
    *   **Once-returner (Sakadāgāmī)**: The **second order**. Attains this with attenuation of passion, aversion, and delusion.
    *   **Non-returner (Anāgāmī)**: The **third order**.
    *   **Arahant (Worthy One)**: One whose **effluents are ended, who has reached fulfillment, done the task, laid down the burden, attained the true goal, totally destroyed the fetter of becoming, and is released through right gnosis**.
        *   They are **incapable of intentional killing, stealing, sexual intercourse, deliberate lying, or consuming stored-up sensual things** as a householder.
        *   They do not follow bias based on desire, aversion, fear, or delusion.
        *   They are **unshakeable contemplatives**.
        *   They have **no task to do with heedfulness**.
*   **The Tathāgata (The Awakened One)**: The supreme guide and teacher.
    *   **Arouser, begetter, expounder of the unarisen/unbegotten/unexpounded path**: He knows the path, is expert and adept at it.
    *   **The Eye, Knowledge, Dhamma, Brahmā, speaker, proclaimer, elucidator of meaning, giver of the deathless, lord of the Dhamma**.
    *   Does in line with what he teaches, and teaches in line with what he does.
    *   His Dhamma is **unrefuted, undefiled, blameless, not faulted by knowledgeable contemplatives & brahmans**.
