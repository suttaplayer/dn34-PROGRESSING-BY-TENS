The query focuses on understanding the detailed meaning of the statement "Which four dhammas should be realized? Four fruits of the contemplative life: the fruit of stream-entry, the fruit of once-returning, the fruit of non-returning, the fruit of arahantship", from the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34). This statement refers to the progressive stages of attainment for those practicing the Dhamma.

***

**Situation**

The Dhamma, taught by the Blessed One, provides a direct path for the purification of beings, the overcoming of sorrow and lamentation, the disappearance of pain and distress, the attainment of the right method, and the realization of unbinding. It is a holy life lived with training as a reward, with **discernment as its surpassing state, release as its heartwood, and mindfulness as its governing principle**. This path is intended to put an end to suffering and stress. The core of this teaching involves understanding and realizing the four noble truths: **stress, the origination of stress, the cessation of stress, and the path of practice leading to the cessation of stress**.

**Complication**

Progress along this path, leading to the realization of the "four fruits of the contemplative life," requires overcoming fundamental defilements and misapprehensions. Human beings often engage in unskillful actions rooted in greed, aversion, and delusion, which are identified as causes for the origination of actions. These qualities produce blindness, lack of vision, and knowledge, leading to the cessation of discernment and preventing unbinding. The path also involves abandoning self-identification, clinging, and various forms of ignorance. Without proper development and pursuit of key practices, genuine progress is not possible. For instance, it's considered an impossibility to achieve the right ending of stress without having broken through to the four noble truths.

**Resolution**

The realization of the "four fruits of the contemplative life" represents a progressive shedding of defilements and an increasing depth of understanding and liberation, ultimately culminating in total unbinding.

1.  **Stream-Entry (Sotāpanna):** A stream-enterer is an individual who, **through the ending of the first three fetters (self-identity view, doubt, and grasping at habits & practices), is certain of the true Dhamma, not subject to falling back, never again destined for the lower realms, and headed for self-awakening**. This stage is attained by associating with people of integrity, hearing the True Dhamma, appropriate attention, and practicing the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhamma.
2.  **Once-Returning (Sakadāgāmī):** A once-returner is one who, **by abandoning the first three fetters and attenuating (reducing) passion, aversion, and delusion**, is destined to return to this world only one more time before achieving full liberation.
3.  **Non-Returning (Anāgāmī):** A non-returner is a person who, **through the abandoning of the five lower fetters (self-identification views, grasping at habits & practices, uncertainty, sensual passion, and irritation)**, is due to arise spontaneously in the Pure Abodes, there to be totally unbound, never again to return from that world.
4.  **Arahantship (Arahant):** The arahant is the most refined contemplative, one whose training is complete. An arahant is a monk who **has cut through craving, has ripped off the fetter, and — from rightly breaking through conceit — has put an end to suffering & stress**. This state is characterized by **the ending of effluents (sensuality, becoming, and ignorance)**. They enter and remain in **the effluent-free awareness-release and discernment-release, having directly known and realized them for themselves right in the here and now**. Such a person discerns that **"Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world"**. The experience of **unbinding is described as pleasant**, a state where there is nothing felt in the sense of sensual pleasure, but rather the cessation of feeling tied to the five strands of sensuality. This ultimate release, the "ending of passion, the ending of aversion, the ending of delusion," is called the unfabricated, and it is the "gnosis of emancipation, the breaking open of ignorance". The path to this liberation is diligently pursued by being **heedful, ardent, and resolute**, constantly working to abandon unskillful mental qualities and cultivate skillful ones. Ultimately, the aim is for one to be **unagitated from lack of clinging or sustenance**, leading to no future birth, aging, death, or stress.
