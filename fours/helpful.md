In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta outlines a framework of Dhammas, including those that are "very helpful," which progress in categories of tens. Your query focuses on the four "wheels" that are described as "very helpful".

Here is an analysis of the detailed meaning of this statement in the requested Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation:**
Human beings and devas aspire to **greatness and abundance in terms of wealth** and wish to **develop a four-wheeled prosperity**. People naturally desire **welfare and happiness**. However, the world is characterized by being "swept away" and not enduring, lacking shelter or protector, being without ownership, and is inherently "insufficient, insatiable, a slave to craving". Beings are also "owners of their actions, heirs of their actions, born of their actions, related through their actions, and have their actions as their arbitrator".

**Complication:**
Despite the aspiration for well-being, individuals can easily "go off course through **desire, aversion, delusion, [and] fear**". If one follows a path where "unskillful mental qualities increase while one's skillful mental qualities decline," that path is "fruitless". Thinking imbued with sensuality, ill-will, or harmfulness can "produce blindness, produce lack of vision, produce lack of knowledge, lead to the cessation of discernment, side with vexation, and are not conducive to Unbinding". The inherent dangers are that one's craving, "when taking birth, takes birth" for things like cloth, alms food, lodging, or future becoming.

**Resolution:**
The sources present **four "wheels"** that are "very helpful" and, when endowed with them, human beings and devas can develop "four-wheeled prosperity" and achieve "greatness & abundance in terms of wealth". These "qualities, when adopted & carried out, lead to welfare & to happiness".

These four wheels are:

*   **Living in a civilized land (Paṭirūpadesavāsa)**:
    This refers to being in an environment conducive to spiritual development. An example of such a favorable condition is being born in the **Middle Country**, where one is "discerning, not dull, not drooling, capable of knowing the meaning of what is well-said or ill-said". In such a land, the Dhamma is taught by the Blessed One as "admirable in the beginning, admirable in the middle, admirable in the end" and expounds "the holy life both in its particulars & in its essence, entirely perfect, surpassingly pure".

*   **Associating with people of integrity (Sappurūpasaṃsevo)**:
    This is explicitly identified as a "factor for stream entry" and is considered "very helpful". Associating with such individuals "fills [the conditions for] hearing the true Dhamma… conviction… appropriate attention… mindfulness & alertness… restraint of the senses… the three forms of right conduct… the four establishings of mindfulness… [and] the seven factors for awakening". When these factors are "made full, they fill [the conditions for] clear knowing & release". A "great benefactor" is one "through whom one has gone to the Buddha for refuge, has gone to the Dhamma for refuge, has gone to the Saṅgha for refuge". Having "verified confidence in the Saṅgha" means acknowledging that its disciples "have practiced well… straight-forwardly… methodically… masterfully" and are "deserving of gifts, deserving of hospitality, deserving of offerings, deserving of respect, the incomparable field of merit for the world". This admirable friendship is "on the side of distinction".

*   **Directing oneself rightly (Attasammāpaṇidhi)**:
    This involves cultivating **good bodily conduct, good verbal conduct, & good mental conduct**. The rewards of such conduct include not faulting oneself, receiving praise from observant people, spreading a good reputation, dying unconfused, and reappearing in a good destination or heavenly world. It entails **abandoning what is unskillful, developing what is skillful, abandoning what is blameworthy, [and] developing what is unblameworthy**. The **noble eightfold path** (right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration) is the "path" that "puts an end to suffering & stress". It is described as "kamma that is neither dark nor bright with neither dark nor bright result, leading to the ending of kamma". This path is suitable for those who are **modest, content, reclusive, whose persistence is aroused, whose mindfulness is established, whose mind is concentrated, [and] who are endowed with discernment**. It also includes developing **tranquility & insight** and cultivating **mindfulness & alertness**.

*   **Having done merit in the past (Pubbekatapuññatā)**:
    "Acts of merit" are synonymous with what is "blissful, desirable, pleasing, endearing, charming". The Buddha himself, "having long performed meritorious deeds," experienced such positive results, noting that his power and might were the "fruit of my three [types of] action… generosity, self-control, & restraint". When facing the inevitable "aging & death," one should "salvage (your wealth) by giving: What's given is well salvaged". A "disciple of the noble ones" practices this by living "at home with an awareness cleansed of the stain of stinginess, freely generous, openhanded, delighting in being magnanimous, responsive to requests, delighting in the distribution of alms," which is considered a "bonanza of merit". Ultimately, "acts of merit are the support for beings in their after-death world", and "generosity" is recognized as one of the "seven noble treasures".
