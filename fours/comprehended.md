Here is an analysis of the detailed meaning of the statement "Which four dhammas should be comprehended? Four nutriments: physical food, gross or refined; contact as the second; intellectual intention the third; and consciousness the fourth", presented in the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation:**
The Buddhist teachings, as presented by Venerable Sāriputta in the DASUTTARA SUTTA, offer a comprehensive framework of Dhammas categorized by numerical lists, intended to guide practitioners towards unbinding and the end of suffering. Among these categories are certain Dhammas that **"should be comprehended"**. One such fundamental teaching highlights the **four nutriments**: physical food (gross or refined), contact, intellectual intention, and consciousness. These nutriments are not merely substances or processes but are considered essential for the **maintenance of beings** who have come into being and for the support of those in search of a place to be born.

**Complication:**
A lack of proper **comprehension** of these four nutriments perpetuates the cycle of suffering and continued existence. Where there is **passion, delight, and craving for any of these nutriments**, consciousness "lands there and increases," which in turn leads to the alighting of name-&-form, the growth of fabrications, and the production of renewed becoming in the future, culminating in future birth, aging, death, sorrow, affliction, and despair. This means that what one **intends, arranges, and obsesses about** acts as a "support for the stationing of consciousness," leading to a landing of consciousness, its growth, and consequently, the production of renewed becoming and the entire "mass of suffering & stress". The five clinging-aggregates—which are forms of stress themselves—are dependently co-arisen, and any desire, embracing, grasping, or holding-on to them is considered the **origination of stress**. An uninstructed, run-of-the-mill person often fails to comprehend these realities, perceiving phenomena as "mine" and delighting in them, simply "because he has not comprehended it". This lack of understanding about the allure, drawback, and escape from such phenomena means one "will not manage an answer and... will get themselves into trouble".

**Resolution:**
The **comprehension** of these four nutriments is therefore presented as a crucial step towards liberation from suffering. A disciple of the noble ones who **discerns** nutriment, its origination, its cessation, and the way of practice leading to its cessation, is considered to have **right view** and has "arrived at this true Dhamma". This process involves recognizing that "from the origination of craving comes the origination of nutriment" and "from the cessation of craving comes the cessation of nutriment". The path leading to the cessation of nutriment is precisely the **noble eightfold path**: right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration. Following this path is the "extraction of arrows" and leads to "the end to suffering & stress". By discerning and abandoning passion-obsession, aversion-obsession, and conceit-obsession, and giving rise to clear knowing, one can "put an end to suffering & stress right in the here & now". The Dhamma itself is "well taught by the Blessed One, to be seen here & now, timeless, inviting verification, pertinent, to be experienced by the observant for themselves". The ultimate outcome of this **comprehension** and practice is to cut through craving, rip off fetters, and achieve "awareness-release & discernment-release", leading to "utter disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding". When one "knows & sees" the origination and disappearance of the aggregates (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness)—which are maintained by these nutriments—this leads to the "ending of effluents". Through right discernment of "this has come into being" and its origination and cessation from nutriment, one becomes "released from what has come into being", fulfilling the purpose of the Dhamma which aims "for attaining unbinding, for putting an end to suffering & stress, for releasing from all ties". The ultimate result is the "end of stress".
