Here is an analysis of the statement "Which four dhammas should be abandoned? Four floods: the flood of sensuality, the flood of becoming, the flood of views, the flood of ignorance" from the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34) in the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation:**
The sources describe four "floods" (ogha) that are to be abandoned: the flood of sensuality, the flood of becoming, the flood of views, and the flood of ignorance. These are also identified as "yokes" that bind beings. The "noble eightfold path" is specifically taught for the **direct knowledge, comprehension, total ending, and abandoning** of these four floods.

**Complication:**
These four floods represent fundamental obstacles and unskillful mental qualities that lead to suffering, continued existence, and hinder spiritual progress.
*   **The flood of sensuality (kāmogha)** refers to things that excite passion and are described as **"inconstant, hollow, vain, deceptive,"** and **"illusory, the babble of fools"**. Sensuality is considered **"Māra's realm, Māra's domain, Māra's bait, Māra's range,"** leading to **"evil, unskillful mental states: greed, ill will, & contentiousness"**. Relishing or clinging to forms, sounds, aromas, flavors, or tactile sensations **gives rise to delight, which in turn leads to the origination of suffering & stress**. Sensual pleasures are depicted as having **"little satisfaction, much stress, much despair, & greater drawbacks,"** and are compared to a "chain of bones," a "lump of flesh," or a "snake's head". Sensual desire itself is an **"obstacle, a hindrance that overwhelms awareness and weakens discernment,"** making it impossible to realize a superior human state.
*   **The flood of becoming (bhavogha)** is deeply connected to craving. The Blessed One states, **"I will teach you craving: the ensnarer that has flowed along, spread out, and caught hold, with which this world is smothered & enveloped like a tangled skein, a knotted ball of string, like matted rushes and reeds, and does not go beyond transmigration, beyond the planes of deprivation, woe, & bad destinations"**. Greed, aversion, and delusion are causes for the origination of actions, which propel becoming. The underlying issue is that beings are **"held bound together"** in action, leading to **"further becoming"**.
*   **The flood of views (diṭṭhogha)** encompasses clinging to various beliefs and theories about oneself and the cosmos. Holding **"wrong view"** is listed as a **"root of what is unskillful"** and is among the **"ten forms of wrongness"** to be abandoned. Such views can lead to **"agitation & vacillation"** for those not truly knowing or seeing. The sources describe various types of theorists (e.g., about the past, future, annihilationists) whose standpoints, if grasped at, lead to specific destinations in the world beyond. If one does not abandon a wrong view, they risk being **"placed in hell"**. Similarly, **"self-identification views"** and **"grasping at habits & practices"** are considered lower fetters that hinder one's progress.
*   **The flood of ignorance (avijjogha)** is a fundamental defilement. **"Defiled by ignorance, discernment does not develop"**. From **"ignorance as a requisite condition come fabrications,"** and this leads to the entire mass of stress and suffering, including aging, death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, & despair. Ignorance is considered one of the **"three effluents"** and one of the **"inside stains, inside enemies, inside foes, inside murderers, inside adversaries,"** along with greed and aversion. The world's continued **"wandering-on through birth & death"** is due to **"ignorance,"** which is described as a **"great delusion"**.

**Resolution:**
The path to abandoning these floods involves cultivating skillful qualities and practices that lead to disenchantment, dispassion, cessation, and ultimately unbinding.
*   **The Noble Eightfold Path** is the primary means for this abandonment. This path, including **"right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration"**, leads to the **"ending of kamma"** and is the means by which **"stress"** is ceased.
*   **Virtue, Concentration, and Discernment** are crucial. The mind nurtured with discernment is **"rightly released from the effluents"**.
    *   **Virtue (sīla):** Bodily, verbal, & mental misconduct should be avoided. **"Restraint with the body is good, good is restraint with speech. Restraint with the heart is good, good is restraint everywhere"**.
    *   **Concentration (samādhi):** **"Tranquility & insight"** should be developed. **Five hindrances**—sensual desire, ill will, sloth & drowsiness, restlessness & anxiety, uncertainty—**"overwhelm awareness and weaken discernment"** and must be abandoned. When listening to the Dhamma, if one is receptive and attentive, these hindrances do not arise. The practice of **jhāna** leads to **"cessation of perception & feeling,"** thoughts, and breaths.
    *   **Discernment (paññā):** It is the **"surpassing state"** and is gained by knowing the origination, passing away, allure, drawbacks, and escape from phenomena like the five faculties. Discernment allows one to **"discern what is unskillful, discerns the root of what is unskillful, discerns what is skillful, and discerns the root of what is skillful"**, leading to **"right view"**. The **"gnosis of emancipation"** is described as **"the breaking open of ignorance"**. Through **"the fading of passion is there awareness-release. From the fading of ignorance is there discernment-release"**.
*   **Mindfulness (sati) and Appropriate Attention (yoniso manasikāra):** **"Appropriate attention"** is fundamental because **"when a monk attends appropriately, unarisen effluents do not arise, and arisen effluents are abandoned"**. The **"four establishings of mindfulness"** are highlighted as the **"direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding"**.
*   **Abandoning Craving and Clinging:** The ultimate goal is to abandon craving and clinging. **"Unclinging, he is not agitated. Unagitated, he totally unbinds right within. He discerns: 'Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for this world'"**. The **"ending of craving"** leads to **"unbinding"** and the **"ending of action; from the ending of action, the ending of stress"**.
*   **Heedfulness:** Remaining **"heedful"** in the Dhamma and Vinaya is essential to **"make an end of stress"** and leave **"the wandering-on through birth"**.
