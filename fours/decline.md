The statement from the DASUTTARA SUTTA identifies four "yokes" that are "on the side of decline," highlighting obstacles to spiritual progress: the yoke of sensuality, the yoke of becoming, the yoke of views, and the yoke of ignorance. These yokes represent **evil, unskillful mental qualities** that prevent a person from finding rest and lead to suffering and continued cycles of existence.

Here's a detailed analysis of this statement, presented in a Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

**Situation**

The human condition is characterized by various forms of attachment and misunderstanding that bind beings and lead to undesirable outcomes. These binding forces are categorized as "yokes". When an individual is "conjoined with evil, unskillful mental qualities," which are defiling and lead to further becoming, unhappiness, suffering, and stress, they are said to "not be at rest from the yoke".

**Complication**

The four yokes intricately weave together to perpetuate suffering and continued existence:

*   **Yoke of Sensuality (Kāma-yoga)**: This refers to attachment and craving for agreeable forms, sounds, aromas, flavors, and tactile sensations cognizable via the senses. Sensual pleasures are inherently **"of little satisfaction, much stress, much despair, & greater drawbacks"**. They are **"inconstant, hollow, vain, deceptive,"** and are described as Māra's (the Evil One's) realm, bait, and domain, leading to unskillful mental states like greed, ill will, and contentiousness. Relishing and clinging to sensuality leads to delight, which in turn leads to the origination of suffering and stress.
*   **Yoke of Becoming (Bhava-yoga)**: This yoke ties beings to the cycle of **"wandering-on leading to birth & death"**. It means that intentions, arrangements, and obsessions serve as "a support for the stationing of consciousness," leading to the "production of renewed becoming in the future," and subsequently, "future birth, aging-&-death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, & despair," thus creating "this entire mass of suffering & stress". Even those who have abandoned sensuality but have not reached the end of effluents can still be tied by the yoke of becoming.
*   **Yoke of Views (Diṭṭhi-yoga)**: This involves clinging to various "view standpoints" or "idiosyncratic truths" that are not conducive to liberation. Examples include views that "whatever a person experiences... is all without cause & without condition". Such views lead to "no desire, no effort" for what should or should not be done, leaving one "bewildered & unprotected". These seized and grasped standpoints **"lead to such & such a destination, to such & such a state in the world beyond,"** causing agitation and disputes. If one does not abandon such wrong views, they may be "placed in hell".
*   **Yoke of Ignorance (Avijjā-yoga)**: Ignorance is a fundamental lack of understanding of reality. It is a "great delusion" that causes beings to wander on for a long time. When the mind is "defiled by ignorance, discernment does not develop". This lack of understanding prevents individuals from going "beyond transmigration, beyond the planes of deprivation, woe, & bad destinations". Ignorance is the requisite condition for fabrications, which in turn lead to consciousness, name-and-form, and eventually the entire mass of suffering and stress. Unskillful thinking, rooted in delusion (a form of ignorance), "produces blindness, produces lack of vision, produce lack of knowledge, leads to the cessation of discernment, sides with vexation, and is not conducive to Unbinding".

**Resolution**

The path to overcoming these yokes and achieving release from suffering involves **"unyoking"** from each of them. This is achieved by **discerning "the origination, the passing away, the allure, the drawbacks, & the escape"** from each yoke. The ultimate goal is the right release of the mind from effluents—sensuality, becoming, and ignorance—through the cultivation of discernment.

Key aspects of this resolution include:

*   **For Sensuality:** By discerning the drawbacks and escape from sensuality, one becomes **"not obsessed with sensual passion, sensual delight, sensual attraction, sensual infatuation, sensual thirst, sensual fever, sensual fascination, sensual craving"**. This can involve cultivating a mind that **"doesn't leap up at sensuality,"** instead finding confidence and release in renunciation, leading to freedom from "effluents, torments, & fevers that arise in dependence on sensuality". The practice of jhānas, described as "devotions to pleasure that lead exclusively to disenchantment, dispassion, cessation, calming, direct knowledge, self-awakening, & unbinding," is also instrumental.
*   **For Becoming:** Discerning the origination, passing away, allure, drawbacks, and escape from becoming allows for disenchantment, dispassion, and the cessation of becomings. The ending of becoming implies the end of future birth, aging, and death.
*   **For Views:** The path involves **abandoning and transcending view-dependencies**. This means **shaking off "run-of-the-mill idiosyncratic truths"** and various theoretical judgments, leading to a state of inner peace where one "doesn't get into disputes". Right view, a component of the noble eightfold path, is crucial for abolishing wrong views.
*   **For Ignorance:** The "gnosis of emancipation" is described as the **"breaking open of ignorance"**. The general path involves developing virtue, concentration, and discernment. Specifically, the Noble Eightfold Path—comprising right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration—is the "middle way" that leads to "stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding". This path leads to the cessation of effluents, including the effluent of ignorance. The ultimate release is achieved through **"lack of clinging or sustenance,"** allowing unbinding to be experienced "right within".
