In the DASUTTARA SUTTA, Venerable Sāriputta presents a comprehensive framework of Dhamma principles, categorized by increasing numerical values, detailing what should be developed for the attainment of unbinding and the end of suffering and stress. Among these, the query specifically focuses on the statement: "Which four dhammas should be developed? Four establishings of mindfulness".

Here is a detailed analysis of this statement, presented in a Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

### Situation

The statement "Four establishings of mindfulness" refers to a fundamental practice taught by the Blessed One, designed for profound spiritual development. These four involve remaining focused on:
*   The **body** in and of itself.
*   **Feelings** in and of themselves.
*   The **mind** in and of itself.
*   **Mental qualities** in and of themselves.

This practice is explicitly described as the "**direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding**". It is a core element of the Dhamma, serving as a pathway to liberation, which the Tathāgata (Buddha) realized through direct knowledge and proclaimed.

### Complication

The sources highlight the severe consequences of neglecting the development of these four establishings of mindfulness. Primarily, the **True Dhamma does not last long** when they are not developed and pursued after the Tathāgata's total unbinding. This implies a critical reliance on this practice for the continuity and efficacy of the teachings.

Furthermore, neglecting these practices leaves an individual susceptible to various mental defilements and suffering. Without proper development of mindfulness, the "five hindrances—those defilements of awareness that weaken discernment" persist. These hindrances include:
*   Sensual desire
*   Ill will
*   Sloth & drowsiness
*   Restlessness & anxiety
*   Uncertainty

When these hindrances are present, skillful mental qualities decline, and unskillful qualities increase, rendering practices fruitless. Such a state prevents the mind from becoming "**pliant, malleable, luminous, & not brittle**," which is necessary for the ending of effluents. Without this foundational practice, beings remain "**lost,**" unable to hear the Dhamma sequence. The "noble eightfold path leading to the right ending of suffering & stress is neglected" in those who neglect the four establishings of mindfulness.

### Resolution

The development of the four establishings of mindfulness is crucial for advancing on the spiritual path and realizing liberation. The practice itself entails being "**ardent, alert, & mindful—subduing greed & distress with reference to the world**" while focusing on the body, feelings, mind, and mental qualities. Being "mindful and alert" involves knowing feelings, thoughts, and perceptions as they arise, persist, and subside.

This development is connected to broader principles and leads to specific fruits:
*   **Longevity of the Dhamma:** The **True Dhamma lasts long** when the four establishings of mindfulness are developed and pursued. This ensures the continuous welfare and happiness of many beings.
*   **Ending of Suffering:** Developing these practices, even for a short period like seven days, can lead to "**either gnosis right here & now, or—if there be any remnant of clinging-sustenance—non-return**". This is because it helps one to "know & see" the origination and disappearance of "form, feeling, perception, fabrications, consciousness," leading to the "ending of effluents".
*   **Path to Release:** Mindfulness is considered the "**governing principle**" of the holy life, leading to discernment as its "surpassing state" and release as its "heartwood". The practice leads to "**disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding**".
*   **Integration with the Noble Eightfold Path:** The development of the four establishings of mindfulness is deeply intertwined with the noble eightfold path, which is described as the "path of practice to the development of the establishing of mindfulness". The eightfold path includes aspects like right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration.
*   **Purification and Freedom:** Through developing these establishings, a monk is "**released from the effluent of sensuality, the mind is released from the effluent of becoming, the mind is released from the effluent of ignorance.**" This leads to the realization that "Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for the sake of this world". Such a practitioner is said to be "**consummate in faculties**".
*   **Qualities of the Practitioner:** The Dhamma is for those who are modest, content, reclusive, persistent, mindful, concentrated, and discerning, leading to a state of "non-objectification". This continuous development ensures that one's **bodily, verbal, and mental actions are peaceful** and that one renders "peaceful service to our fellows in the holy life".

In essence, the development of the four establishings of mindfulness is presented as a transformative practice that purifies the mind, eradicates suffering, and is essential for the attainment of liberation, thereby ensuring the enduring presence of the True Dhamma in the world.
