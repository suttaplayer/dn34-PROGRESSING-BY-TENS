Venerable Sāriputta's statement in the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34) highlights the **Four Noble Truths** as fundamental aspects of reality that should be **directly known**. This direct knowing is not merely intellectual understanding but a profound realization that leads to liberation from suffering. The Dhamma, as taught by the Blessed One, is described as **deep, hard to see, hard to realize, tranquil, refined, beyond the scope of conjecture, subtle, to-be-experienced by the wise**. The entire holy life is lived **for the sake of direct knowledge & full comprehension**.

Here is an analysis of this statement using the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) framework:

### Situation

The fundamental **situation** of sentient beings in the world is one steeped in **stress** (Dukkha). This stress encompasses birth, aging, death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, despair, association with what is disliked, separation from what is loved, and not getting what is desired. In essence, the **five clinging-aggregates** (form, feeling, perception, fabrications, and consciousness) are stress. This state of being, where **all fabrications are inconstant and stressful, and all phenomena are not-self**, is a fundamental property that stands whether or not Tathāgatas arise. Without a direct understanding of these realities, beings are caught in a continuous cycle of **transmigration and wandering-on** through various births and realms. The absence of this understanding leads to minds overcome with **passion, aversion, and delusion**, which are the roots of what is unskillful.

### Complication

The **complication** arises from the fact that this stress (dukkha) is not random but has an **origination**. This origination is rooted in **craving**—sensuality-craving, becoming-craving, and non-becoming-craving—which leads to further becoming, accompanied by passion and delight. Furthermore, **ignorance** is a key requisite condition for fabrications to come into play, perpetuating the cycle. Without knowing and seeing these truths directly, beings remain trapped. The world is afflicted by clinging, especially to the **five strings of sensuality** (forms, sounds, aromas, flavors, tactile sensations) and the heart as the sixth. Failing to discern these truths, contemplatives and brahmans are considered **lowly in their awareness-release & discernment-release, incapable of making an end, they're headed to birth & aging**. Those who do not discern the Four Noble Truths as they have come to be are **incapable of bringing about the right ending of stress**. This lack of discerning what is unskillful and its roots, as well as what is skillful and its roots, prevents the path to release. The Dhamma, being profound and subtle, is **not easily realized by those overcome with aversion & passion**.

### Resolution

The **resolution** lies in the **direct knowledge and realization of the Four Noble Truths**. When these truths—stress, its origination, its cessation, and the path to its cessation—are **awakened to & penetrated**, craving for becoming is crushed, and the guide to becoming is ended, leading to **no further-becoming**. The path to this liberation is precisely the **Noble Eightfold Path**: **right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration**.

**Right view** is specifically defined as **knowledge with reference to stress, its origination, its cessation, and the way of practice leading to its cessation**. This direct knowledge leads to the **ending of effluents** (sensuality, becoming, ignorance), and the realization that **birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done, and there is nothing further for this world**.

The practice involves developing qualities such as:
*   **Mindfulness** (especially the **four establishings of mindfulness** focused on body, feelings, mind, and mental qualities). This is described as the **direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding**.
*   **Concentration** (leading to discernments and eventually the ending of effluents).
*   **Discernment** (which is nurtured by concentration and leads to release). The holy life is lived with training as a reward, discernment as its surpassing state, release as its heartwood, and mindfulness as its governing principle.

Ultimately, for those who **see with discernment** that all fabrications are inconstant, stressful, and not-self, they **grow disenchanted with stress**, which is the path to purity. By following the Dhamma, one gains **release from all suffering & stress**. The profound significance of the Four Noble Truths is continually emphasized as the duty of monks is the constant contemplation of these truths for their full realization.
