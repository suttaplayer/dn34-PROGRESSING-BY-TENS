Here is a detailed analysis of the statement from the DASUTTARA SUTTA, presented in a Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) format:

### Situation

The fundamental aim of the holy life is the acquisition, increase, development, and culmination of discernment. Discernment is considered the "surpassing state" among qualities that lead to the ending of suffering and stress, and the Dhamma is entirely for the "right ending of suffering & stress," scrutinized through discernment. Indeed, the Dhamma itself is deep, subtle, and "to-be-experienced by the wise".

### Complication

However, the path to discernment is not without its challenges, and its unacquired state or slow development can be attributed to several factors. A primary obstacle is the increase of unskillful mental qualities and the decline of skillful ones, rendering practices fruitless. More specifically, "five obstacles, hindrances that overwhelm awareness and weaken discernment" prevent understanding one's own benefit, the benefit of others, or realizing a "superior human state". These hindrances include sensual desire, ill will, sloth and drowsiness, restlessness and anxiety, and uncertainty. Furthermore, a person may be "without conviction," lack "desire (to listen)," or have "dull discernment," rendering them "incapable of alighting on the orderliness, on the rightness of skillful qualities" even when hearing the true Dhamma. Not listening well, not paying attention, or failing to apply one's mind to gnosis, leading to grasping at the worthless and rejecting the worthwhile, also contribute to the difficulty in comprehending the Dhamma.

### Resolution

To address these complications and foster the "acquiring of the as-yet-unacquired discernment" and its "increase, plenitude, development, & culmination", the Blessed One outlined eight essential causes or requisite conditions:

1.  **Living in Apprenticeship to the Teacher or Respectable Companions**: This involves cultivating a strong sense of shame, compunction, love, and respect for those who are learned and uphold the tradition. Associating with people of integrity is described as "very helpful" and can lead to the full development of various skillful qualities, including "hearing the true Dhamma… conviction… appropriate attention… mindfulness & alertness… restraint of the senses… the three forms of right conduct… the four establishings of mindfulness… (and) the seven factors for awakening".
2.  **Regular Cross-Questioning**: The monk should periodically approach learned companions and ask questions like, "'How is this, venerable sirs? What is the meaning of this?'". This practice is crucial for "mak[ing] open what isn't open, mak[ing] plain what isn't plain, (and) dispel[ling] doubt on its various doubtful points". It defines an assembly "trained in cross-questioning" as opposed to one trained in "bombast". The ability to answer questions appropriately—categorically, analytically, with a counter-question, or by putting aside—is a mark of skill.
3.  **Achieving Twofold Seclusion**: After hearing the Dhamma, the student should achieve both "seclusion in body & seclusion in mind". This means not neglecting seclusion and devoting oneself to "internal tranquility of awareness".
4.  **Being Virtuous**: A monk must be virtuous, living "restrained in accordance with the Pāṭimokkha, consummate in his behavior & sphere of activity," and training himself by "seeing danger in the slightest faults". Virtue is a foundational quality, described as "admirable," "appealing to the noble ones," and leading to concentration. It is considered a "treasure" and is one of the "seven true dhammas" of a monk.
5.  **Extensive Learning and Penetration of Views**: The practitioner should have "heard much, has retained what he has heard, has stored what he has heard". This involves teachings that are "admirable in the beginning, admirable in the middle, admirable in the end," which are listened to often, retained, discussed, accumulated, and "well penetrated in terms of his views". Listening to the Dhamma helps one clarify what has been heard and straighten one's views.
6.  **Arousing Persistence**: This involves keeping one's "persistence aroused for abandoning unskillful qualities and for taking on skillful qualities," being "steadfast, solid in his effort, not shirking his duties". Persistence is a crucial faculty for development and a strength of those whose effluents are ended. The Dhamma is specifically for one whose "persistence is aroused, not for one who is lazy".
7.  **Appropriate Dhamma Talk and Noble Silence**: When among the Saṅgha, a monk "doesn't talk on & on about a variety of things" but either "speaks Dhamma himself or he invites another to do so, and he feels no disdain for noble silence". This reflects the qualities of a "person of integrity" who knows "what's worthwhile & what's not". Well-spoken statements are "spoken at the right time," "in truth," "affectionately," "beneficially," and "with a mind of goodwill".
8.  **Focusing on the Arising and Passing Away of the Five Aggregates**: The monk should remain "focused on arising & passing away with regard to the five aggregates: 'Such is form, such its origination, such its disappearance. Such is feeling… Such is perception… Such are fabrications… Such is consciousness, such its origination, such its disappearance'". This practice leads to disenchantment, dispassion, and release from suffering and stress. This understanding is critical for the "immediate ending of fermentations".
