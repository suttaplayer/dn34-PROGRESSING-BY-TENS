Here is an analysis of the statement "Which eight dhammas should be developed? The noble eightfold path, i e, right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration", using the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) framework:

**Situation**
The sources present the noble eightfold path as a fundamental and supreme teaching within the Dhamma, outlining a direct and unexcelled way to spiritual liberation. Venerable Sāriputta states that these eight dhammas "should be developed", implying their crucial role in one's practice. This path is explicitly identified as the **middle way** realized by the Tathāgata, which produces vision and knowledge, leading to **stilling, direct knowledge, self-awakening, and unbinding**. It is further described as the "direct path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow & lamentation, for the disappearance of pain & distress, for the attainment of the right method, & for the realization of unbinding". The path is considered the **best among all paths**, serving to "purify vision" and "put an end to suffering & stress," thereby causing Māra's bewilderment. It is also known as a "sublime vehicle," a "Dhamma-vehicle," and an "unexcelled victory in battle", highlighting its effectiveness and superiority.

**Complication**
The sources emphasize that the proper ending of suffering and stress is impossible without breaking through to the **four noble truths**, which are inherently linked to the noble eightfold path. It is stated that if one were to claim to bring about the end of stress "without having broken through to the noble truth of stress...origination...cessation...[and] path of practice leading to the cessation of stress," that "would be an impossibility". This means that merely wishing for results or practicing inappropriately will not suffice. For those who harbor "wrong view, wrong resolve, wrong speech, wrong action, wrong livelihood, wrong effort, wrong mindfulness, & wrong concentration," even if they wish for results, "they are incapable of obtaining results". The very **roots of what is unskillful**—greed, aversion, and delusion—must be addressed. A failure to discern that "All fabrications are inconstant," "All fabrications are stressful," and "All phenomena are not-self" also represents a fundamental complication, as it prevents disenchantment and release. Moreover, **ignorance and craving for becoming** are identified as root causes of suffering, which the path aims to abandon.

**Resolution**
The development and pursuit of the noble eightfold path lead to profound spiritual accomplishments, including the **culmination of various other spiritual factors** such as the four establishings of mindfulness, the four right exertions, the four bases of power, the five faculties, and the seven factors for awakening. The path helps the mind become rightly released from effluents like sensuality, becoming, and ignorance.

The eight factors of the path, and their development, are detailed as follows:

*   **Right View** is the "forerunner" of the path. It involves having **knowledge with regard to stress, its origination, its stopping, and the way of practice leading to its stopping**. It also means discerning what is skillful and unskillful and their roots (lack of greed, aversion, and delusion). Furthermore, right view involves understanding the origination, cessation, and path to cessation of various phenomena such as nutriment, aging & death, birth, becoming, clinging, craving, feeling, contact, the six sense media, name-&-form, consciousness, fabrication, and ignorance. Ultimately, this leads to the abandoning of passion-obsession, aversion-obsession, and the conceit "I am," resulting in the end of suffering and stress.
*   **Right Resolve** involves thoughts imbued with **renunciation, non-ill will, and harmlessness**, moving away from sensuality, ill will, and harmfulness.
*   **Right Speech** is characterized by abstaining from telling lies, divisive speech, harsh speech, and idle chatter. Instead, one "speaks words that are soothing, pleasing to the ear, that are affectionate, that go to the heart, that are polite, appealing & pleasing to people at large." This speech is "in season, speaks what is based in fact, what is in accordance with the goal, the Dhamma, & the Vinaya," and includes "words worth treasuring, timely, reasonable, circumscribed, connected with the goal".
*   **Right Action** means abstaining from "taking life, taking what is not given [stealing], [and] engaging in illicit sex".
*   **Right Livelihood** means abstaining from "wrong livelihood" such as "animal" arts.
*   **Right Effort** involves keeping one's "persistence aroused for abandoning unskillful qualities and taking on skillful qualities." This effort is steadfast and solid, not shirking duties with regard to skillful qualities.
*   **Right Mindfulness** is developed by remaining "focused on the body in & of itself—ardent, alert, & mindful—subduing greed & distress with reference to the world." This extends to focusing on feelings, mind, and mental qualities in the same manner. It also means one's mindfulness is "established and not confused".
*   **Right Concentration** is defined as "any singleness of mind equipped with these seven factors" (right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, & right mindfulness). When developed, it makes the mind "pliant, malleable, luminous, & not brittle," and "rightly concentrated for the ending of the effluents".

This comprehensive development of the noble eightfold path leads to the mind becoming "unagitated" from lack of clinging, ensuring no future birth, aging, death, or stress.
