In the DASUTTARA SUTTA (Dīgha Nikāya 34), Venerable Sāriputta presents various categories of Dhammas, including those that are "hard to penetrate". Among these are "Eight inopportune, untimely situations for leading the holy life". These situations describe circumstances that make it exceptionally difficult or impossible for an individual to progress on the path to unbinding, even when the Dhamma is available.

Here is a detailed analysis of this statement using the Situation, Complication, Resolution (SCR) framework:

### Situation

The eight inopportune, untimely situations for leading the holy life are conditions that hinder or prevent one from engaging with the Dhamma, even if a Tathāgata has appeared in the world. These are considered "hard to penetrate" because they represent profound obstacles to spiritual progress. The Dasuttara Sutta (Dīgha Nikāya 34) specifies the following situations:

*   **Rebirth in Lower Realms, even when the Dhamma is taught:**
    *   A Tathāgata appears in the world, worthy and rightly self-awakened, and the Dhamma leading to stilling, total unbinding, and self-awakening is taught. However, the individual is **reborn in hell**.
    *   Similarly, the individual is reborn in an **animal womb**.
    *   Or, they are reborn in the **realm of the hungry ghosts**.
*   **Rebirth in Higher, but Ultimately Unsuitable, Realms:**
    *   The individual is reborn in a **host of long-lived devas**.
*   **Birth in Isolated or Unenlightened Regions:**
    *   The individual is born in a **border region among ignorant barbarians** where there is no opportunity for interaction with the Sangha (monks, nuns, male or female lay followers).
*   **(Two additional intermediate situations are implied in the source's numbering but not explicitly detailed in the provided excerpt.)**
*   **Absence of a Buddha and Dhamma Teaching:**
    *   A Tathāgata does not appear in the world, and the Dhamma leading to stilling, total unbinding, and self-awakening is not taught. This occurs even when the person is born in the Middle Country and is discerning, capable of understanding well-said or ill-said meaning.

### Complication

These situations are considered "untimely" because they prevent individuals from hearing, understanding, or practicing the Dhamma effectively, thereby hindering their release from suffering and stress.

*   **Inaccessibility of Dhamma:** In rebirths in hell, animal wombs, or hungry ghost realms, beings are overwhelmed by "stress, experienced pain, experienced loss", making it impossible to focus on spiritual practice. Even rebirth as a long-lived deva is considered untimely because, despite temporary bliss, it leads to a state "without learning" or "lack of Dhamma", prolonging the cycle of transmigration without liberation. Being in a "border region" means "no opening for monks, nuns, male lay followers, or female lay followers to go", implying a lack of access to the teachings and the community that upholds them.
*   **Absence of the Awakened Teacher:** The final situation highlights that even if an individual is intelligent and born in a suitable region, the **absence of a Tathāgata and the Dhamma teaching** makes the path to unbinding undiscoverable. The Dhamma is described as "deep, hard to see, hard to realize, peaceful, refined, beyond the scope of conjecture, subtle, to-be-experienced by the wise". Without the guidance of a Buddha, "those delighting in passion, cloaked in the mass of darkness, won't see". Such teachings are "not conducive to the goal, are not conducive to the Dhamma, are not basic to the holy life. They don't lead to disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to calm, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding". This emphasizes that "The world is lost! The world is destroyed!" when a Tathāgata does not teach the Dhamma, as "beings with little dust in their eyes are falling away because they do not hear the Dhamma".

### Resolution

The resolution to these untimely situations is the **presence and practice of the Dhamma**, which offers a means to transcend suffering and attain unbinding. The Dhamma is specifically "declared by the Blessed One" because it "is connected with the goal, is connected with the Dhamma, is fundamental to the holy life. It leads to disenchantment, dispassion, cessation, calming, direct knowledge, self-awakening, unbinding".

Key aspects of the resolution include:

*   **The Four Noble Truths:** These are central to the Dhamma and lead to the cessation of stress. The holy life is lived "with the purpose of knowing, seeing, attaining, realizing, & breaking through" these truths.
*   **The Noble Eightfold Path:** This is "the path" and is described as "supreme" among fabricated qualities. It is the "middle way realized by the Tathāgata that—producing vision, producing knowledge—leads to stilling, to direct knowledge, to self-awakening, to unbinding". This path is to be "developed for direct knowledge of, comprehension of, the total ending of, & the abandoning of these four floods" (sensuality, becoming, views, ignorance).
*   **Heedfulness:** The Buddha exhorted monks to "Reach consummation through heedfulness", calling it "very helpful". Heedfulness drives the effort to realize the "as-yet-unattained".
*   **Cultivation of Skillful Qualities:** The Dhamma is taught "for the abandoning of passion, for the abandoning of aversion, for the abandoning of delusion", which are the "roots of what is unskillful". Conversely, the "lack of greed," "lack of aversion," and "lack of delusion" are the "roots of what is skillful".
*   **Development of Mindfulness and Discernment:** Mindfulness, particularly "mindfulness immersed in the body," is described as being "of great fruit & great benefit" and a "governing principle". Discernment is the "surpassing state", crucial for "knowing & seeing" that leads to the "ending of effluents". Wise persons are "endowed with discernment".
*   **Listening to the True Dhamma:** One of the "five rewards in listening to the Dhamma" is that "One's mind grows serene". It is through "hearing the Dhamma" that one can remember and penetrate its meaning. For a disciple with conviction, "the Teacher's message is healing & nourishing".
*   **The Goal of Unbinding:** The ultimate resolution is the "cessation of perception & feeling", leading to the "effluent-free awareness-release & discernment-release". This state is "without clinging or sustenance". The "open doors to the deathless" allow "those with ears" to "show their conviction".
